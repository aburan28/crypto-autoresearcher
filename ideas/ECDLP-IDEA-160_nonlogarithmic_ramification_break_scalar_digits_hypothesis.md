## Falsifiable hypothesis

A public target-uniform lift of the prime-order elliptic subgroup into a tower of local fields has a scalar-sensitive upper/lower ramification-break or field-of-norms invariant that is not a formal logarithm. Successive bounded-cost invariants recover enough digits of `x` from `Q=[x]P` to complete target recovery with time and memory below rho and BSGS, without an `N`-torsion orientation dictionary.

## Mechanism-new operation

The operation is **nonlogarithmic ramification-filtration digit extraction**. It reads jumps or field-of-norms data of a functorially constructed tower rather than mapping the prime-to-`p` subgroup into an additive formal kernel. IDEA-004/129/140 close additive-logarithmic targets; this survives only if one explicit scalar-sensitive ramification invariant avoids those maps and has a typed return to the original scalar.

The theorem-only producer receipt now closes towers functorially generated by the
subgroup, full torsion, or full division fibers. Every nonzero `Q=[x]P` generates
the same subgroup and field as `P`; at good reduction the `N`-primary tower is
unramified, and full `p`-division fibers add only target-independent torsion fields.
Only an explicitly oriented nonfunctorial branch selector remains outside scope.

## Assumptions

1. Public `E/F_p,P,N,Q` with `gcd(N,p)=1` and a canonical lift/local tower are frozen.
2. Tower construction is scalar blind, target uniform, and independent of an `N`-torsion basis or hidden orientation.
3. A computable ramification invariant changes predictably under `[k]` and returns typed scalar digits.
4. Tower degree, discriminant, precision, branch enumeration, digit ambiguity, and conversion back to `Z/NZ` remain sub-rho.
5. Every preprocessing, failed digit, verification, and peak bit-memory cost is charged.

## Complete factor-base-to-target-descent path

1. Freeze `E,P,N,Q`, the lift, tower functor, ramification numbering, precision, digit convention, and verifier.
2. Prove tower construction uses no scalar-labelled torsion basis or orientation advice.
3. For known `[r]P` samples, compute the invariant, derive its exact transformation under multiplication, and fit no post-hoc selector.

6. Apply the identical frozen procedure to `Q` or masked `Q+[t]P`.
7. Remove masks, enumerate all remaining residues, and accept only `x` satisfying `[x]P=Q`.
8. Report tower degree, precision, discriminant, attempts, ambiguity, reconstruction, time, and memory against rho and BSGS.

## Full rho/BSGS cost model

Pollard rho costs `N^(1/2+o(1))` time with constant state; BSGS costs `N^(1/2+o(1))` time and memory. For compatibility with factor-base controls let `B=N^beta`. Let tower setup cost `N^a,N^a_m`; reciprocal usable-invariant and target-digit densities be `N^delta,N^delta_t`; one invariant/digit query cost `N^q,N^q_m`; digit output and final ambiguity be `N^o,N^u`; and any auxiliary factor-log/reconstruction algebra cost `N^ell,N^ell_m`. Then

`lambda=max(a,beta+delta+q+o,ell,delta_t+q+o+u,beta)`

`mu=max(a_m,q_m,beta+o,ell_m,u)`.

These are the complete time and peak-memory exponents.

For a direct digit route the unused factor-base terms may be zero, but tower degree, precision, and all returned branches remain charged.

## Likely fatal obstruction

The producer theorem confirms that functorial full-tower ramification data is constant across every nonzero generator of the prime-order subgroup. A scalar-sensitive value requires a marked branch or orientation not determined by the ramification filtration; constructing and typing that selector is the remaining unsupplied operation.

## Proof track

Construct one public tower and invariant, prove scalar sensitivity without a formal logarithm, bound tower/precision/ambiguity exponents, and give a typed full-scalar reconstruction with `lambda,mu<=0.45`.

## Disproof track

Prove the invariant is constant on the prime-to-`p` orbit, reduce it to an additive map killed by `N`, expose an `N`-degree orientation field, or derive either complete exponent at least `0.5`.

## Positive and negative controls

- Lubin-Tate and field-of-norms towers with known p-primary characters.
- Good-reduction prime-to-p torsion where inertia acts trivially.
- Towers built with a supplied torsion orientation, charged as forbidden advice.
- Rho, BSGS, known-scalar, masked-target, and random-invariant controls.

## Quantitative promotion and falsification gates
