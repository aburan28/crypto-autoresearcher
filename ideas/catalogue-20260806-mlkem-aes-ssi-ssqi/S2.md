### 0.2 Tracked objects and the lossy-projection test

Four objects are shared across entries. Each is stated with its projection, what
it **discards**, and why the discard is structural rather than a change of
coordinates (`docs/inventor-protocol.md` §2). An object recoverable from the
original by an invertible map is a repackaging and is not written down here.

**TO-ORD — the multiplicative type of the acting group's order.**
`(p, Cl(O), E_0, secret d) ↦ the multiset of prime factors of #Cl(O)`, split at a
smoothness bound into (smooth part, rough part).
*Lossy?* Catastrophically. The fibre over a given factorisation contains every
prime `p` with that class number, every base curve, and every secret; `#Cl` is a
single integer and `d` is one of `#Cl` elements. Nothing about the group
elements, the curves, or the isogenies survives.
*Compatible with the target's operations?* Yes, and this is the load-bearing
point: **digit-recursion** hidden-shift algorithms — the family in which Regev's
and Simon's erasure steps live — decompose `Z/N` along a chain of subgroups, and
the chain is a function of `N`'s factorisation alone. How many bits of `d` such
an algorithm can peel is determined by TO-ORD without reference to anything
else.
*Not a change of coordinates.* `#Cl` does not determine `Cl`'s action, `p`, or
`d`; the map is not invertible even up to isomorphism of the acting group,
because it forgets which curve set is acted on.
*Used by:* S2-1 (primary), S2-2 (parameter axis), S2-6 (what the setup must
compute).

**TO-EVAL — the per-branch evaluation profile of the coherent action circuit.**
`(superposition over ideal classes a, base curve E) ↦ the histogram, over
branches of the superposition, of the number of randomized retries and the
number of small-prime isogeny steps the reversible circuit must perform`.
*Lossy?* Yes. The histogram destroys which class, which curve, which isogeny,
and the entire quantum amplitude structure; exponentially many branch
assignments give one histogram.
*Compatible?* Yes, in the only sense that matters for cost: a reversible circuit
has one data-independent depth, so its depth is a functional of the histogram's
**support** (specifically its upper tail), not of any individual branch.
*Not a change of coordinates.* The action `[a]E` is not recoverable from a retry
histogram; the map collapses the entire group action to a distribution on
integers.
*Used by:* S2-3 (primary), S2-9 (as the weight `A` on a query), S2-5 (as the
node cost inside the DAG).

**TO-DAG — the sieve's dependency graph with liveness annotations.**
`(a collimation-sieve execution) ↦ a directed acyclic graph whose nodes are
phase vectors and whose edges are collimation steps, annotated per node with
(birth index, death index) in the classical control schedule`.
*Lossy?* Yes. Labels, residues, moduli, amplitudes and the shift itself are all
discarded; only an interval graph remains.
*Compatible?* Yes. Peak concurrent liveness and longest path are computed from
the DAG alone, and both are exactly the quantities a width cap and a depth cap
act on. Composition of two sieve stages is concatenation of DAGs.
*Not a change of coordinates.* The DAG has no group in it. Two sieves on
different groups with the same schedule have identical DAGs; the execution is
not recoverable.
*Used by:* S2-5 (primary), S2-7 (as the width cap's argument), S2-8 (as where a
charge type attaches).

**TO-CHARGE — the charge-type signature of a cost expression.**
`(a term in a published cost equation) ↦ (resource type ∈ {gate, space,
latency, query}, its argument, its growth order)`, discarding the numeric
coefficient entirely.
*Lossy?* Yes: `36·L̃` and `4D` and `9·4D` all map to `(gate, ·, linear)`; the
constants are gone.
*Compatible?* Yes. Cost composition acts on signatures — a product of
gate-charges is a gate-charge, a gate-charge times a latency is a latency-scaled
gate-charge — so the signature algebra is closed under the operations that
assemble a cost model.
*Not a change of coordinates.* The equation is unrecoverable from its signature;
that is the point, because the question "has this resource been charged at all?"
is a question about signatures and is answerable without any number.
*Used by:* S2-8 (primary), S2-6 (which column a setup cost lands in), S2-9.

## 1. The ten ideas

### S2-1. The smooth part of the class number as a ceiling on every digit-recursion hidden-shift algorithm

**Tracked object + what it discards.** TO-ORD: the factorisation of `#Cl(O)`
into (smooth part, rough part). Discards the group, the curves, the base point,
the secret ideal, and `p` itself. Structural, not coordinates: the fibre over a
factorisation is the entire family of CSIDH instances with that class number.

**Mechanism.** Regev's and Simon's DCP algorithms are *digit recursions*. They
work because `N = 2^n`: the subset sum `z = Σ b_i y_i` splits as `z' + h·2^{n−1}`,
the residual phase is `ω^{z'd}·(−1)^{h·d_n}`, and the top bit `h` carries the
**last bit** of `d` in a relative phase that a Hadamard measurement can read
(`KN-TECH-d1bc4f`; `KN-LIT-e204ab` step 4–5). Recursion to the remaining bits is
the same move one subgroup down. Generalising the split to arbitrary cyclic `N`
requires, for each recursion level, a subgroup of index `q` with `q | N` and a
`q`-ary Fourier measurement in place of the Hadamard; the level's post-selection
and its phase alphabet both change with `q`. **Therefore the number of levels,
and hence the number of bits of `d` a digit recursion can peel, is bounded by
the factorisation of `N`.** For CSIDH the acting group is `Cl(Z[√−p])` with
`p ≡ 3 mod 8`; `disc = −4p` is **not fundamental** (`−p ≡ 1 mod 4`), the order
has conductor 2 in the maximal order of discriminant `−p`, and the conductor
formula gives `#Cl(Z[√−p]) = 3·h(−p)` with `h(−p)` **odd**. So the group order
is odd, divisible by 3, and — on every published CSIDH class number this program
has seen quoted — carries large prime factors. A digit recursion peels
`log2(smooth part)` bits and then faces a hidden shift in a group of large prime
order, where it has no digits left.

**Prediction.** Recoverable-by-digit-recursion `= log2(B-smooth part of #Cl)`
for whatever `B` the recursion can afford a `q`-ary measurement at. For a
CSIDH-512-shaped class number whose small factors are `3 · 37 · 1407181` (the
shape this program has seen quoted, to be **obtained from a committed source or
recorded as a corpus gap**, never asserted from memory), that is
`1.58 + 5.21 + 20.42 ≈ 27.2` bits out of `≈ 257`, leaving a prime-order residual
instance of `≈ 135` bits on which the recursion is inert. **Ceiling consequence,
stated so it cannot be misread: 27 bits is not a break, is not a parameter
threat, and is in any case dominated — the collimation sieve already recovers
*all* `257` bits at `2^{O(√log N)}`, which the incumbent-comparison entry S2-2
prices.**

**Minimal discriminating test.** Zero curve compute, pure integer arithmetic.
(1) Obtain `#Cl(Z[√−p])` for each CSIDH parameter set from a committed in-repo
source; if none exists, record `blocked_no_committed_source` with the named fetch
that would unblock it and proceed on the *derived* form `3·h(−p)` only.
(2) Factor the smooth part at `B ∈ {2^10, 2^20, 2^30}` and report
`log2(smooth)/log2(#Cl)` per parameter set. (3) State, for each `q` in the smooth
part, the modified post-selection probability the `q`-ary level would need
(Simon's all-zero-group event has probability `n^{−c}` for binary `b_i`; the
`q`-ary variant changes the *measurement*, not the `b_i`, so the entry must say
which of the two it is and mark it `derivation_required` if the pinned text does
not settle it).
**Known-answer gate.** Two, both of which catch instrument failure before any
real row is scored. **(G-a)** The class-number counter must reproduce
`h_primitive(−4p) = 3·h(−p)` for every prime `p ≡ 3 mod 8` below 300. A naive
reduced-form counter returns ratio **4**, not 3, because it counts the
imprimitive forms of content 2 — this session hit exactly that failure, so the
gate is known to have discriminating power and is not decorative. An instrument
returning 4 is broken and no row it produces may be read. **(G-b)** Feed the
pipeline `N = 2^n`: the smooth part must be all of `N` and the predicted
recoverable bits must equal `n`, i.e. the machinery must reproduce the source
claim it generalises. Failure voids the entry.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric).** **F-a** If the smooth part of `#Cl` exceeds
`2^{(1/2)·log2 #Cl}` for any proposed CSIDH parameter set, the ceiling is not
binding there and this entry's CLOSED verdict fails for that set. **F-b** If a
`q`-ary level can be constructed for `q` a *large prime* factor — i.e. if the
erasure step does not in fact require the residual phase to live in a small
cyclic alphabet — the tracked object is the wrong invariant and the entry is
dead at the whiteboard. **F-c** If gate G-a returns 4, or G-b fails, the run is
reported as instrument failure, not as a classification.

**Charged cost.** Implementation: one zero-compute arithmetic task, ~120 lines
of standard-library Python (binary-quadratic-form reduction and trial division;

none consumed and none claimed.** Where the entry states a recoverable-bit

**Risk band.** `conservative`.

### S2-2. The polynomial that loses: the sample-exponent crossover locus between a poly-time DCP route and the collimation sieve

**Tracked object + what it discards.** The **charge-exponent pair**
`n ↦ (log2 Q_poly(n), log2 Q_sieve(n))` where `n = log2 #Cl` and `Q` is the count
of coherent group-action oracle evaluations. Discards the algorithms entirely —
their states, their memory models, their correctness arguments, the group, and
the curves — retaining two real numbers per group size. Structural: infinitely
many algorithm pairs share a charge-exponent pair, and neither algorithm is
recoverable from it.

**Mechanism.** Both routes consume the *same* unit: one coherent evaluation of
the class-group action on a superposition. The sieve consumes `2^{c√n}` of them
for an undetermined `c` (`KN-OPEN-014`; `B2-8` exists precisely because `c` is
not recorded anywhere in this corpus). A Simon-shaped route consumes
`Q = k·n^{E}` with `E = c_S + 1`, the draft's stated condition being `c_S ≥ 12`,
`k > c_S` (`KN-LIT-e204ab`). Because the per-evaluation cost is **common to
both**, it cancels in the ratio and the crossover locus is *independent of the
action-evaluation cost* — which is what makes this comparison decidable in a
corpus that does not know that cost. The decision-relevant object is therefore
the curve `E*(n) = (c√n − log2 k)/log2 n`: the largest sample exponent at which
the polynomial route is not dominated.

### S2-3. The coherent-evaluation surcharge: data-independent depth for a randomized Vélu step, charged

**Tracked object + what it discards.** TO-EVAL: the per-branch retry histogram of
the reversible class-group-action circuit. Discards which class, which curve,
which isogeny, and every amplitude; retains a distribution on non-negative
integers. Structural: the action is not recoverable from a histogram, and
exponentially many branch assignments give the same one.

**Mechanism.** Every hidden-shift attack on CSIDH needs `|a⟩|E⟩ ↦ |a⟩|[a]E⟩`
**as a reversible circuit acting on a superposition**. The classical evaluation
is a chain of small-prime isogeny steps; each step needs a point of order `ℓ_i`,
found by sampling a random point `P` and computing `[(p+1)/ℓ_i]P`, which fails
when `P` lies in the `ℓ_i`-divisible part — probability `≈ 1/ℓ_i` per trial.
Classically you retry until success and the *expected* number of trials is
`1/(1 − 1/ℓ_i)`, which is `1.50` at `ℓ = 3` and `1.00` at `ℓ = 587`. **A
reversible circuit has one depth for all branches.** It must therefore budget
`k_i` trials such that the residual failure mass over the whole attack is
negligible, and "negligible" is set by the *query count*: a branch that fails is
a branch whose action output is wrong, and a wrong output in one branch of one
coset state corrupts the phase relation the sieve is built on. There is no free
post-selection: measuring the failure flag collapses the state, and not measuring
it leaves the failure amplitude in the register. **This surcharge appears in no
cost equation in this corpus.**

**Prediction.** The budget is `k_i = log2(Q·n_primes/ε)/log2(ℓ_i)`, so the
per-step surcharge factor `k_i/E[trials]` is **largest at the smallest primes and
decays in `ℓ`** — the canonical decay tell, present by construction, so its
absence in measurement is an instrument failure. At a `2^{−40}` residual failure
budget: `ℓ = 3` needs `25.2` trials against a mean of `1.50` (a `16.8×` factor
on that step); `ℓ = 5` needs `17.2` against `1.25`; `ℓ = 7` needs `14.2` against
`1.17`; `ℓ = 587` needs `4.3` against `1.00`. At `2^{−96}`: `60.6 / 41.3 / 34.2 /
10.4`. Weighted by each prime's share of the total step count and by `O(ℓ)`
Vélu cost per step, the **predicted total surcharge on the action circuit's
depth is 3–7 bits at a `2^{−40}` budget and 5–9 bits at `2^{−96}`**, growing
logarithmically in the query count — i.e. **the surcharge grows as the attack
gets bigger**, which is the direction that matters and the opposite of a
constant-factor nuisance.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Small pure-Python, no curves needed for the
primary arm. (1) Instantiate the retry model per prime with the exact failure
probability, compute `k_i(ε, Q)` and the weighted depth budget against the
weighted expected depth, for the CSIDH-512-shaped prime list and for two larger
shapes, at `ε ∈ {2^{−40}, 2^{−64}, 2^{−96}}` and `Q ∈ {2^{20}, 2^{40}, 2^{64}}`.
(2) Report the surcharge in bits with the `(ε, Q)` grid visible, never a single
number. (3) Second arm, optional and clearly separated: a toy `F_p` curve check
at `p` of 20–30 bits that the empirical failure rate per trial matches `1/ℓ`.
**Known-answer gate.** At `ℓ = 2` the failure probability is exactly `1/2` and
`k = log2(Q·n/ε)` **exactly**, a closed form with no approximation. The
instrument must reproduce it to the last bit. Second gate: at `ε = 1` (no
reliability demanded) the budget must collapse to `k = 1` and the surcharge to
`0.00` bits — a degenerate corner with a known answer. A tool that reports a
nonzero surcharge at `ε = 1` is reading its own defaults.
**Null / decay control.** Surcharge must **decrease monotonically in `ℓ`** and
**increase logarithmically in `Q`**. A surcharge invariant in `ℓ` is the
canonical artifact signature and voids the run.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric).** **F-a** Total surcharge below `1.0` bit at
`ε = 2^{−40}`, `Q = 2^{40}` → the lever is a rounding error and the entry closes
as "charged and negligible", which is still a useful line in a cost table but
not a finding. **F-b** Surcharge not growing in `Q` → the "grows with the attack"

**Charged cost.** Implementation: ~150 lines of standard-library Python; the

### S2-4. Pooled multi-target collimation: does the shared class group buy a `√T`, as it does classically?

**Tracked object + what it discards.** The **target-difference lattice
transcript**: a pooled multi-target sieve execution `↦` for each phase vector,
the pair (collimated residue modulus, the integer vector `v ∈ Z^T` recording
which targets' coset states were combined into it, with signs). Discards the
residues' values, the amplitudes, every curve, every ideal, and all `T` secrets.
Structural: the vector `v` lives in `Z^T` while the state lives in a space of
dimension `#Cl`; the execution is not recoverable, and `v` is invariant under
relabelling every group element.

**Mechanism.** Classically, `T` discrete logs in one generic group of order `N`
cost `√(NT)`, not `T√N` — a `√T` saving from pooling (the Kuhn–Struik shape,
carried in this corpus's `A4` slice for ECDLP). The CSIDH analogue is exactly
posed: `T` public keys `[a_1]E_0, …, [a_T]E_0` are `T` hidden shifts **in the
same group, from the same base point**, and the class group and its relation
lattice are shared. A phase vector built from target `i`'s coset states carries a
character of `d_i`; **collimating a vector from target `i` against one from
target `j` produces a phase carrying `d_i − d_j`.** So a pooled sieve naturally
computes the difference lattice `⟨d_i − d_j⟩ ⊂ Cl`, of rank `T − 1`, and one
absolute recovery then yields all `T`. The question with a definite answer is
whether the *total* charge to recover the rank-`(T−1)` difference lattice plus
one absolute shift is sublinear in `T`.

**Prediction (direction and metric stated).** Two readings predict opposite
things. **(H-amortise)** total charge scales as `T^{α}` with `α < 1` — because
the sieve's dominant cost is building structure in the *group*, which is shared,
and target identity is a cheap tag. **(H-linear)** total charge scales as
`α ≈ 1` — because each phase vector's phase is a character of exactly one
difference, and there are `T − 1` independent differences to resolve, each
needing its own sieve to the same depth. This slice's prior is **H-linear**, and
the entry is written to make that verdict cheap and reportable: if CSIDH has no
multi-target amortisation, then CSIDH sizing needs **no multi-target margin** —
a genuinely useful negative, and the opposite of the ECDLP situation.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Small pure-Python, label-only, no quantum, no
curves, no isogenies — the `KN-LIT-127` "classical simulator" move applied to a
pooled instance. (1) Implement the collimation recursion on **labels and a
target tag only** (residues in `Z/N` for toy `N`, a target index vector in
`Z^T`), with the standard combine-and-collimate step extended to record `v`.
(2) Measure total consumed coset states to resolve all `T` shifts, for
`T ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}` at fixed toy `N`, five seeds each. (3) Fit `α` in
charge `∝ T^α` and report with its confidence interval.
**Known-answer gate.** At `T = 1` the simulator must reproduce the single-target
charge of the **same simulator** exactly (identical seed, identical schedule) —
a self-consistency gate that catches a tag-handling bug. Second gate: plant
`d_1 = d_2` (two identical targets); the difference sieve must resolve
`d_1 − d_2 = 0` in **one** step, a known answer. If it does not, the difference
channel is not being read and no `α` may be reported.
**Null control.** Run the identical pooled simulator on `T` **independent**
groups (each target in its own `Z/N`, no shared structure). There, amortisation
is impossible by construction and the fit **must** return `α = 1.00 ± tolerance`.
A simulator returning `α < 1` on the null is reading its own bookkeeping, and
the real run is void.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric).** **F-a** `α ≥ 0.95` on the real pooled run
with the null at `1.00` → **CLOSED**: no multi-target amortisation for CSIDH,
recorded with its mechanism (each independent difference needs its own sieve
depth). **F-b** `α ≤ 0.75` → amortisation exists at toy scale and the entry
becomes a live lever requiring a crypto-scale conditional statement; it still
claims nothing about parameters. **F-c** Null returning `α < 0.95` → instrument
failure, nothing read. **F-d** If the combine step cannot be defined on mixed-target
inputs without an unstated assumption about the coset states' relative phases,
the object is ill-posed and the entry dies at the whiteboard — check this first.

**Charged cost.** Implementation: ~200 lines standard-library Python; pre-register
`N`, `T`, seeds and the collimation modulus schedule. Compute: minutes at toy
`N`. **Quantum charging:** the simulator models **labels only**. It does **not**
model qubit width (which for a pooled sieve is at least `T`× the target tag plus
the per-vector `≈ log #Cl` label register), T-depth, QRACM, or the action
evaluations, and **no memory, qubit, or depth claim follows from this entry and
none may be attributed to it.** The charge measured is coset states consumed —
one resource, named.

**Ceiling.** `toy`. Explicitly toy-scale and explicitly labelled as such; never
presentable as crypto-scale validation (`AGENTS.md` rule 7). The `α` is a fitted
exponent under one simulator, not a security statement.

**Risk band.** `high_risk` — the difference-channel construction may not survive
the relative-phase question in F-d, and that is the entry's declared weakest link.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `non_uniform_preprocessing_model|
for_all_p_exists_advice_A_p_of_size_S|one_prime_per_security_level_amortised_across_all_keys|
(S,T)_frontier_for_OneEnd|changes_the_quantifier_of_all_twelve_closures`
(`IDEA-20260806-9c2f80`).
**Why it is not that.** 9c2f80 is a **classical non-uniform advice** model for

### S2-5. Width or depth? The collimation sieve's dependency DAG, its peak liveness, and its critical path under a MAXDEPTH cap

**Tracked object + what it discards.** TO-DAG: the sieve's execution as an
annotated DAG with per-node (birth, death) intervals. Discards labels, residues,
moduli, amplitudes, the group and the shift. Structural: two sieves on different
groups with the same schedule have **identical** DAGs, so the execution is
provably unrecoverable.

**Mechanism.** The corpus records the collimation sieve's headline as
"exponentially less quantum memory" (`KN-LIT-127` via `KN-TECH-051`) but records
no qubit width at all — `B2-7` had to carry width as an explicit `unknown`
column, and was screened REFUTED on other grounds. Width is not an unknown of the
*paper*; it is a **computable functional of the recursion's traversal order**,
and so is depth. A depth-first traversal of a depth-`d` combination tree holds
`d + 1` live vectors; a breadth-first traversal holds `2^d`. The pinned text's
own hard cap `L̃_max = 8L` enforced by partial measurement (Figure 1) is a
statement about *list length*, which is a different quantity from *peak
concurrent live phase vectors*, and the two have never been distinguished in this
corpus. Separately, the **critical path** — the longest chain of
data-dependent collimations — is what a decoherence or MAXDEPTH constraint acts
on, and it is likewise a DAG functional. **These two numbers decide which of the
three `KN-TECH-051` positions the sieve actually occupies.**

**Prediction.** Under the source's own traversal, peak liveness is `Θ(d)` (not
`Θ(L̃)`), so **peak qubit width is `Θ(d · log #Cl)` — logarithmic in the total
cost, which is what "exponentially less quantum memory" means when made
precise** — while the critical path is `Θ(number of collimation steps)`, i.e.
**the same order as the total gate count**, because each collimation consumes
its predecessors. Consequence, which is the decision-relevant half: the sieve is
**depth-bound, not width-bound**. A MAXDEPTH cap of `2^{40}` therefore binds
whenever the sieve's step count exceeds `2^{40}`, and it binds *without any
width relief being available*, because there is nothing to parallelise along a
chain. That is the opposite of Grover, where a depth cap is paid for in
parallel instances at a `√` rate.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Zero quantum compute; small pure Python.
(1) Write the recursion's data dependencies from the committed locators
(Eq. (4.1)'s tree-traversal derivation, Figure 1's `L̃_max = 8L` enforcement)
and, where the locators do not determine the traversal order, enumerate the
**two** admissible orders rather than guessing one, reporting both columns.
(2) Compute peak concurrent liveness and longest path for `(d, δ)` on a
pre-registered grid. (3) Report the pair `(peak width in vectors, critical path
in collimations)` and locate each `KN-TECH-051` position by **region**, never by
number.
**Known-answer gate.** Combinatorially exact and independently known: on a
complete binary tree of depth `d`, depth-first traversal must return peak
liveness exactly `d + 1` and breadth-first exactly `2^d`; longest path must be
exactly `d` for both. The instrument must return those three integers on the bare
tree before it is pointed at the sieve's DAG. A liveness analyser that misses
`d + 1` is broken and its sieve numbers are void.
**Null control.** Apply the identical analyser to a **fully parallel** DAG (all
nodes independent, no edges): it must return critical path `1` and peak liveness
`= node count`. An analyser that inflates the critical path of an
embarrassingly-parallel graph is mis-applied and the sieve result may not be read.

**Falsifier (reachable, categorical + numeric).** **F-a** Peak liveness `Θ(L̃)`
rather than `Θ(d)` → the sieve is width-bound, the "exponentially less quantum
memory" reading is about list *storage* rather than live qubits, and the
prediction is refuted. **F-b** Critical path `O(d · polylog)` → MAXDEPTH never
binds, and the depth-bound claim is refuted. **F-c** If the committed locators
admit **more than two** traversal orders with materially different liveness, the
quantity is under-determined by the pinned text; that is a legitimate outcome and
must be reported as `under-determined, N orders enumerated` — but note that this
is **not** a source-hunting request: it must not trigger another pass over the
FC0 obligation ledger (OL-1).

**Charged cost.** Implementation: ~180 lines standard-library Python (interval
liveness + longest path on a DAG); zero quantum compute; zero network (source in
repo). **Charging:** the deliverable **is** a charge — qubit width in vectors and
depth in collimations. It converts to physical qubits only by multiplying by
`log #Cl + ancilla`, and the entry states the ancilla as `unknown` rather than
assuming zero. No T-count, no QRACM bits, no security level is produced.

**Ceiling.** `toy` for the measured DAG functionals at pre-registered `(d, δ)`;
`control` for the symbolic statement about traversal orders. **No CSIDH security
bit, no NIST level, no parameter size.**

**Risk band.** `representation_changing` — it replaces the scalar cost figure
that every entry in this corpus manipulates with a two-dimensional
(width, critical-path) object, and the two can move in opposite directions.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `eq3.5_QRACM_bits_times_eq4.1_T_gates_frontier|
(T-gate,QRACM-bit,oracle-query)_surface|qubit_width_as_explicit_unknown_column|
delta->0_and_d=1_boundary_degeneracy_control` (`B2-7`, screened REFUTED by all

**Why it is not that.** B2-7 assembles the two published equations into a

### S2-7. The classical–quantum budget split on a group action is a corner solution — except under a width cap, where the corner moves computably

**Tracked object + what it discards.** The **budget point**
`(k, m)` = (number of bits of the shift resolved by classical or Grover search,
size of the residual group the sieve is run on). Discards which bits, which
subgroup, which coset representatives, the entire algebra of the action, and both
algorithms' internal state. Structural: a budget point is two integers; the
attack is not recoverable, and every group of the same order gives the same point.

**Mechanism.** The natural hybrid is: resolve `k` bits of `d` by search, run the
hidden-shift algorithm on the residual instance of size `2^{n−k}`. Total charge
`2^{k}` (classical) or `2^{k/2}` (Grover) plus `2^{c√(n−k)}`. The second term is a
**square root in the exponent**, so it decreases in `k` at rate
`c/(2√(n−k))` — a vanishing marginal return — while the first grows at rate `1`
(or `1/2`). **The surface therefore has no interior optimum and the sieve is a
corner solution at `k = 0`.** That is a ceiling on an entire family of proposals,
and it is derivable in three lines. The non-trivial half is what happens when a
resource cap binds: if peak qubit width is capped at `W` and the sieve's width
grows with `n` (S2-5 supplies the growth law), then the largest residual instance
the sieve may be run on is fixed by `W`, and `k*(W)` is determined — the corner
moves to a computable interior point **driven entirely by the cap**, not by any
efficiency trade.

**Prediction.** (i) Uncapped: `k* = 0` for every `(n, c)` with `c ≤ 2` and
`n ≥ 64`, for both classical and Grover presearch, with the Grover variant no
closer to an interior optimum than the classical one — the `√` in Grover does not
change the sign of the comparison, only its magnitude. (ii) Capped: `k*(W)` is
the unique `k` solving `width(n − k) = W`, and the resulting total is
`2^{k*(W)/2} + 2^{c√(n−k*(W))}` with the **first** term dominant for small `W`
— i.e. under a tight width cap, a CSIDH attack degenerates toward brute-force
search and the sieve's subexponential advantage evaporates. That is a statement
about the *shape* of the resource-constrained position (`KN-LIT-129`) that the
corpus can make without any of its unverified numbers.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Zero compute; three-line calculus plus a
tabulation. (1) Differentiate the uncapped total in `k`, exhibit that the
derivative is positive for all `k ≥ 0` in the declared `(n, c)` range, and
tabulate the total at `k ∈ {0, 8, 16, 32, 64}` to show the corner. (2) Take the
width growth law from S2-5 (or, if S2-5 has not run, from **both** admissible
traversal orders, reported as two columns) and solve for `k*(W)` at
`W ∈ {2^{10}, 2^{20}, 2^{30}}` logical qubits.
**Known-answer gate.** Two degenerate corners with analytically known answers:
at `k = 0` the total must reproduce the pinned sieve cost **exactly** (same `c`,
same `n`), and at `k = n` it must reproduce brute-force `2^n` (classical) or
`2^{n/2}` (Grover) **exactly**. A surface that misses either endpoint is
mis-assembled. Note the `INDEX.md` §2 screen explicitly: the gate must be
evaluated where the readings **disagree**, so a third evaluation is added at the
`k` where the two terms are within one bit of each other — if no such `k` exists
in range, that fact is itself the corner-solution finding and is reported as
such rather than as a passed gate.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric).** **F-a** An interior optimum in the uncapped
surface at any `(n, c)` in range → the corner-solution claim is refuted. **F-b**

### S2-8. Is the pinned model's `4D` T-gates per lookup already an access charge? A charge-type audit upstream of every QRAM-latency proposal

`(resource type, argument, growth order)` of each term in the pinned cost

is not recoverable from its signature, which is exactly what makes "has this

**Mechanism.** The committed locators record Eq. (4.1) as `36·L̃·(2/(1−δ))^d`
T-gates with a derivation charging **nine lookups into QRACM of `D` indexable
cells at `4D` T-gates per lookup** (`page_equation_mapping.yaml`,
`EQ_4_1_SIEVE_T_GATES`, `EQ_4_1_D_BOUND_DERIVATION`). A `4D` charge for one
lookup into a `D`-cell table is **linear in the table size** — which is the
fanout cost of a *sequential* access, not the `polylog(D)` an idealised QRACM is
usually credited with. If that reading is right, the pinned model is

double-charging. All three are falsifiable from committed text alone.

### S2-9. Re-optimising `(d, δ)` under a per-query action weight: does the sieve's published operating point survive charging the group action?

**Tracked object + what it discards.** The **weighted operating point**
`(d, δ) ↦ total(d, δ; A) = gates(d, δ) + A · queries(d, δ)`, where `A` is the
charged cost of one coherent class-group-action evaluation (S2-3's deliverable).
Discards the sieve's state, the group, the curves, and the value of the total —
retaining only the **argmin location** as a function of `A`. Structural: the
argmin is a point in a 2-D parameter box; the algorithm is not recoverable from
it, and two algorithms with different totals can share an argmin trajectory.

**Mechanism.** The pinned analysis optimises the sieve's own gate count over
`(d, δ)`. But the attacker pays `gates + A·queries`, and `A` for CSIDH is large:
a coherent action evaluation is a chain of `Θ(n_primes)` Vélu steps, each itself
a non-trivial circuit, with S2-3's data-independent-depth surcharge on top. In
the published objective `A` is effectively `1` (or absent). If `A` is `2^{20}` or
`2^{40}`, the optimiser is minimising a **different function**, and the argmin
moves in a computable direction: `queries` and `gates` depend on `(d, δ)` through
`L̃` and `(2/(1−δ))^d` respectively, so weighting queries up pushes the optimum
toward parameter choices that trade gates for fewer queries. **If the argmin
moves by even one unit in `d`, every concrete estimate computed at the published
operating point is computed at the wrong point** — which is a cost-model finding
requiring no new algorithm and no security claim.

**Prediction.** The argmin `(d*, δ*)` moves monotonically in `A`, with `d*`
**increasing** and `δ*` **decreasing** as `A` grows (deeper trees consume fewer
initial coset states per unit of collimation, so a heavier query price buys more
tree). Direction stated so it can be wrong. Magnitude: at least one integer step
in `d*` somewhere in `A ∈ [2^0, 2^{40}]`, and the entry reports the exact `A` at
which each step occurs — a **threshold list**, which is a sharper deliverable
than a single re-optimised point and is robust to `c` being unknown.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Zero-to-trivial compute. (1) Write
`total(d, δ; A)` from committed locators only, marking any coefficient not in the
locators as `unknown` and carrying it symbolically rather than assuming a value.
(2) Grid-minimise over a pre-registered `(d, δ)` box for
`log2 A ∈ {0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40}`, reporting the argmin and the `A`-thresholds at
which it steps. (3) Report `d(log2 total)/d(log2 A)` at each optimum, so
sensitivity is visible and not just the location.
**Known-answer gate.** At `A = 0` the optimiser must reproduce the **published**
argmin exactly — i.e. the machinery must recover the operating point it
generalises before it is trusted to move it. Second gate at the degenerate
corners `δ → 0` and `d = 1`, where the objective must reduce to its known trivial
forms (`B2-7`'s boundary control, reused here because it is a genuinely good
control even though B2-7's frontier was refuted). **Screen check applied:** the
`A = 0` gate is evaluated where the two readings (weighted vs unweighted)
**coincide by construction**, so it is a *reproduction* gate, not a
discrimination gate; the discriminating evaluation is the threshold list at
`A > 0`, and the entry says so rather than claiming a PASS on an unexercised
branch (`INDEX.md` §2, the `48e258` lesson).

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric).** **F-a** Argmin invariant over
`log2 A ∈ [0, 40]` → the query weight does not move the operating point, the
"action cost is the bottleneck for parameter selection" thesis is refuted, and
the honest residual is that `A` moves the *total* but not the *choice*.
**F-b** Argmin moves in the **opposite** direction (`d*` decreasing in `A`) → the
stated mechanism is backwards. **F-c** If `queries(d, δ)` cannot be separated
from `gates(d, δ)` in the committed locators — i.e. if the source's query count
is not a distinct function of `(d, δ)` — the objective cannot be written and the
entry dies; this is exactly `B2-9`'s two-account question, so S2-9 declares a
dependency on it rather than assuming the separation.

**Charged cost.** Implementation: one near-zero-compute symbolic task plus a
grid minimisation in plain Python (no SageMath). Compute: seconds. **Charging:**
`A` is imported from S2-3 as a **depth** figure and converted to the objective's
units only with the conversion stated; if S2-3 has not run, `A` is swept as a
free parameter over 12 orders of magnitude and no specific value is claimed.
QRACM bits, qubit width and critical path are **not** in this objective and the
entry says so — a three-resource optimum is a different and larger problem, and
pretending otherwise would be the uncharged-resource defect in reverse.

**Ceiling.** `control`. A threshold list for an argmin under a swept weight,
computed from committed locators in the source's own variables. **No security
bit, no NIST level, no parameter size, and explicitly no claim that the published
parameters are wrong** — an argmin shift under a *hypothetical* weight is not a
statement that anyone's analysis is in error.

**Risk band.** `high_risk` — the entry's whole content evaporates under F-c, and
F-c is a real possibility given that only three of twenty-five pages carry
committed locators.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `eq3.5_QRACM_bits_times_eq4.1_T_gates_frontier|…`
(`B2-7`).
**Why it is not that.** B2-7 maps the surface at the published parameters and was
refuted for re-assembling what Eq. (4.1) already assembles. S2-9 does not build a
frontier: it **moves the operating point** under a weight the source does not

### S2-10. The key box is not uniform on the class group: an entropy-deficit ceiling on every "exploit short exponent vectors" quantum lever

**Tracked object + what it discards.** The **pushforward density**
`μ = π_*(Unif(box))` where `box = {e ∈ Z^{n_primes} : |e_i| ≤ m}` and
`π : Z^{n_primes} → Z^{n_primes}/Λ ≅ Cl` is reduction modulo the relation
lattice. Discards the exponent vector, the ideal, the curve, and the isogeny;
retains a probability measure on an abstract cyclic group. Structural and
strongly lossy: the fibre of `π` over a class contains `|box|/#Cl` vectors on
average, and `μ` is invariant under every automorphism of the labelling.

**Mechanism.** Kuperberg-family analyses treat the hidden shift as **uniform on
`Cl`**. CSIDH's secret is not: it is uniform on a **box** of small exponents, and
the box's image under `π` is uniform only if the box tiles a fundamental domain
of `Λ` exactly, which it does not. So there is a real, computable **entropy
deficit** `Δ = log2 #Cl − H(μ)` — and this is the quantity that bounds *every*
proposal of the form "exploit the shortness of CSIDH keys quantumly". No such
lever can gain more than `Δ` bits, because `Δ` is exactly the information the
non-uniformity carries. Writing `Δ` down converts an open-ended family of
proposals into a single number with a threshold.

**Prediction.** For a lattice of the CSIDH shape (`det Λ = #Cl ≈ √p`,
`n_primes ≈ 74` at CSIDH-512, box half-width `m` chosen so `(2m+1)^{n_primes} ≈
#Cl`), the deficit is **small in absolute terms and shrinking in relative
terms**: `Δ = O(log n_primes)` bits, i.e. single digits, against `log2 #Cl ≈ 256`.
Consequence, stated as the ceiling it is: **every "short-key" quantum lever is
capped at a few bits and is therefore dominated by the sieve's own exponent
uncertainty (`c` unknown across `[1.0, 2.0]` is worth tens of bits at these
sizes).** Expected verdict: **CLOSED**, declared in advance.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Small pure Python, no curves, no isogenies.
(1) Build toy relation lattices of the CSIDH shape at
`n_primes ∈ {6, 8, 10, 12}` with `det Λ` matched to `(2m+1)^{n_primes}` and a
basis profile matched to the reduced CSIDH basis's aspect ratio (which must be
**declared as an assumption**, since this corpus holds no committed CSIDH basis).
(2) Enumerate the box exactly, reduce modulo `Λ`, tabulate `μ`, and compute
`H(μ)` and `Δ`, plus `max μ / mean μ`. (3) Fit `Δ` against `n_primes` and report
the growth law with its extrapolation to `n_primes = 74` **labelled as an
extrapolation, not a measurement**.
**Known-answer gate.** For the **orthogonal** lattice `Λ = diag(2m+1, …, 2m+1)`
the box tiles a fundamental domain exactly, so `μ` is exactly uniform and
`Δ = 0.000` and `max/mean = 1.000` — a closed form the instrument must reproduce
to the last reported digit. Second gate: for `Λ = diag(2(2m+1), …)` (box covers
half of each fundamental cell) the deficit is exactly `n_primes` bits by
construction. An instrument that misses either is void; the two gates together
also fix the sign convention, which is the standard failure mode for an entropy
computation.
**Null / decay control.** `Δ` must **decrease** as the box is enlarged toward
covering several fundamental domains, and must **increase** as the lattice basis
is made more skew at fixed determinant. Invariance under either is the canonical
artifact signature and voids the run.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric).** **F-a** Extrapolated `Δ ≥ 16` bits at
`n_primes = 74` → the deficit is materially exploitable in principle and the
CLOSED verdict fails; the entry then becomes a live open question, not a ceiling.
**F-b** `Δ < 1` bit at every tested size with both gates passing → **CLOSED**
with a mechanism, and the whole "short keys help the quantum attacker" family is
capped below one bit. **F-c** If the basis-profile assumption in step (1) drives
the answer — detectable by re-running at two materially different declared
profiles and seeing `Δ` move by more than the fitted growth — the result is an
artifact of an unsourced assumption and must be reported as
`assumption_dominated`, not as a ceiling.

**Charged cost.** Implementation: ~200 lines standard-library Python (exact box
enumeration and modular reduction at toy sizes; no lattice reduction library, no
SageMath). Compute: minutes; box enumeration is `(2m+1)^{n_primes}` so
`n_primes ≤ 12` is a hard budget bound and is pre-registered as such.
**Charging:** the deliverable is an **information-theoretic bound in bits**, and
the entry states explicitly that a bound in bits is **not** a cost: converting
`Δ` bits of structure into `Δ` bits of attack saving requires an algorithm that
uses it, which this entry neither supplies nor assumes. That gap is the ceiling's
whole content — it caps the family **from above** without asserting anything is
achievable.

**Ceiling.** `toy` for the measured deficits at `n_primes ≤ 12`; `control` for
the ceiling statement. Explicitly not crypto-scale validation; the `n_primes = 74`
figure is an extrapolation and is labelled one everywhere it appears.

**Risk band.** `representation_changing` — it replaces "the hidden shift is
uniform on `Cl`", which every analysis in this corpus assumes, with a measured
density on `Cl`, and the two are different objects even when the numbers turn out
close.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `csidh_classical_floor_row|
MITM_on_exponent_vectors_|Cl|^{2/3}_full_cost|
vOW_distinguished_point_on_class_group_action_|Cl|^{1/2}|
variable_cost_per_step_action|structure_free_surrogate_recovery_must_fail`
(`B2-10`).
**Why it is not that.** B2-10 uses the **size** of the exponent-vector space to
derive a classical full-cost floor; S2-10 forms the **distribution** that space
induces on `Cl` and computes its entropy deficit, converting an open family of
"exploit short keys" proposals into one capped number. B2-10 never forms `μ`, and
its exponents are unaffected by whatever `Δ` turns out to be.

`sota_delta: 0` / `novelty_status: unverified`
