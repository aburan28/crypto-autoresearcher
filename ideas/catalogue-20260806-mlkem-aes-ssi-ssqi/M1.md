# M1 — ML-KEM (FIPS 203) idea catalogue: lattice-algorithmic mechanisms — reduction shape, sieve internals, decoding, and what each is worth in bits

Slice: mechanisms **inside the lattice attack**. BKZ / progressive-BKZ profile shape, sieve
internals and their memory hierarchy, the dual independence heuristic, module structure as
*reduction dynamics* rather than as an algebra, hybrid guessing/lattice dimension trade-offs,
the q-ary and Kannan-embedding structure, the CBD and FIPS-203-compression error laws, and
reduction preprocessing amortised across **block indices** (not across ciphertexts, not across
keys).

**No entry below claims, predicts or implies a break, a key recovery against a fielded
parameter set, an asymptotic exponent improvement, or beating published state of the art.**
Claim ceilings are `control`, `toy` or `medium`. `eprint.iacr.org`, `arXiv` and `nist.gov` are
unreachable from this session, so **novelty is not adjudicated in either direction**: nothing
here is asserted new and nothing here is dismissed as known. Every entry carries
`sota_delta: 0` and `novelty_status: unverified`.

Three of the ten (**M1-2, M1-3, M1-4**) are **ceiling arguments whose expected verdict is
CLOSED**. That is deliberate and it is the point of writing them (AGENTS.md rule 8): a lever
priced at "under 0.3 core-SVP bits, here is the derivation" is a permanent result, and a lever
left unpriced is a permanent liability.

### 0.2 Tracked objects and the lossy-projection test

Four objects carry the slice. Each is stated with the map, the discard, and the argument that
the discard is **structural** rather than a change of coordinates.

**TO-PROFILE — the log Gram–Schmidt profile.**
`B |-> l(B) = (log2 ||b*_1||, ..., log2 ||b*_d||)`, and its derived *shape class*
(count of entries at `log2 q`, count at 0, slope of the middle segment).

- *Lossy?* Yes. The fibre over one profile contains an `O(d)` orbit times a `GL_d(Z)` stabiliser
  action; two **non-isometric** lattices can share a profile exactly (any `q`-ary lattice and a
  suitably scaled random one). The lattice is not recoverable, so this is not a change of
  coordinates.
- *Why the discard is structural, not cosmetic.* **The cost model of BKZ is a function of the
  profile alone; its output is not.** Every published core-SVP number, every simulator, and the
  `2016 estimate` success condition are functionals of `l(B)`. So the profile is exactly the
  layer at which cost claims live, and the discarded fibre is exactly the layer at which
  *correctness* lives. An idea that moves `l(B)` moves every cost row; an idea that moves only
  the fibre moves none of them.
- *Used by:* M1-1, M1-2, M1-6, M1-7, M1-9.

**TO-COUNT — the counted-operation trace of a sieve.**
`(sieve run) |-> (P = inner products computed, R = pool replacements accepted, T = distinct pool
entries touched, {w_b} = per-bucket working-set sizes)`.

- *Lossy?* Yes, and in the direction that matters: it discards every vector, so no geometry
  survives; and it discards the clock, so **no machine survives**.
- *Why the discard is structural.* This program's sieve measurements were blocked by an
  emulation confound — `bgj1` cross-run `T_N` variance excess ratio 1.39 at `p = 0.001`,
  unresolved between genuine and QEMU artifact (`EV-MLKEM-d8f627`, `EV-MLKEM-4a9cfe`).
  A counted trace is **invariant under emulation, compiler and clock by construction**, so the
  confound that stopped the previous campaign cannot reach it. That is not a convenience; it is
  the reason the object is chosen.
- *Used by:* M1-5, M1-6.

**TO-LAW — the per-coordinate error law as an *observed-conditional* family.**
`(instance, public transcript) |-> {P(delta_i | o_i)}_{i<=d}`, where `o_i` is a quantity the
attacker actually reads off the wire.

- *Lossy?* Yes: it discards the realised errors entirely and keeps only a family of laws indexed
  by observables. For M1-8 it degenerates further, to the **index set** `{i : o_i pins delta_i}`
  — a subset of `[d]`, nothing more.
- *Why the discard is structural.* The cost models this program has audited consume exactly one
  number per instance, `sigma`. Replacing a single law by an observation-indexed family is a
  change in the *type* of the input, not in its value, and it cannot be undone by rescaling.
- *Used by:* M1-3, M1-4, M1-8.

**TO-GRAM — the pool's Gram spectrum and the deficit's interaction residual.**
`X (N x m pool) |-> spec((1/N) X^T X)`, and in M1-10 the further map to the two-factor
interaction residual of `log D(pool, candidates)`.

- *Lossy?* Yes: the spectrum discards the `O(m)` frame and all `N` labels; the interaction
  residual additionally discards **both main effects**.
- *Why the discard is structural.* `EV-MLKEM-2cd08b` localised this campaign's only surviving
  residual to "the ALIGNMENT between the pool's Gram matrix and the candidate phase matrix" and
  showed `ROWPERM` is a complete decomposition because `closed_form_cov` depends on `X` only
  through `X X^T` and row norms. So the Gram *is* the sufficient statistic already established
  by the repo; retaining only its spectrum, and then only the interaction, is a further,
  irreversible coarsening of an object the repo proved sufficient.

### 0.3 CEILING-M1 — binds every entry, stated first rather than as a hedge

Every mechanism in this slice enters the cost through exactly one of three additive budgets,
and **none of them can move an exponent**:

- **Budget A — the log-norm / log-volume budget.** An entry that improves the attacker's
  effective `log2 ||(e,s)||` by `dL` bits buys `dbeta = dL / (d/dbeta)[(2beta - d - 1) log2 delta_0(beta)]`
  and then `0.292 * dbeta` core-SVP bits. Under `delta_0(beta) = (beta/(2 pi e) (pi beta)^{1/beta})^{1/(2(beta-1))}`
  this derivative is **0.013461 / 0.011206 / 0.009647** at `(d, beta) = (1000, 400) / (1200, 500) /
  (1400, 600)`, so `dbeta/dL = 74.29 / 89.24 / 103.66`.
- **The slice screening constant.** A **per-coordinate** improvement of `v` bits of log2-variance
  gives `dL = v/2`, hence
  > **`core-SVP bits = K * v`, with `K = 10.85 / 13.03 / 15.14` at the three shapes above.**
  > Equivalently **`v* = 1/K ≈ 0.066 to 0.092` bits per coordinate is the price of ONE core-SVP
  > bit.** Any mechanism in this slice whose per-coordinate information gain is below `v*` is
  > worth under one bit and must say so on the face of its own entry.
  This is a **model readout under a named cost model** (GSA/`delta_0` closed form + core-SVP
  0.292), not a measurement; the three-shape table above IS its sensitivity table, and the three
  `(d, beta)` shapes are illustrative brackets around the ML-KEM family, **not** FIPS parameter
  values. Recomputing `K` at whatever `(d, beta)` an entry actually uses is three lines and is
  required of that entry.
- **Budget B — an additive combinatorial term** (hybrid guessing, enumeration). Bits, never an
  exponent; and its exponent-per-guessed-coordinate is a Rényi quantity, not a Shannon one
  (M1-3).
- **Budget C — a constant-factor charge on the reduction itself** (tour count, warm starts,
  free lifts). Bits, never an exponent, and always paid against a memory charge (rule 6).

**Nothing in this slice touches 0.292 or 0.2075.** Both are exponents of a sieve this slice does
not redesign. And per `RQ-MLKEM-003.claim_tier_ceiling` and AGENTS.md rule 7, **any entry whose
evidence is measured at reduced dimension is capped at `toy` and may make no statement about
FIPS 203 parameter sets.**

## 1. The ten ideas

### M1-1. The Z-shape signature `(n_q, n_1)` and the displacement of the optimal sample count `m`

**Tracked object + what it discards** — TO-PROFILE, coarsened to **two integers**:
`n_q(beta) = #{i : log2||b*_i|| = log2 q}` and `n_1(beta) = #{i : log2||b*_i|| = 0}` after a
progressive-BKZ schedule to block size `beta` on the ML-KEM-shaped q-ary embedding. It discards
the basis, the slope, and the whole middle segment — everything except where the two flat
segments of the Z-shape end. Structural: `(n_q, n_1)` is exactly the pair that decides **how
many of the `d` dimensions are doing any work**, and the estimator's optimisation over the
sample count `m` is a function of that pair alone, while nothing about the recovered vector is.

**Mechanism** — `lwe_primal` picks `m` by optimising a cost functional evaluated on a *simulated*
profile in which the q-vectors are assumed to have been consumed by the time reduction reaches
them. Real progressive BKZ leaves a measurable number of untouched `q`-rows and untouched unit
rows. If `n_q(beta)` exceeds the simulator's value, the reduction is effectively running in
dimension `d - n_q - n_1`, and the argmax over `m` sits at a different place than the estimator
believes. The direction is not obvious a priori — a surviving q-row is both a wasted dimension
and a free short vector — which is what makes it a test rather than a claim.

**Prediction** — At `d` in the 120–200 range with `q = 3329` and an ML-KEM-shaped
`(n, k, eta)` scaled down, the measured `(n_q, n_1)` at each rung of a progressive schedule
`beta in {20, 30, 40, 50, 60}` differs from the BKZ-simulator prediction by at least 2 indices
at some rung, and the induced `argmax_m` of the uSVP success condition moves by at least 1
sample, **with a stated sign**.

**Minimal discriminating test** — `fpylll` only, no g6k, no Sage.
(1) Build 20 seeds of the scaled q-ary embedding; run progressive BKZ; after each rung record
`l(B)` exactly and derive `(n_q, n_1)`.
(2) Run the pinned BKZ simulator on the identical starting profile and record its `(n_q, n_1)`.
(3) Feed both profiles through the *same* uSVP success condition and report `argmax_m`.
**Known-answer gate:** on the **unreduced** basis, `n_q + n_1 = d` exactly and `n_q` equals the
declared number of q-rows, bit-for-bit, on 20/20 seeds; and an LLL-only arm must satisfy
`n_q(LLL) <= n_q(unreduced)` on 20/20. An instrument failing either is broken, not informative.
**Declared false-failure rate** of the 2-index gate under a flawless instrument: computed from
the seed-to-seed dispersion of `(n_q, n_1)` **before** the simulator arm is run, and refused if
above 0.10.

**Falsifier** — (a) `|n_q^meas - n_q^sim| <= 1` and `|n_1^meas - n_1^sim| <= 1` at every rung on
>= 18/20 seeds → the simulator's shape class is calibrated at reachable dimensions and this lane
CLOSES with a number. (b) The profiles differ but `argmax_m` does not move → the discrepancy is
invisible to the cost functional, which is a stronger closure than (a). (c) `argmax_m` moves in
the direction that makes the attack **more** expensive → recorded signed, against the attack.

**Charged cost** — impl low (~200 lines fpylll + numpy). Compute: ~4–8 CPU-hours for 20 seeds ×
5 rungs at `d <= 200`. Memory: negligible (BKZ-60 enumeration, no sieve, no pool). No QRAM, no
quantum. Machine model: classical RAM, and because the observable is an integer count it is
**emulation-invariant** (see TO-COUNT's rationale, which applies here too).

**Ceiling** — `toy`. Every number is measured at reduced dimension; per `RQ-MLKEM-003` it may
support no statement about FIPS 203 parameter sets. The `argmax_m` recomputation at FIPS shapes,
if reported at all, is a **model substitution carrying no claim tier**.

**Risk** — conservative.

**Nearest prior art** — `delta(beta)_root_Hermite_map|measured_by_real_reduction_at_reduced_beta|vs_borrowed_closed_form`
(`IDEA-20260805-f7c912`).
**Why it is not that** — f7c912 measures the **scalar** `delta_0`, which is a functional of the
middle-segment slope and is *blind by construction* to where the two flat segments end; M1-1's
observable is the two segment-boundary integers and its consequent is a displacement of
`argmax_m`, a quantity `delta_0` does not enter.

`sota_delta: 0` / `novelty_status: unverified`

### M1-2. The basis-side twin of best-of-M: what is the sd of achieved `log2 delta_0` over reduction randomness, and what does best-of-R buy?

**Tracked object + what it discards** — TO-PROFILE, coarsened to the **scalar** `log2 delta_0`
achieved by one reduction of **one fixed lattice** under one fixed schedule and one seed. The map
is `(lattice, seed) |-> log2 delta_0`; it discards the basis, the profile shape, and the target
entirely. Structural: the fibre is the full set of reduced bases achieving that root-Hermite
factor, which is enormous and carries all the correctness content; what survives is the single
number every cost model consumes.

**Mechanism** — `GOAL-MLKEM-005` prices a maximum over **targets** at a fixed basis and proves
`G <= log2 M` by convexity. The complementary and unpriced object is a maximum over
**reductions** at a fixed target: run `R` independently randomised reductions of the same
lattice, keep the luckiest. The gain is not governed by convexity of `f(beta)`; it is governed
by the **dispersion of the reduction's own output**, `s = sd(log2 delta_0)`. For an
approximately Gaussian dispersion, `E[max of R] ≈ s * sqrt(2 ln R)`, so the gain in core-SVP
bits is `0.292 * s * sqrt(2 ln R) / |d(log2 delta_0)/dbeta|`. At `beta = 400` that derivative is
`9.920e-06` per unit `beta`, so **one part in `10^5` of `log2 delta_0` is worth roughly one unit
of `beta`, i.e. 0.29 bits.** The whole entry is: measure `s`, put it in that formula, and print
the number.

**Prediction** — `s` at reachable dimensions is of order `1e-4` to `1e-3` in `log2 delta_0`
units; at `R = 2^20`, `sqrt(2 ln R) = 5.27`, so the gain is `0.292 * 5.27 * s / 9.92e-06` bits,
i.e. **roughly 15 bits per `1e-4` of `s`**. The prediction with teeth is the *scaling*: `s` must
**shrink** with dimension (self-averaging over `d` blocks) roughly as `d^{-1/2}`, so the
extrapolated gain at cryptographic `d` is small. Predicted: `s(d) * sqrt(d)` is constant to
within 30% over `d in {80, 120, 160, 200}`.

**Minimal discriminating test** — `fpylll` only.
(1) One fixed lattice per dimension; `R = 64` reductions with distinct randomisation seeds
(random unimodular pre-transform, fixed schedule); record `log2 delta_0` each time.
(2) Fit `s(d)` and report `s(d) * sqrt(d)`.
(3) Report the best-of-R bit gain at `R in {2^10, 2^20, 2^40}` **with the `d(log2 delta_0)/dbeta`
sensitivity table beside it**, per `GOAL-MLKEM-005` C1's discipline.
**Known-answer gate:** with the randomisation **disabled** (identical seed), the `R` outputs must
be bit-identical, `s = 0.0` exactly, 64/64. A pipeline that reports non-zero `s` there is
measuring its own nondeterminism, and that is the single way this measurement fails silently.
**Second gate:** an LLL-only arm must give `s_LLL > s_BKZ` (weaker reduction, more dispersion);
a violation is an instrument fault, not a finding.

**Falsifier** — (a) `s * sqrt(d)` is not constant to 30% and **grows** with `d` → the
extrapolation is unsupported and the entry reports only the measured `d`, no more. (b) The
extrapolated gain at `R = 2^40` is under 1 core-SVP bit → **lane CLOSED with a number**, which is
the expected verdict. (c) `s` is dominated by the schedule rather than the randomisation
(measurable by re-running with two schedules) → the quantity is not a property of the lattice and
the framing is wrong.

**Charged cost** — impl low. Compute: 64 reductions × 4 dimensions, ~6–10 CPU-hours at
`d <= 200` with BKZ-40. **Memory charged explicitly:** `R` reductions are `R`-fold in *time*, not
memory, but a real best-of-R attacker must store `R` profiles (negligible) — and, critically,
**`R` is a multiplier on the whole reduction cost**, so the net is `gain - log2 R` bits and
that subtraction is printed in the same row as the gain. It is almost certainly negative, and
saying so is the deliverable.

**Ceiling** — `control`. This entry's strongest possible product is (i) the **null distribution**
against which any "best-of-anything" claim in this program must be scored, and (ii) a number
closing the basis-randomisation lane. It can never support a claim about ML-KEM, and it is
written to be cited as a control by other entries rather than as a result.

**Risk** — conservative.

**Nearest prior art** — `shared_BKZ_basis|best_of_M_ciphertext|projected_error_ratio_R|convexity_capped_by_log2M`
(`GOAL-MLKEM-005`).
**Why it is not that** — GOAL-005 randomises the **target** against a fixed basis and is capped
by convexity of `f(beta)` at `log2 M`; M1-2 randomises the **basis** against a fixed target,
where that convexity argument does not apply at all, and is capped instead by a *measured*
dispersion `s` and by the `log2 R` cost multiplier that best-of-M does not pay.

`sota_delta: 0` / `novelty_status: unverified`

### M1-3. The hybrid guessing block is charged at the wrong exponent: it is Rényi-1/2, not Shannon and not `log2(2eta+1)`

**Tracked object + what it discards** — TO-LAW, restricted to the guessing block: the map
`s|_G |-> (its rank in the optimal guessing order)`. It discards **which** coordinates hold which
values and keeps only the position in an enumeration. Structural: the fibre over rank `j` is the
entire set of blocks of that probability, whose size is what the whole entry is about; and rank
is not invertible to the block without the ordering, which is the attacker's own construction,
not a coordinate change on `s`.

**Mechanism** — Hybrid lattice/combinatorial attacks split `s` into a guessed block of `g`
coordinates and a reduced block. The guessing term is charged in the literature in at least two
incompatible ways: by the **support size** `g log2(2eta+1)` (pessimistic), or by the **Shannon
entropy** `g H(CBD_eta)` (optimistic). Both are wrong for *expected guessing work*. Arikan's
bound gives `E[G] ≍ 2^{g H_{1/2}}` with `H_{1/2}(P) = 2 log2 sum_x sqrt(P(x))`, and
`H <= H_{1/2} <= log2 |supp|` always. The gaps are exactly computable on the FIPS 203
distributions:

| `eta` | Shannon `H` | Rényi-1/2 `H_{1/2}` | support `log2(2eta+1)` | `H_{1/2} - H` | `supp - H_{1/2}` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 (ML-KEM-768/1024 `eta1`, all `eta2`) | 2.030639 | **2.157728** | 2.321928 | **0.127089** | 0.164200 |
| 3 (ML-KEM-512 `eta1`) | 2.333362 | **2.513581** | 2.807355 | **0.180219** | 0.293774 |

So a Shannon-charged hybrid **undercharges by `0.127 g` bits (`eta = 2`) or `0.180 g` bits
(`eta = 3`)**, and a support-charged one **overcharges by `0.164 g` / `0.294 g`. Both errors are
linear in the guessing dimension** and at `g = 100` they are 12.7 and 16.4 bits respectively —
larger than the entire free-memory margin this program has recorded for any parameter set.

**Prediction** — For any hybrid costing of ML-KEM in this corpus or in a retrievable source, the
guessing term is charged at one of the two wrong exponents, and substituting `H_{1/2}` moves the
optimal `g` by a computable amount. Predicted direction: replacing Shannon by Rényi-1/2 **raises**
the marginal cost of a guessed coordinate, so the optimal `g` **decreases** and the hybrid's
advantage over pure primal shrinks.

**Minimal discriminating test** — Zero compute; exact rational arithmetic.
(1) Compute `H`, `H_{1/2}`, `log2|supp|` for `CBD(2)` and `CBD(3)` exactly (the table above).
(2) **Exhaustive verification of Arikan's constant at small `g`:** enumerate all `5^g` blocks for
`g <= 8` (`eta = 2`) and all `7^g` for `g <= 6` (`eta = 3`), sort by probability, compute
`E[G]` exactly as a rational, and compare `log2 E[G] / g` against `H_{1/2}` and against `H`.
(3) Re-optimise `g` in one named hybrid cost expression under each of the three charges and
report the three optima side by side.
**Known-answer gate:** for the **uniform** distribution on `2eta+1` symbols, all three quantities
must coincide exactly at `log2(2eta+1)`, and the exhaustive `E[G]` must equal
`(5^g + 1)/2` exactly at `eta = 2`. An implementation that misses either has a sorting or
tie-breaking bug, which is the one way this computation fails silently.

**Falsifier** — (a) The exhaustive `log2 E[G]/g` converges to `H` rather than `H_{1/2}` as `g`
grows → the Rényi framing is wrong and the entry is withdrawn. (b) `H_{1/2} - H` is below the
resolution at which any hybrid costing in reach reports `g` (i.e. no costing reports `g` at all)
→ the correction is unattachable and the entry closes as a note. (c) The re-optimised `g` moves
by 0 in all three charges → the guessing term is not binding and the whole trade-off is decided
elsewhere.

**Charged cost** — impl low (~120 lines). Compute: seconds for the table, minutes for the
exhaustive gate at `g <= 8`. **Memory charged:** the exhaustive arm at `eta = 2, g = 8` holds
`5^8 = 390625` rationals — trivial — and the entry explicitly does **not** propose running a MITM,
whose memory would be the dominant and separately-charged term. No QRAM, no quantum. A quantum
reader is owed the note that Grover over the guessing block replaces `H_{1/2}` by a different
Rényi order and that this entry does not compute it.

**Ceiling** — `medium`. The arithmetic is exact and is evaluated on the **actual FIPS 203**
distributions, so there is no toy-to-crypto extrapolation; but nothing is executed at any scale
and the re-optimisation is a cost-model statement, exactly the tier `EV-MLKEM-020` carries.

**Risk** — conservative.

**Nearest prior art** — `exact_CBD_negacyclic_convolution_marginal|FIPS_compression_and_rounding_semantics|estimator_n_fold_union_bound`
(`EV-MLKEM-004`, `H-MLKEM-001`).
**Why it is not that** — EV-MLKEM-004 consumes the exact CBD law inside a **decryption-failure**
marginal, where the quantity is a tail probability; M1-3 consumes the same law to price a
**guessing exponent**, where the quantity is a Rényi order that no failure-rate computation
contains.

`sota_delta: 0` / `novelty_status: unverified`

### M1-4. The ceiling on exact-CBD scoring: replacing the Gaussian error model by the true law is worth under 0.3 core-SVP bits

**Tracked object + what it discards** — TO-LAW with a trivial observable: the map
`e |-> P_CBD` versus `e |-> N(0, eta/2)`, i.e. **the law itself, with the sample discarded**. The
discard is total and obviously structural: the whole content is which of two laws a decoder
assumes, and no invertible map on `e` distinguishes them.

**Mechanism** — A recurring family of proposals replaces the Euclidean norm in a lattice score by
the exact CBD log-likelihood, on the grounds that the true error is not Gaussian. The value of
that replacement is bounded by the per-coordinate divergence between the two laws, and for the
FIPS 203 parameters that divergence is **tiny**, because CBD is a binomial and binomials are
near-Gaussian at `2eta` trials in exactly the entropy sense the decoder cares about:

| `eta` | `sigma^2 = eta/2` | `(1/2) log2(2 pi e sigma^2)` | `H(CBD_eta)` | gap `v` | `K*v` at `K = 10.85` | at `K = 15.14` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1.0 | 2.047096 | 2.030639 | **0.016457** | 0.179 | 0.249 |
| 3 | 1.5 | 2.339577 | 2.333362 | **0.006215** | 0.067 | 0.094 |

Against the slice screening threshold `v* ≈ 0.066 to 0.092` (§0.3), both gaps are **4x to 13x too
small**. Expected verdict: **CLOSED**, with the number.

**Prediction** — `D_KL(P_CBD || Q)` minimised over the family `Q` of discrete Gaussians on `Z`
is below `0.02` bits per coordinate at `eta = 2` and below `0.01` at `eta = 3`, so no
exact-CBD-scoring proposal can be worth one core-SVP bit at any `(d, beta)` in the range of §0.3.

**Minimal discriminating test** — Zero compute.
(1) Compute `H(CBD_eta)` and `(1/2) log2(2 pi e sigma^2)` exactly (table above).
(2) Compute `min_Q D_KL(P_CBD || Q)` over discrete Gaussians `Q` on `Z` by 1-D minimisation over
the width parameter, to 1e-12.
(3) Convert via `K` and print the `K`-sensitivity table from §0.3 beside it, labelled a model
readout.
**Known-answer gate:** `D_KL(P||P) = 0` to machine zero; `D_KL(CBD_2 || CBD_3)` must equal a
hand-computable rational-log expression bit-for-bit; and the discrete-Gaussian normaliser must
match the Jacobi theta series to 1e-14 at `sigma^2 = 1` (the tail is `O(e^{-2 pi^2 sigma^2})`, so
this is checkable in closed form, not by simulation).

**Falsifier** — (a) `min_Q D_KL > 0.066` bits at `eta = 3` → the ceiling does not bind and
exact-CBD scoring is worth pursuing; this is the outcome that would make the entry a
counter-result rather than a closure. (b) The relevant functional is argued to be something other
than a per-coordinate divergence — e.g. a tail quantile rather than a mean — in which case the
entry is **scoped**, not withdrawn, and must say which regime it does not cover. That caveat is
recorded here in advance and not left to be discovered: `EV-MLKEM-94c773` established, on a
different statistic, that a second-order-correct derivation can be wrong about a `2^-10` tail
quantile, and this entry is a second-order (entropy/divergence) argument.

**Charged cost** — impl low (~80 lines, `mpmath`). Compute: seconds. Memory: none. No sieve, no
lattice, no g6k, no estimator run. Machine model irrelevant.

**Ceiling** — `medium`. Exact arithmetic on the actual FIPS 203 error distributions; the
conversion to core-SVP bits is a labelled model readout with its sensitivity table.

**Risk** — conservative.

**Nearest prior art** — `exact_per_coordinate_log_likelihood_scoring|-sum_log_p_j(v_j)_replacing_Euclidean_norm|CBD_char_function_cos^2eta`
(`IDEA-20260805-2b94fe`).
**Why it is not that** — M1-4 is **the ceiling on** 2b94fe, not an instance of it: 2b94fe proposes

### M1-5. Calibrating a memory-charged sieve model with a **counted**, emulation-invariant observable: the pairs-per-vector exponent

**Tracked object + what it discards** — TO-COUNT: `(sieve run) |-> (P, R, T, {w_b})`. It discards
every vector, so no geometry survives; and it discards the clock, so **no machine survives**. The
discard is structural precisely because the previous campaign's sieve measurements died on a
machine confound (`bgj1` cross-run `T_N` variance ratio 1.39, `p = 0.001`, unresolved between
genuine and QEMU artifact — `EV-MLKEM-d8f627`, `EV-MLKEM-4a9cfe`), and a counted trace is
invariant under emulation by construction.

**Mechanism** — `EV-MLKEM-020`'s memory charge is **borrowed**: the estimator supplies no memory
figure for `primal_bdd` at all, the `0.2075` literals sit in an uninvoked function annotated
"# pick something", and `GOAL-MLKEM-003`'s completion criterion 1 names **calibration by
measured sieve behaviour** as never attempted in twelve batches. The calibration handle is the
**time-to-memory exponent ratio**: `bgj1` is `2^{0.3113 d}` time on `2^{0.2075 d}` memory, ratio
`1.5001`; BDGL is `2^{0.292 d}` on the same memory, ratio `1.4072`. Both are asymptotic. The
measurable question is whether the *counted* pairs-per-vector exponent
`gamma(d) = log2(P) / log2(N)` approaches those constants at reachable dimensions, and from which
side. `gamma` is exactly the quantity a memory-charged model needs and never measures.

**Prediction** — `gamma(d)` measured over `d in {50, 55, 60, 65, 70, 75}` is **below** its
asymptotic value and rising, with `gamma(75) - gamma(50) > 0.02` and
`|gamma(75) - 1.500| < 0.15` for `bgj1`. The load-bearing prediction is the **sign and
monotonicity**, not the value: a memory charge calibrated at `d = 60` and extrapolated to
`d = 800` inherits whatever finite-size deficit `gamma` shows here.

**Minimal discriminating test** — g6k, instrumented with counters only (no timers anywhere in
the measured path).
(1) Patch `bgj1` to count `P` (inner products), `R` (accepted replacements), `T` (distinct pool
indices touched) and the per-bucket working set. Run 8 seeds per dimension over
`d in {50,...,75}`.
(2) Fit `gamma(d) = log2 P / log2 N` and report the increments.
(3) Recompute `EV-MLKEM-020`'s charge interval with the **measured** `gamma` substituted for the
borrowed exponent — reported as a conditional row, **never as a revision of `c*`**, which is an
identity and is not this entry's to move.
**Known-answer gate:** an **all-pairs (brute-force) sieve arm** must return `gamma = 2.000`
exactly (up to the `log2 N` discretisation), 8/8 seeds, at every dimension. That is a closed-form
known answer with no free parameter, and an instrument that misses it is miscounting. **Second
gate:** `R <= N` per iteration by construction; a violation is a counter bug.
**Declared false-failure rate:** the `gamma = 2.000` gate is exact under correct counting, so its
false-failure rate is 0 by construction; the `gamma(75) - gamma(50) > 0.02` gate has its
false-failure rate computed from the 8-seed dispersion at `d = 50` **before** the `d = 75` arm is
run, and is refused above 0.10.

**Falsifier** — (a) `gamma(d)` is flat to within 0.01 across the range → no finite-size trend is
measurable at reachable dimensions and **the calibration route is closed with a named

**Charged cost** — impl medium (g6k patch + counters; the ephemeral container recipe of

is the compute-dominant arm, charged explicitly. No QRAM, no quantum. **Machine model: counted
RAM operations, deliberately clock-free**, which is the entry's whole design point.

**Ceiling** — `toy`. Measured at `d <= 75`; per `RQ-MLKEM-003` it may make no statement about

**Risk** — high_risk. The instrument is the same g6k stack whose previous campaign was blocked;

**Nearest prior art** — `memory_charged_re_derivation|c_star_erasing_the_undercut|c_star<1/3_invariant|memory_exponent_sweep_0.05_0.40`

### M1-6. Pool survival across a sliding BKZ window: is the tour charge `d * SVP(beta)` or `d * rho * SVP(beta)`, and what does holding the pool cost?

**Tracked object + what it discards** — TO-PROFILE and TO-COUNT jointly, coarsened to a single
ratio per slide: `rho = P_warm / P_cold`, the counted inner products needed to re-saturate the
pool after the block window moves by one index, divided by those needed from a cold start. It
discards the pool, the vectors, the profile and the clock, keeping one dimensionless number per
block index. Structural: `rho` is exactly the quantity the cost model asserts to be 1 and never
measures, and it is not recoverable from any per-block quantity because it is a *ratio across two
counterfactual runs of the same block*.

**Mechanism** — Every core-SVP number charges a BKZ tour as `d` independent SVP calls at
`2^{0.292 beta}`. Real progressive-BKZ implementations warm-start: the pool from block `[i,
i+beta)` is projected and reused for `[i+1, i+1+beta)`. If `rho` is materially below 1 and roughly
constant in `beta`, the tour charge overstates by `log2(1/rho)` bits — a **constant**, never an
exponent (CEILING-M1 Budget C). The counterweight is that warm-starting requires the pool to
persist across blocks, so the `2^{0.2075 beta}` memory is held for the whole tour rather than per
call: **the saving in Budget C is bought with a strictly worse memory-residency profile**, and an
entry that reports one without the other is defective by rule 6.

**Prediction** — `rho in (0.1, 0.6)` at `d in {50,...,75}`, roughly constant in `beta`, giving
`log2(1/rho) in (0.7, 3.3)` bits of overstatement in the tour charge; and the memory-residency
integral (pool-size x blocks-held) rises by a factor equal to the number of blocks in a tour.

**Minimal discriminating test** — g6k, counters only.
(1) For each of 8 seeds and each `beta in {50, 60, 70}`, run the block at index `i` to
saturation; then run index `i+1` twice — once warm-started from the projected pool, once cold —
counting `P` in each.
(2) Report `rho(beta, i)` and its dispersion; report the memory-residency integral for both.
**Known-answer gate:** a **zero-slide** arm (re-run block `i` warm-started from block `i`'s own
finished pool) must give `rho = 0` up to the saturation-check cost — the pool is already
saturated, so no reduction work is needed. A pipeline returning `rho ≈ 1` there is not actually
warm-starting, which is the single way this measurement fails silently. **Second gate:** a
`beta`-slide arm (window moves by `beta`, i.e. no overlap) must give `rho ≈ 1`; the two gates
bracket the observable from both ends.
**Declared false-failure rate:** computed from the 8-seed dispersion of `rho` at `beta = 50`
before the other `beta` arms are run; refused above 0.10.

**Falsifier** — (a) `rho > 0.9` at every `beta` → warm-starting buys under 0.15 bits and the lane
CLOSES. (b) `rho` falls with `beta` faster than `beta^{-1}` → the saving vanishes at
cryptographic block sizes and the extrapolation is against the attack. (c) The memory-residency
increase exceeds the Budget-C saving under any of `EV-MLKEM-020`'s two charge models → **charged
away**, and that is the honest headline.

**Charged cost** — impl medium (g6k, pool serialisation between arms). Compute: ~15–25 CPU-hours
(three arms per block per seed). **Memory is the whole point and is charged twice:** peak pool
`2^{0.2075 beta}` vectors, plus the *residency integral* which the cold arm does not pay. No
QRAM, no quantum. Machine model: counted RAM, clock-free.

**Ceiling** — `toy`.

**Risk** — high_risk.

**Nearest prior art** — `saturation_ball_lattice_point_census|Siegel_mean_value_(4/3)^(d/2)|module_orbit_divisibility_by_2n|memory_saving_log2(2n)`
(`H-MLKEM-fef5ae`, `IDEA-20260805-fc6361`).
**Why it is not that** — fef5ae takes a **census at one block** and claims a memory saving from
orbit divisibility (an algebraic quantity); M1-6 measures a **ratio across two runs of the
adjacent block** and prices an amortisation over the block index, with no orbit, no isometry and
no algebra anywhere in it.

### M1-7. The free-lift charge: does the sieve at block `beta` actually solve SVP at `beta + f` for the `f` the closed form asserts?

**Tracked object + what it discards** — TO-PROFILE, coarsened to the **lift-success profile**
`s(f) = P[the dim-beta pool, greedily lifted through f coordinates, contains the shortest vector
of the dim-(beta+f) lattice]`. The map sends (pool, lattice) to a scalar per `f`; it discards the
pool, the lifted vectors, and which vector succeeded. Structural: `s(f)` is a property of the
profile's tail and the pool's saturation radius jointly, and no vector-level datum survives it.

**Mechanism** — Dimensions-for-free is baked into every modern core-SVP number as a **free block
size discount** `f ≈ beta ln(4/3) / ln(beta/(2 pi e))`. `EV-MLKEM-020` confirmed programmatically
that `RC.MATZOV` genuinely executes the `d4f` path and reported effective sieve dimensions
`bkz 353.20 / 557.15 / 792.14` against `svp 385.08 / 590.13 / 825.24` — a `d4f` of roughly 32 /
33 / 33 dimensions, i.e. **9.3 to 4.0 core-SVP bits handed to the attacker by a closed form
nobody in this program has measured against.** M1-7 measures `s(f)` directly at dimensions where
ground-truth SVP is computable by enumeration, and locates `f_{1/2}`, the `f` at which
`s(f) = 1/2`.

**Prediction** — `f_{1/2}(beta)` at `beta in {45, 50, 55, 60, 65}` differs from the closed form by
more than 1 dimension at some `beta`, **with a consistent sign across `beta`**; and the sign is
the deliverable, because a consistent shortfall of `delta_f` dimensions is worth
`0.292 * delta_f` core-SVP bits against the attack at every parameter set simultaneously.

**Minimal discriminating test** — g6k for the pool, fplll enumeration for ground truth.
(1) For each `beta` and 10 seeds, sieve in the projected sublattice of dimension `beta`; for
`f = 0, 1, ..., 25`, greedily lift the pool through `f` coordinates and record whether the
resulting shortest lift equals the true shortest vector of the dim-`(beta+f)` lattice, obtained
by exact enumeration.
(2) Fit `s(f)` and interpolate `f_{1/2}`.
**Known-answer gate:** `s(0) = 1.000` on 10/10 seeds at every `beta` — at `f = 0` the sieve is
solving the lattice it sieved, so any value below 1 is a saturation or comparison bug and nothing
downstream is interpretable. **Second gate:** `s(f)` must be non-increasing in `f` on every seed;
a non-monotone trace is a lifting bug.
**Declared false-failure rate:** the `s(0) = 1` gate is exact; the "> 1 dimension, consistent
sign" gate has its false-failure rate computed from the seed dispersion of `f_{1/2}` at
`beta = 45` before the other arms run, and is refused above 0.10.

**Falsifier** — (a) `|f_{1/2} - f_closed| <= 1` at every `beta` → the closed form is calibrated at
reachable dimensions and this lane CLOSES with a number, which is a real answer about a discount
that has never been checked here. (b) The sign is inconsistent across `beta` → the deviation is
noise and no extrapolation is supportable. (c) `f_{1/2}` grows faster than the closed form's
`beta / ln beta` shape → **pro-attack**, and it must be recorded signed and without softening.

**Charged cost** — impl medium-high (g6k pool + fplll exact SVP + lifting harness). Compute:

up to 90 is hours per instance at the top of the range, so the design caps `beta + f <= 85` and
says so. Memory: pool `2^{0.2075 * 65} ≈ 2^{13.5}` vectors, negligible; enumeration is
polynomial-space, which is why it is the ground truth. No QRAM, no quantum.

**Ceiling** — `toy`. The 9.3 / 4.0 bit figures quoted above are context from `EV-MLKEM-020`, not
a claim of this entry, and no measured `f_{1/2}` may be extrapolated to FIPS 203 block sizes.

**Risk** — high_risk. The enumeration ceiling limits `beta` to a range where the closed form's own

**Nearest prior art** — `g6k_bgj1_sieve_dual_vectors|dual_membership_certificate|score_distribution|MATZOV_Nf_independence_law`

**Why it is not that** — GOAL-004 scores sieve-produced dual vectors against a distinguisher's

### M1-8. Conditioning on the ciphertext: FIPS 203's `Compress` partitions coordinates into noise classes, and for `d_u = 11` one class is exactly noiseless

**Tracked object + what it discards** — TO-LAW in its most degenerate form: the map
`c |-> S(c) = {i : Compress_{d_u}^{-1}(c_i) is a singleton}` — a **subset of coordinate indices**,
and nothing else. It discards the ciphertext values, the message, the randomness, and every
coefficient. Structural and unmistakably non-invertible: `c` has `~2^{d_u * n * k}` values and
`S(c)` has `2^{n k}`; and `S(c)` is computed from public data with no secret and no oracle.

**Mechanism** — `Compress_d(x) = round(2^d x / q) mod 2^d` with `q = 3329` has **non-uniform
fibres**, because `q` is not a power of two. Exact fibre census (computed, not asserted):

| `d` | used for | fibre sizes | counts | `E[Var(delta | bin)]` | marginal `Var(delta)` | `log2` ratio | `I(delta ; bin)` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | `u`, ML-KEM-512/768 | 3, 4 | 767, 257 | 0.846801 | 0.924001 | 0.125871 | **0.218912** |
| 11 | `u`, ML-KEM-1024 | **1**, 2 | **767**, 1281 | 0.192400 | 0.384800 | **1.000000** | **0.576561** |
| 4 | `v`, ML-KEM-512/768 | 208, 209 | 15, 1 | 3607.43 | 3607.66 | 0.000093 | 0.004646 |
| 5 | `v`, ML-KEM-1024 | 104, 105 | 31, 1 | 901.80 | 902.04 | 0.000387 | 0.009464 |

The `d_u = 11` row is the sharp one: **767 of 3329 residues, i.e. 23.04% of every ML-KEM-1024

compression noise on that coordinate is identically zero** — and which coordinates those are is
read off the ciphertext. The remaining 77% carry a 1-of-2 ambiguity. So the ciphertext-side

mechanism must reproduce.

expected verdict is still CLOSED**: the ciphertext-side lattice carries `Var(e1) + Var(delta_u)`

does not close that gap. Saying so with the number is the deliverable.

for the block sizes.

`inputs/MLKEM-DUAL-SOURCES-20260802/fips203` (the table above).
(2) Feed (M0), (M1), (M2) through the **same** norm-ratio-to-block-size conversion instrument the
program already has a KAT for, and print the three `beta` values with the `K`-sensitivity table.

implied.
**Known-answer gate:** `sum over bins of (fibre size) = q = 3329` exactly for every `d`;
`Decompress(Compress(x)) = x` for **every** `x` in a singleton fibre, 767/767 at `d_u = 11`, and

singleton and `I = H(delta) = 0` exactly.

**Falsifier** — (a) The singleton count at `d_u = 11` is not 767 → the census is wrong and nothing
follows. (b) `beta(M2) >= beta(M0)` → the conditioning does not help even on its own lattice, and

seconds for the census, minutes for the conversions. Memory: none. No oracle, no chosen

parameters, so there is no toy extrapolation in it; the block-size conversion is a labelled model

### M1-9. Module structure as reduction *dynamics*, not as algebra: tours-to-target and the lag-`n` profile autocorrelation against a matched unstructured null

**Tracked object + what it discards** — TO-PROFILE as a **trajectory**: the sequence
`(l(B_0), l(B_1), ..., l(B_t))` of log-GS profiles after each BKZ tour, coarsened to two scalars
per run — the tour index `t*` at which a target root-Hermite factor is first reached, and the
lag-`n` autocorrelation `A_n(l(B_{t*}))` of the final profile. It discards every basis vector,
every lattice, and the ordering within a tour.

- *Lossy and structural.* A profile trajectory does not determine the lattice; two non-isometric
  lattices share trajectories. More to the point for this slice: **the trajectory is not a
  `Z_q`-linear function of the samples**, so the 17-bit algebra closure — which is an argument
  that every algebraically derived instance leaves the posterior on `s` unchanged
  (`GOAL-MLKEM-005.inherited_constraints`, `EV-MLKEM-002`) — **does not constrain it**. That
  closure is about information; this is about how fast an algorithm moves. The distinction is the
  entry's licence to exist and is stated here rather than buried.

**Mechanism** — `KN-OPEN-012` asks whether module lattices admit structure-exploiting attacks
beyond generic BKZ; `KN-OPEN-026` notes the only structure-dependent number this program ever had
(the 0.0–0.8 bit isometric gain) is a difference of two seedless randomized optimizer outputs with
no matched null. Both are posed as *information/gain* questions. M1-9 poses the **dynamics**
question that neither asks: at matched `(d, q, det, error law)`, does BKZ **converge in a
different number of tours** on a module q-ary lattice than on an unstructured one, and does the
reduced profile retain a lag-`n` periodicity that the unstructured family cannot produce? A

### M1-10. Does a **pool-only** repair of the independence law exist? A separability test on the alignment deficit

**Tracked object + what it discards** — TO-GRAM at its most coarsened: for an `r x c` factorial
layout of `r` sieve pools against `c` candidate sets, the map
`(pool, candidates) |-> log D` followed by removal of **both main effects**, leaving only the
**interaction residual**. It discards the pools, the candidates, the Gram matrices, the scores,
and both marginal effects. Structural: the interaction residual is by construction unrecoverable
from either factor alone — that is precisely its definition — so no map on pools and no map on
candidates can reproduce it.

**Mechanism** — `EV-MLKEM-2cd08b` localised this campaign's only surviving residual to "the
ALIGNMENT between the pool's Gram matrix and the candidate phase matrix", and established that
`ROWPERM` is a **complete** decomposition because `closed_form_cov` depends on `X` only through
`X X^T` and row norms. That localisation answers *where* the deficit lives and does not answer the
one question a cost model needs: **is it charge-able as a single pool-side constant?** If
`log D` is additive in (pool factor, candidate factor), then one number measured once repairs
`MATZOV.Nf` for every candidate set, and it can be charged as `log2(1/(1-D))` added to the
required `N`. If the interaction is significant, **no pool-only repair exists**, and that is a
named obstruction on `KN-OPEN-016`'s residual rather than another negative result.

**Prediction** — Over `r = 4` real sieve databases and `c = 4` candidate families (uniform,
near-miss at two shared fractions, secret-distribution), the interaction sum of squares is **below
5% of the pool main-effect sum of squares**, i.e. `log D` is separable and a pool-only constant
exists; and that constant is **near zero on uniform candidates**, consistent with
`EV-MLKEM-da9e3b`'s already-measured ratio 1.00 on uniform wrong-candidate noise (sd 0.005275
against the iid 0.005282). Any prediction inconsistent with that measured 1.00 would be wrong
before it was made, and this entry is built to be consistent with it.

**Minimal discriminating test** — g6k for the pools, numpy for everything else.
(1) Build `r = 4` certified dual databases (dual-membership certificate re-verified by integer
arithmetic independent of the sieve, as `EV-MLKEM-da9e3b` did).
(2) Score `c = 4` candidate families against each, computing `D` on the `r x c` grid.
(3) Fit `log D ~ pool + candidates` and test the interaction with a pre-registered F-test whose
false-failure rate under the null is computed from a synthetic replicate **before** the real grid
is scored, and refused above 0.10.
(4) Report the fitted pool-side constant with a CI, or the obstruction.
**Known-answer gate (two, and they bracket):** (i) a **synthetic x synthetic** cell — iid Gaussian
pool of matched row norms against a synthetic candidate family — must give `D = 1` and zero
interaction to Monte-Carlo error, on all `r x c` synthetic replicates; (ii) the **real x real**
cell must reproduce `EV-MLKEM-2cd08b`'s committed `ROWPERM` deficit for the corresponding group
to within its reported dispersion. An instrument failing (i) is manufacturing an effect; one
failing (ii) is not measuring the same object the repo already measured.

**Falsifier** — (a) Interaction >= 5% of the pool main effect with `p < 0.01` → **no pool-only
repair exists**; the independence law's failure is irreducibly joint and that is a named
obstruction, recorded as such. (b) Interaction < 1% and the pool constant's CI contains 0 → the
correction is below 0.015 bits of score sd and `KN-OPEN-016`'s residual **closes on this axis with
a number**. (c) The synthetic gate fails → instrument outcome, no disposition, exactly as
`GOAL-MLKEM-005` BATCH-a51f91 was adjudicated.

**Charged cost** — impl medium (reuses the certified-database harness). Compute: 4 sieves at
`d <= 70` plus 16 scorings; ~8–15 CPU-hours. **Memory charged:** 4 pools held simultaneously for
the crossed design, `4 * 2^{0.2075 * 70} ≈ 2^{16.5}` vectors, plus the `r x c` score arrays. No
QRAM, no quantum. **Query/oracle count: zero** — no decapsulation, no failure oracle. Machine
model: classical RAM; the design is clock-free, so the `bgj1` QEMU confound cannot reach the
observable.

**Ceiling** — `toy`. One instance class, four databases, `d <= 70`; per `RQ-MLKEM-003` no
statement about FIPS 203, and a separability result is a statement about **the form a repair could
take**, never about whether the dual attack beats the primal.

**Risk** — representation_changing. It replaces the scalar `N` of the independence law by a
question about whether `N` admits a pool-indexed correction at all.

**Nearest prior art** — `ROWPERM_decomposition|Gram_matrix_vs_candidate_phase_matrix_alignment|deficit_fully_localised`
and `object_specific_residual_4_to_9_percent|survives_all_ablations|X_fixed_Y_ablated|carries_no_sieve_information`
(both `EV-MLKEM-2cd08b`).
**Why it is not that** — 2cd08b **localises** the deficit and proves it carries no sieve
information, using nested ablations of one pool against one candidate set; M1-10 crosses `r` pools
against `c` candidate sets and asks a different, decision-relevant question — whether the deficit
**factorises**, hence whether any pool-only constant can repair `Nf` — a question a nested-ablation
design cannot pose because it has only one level of the pool factor.
