Two meta-objects appear once each and are declared as such rather than smuggled in:
S1-4 tracks the **endomorphism monoid of the instance functor** (a property of the problem,
not of an instance) and S1-10 tracks the **maximal-order type** (see the rule-4 disclosure
below).

**Lossy-projection test, applied.**

- **TO-BALL and TO-THETA are the same discard seen twice**, and the discard is structural.
  Both are *truncations*: a bounded-length prefix of a sequence whose full value is
  (reportedly, Chevyrev–Galbraith; recollection, unverified) a complete invariant of the
  order. The fibre over a truncated value is not merely large, it is **everything**: for
  `N` below the first nonzero coefficient the truncation is the zero vector on a
  `1 − N^{3/2}/p` fraction of the graph *(session derivation, §0.3)*. That is not a change
  of coordinates in any regime — the map cannot be inverted because on almost the whole
  graph it is constant. TO-BALL propagates deterministically under one graph operation:
  `N_{2k}` is the diagonal of the `ℓ^k`-Brandt matrix, so the Hecke recursion
  `T_{ℓ}T_{ℓ^k} = T_{ℓ^{k+1}} + ℓ T_{ℓ^{k-1}}` constrains the census along an edge.
- **TO-CMINC** erases exactly the vertex labels and keeps the incidence pattern. The
  discard is structural and, better, **provably non-invertible in a specific direction**:
  `End(E^{(p)}) ≅ End(E)^{op}` has the same optimal embeddings, so the CM-incidence
  structure **cannot distinguish `E` from `E^{(p)}` under any labelling** (S1-3). The
  fibre therefore has size ≥ 2 by a forced symmetry, before any counting argument.
- **TO-SPEC** is lossy only in truncation: the full coordinate vector is an orthonormal
  change of basis of the delta function, hence invertible. **S1-5 therefore tracks only
  truncations `|S| = O(1)` and says so**; its content is the trade between how many
  coordinates identify a vertex and what one coordinate costs.
- **TO-LAND** discards everything except `k` integers, each of which the entry predicts to
  be constant to within `O(1)` across the entire graph. If the prediction holds, TO-LAND is
  the extreme case of a legitimate lossy projection: one whose image has `O(1)` elements.
- **TO-COSET** discards the coset representative and keeps only the reduced word length.
  Non-invertible: `ℓ^n` words share a length.
- **Rule-4 disclosure for S1-10.** The maximal-order **type** is, by the classical Deuring
  statement, in bijection with the Galois orbit `{j, j^p}` — so it is **near-invertible**
  and would be a repackaging if proposed as an attack object. S1-10 does not propose it as
  one. Its deliverable is precisely the invertibility, with the inversion **cost measured**,
  and its consequence for how a lattice-invariant result would have to be read. It is
  admitted as a scope/ceiling entry under house rule 8, not as a tracked-object proposal.

## 1. The ten ideas

### S1-1. The local information-yield curve: how many bits a radius-`r` ball carries, and the forced threshold at `p^{2/3}`

**Tracked object + what it discards.** TO-BALL / TO-THETA: the census
`(N_2(E), …, N_{2r}(E))` of closed non-backtracking walks, equivalently the truncated theta
series `(r_E(n))_{n ≤ N}` of the traceless lattice with `N = ℓ^{2r}`. It discards every
curve in the ball, every isogeny, the lattice, and the order. **The discard is structural,
not a change of coordinates, for a reason that can be stated as an identity rather than as
an intuition: below `N ≈ p^{2/3}` the projection is the constant zero vector on a
`1 − N^{3/2}/p` fraction of vertices**, so it is not injective on any positive-density set,
and no re-coordinatisation recovers what it drops.

**Mechanism.** By §0.3(2), the expected number of non-scalar endomorphisms of norm `≤ N` at
a fixed `E` is `≈ N^{3/2}/p`. So the truncated theta series is:

- **identically zero** with probability `1 − Θ(N^{3/2}/p)`;
- carrying, when nonzero, `≈ N^{3/2}/p` "events" whose positions are the informative data.

Hence a **predicted information-yield curve** *(session derivation, unverified)*:

```
I(N)  ≈  (N^{3/2}/p) · log_2( p / N^{3/2} )   bits per vertex,     N ≲ p^{2/3}
I(N)  ≈  Θ( (N^{3/2}/p) · log_2 N )           bits per vertex,     N ≳ p^{2/3}
```

with two derived thresholds: the **one-bit threshold** `N_1 ≈ p^{2/3}`, and the
**separation threshold** `N_sep` at which the truncation is injective on the whole vertex
set, obtained by demanding `I(N) ≳ log_2(p/12)`, i.e.
**`N_sep ≈ (p log p)^{2/3}`** — the same exponent with a `(log p)^{2/3}` correction. The
correction is the falsifiable part: an exponent alone would be unfalsifiable at toy scale,
a fitted exponent plus a predicted log power is not.

**Prediction.** Fit `N_sep(p) = c · p^{α} (log p)^{β}` over three toy primes. Predicted
`α = 2/3 ± 0.05`, `β` consistent with `2/3` and in any case `β < 2`. Predicted `I(N)` curve
matches the two-regime form above to within a factor 2 at every `N` on a log grid.

**Minimal discriminating test.** On `build_isogeny_graph.py` at three primes with
`⌊p/12⌋ ≈ 10^2, 10^3, 10^4` vertices: build the full `2`-isogeny graph; for every vertex
compute `N_{2k}` for `k = 1 … r_max` by exhaustive non-backtracking BFS (the ball is the
graph at `r_max ≈ log_2 p`, so this is one full traversal per vertex, `O(n^2)` at these
sizes and therefore free); tabulate (a) the empirical `I(N)` as the Shannon entropy of the
truncated census across vertices, and (b) the smallest `N` at which all census values are
distinct **up to the Galois pairing**. Independently, on the quaternion arm, compute the
true `r_E(n)` from the traceless lattice and check the two agree.

**Known-answer gate.** Three, all forced, all evaluated *before* the primary measurement:
(a) **`N = 1`**: the census must be identically zero at every vertex and `I = 0` exactly —
a nonzero reading means the non-backtracking exclusion is mis-implemented;
(b) **`N ≥ p^{2/3}·C` for a generous `C`**: every vertex must have a nonzero census, and the
count of vertices with `N_{2k} > 0` must match `min(⌊p/12⌋, ℓ^{3k})` to within the
`ε_p` term;
(c) **Eichler mass**: on the quaternion arm, `Σ_E 1/|Aut(E)| = (p−1)/24` exactly, as a
rational equality. Gate (b) is the one that matters, because it is evaluated where the two
candidate readings of the entry (`α = 2/3` versus `α = 1/2`) actually disagree — the
`SCREENING.md` §2 "no tautological gate" criterion.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric).** Fitted `α ≤ 0.60` or `α ≥ 0.73` across the three sizes,
with non-overlapping confidence intervals ⇒ the §0.3(2) counting law that CEILING-S1 and
S1-2 both stand on is wrong, and this whole slice must be re-derived. Or: `β ≥ 2` ⇒ the
separation threshold is superpolynomially above the one-bit threshold and TO-THETA is
useless even in principle. Or the entropy curve is **flat** in `N` ⇒ the two-regime form is
wrong and the mechanism is mis-specified.

**Charged cost.** Full graph construction `O(n)` `Φ_2` root-findings; per-vertex BFS `O(n)`
edges, so `O(n^2)` total field operations, `n ≤ 10^4` ⇒ minutes. **Memory: `O(n)` for the
graph, `O(n · r_max)` for the census table — the census table, not the graph, is the peak,
and it is charged.** No preprocessing, no oracle, no QRAM, no quantum resource. Machine
model: single-threaded RAM, and the entry claims nothing outside it.

**Ceiling.** `toy`. Exhaustive enumeration at `⌊p/12⌋ ≤ 10^4` supports statements about
those graphs. The **law** has a crypto-scale counterpart through the quaternion sampler
(the shortest-vector-of-`(P, Nrd/p)` route needs no curve arithmetic), the **entropy** does
not, and no write-up may blur the two.

**Risk band.** `conservative`. This is instrumentation plus a counting law; its value is
that S1-2, S1-7 and S1-8 all rest on it and none of them is interpretable if it fails.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** B1-9 (per-vertex terminal filter `A_M`, cost curve
`p·C(M)/M^{3/2}`). **Why it is not that:** B1-9 *measures enrichment* of a hand-listed
menu of candidate statistics and can only ever speak about the menu; S1-1 computes the
entropy of the **maximal** local statistic (the complete ball census, of which every menu
item is a function) and therefore bounds the whole family at once, including candidates
nobody has thought of. Second neighbour: `IDEA-20260806-a3ef00` (genus/spinor local-global
split), which cuts the same lattice **at the finite places**; S1-1 cuts it **at the
archimedean/size place**. The two decompositions are transverse.

### S1-2. The terminal-filter exponent identity `1/(2(2−c))`: one number decides the whole family, and the difference between `1/3` and `1/4` is exactly whether the membership test is charged

**Tracked object + what it discards.** TO-BALL again, but used as a **decision predicate**:
`E ∈ A_M ⟺ ∃ non-scalar α ∈ End(E), Nrd α ≤ M`. One bit per vertex. It discards the
endomorphism, its norm, the lattice and the order — and it is exactly the bit that a
Delfs–Galbraith-shaped walk would need in order to stop.

**Mechanism.** B1-9 wrote the family's cost as `p · C(M) / M^{3/2}` and left `C(M)` open.
That expression is **incomplete in two places and the omissions are load-bearing**:

1. it charges the *scan* but not the *extraction* — after the walk lands in `A_M` you must
   still produce `α`, at cost `C_find(M)`;
2. it does not charge or credit the *terminal stage* — what `α` is worth once you have it.

Filling both in *(session derivation, unverified)*:

```
T(M)  =  p · C_test(M) / M^{3/2}   +   C_find(M)   +   T_OneEnd→EndRing
```

The third term is **polynomial** by the corpus's own cascade: `OneEnd → EndRing` is a
polynomial-time reduction (`IDEA-20260806-62ba9d`, `GOAL-SSIQ-001` standing conditions),
subject to **SC-2**, which is satisfied here because `log Nrd α ≤ log M ≤ log p`. That is
the one place SC-2 bites and it is checked, not assumed. Writing `C_test(M) = M^{c}` and
`C_find(M) = M^{c'}` with `c' ≥ c`, the optimum is at `M = p^{1/(2−c)}` and

> **`T = p^{ 1 / (2(2−c)) }`.**

This one-parameter law is the entry. It **retrodicts the record**:

| `c` (test-cost exponent) | optimal `M` | resulting exponent | matches |
|---|---|---|---|
| `c = 1` (test costs its own threshold) | `p` | `1/2` | vOW full-cost baseline `p^{1/2}` |
| `c = 1/2` (test costs the ball, §0.3(3)) | `p^{2/3}` | **`1/3`** | the incumbent `p^{1/3+o(1)}` |
| `c = 0` (**test uncharged**) | `p^{1/2}` | `1/4` | — |

The third row is the entry's sharpest content, and it is a **defect report, not a result**:
*the entire numerical distance between `1/3` and `1/4` inside this family is the decision to
charge or not charge the membership test.* House rule 6 says an uncharged resource is a
defect; here the defect is worth exactly one third of the exponent. This is a structural
observation about the shape of the accounting and is explicitly **not** a claim about the
provenance of the corpus's contested `F_p` `Õ(p^{1/4})` figure (`KN-TECH-058` RC4), which
arises from vOW on the `F_p` subgraph and is a different object; the resemblance is flagged
as something to check, not asserted.

The remaining question is therefore **`c` alone**, and §0.3(3) supplies a bound: in the

### S1-3. The CM-incidence hypergraph: the combinatorics is free from `p`, the labelling is not, and the rigidity ceiling is forced to 2

**Tracked object + what it discards.** TO-CMINC: the bipartite incidence
`I_X = {(D, E) : O_D optimally embeds in End(E), |D| ≤ X}` with **the `E`-side labels
erased**. It keeps the abstract incidence structure — degrees, co-degrees, the whole
hypergraph up to isomorphism — and discards which vertex is which. The discard is
structural and, unusually, **provably bounded below**: `End(E^{(p)}) ≅ End(E)^{op}` admits
the same optimal embeddings, so `σ: E ↦ E^{(p)}` is an **automorphism of `I_X` for every
`X`**. No amount of incidence data can separate a Galois pair. The fibre therefore has size
`≥ 2` by an identity, before any counting.

**Mechanism.** Two halves that meet.

*Free side.* The **existence** half of the incidence is computable from `p` alone by pure
integer arithmetic: `O_D` embeds in some supersingular order iff `p` is non-split in
`Q(√D)`; the total incidence count at `|D| ≤ X` is `Σ_{|D| ≤ X} h(D) · (local factor) ≈
X^{3/2}` — matching §0.3(2) at `X = p^{2/3}` where it saturates at `p`. Moreover the
**pairwise** data is (reportedly) also free: Gross–Zagier factors `N(j(D_1) − j(D_2))` into
primes with explicit exponents given by Gross–Keating local multiplicities, so for our
fixed `p` one can compute, from integers alone, **whether and with what multiplicity `D_1`
and `D_2` share a supersingular vertex** *(recollection, unverified; flagged in §0.5(ii))*.

*Costly side.* Everything about **which** vertex. Evaluating `O_D ↪ End(E)` at a given `E`
is exactly S1-2's `A_M` membership test at `M ≈ |D|`, with `c ≥ 1/2`.

So the entry's question is sharp: **how much of the labelling is forced by the free side?**
Formally, run colour refinement (1-WL) on `I_X` and ask at which cutoff `X` the colour
classes stop shrinking, and what they stop at.

**Prediction.** *(session derivation, unverified)*
(i) Colour classes **never** reach size 1: the terminal refinement has all classes of size
exactly 2, the Galois pairs, for every `X`. (ii) The **rigidity threshold** `X*(p)`, defined
as the least `X` at which every colour class has size 2, satisfies `X*(p) = c · p^{α}` with
predicted `α = 2/3 ± 0.05` — the same threshold as S1-1, arrived at by a completely
different route (hypergraph refinement rather than entropy), which is what makes the
agreement informative rather than circular. (iii) Consequently the free combinatorial side
determines the structure **only at the same cutoff where evaluating one incidence already
costs `p^{1/3}`** — the information is free, the **access** is not, and the entry's
deliverable is that separation stated with two numbers.

**Minimal discriminating test.** On `build_isogeny_graph.py` at three toy primes:
(1) compute the **true** incidence `I_X` exhaustively from the quaternion arm (for each
vertex, enumerate traceless elements of small norm, read off discriminants);
(2) compute the **free** prediction of the pairwise co-degrees from the integer formulas and
**check the two agree exactly** — this is the gate;
(3) run 1-WL on the unlabelled `I_X` for `X` on a log grid, record the colour-class size
histogram, extract `X*(p)`, fit `α` across the three primes.

**Known-answer gate.** (a) **The Galois gate, which is the sharp one:** colour refinement
must **never** separate `E` from `E^{(p)}`. If it does, the instrument has leaked a label
and every number it produces is void. This gate is evaluated where the candidate readings
disagree (a broken instrument would report class size 1 and a spurious `α`), so it is not a
PASS-by-construction. (b) `X = 3, 4`: the incidence at `|D| = 3, 4` must pick out exactly
the `j = 0` and `j = 1728` vertices when they are supersingular (`p ≡ 2 mod 3`,
`p ≡ 3 mod 4`) and be empty when they are not — a two-line hand check.
(c) The saturation identity: `#{E : incident to some |D| ≤ X}` must equal
`min(⌊p/12⌋, ≈X^{3/2})`.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric).** Fitted `α ≤ 0.55` across three primes with
non-overlapping intervals ⇒ the free combinatorial data pins the structure **strictly
below** the incumbent threshold, and the entry becomes a lead requiring an immediate,
separately charged answer to "what does a pinned structure cost to *use*, given that every
incidence evaluation is an `A_M` query". Or: some colour class has size 1 at any `X` ⇒
instrument broken (gate (a)), stop. Or: some colour class has size `> 2` at every `X` up to
saturation ⇒ the Galois symmetry is not the only forced one, and there is a **second**
symmetry of the supersingular set that this corpus has never named — the most interesting
outcome available in this entry.

**Charged cost.** Incidence enumeration: `O(n · X^{3/2}/p)` traceless-lattice enumerations,
dominated by the `X = X*` pass; 1-WL is `O(|I_X| log |I_X|)` per round with `O(log n)`

affordable.** No oracle, no preprocessing beyond the class-number tables (whose computation

**Ceiling.** `toy`. A rigidity threshold measured at `⌊p/12⌋ ≤ 10^4` supports a fitted
exponent over three points and nothing at `2^{256}`. The **forced Galois symmetry** in (i)

**Risk band.** `representation_changing`. The object — an unlabelled incidence hypergraph

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `IDEA-20260804-84328c` (Frobenius-orbit unordered bucket

### S1-6. Landmarks are worthless on a Ramanujan graph and the pruning factor is measurable: pricing the entire heuristic-search family in one experiment

**Tracked object + what it discards.** TO-LAND: the landmark distance vector
`(d(E, L_1), …, d(E, L_k))` to a fixed, precomputed set of `k` vertices. It discards the
graph, the curve and the isogenies, retaining `k` integers. The entry predicts the image of
this projection has `O(1)` elements — the extreme legitimate lossy projection, and one whose
uselessness is the deliverable.

**Mechanism.** Heuristic graph search — A\*, ALT (A\* with landmarks and the triangle
inequality), bidirectional heuristic search, contraction hierarchies — is the standard
toolkit anyone outside isogeny cryptography would reach for when asked to find a path in a
huge graph, and this corpus has **never priced it**. It is priced by one property:
**distance concentration**. In an `(ℓ+1)`-regular Ramanujan graph on `n` vertices the sphere
of radius `r` around a landmark has `≈ ℓ^r` vertices, so the fraction of vertices at
distance `≤ log_ℓ n − k` is `≈ ℓ^{−k}`: **essentially every vertex is at distance
`log_ℓ n ± O(1)` from every landmark.** The ALT lower bound `|d(E,L) − d(E',L)|` is then `0`
for a `1 − O(1/ℓ)` fraction of pairs, the heuristic is `≈ 0` everywhere, and A\* degenerates
to breadth-first search exactly. The same argument kills contraction hierarchies (no
highway structure: the graph has maximal "highway dimension") and any potential-function
method.

is **cheap to close permanently**, and that until it is, "path finding is hard" rests on an
unstated assumption that a competent algorithms engineer would query in the first meeting.

**Prediction.** Let `ρ(p) = (#vertices expanded by ALT-A\*) / (#vertices expanded by
bidirectional BFS)` on random source–target pairs. Predicted `ρ(p) → 1` from below, with
`1 − ρ(p) = O(1/ℓ)` and **no** decrease with `p`; and predicted landmark-value entropy

**Minimal discriminating test.** On S1-1's graphs at three sizes, with landmark sets of size

(a) a cycle `C_n` with one landmark — ALT must expand `≈ n/4` versus BFS's `≈ n/2`, i.e.

(b) a 2-D grid with 4 corner landmarks — ALT must show the textbook large speedup,
`ρ ≪ 0.1`.
If the harness does not reproduce these, it cannot measure `ρ` on anything, and a null
result on the supersingular graph would be an infrastructure failure masquerading as
mathematics (`AGENTS.md` rule 5). A third, degenerate gate: `k = 0` must give `ρ = 1`
exactly.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric).** `ρ ≤ 0.5` at any `k` **and** decreasing across the three

what the mechanism says, and the entry converts into an obligation to charge landmark

**Charged cost.** Graph build as in S1-1; `k` landmark BFS runs at `O(k·n)`; 10^3 search

**Ceiling.** `toy` for the measurement; the **concentration argument** is a derivation and

### S1-7. The small-endomorphism count field fluctuates like a central `L`-value, not like a Poisson variable — and that caps every enrichment-hunting filter at `p^{o(1)}`

**Tracked object + what it discards.** TO-THETA restricted to one discriminant: the
**optimal-embedding count field** `m_D : E ↦ #{optimal embeddings of O_D into End(E)}/(units)`,
for a fixed `D`. One non-negative integer per vertex. It discards the embeddings, the
lattice, the order and the curve; the fibre over a value is a positive fraction of the
graph. It propagates in a constrained way: Eichler's formula fixes the **exact total**
`Σ_E m_D(E)/w_E = h(D) · (local factors)`, so the field is a random non-negative integer
vector with a **known sum** — which is what makes its fluctuation, and only its fluctuation,
the interesting quantity.

**Mechanism.** Every "look for vertices with unusually many small endomorphisms" filter —
and B1-9's enrichment hunt is exactly that — is asking for a **large upper deviation of
`m_D`**. The size of the largest achievable deviation is not a free parameter: the vector
`(m_D(E))_E` is the `D`-th Fourier coefficient vector of the Brandt/theta correspondence,
and its projection onto a cuspidal Hecke eigenform `f` is governed (reportedly, via
Waldspurger's formula in weight `3/2`) by the **central value `L(f ⊗ χ_D, 1/2)`**
*(recollection, unverified, flagged in §0.5(iii))*. Central `L`-values in this family are
bounded by convexity and improved by subconvexity; both bounds are **`D^{o(1)}` relative to
the trivial scale**. Consequence, if the recollection holds: the enrichment available to any
`m_D`-based filter is **`p^{o(1)}` multiplicative, never polynomial** — a named obstruction
with a mechanism and a literature address, replacing the corpus's current position that
Ramanujan mixing "kills local-to-global hints" (true, but about the *mean*, not the
*fluctuation*).

**The entry is deliberately built so the test does not depend on the recollection.** The
measurement asks only: is the fluctuation Poisson (the naive null) or is there a polynomial
excess?

**Prediction.** Model `Var_E[m_D(E)] = μ · (1 + c·D^{b})` where `μ = mean_E[m_D]`.
Predicted `b = 0` (excess variance is `p^{o(1)}`, i.e. flat or logarithmic in `D`), against
a Poisson null of `Var = μ` exactly and against an "enrichment lives" alternative of
`b ≥ 1/2`. Predicted maximum enrichment `max_E m_D(E) / μ` grows like a power of `log D`,
not of `D`.

**Minimal discriminating test.** On the quaternion arm at three toy primes: for each `E`
enumerate traceless elements of norm `≤ X` (this is S1-1's census, already computed) and
read off `m_D(E)` for every `|D| ≤ X`; compute `μ`, `Var`, and `max/μ` per `D`; fit `b` by
regression over `D` on a log grid; repeat across the three primes to test stability in `p`.

**Known-answer gate.** (a) **Eichler mass identity, exact:** `Σ_E m_D(E)/w_E` must equal
`h(D)` times the stated local factor **as an exact rational equality** for every `D` tested.
This is the strongest gate in the slice: it is a hard identity, it fails loudly under any
counting error (double-counting conjugate embeddings, missing the `w_E` weight, mishandling
non-maximal orders), and it is evaluated on exactly the quantity being measured.
(b) `D = −3, −4`: `m_D` must be supported precisely on the `j = 0` / `j = 1728` vertices
when those are supersingular — two hand-checkable rows.
(c) **Poisson-null calibration:** run the identical variance-fitting pipeline on a synthetic
field drawn from a Poisson distribution with the same mean; the pipeline must return
`b = 0 ± ` its own noise floor. Without this the fitted `b` has no reference scale.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric).** Fitted `b ≥ 0.25` with confidence intervals excluding 0
at all three primes ⇒ polynomial enrichment exists in the `m_D` field, the `L`-value ceiling
as stated is wrong, and B1-9's enrichment hunt has a target worth building. Or: `b = 0`
within the calibrated noise floor at all three primes and all `D` ⇒ the family is capped and
the closure is recorded **with the mechanism named**, which is the expected and useful
outcome. Or gate (a) fails ⇒ instrument broken, stop, and note that a mass-formula failure
also invalidates S1-1's gate (c) and S1-8's constructor.

**Charged cost.** Reuses S1-1's census entirely; the incremental cost is the `(E, D)`
tabulation `O(n · X^{3/2}/p)` and the regressions. **Memory `O(n · #D)` for the field table
— charged, and it caps `X` at these graph sizes.** No preprocessing, no oracle, no quantum
resource.

**Ceiling.** `toy` for the measurement. The `L`-value mechanism, if the recollection is
confirmed at primary source, would be a `derivation`-grade ceiling; until then it is
**stated as the interpretation, not as the finding**, and the finding is the fitted `b`.

**Risk band.** `conservative`. Expected verdict: the enrichment family is capped, with a
number.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `IDEA-20260806-bcbcf5` (random-matrix analysis of isogeny
graphs has the wrong null: eigenvalues are Hecke eigenvalues, so GOE deviation is the
arithmetic, not a weakness). **Why it is not that:** `-bcbcf5` says what the null is *not*
and warns of a guaranteed false positive; S1-7 supplies the **correct null for a specific
field** (Poisson, calibrated), measures the deviation from it, and attaches a **named

S1-7 the **variance** of the embedding-count field.

### S1-8. Planted-orientation instances: the positive control this whole slice is missing, and the filter sensitivity horizon it measures

**Tracked object + what it discards.** TO-CMINC / TO-BALL used **in reverse**: instead of
measuring a field on random curves, *construct* curves with a **known planted** small
endomorphism of prescribed discriminant `D`, and track what each candidate filter reports.
The tracked object is the pair (planted `D`, filter verdict); it discards the curve, the
embedding, and everything else, keeping a single hit/miss bit per (instance, filter, `D`).

**Mechanism.** The corpus's single largest recorded methodological failure is missing or
unrun positive controls: `CALIBRATION.md` retracts an INERT verdict because the blind
control did not run; `SCREENING.md` §2 makes "instrument fidelity before measurement" a
screen criterion; `DEC-20260805-cc2b32` rejects a contract for lacking a reachable negative.
Every entry in this slice that measures a per-vertex statistic (S1-1, S1-2, S1-7, and B1-9
before them) has the same latent defect: **a filter that never fires cannot be distinguished
from a filter that is broken.**

Planted instances fix that, and they are constructible here without Sage: for a
class-number-small discriminant `D`, the Hilbert class polynomial `H_D` has small degree and
integer coefficients; reduced mod a prime `p` **inert** in `Q(√D)`, its roots are
supersingular `j`-invariants with a **guaranteed** optimal embedding of `O_D`
(Deuring reduction). `build_isogeny_graph.py` **already does exactly this** for its seed
vertex and **already verifies supersingularity independently by point counting** rather than
trusting the construction — so the constructor exists, is validated, and needs only to be
run over a ladder of `D` rather than one `D`.

The research payload is the **filter sensitivity horizon**: for each candidate filter, the
largest `|D|` at which it still fires on planted instances at rate `≥ 0.9`. §0.3(3) predicts
the horizon is set by the ball radius the filter can afford, `|D|_max ≈ (ball size)^2`, and
S1-2 predicts the horizon and the cost exponent `c` are two views of one number.

**Prediction.** *(session derivation, unverified)* Every ball-computable filter has a
horizon `|D|_max ≈ ℓ^{2r}` where `r` is the radius it explores, i.e. `|D|_max = C_test^2`.
Cross-check with S1-2: `c = 1/2` is equivalent to "horizon = (test cost)^2". If the two
independent measurements agree to within 10%, the S1-2 identity is corroborated by a route
that does not use its own derivation; if they disagree, one of the two is wrong and the
disagreement localises which.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Build a **ladder** of planted instances at each toy prime:
`D ∈ {−3, −4, −7, −8, −11, −19, −43, −67, −163}` (class number 1, degree-1 `H_D`, trivially
computable) extended by small-class-number `D` up to `|D| ≈ p^{2/3}` where planting becomes
vacuous because every curve qualifies. For each candidate filter from S1-2's list, measure
the firing rate on planted instances and on matched random instances, and extract the
horizon. Report a confusion table: (planted, fires), (planted, misses), (random, fires),
(random, misses).

**Known-answer gate.** (a) `D = −4` at `p ≡ 3 mod 4` must produce `j = 1728` and `D = −3`
at `p ≡ 2 mod 3` must produce `j = 0` — two known `j`-invariants, checkable against a
constant. (b) Every planted `j` must pass the **independent** supersingularity check (point
counting, not the CM construction) — the same independence discipline the existing file
already enforces. (c) At `|D| ≥ p^{2/3}·C` the planting must become **statistically
invisible**: firing rates on planted and random instances must coincide, because the
property is now generic. Gate (c) is the one that discriminates: a filter that "fires" on
everything at large `|D|` and is scored as sensitive would be a false positive, and this
gate catches it.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric).** Some filter fires at rate `≥ 0.9` on planted instances
with `|D| ≫ C_test^2` ⇒ the horizon law is wrong, filters see further than their ball, and
S1-2's `c ≥ 1/2` bound is in trouble — the interesting branch, and it is reachable.
Or: **every** filter, including exhaustive BFS at the exact planted radius, misses planted
instances ⇒ the constructor is broken (most likely a splitting-field or inertness error) and
nothing else in the slice can be trusted until it is fixed. Or gate (a) fails ⇒ stop
immediately.

**Charged cost.** `H_D` for class number `≤ 4` at small `|D|`: closed-form or short

class polynomials of degree `≈ h(D) ≈ p^{1/3}`, which is infeasible even at toy scale beyond
small `h`. The ladder is therefore SPARSE at the top and the write-up must say so, or the
horizon fit will be quoted over a range it never covered.** No oracle, no quantum resource.

**Ceiling.** `control`. This is instrumentation whose product is a confusion table and a
horizon per filter. It certifies nothing about the hardness of anything; it certifies that
the measurements in S1-1, S1-2 and S1-7 are being made by instruments that can fail.

**Risk band.** `conservative`.

### S1-9. Path finding is coset identification in a virtually free group: the group-theoretic half is linear time, so the whole cost is the half nobody models

**Tracked object + what it discards.** TO-COSET: the word-length class of the coset
representing `E` in `Γ \ T_{ℓ+1}`, where `T_{ℓ+1}` is the Bruhat–Tits tree of `PGL_2(Q_ℓ)`
and `Γ = O[1/ℓ]^× / Z[1/ℓ]^×` for a maximal order `O ⊂ B_{p,∞}` — the identification being
the Ihara / strong-approximation statement that the `ℓ`-isogeny graph **is** `Γ \ T_{ℓ+1}`
*(recollection, unverified, flagged in §0.5(iv))*. It discards the coset representative and
retains one integer; `ℓ^n` cosets share a length, so it is not invertible.

**Mechanism.** `Γ` acts on a tree with finite stabilisers, hence is **virtually free**
(Karrass–Pietrowski–Solitar), hence has a rational normal form, an automatic structure, and
a **linear-time word problem and shortest-word problem**. Naively this looks like a route:
path finding is "find a short word in `Γ`", and short words in virtually free groups are
easy. The mechanism says exactly where the route dies, and the statement is sharper than
"it doesn't work":

> **Path finding decomposes into (1) coset identification — given `j(E)`, produce the coset
> — and (2) the word problem in `Γ`. Step (2) is linear time. Therefore 100% of the cost is
> step (1), and step (1) is not a group-theoretic problem at all.**

Two corollaries worth recording because they close a family and redirect a second:
(a) the whole geometric-group-theory toolkit (Dehn functions, automatic structures,
hyperbolicity, small cancellation) acts only on step (2) and is therefore **exponent-neutral
by construction**; (b) the word-length metric on `Γ` and the reduced-norm metric are related
by `length = log_ℓ Nrd` **exactly**, by multiplicativity of the norm — so there is **no
metric distortion to exploit**, a forced identity that removes the one quantity a
geometric-group-theoretic attack would look for.

**Prediction.** In an end-to-end toy implementation, the measured fraction of total
field-operation cost attributable to step (2) is `< 1%` at every graph size and **decreases**
with `p`; the measured word-length/log-norm relation is exact (residual identically zero,
not approximately zero).

**Minimal discriminating test.** On S1-1's instrument at three toy primes: implement the
decomposition explicitly — represent each vertex by a normal-form word obtained from a BFS
spanning tree of the graph plus the extra generators (the graph is `Γ \ T`, so a spanning
tree plus the non-tree edges is a presentation), then for random `(E, E')` (i) time the coset
identification (find which vertex `j(E)` is, i.e. locate it in the vertex table) and (ii)
time the word-problem solve (concatenate and reduce). Report the split. Separately, verify
`length = log_ℓ Nrd` on 10^3 random elements against the quaternion arm.

**Known-answer gate.** (a) **Free-group positive control:** run the same normal-form and
reduction machinery on a genuinely free group with an explicit faithful action where short
words are known in advance (e.g. the `ℓ+1`-regular tree itself, no quotient), and confirm it
returns the known shortest words in linear time. If the machinery is slow *there*, the `<1%`
figure on the real graph would be measuring the implementation, not the mathematics.
(b) **Metric gate:** the identity `length = log_ℓ Nrd` must hold with residual exactly 0;
any nonzero residual means the generator set or the norm normalisation is wrong.
(c) Degenerate gate: at `E = E'` the word must be empty and both timings zero.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric).** Step (2) accounts for `> 10%` of total cost at any of
the three sizes, or its share **grows** with `p` ⇒ the decomposition's central claim (the
group half is free) is false as stated and the entry is withdrawn. Or: the metric residual
is nonzero on any element ⇒ gate (b) fails, stop. Note the honest asymmetry, stated because
`DEC-20260805-cc2b32` requires it: the *positive* branch here ("a cheap coset-identification
method exists") is **unreachable by this test**, and the entry does not pretend otherwise —
what is reachable is the decomposition, its cost split, and the two corollaries.

**Charged cost.** Graph build as in S1-1; spanning tree `O(n)`; 10^3 word reductions at
`O(log n)` each. **Memory `O(n · log n)` for the normal-form table — and note that this
table *is* the coset-identification oracle, so an implementation that keeps it and then
reports coset identification as cheap has charged nothing: the table costs `Θ(n) = Θ(p)` to
build, which is the whole point. The write-up must state that the toy implementation pays
`Θ(p)` for exactly the step the real attacker cannot pay.** That self-indicting note is the
entry's most important sentence and must not be dropped. No oracle beyond the counted
lookups, no quantum resource.

**Ceiling.** `control`. A decomposition plus two forced identities plus a measured cost
split at toy scale. It certifies nothing about hardness; it relocates where the hardness is
allowed to live and closes a toolkit.

**Risk band.** `conservative`. Expected verdict: the geometric-group-theory family is closed
as exponent-neutral, with the relocation recorded.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `IDEA-20260806-d5a34e` (isogeny-graph query model `IGQ`,
aiming at a matching `Ω(p^{1/3−o(1)})` query lower bound). **Why it is not that:** `-d5a34e`
proposes a **cost model** in which a lower bound might be proved; S1-9 proposes a
**structural decomposition** of the problem into two named subproblems and measures which
one carries the cost. `-d5a34e` would benefit from S1-9's conclusion (it identifies exactly

extracted from local transport as a distinguishing statistic; S1-9 tracks the *word length*
of a coset and its conclusion is a cost split, not a statistic. No statistic is computed
here and no distinguisher is proposed.

### S1-10. The marking is free: maximal-order **type** and **marked** endomorphism ring are polynomially equivalent, so a lattice-invariant result would be a full route and not a structural curiosity

**Tracked object + what it discards.** The **maximal-order type**: the isomorphism class of
the Gross lattice (a rank-3 positive-definite integral quadratic form of discriminant `p^2`,
up to `GL_3(Z)`), forgetting the marking — i.e. forgetting any explicit isomorphism
`End(E) ≅ O` and every endomorphism as an explicit rational map.

**Rule-4 disclosure, restated in place.** By the classical Deuring statement, types are in
bijection with Galois orbits `{j, j^p}`, so this object is **near-invertible** and would be a
repackaging if offered as an attack object. It is not offered as one. The entry's deliverable
**is** the invertibility, with the inversion charged, and the entry is admitted under house
rule 8 as a scope/ceiling argument whose expected verdict is CLOSED.

**Mechanism.** There is a tempting and, this entry argues, **false** hope in the air around
`IDEA-20260806-a3ef00` and `KN-LIT-7648` (DEFI: genus = spinor genus = class collapses an
isomorphism problem in classical polynomial time): the hope that even if some lattice
invariant determined `End(E)` *as an abstract lattice*, that would be a merely structural
result, because you would still not have any endomorphism as a map. The entry's claim is the
opposite, and it is a short argument:

> Given the **abstract** type `O`, one can (1) compute a connecting ideal `I` between the
> fixed known order `O_0` and `O` by pure quaternion linear algebra in polynomial time,
> (2) smooth it by ideal-side techniques, and (3) translate to an explicit isogeny
> `E_0 → E''`. Since the type determines the curve **up to Galois**, `E''` is `E` or
> `E^{(p)}` — a two-way branch resolved by one comparison. **Hence Type ⟶ Marked in
> polynomial time, and a lattice-invariant attack is a full route, not a curiosity.**

The contrapositive is the part that matters for planning: `IDEA-20260806-a3ef00`'s
genus/spinor route is therefore **worth more** than a purely structural framing suggests,
and simultaneously the corpus's twelve closures — all stated for the marked problem — apply
verbatim to the type problem. That is a scope statement that changes how two live records
are read, and it costs one page.

Note the honest caveats, both of which the test evaluates rather than assumes: step (2) is
KLPT-family translation used as a **charged black box** (OL-4: not re-tuned, not improved,
its cost taken as given and reported), and step (1) presumes the type is delivered in a form
from which a connecting ideal is computable — the same "input encoding" question
`IDEA-20260805-d66193`'s ladder rung G4 ("abstract order") raises. If the type is delivered
only as an abstract isometry class with no embedding into a fixed `B_{p,∞}`, step (1) needs
an explicit isometry first, and **that** is where a real cost could hide. Measuring it is the
test.

**Prediction.** *(session derivation, unverified)* Measured cost of Type ⟶ Marked at toy
scale grows as `(log p)^{O(1)}`, with fitted exponent in `p` **statistically indistinguishable
from 0** across three primes; and the type number equals the number of Galois orbits of
supersingular `j`-invariants **exactly** at every toy prime.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Two arms, both on existing tooling.
*(A) Bijection check, exhaustive.* At three toy primes, compute the full vertex set from
`build_isogeny_graph.py`, form Galois orbits, and independently enumerate maximal-order
types on the quaternion arm; check `#types = #orbits` **exactly** and that the induced map is
a bijection.
*(B) Marking cost.* Implement Type ⟶ Marked: given a Gram matrix, recover an embedding into
`B_{p,∞}`, compute a connecting ideal from `O_0`, and translate to a path in the graph;
measure field operations as a function of `p` and fit the exponent. Report the three stages
separately, because the entry's whole risk is that stage 1 (explicit isometry from a Gram
matrix) is the expensive one and gets averaged away.

**Known-answer gate.** (a) **`O = O_0`**: the pipeline must return the empty path with zero
translation cost. A pipeline that returns a nonempty path at the base point is not computing
what it claims. (b) **The type-number identity** in arm (A) is itself a gate for arm (B): if
`#types ≠ #orbits` at a toy prime, the Deuring recollection §0.5(v) is mis-stated and the
argument's second step collapses. (c) **Galois branch gate:** feed the pipeline the type of a
vertex with `j ∈ F_p` (self-conjugate) and confirm the two-way branch degenerates to one —
the degenerate parameter setting whose correct output is known in advance.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric).** The fitted exponent of Type ⟶ Marked is `≥ 0.1` in `p`
across three primes, with stage 1 (Gram matrix ⟶ explicit embedding) identified as the
culprit ⇒ **the marking is not free**, type-only results are strictly weaker than marked
ones, the corpus's twelve closures do **not** transfer to the type problem, and
`IDEA-20260806-a3ef00`'s value must be re-assessed downward. That is a fully reachable
negative and it is the branch that would matter most. Or: `#types ≠ #orbits` ⇒ stop and
re-derive. Or gate (a) fails ⇒ implementation error.
