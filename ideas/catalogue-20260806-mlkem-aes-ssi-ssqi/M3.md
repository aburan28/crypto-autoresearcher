# M3 — ML-KEM: physical cost models, side channels, fault, and conformance instrumentation

Slice: the **mechanisms in the cost model and in the physical realisation** — memory- and
wiring-charged sieve accounting, the core-SVP convention's deliberate omissions, quantum
resource accounting under differing gate/depth/QRAM conventions, the intrinsic identifiability
of ML-KEM intermediates under an abstract leakage law, the query economics of chosen-ciphertext
oracles, fault against implicit rejection, and differential conformance instrumentation for
defect classes that are **not** the re-encryption comparison class.

`GOAL-MLKEM-002` owns the re-encryption-comparison class (fixed-bound hand-written vector tails,
wolfSSL AVX2/NEON, byte-complete differential gate, `second_impl_unavailable`). **Three
conformance entries appear below and none of them is that class.** M3-8 is decapsulation-key
field integrity, M3-9 is comparison surrogates with a nontrivial kernel, M3-10 is XOF
block-budget conformance in `SampleNTT`. Each names a mechanism the ciphertext-mutation
generator provably cannot reach.

**No entry below claims, predicts, or implies a break, a key recovery against a fielded
parameter set, an asymptotic exponent improvement, or beating published state of the art.**
Claim ceilings are `control`, `toy` or `medium`. Every entry carries `sota_delta: 0` and
`novelty_status: unverified`: eprint.iacr.org, arXiv and nist.gov are unreachable from this
environment, so nothing here is asserted new and nothing here is dismissed as known.

### 0.2 Tracked objects and the lossy-projection test

Three tracked objects are shared across the slice. Each is a genuine projection: the original
is not recoverable, and the discard is structural rather than a change of coordinates.

**TO-COST — the charged-resource vector.** Real object: one physical execution of an attack on
one machine. Tracked object:
`R(A) = (ops, peak live cells, total inter-cell message-hops, maximum sequential circuit depth,
oracle calls, machine spatial dimension D)`.

- *Lossy?* Yes, catastrophically. `R` is a five-integer summary of a `2^Θ(β)`-step computation;
  every vector, every coordinate, every lattice, the success probability and the correctness
  argument are discarded. The fibre over a given `R` contains attacks on different lattices,
  in different dimensions, with different success probabilities.
- *Not a change of coordinates.* The incumbent representation is the exponent **pair**
  `(c_T, c_M)`; `TO-COST` adds two coordinates the pair does not determine (hops, depth) and
  one the pair cannot even express (`D`). Section 0.3's ceiling is precisely the statement that
  the missing coordinate `D` is what the accounting family `Cost_α = T·M^α` was silently
  parameterising all along.
- *Compatible with the operations that matter.* `R` composes additively under sequencing and
  takes a maximum under time-sharing, which is what makes stage-wise charging well posed.

**TO-LEAK — the induced observation law.** Real object: a physical measurement of a running
implementation. Tracked object: the conditional law `P(ℓ | x)` of an *abstract* observable `ℓ`
given a named intermediate `x`, and the scalar functionals derived from it (total variation,
mutual information, channel capacity).

- *Lossy?* Yes. Time alignment, device physics, noise autocorrelation, burst structure,
  temperature, the trace itself, and every constant of proportionality are discarded. What
  survives is only the *identifiability* of `x` from `ℓ` — an upper bound on what any decoder
  could extract, independent of the decoder.
- *Not a change of coordinates.* You cannot reconstruct a trace from a mutual information.
  The whole value of the projection is that it is computable **before** any acquisition, and
  the whole cost of the projection is that it can never certify that a real device attains it.
  Every M3 entry using `TO-LEAK` states an *upper* bound on extractable information and never
  a lower bound on attacker cost in the field.

**TO-SIG — the observable-difference signature.** Real object: an implementation of ML-KEM
(source, compiler, backend, dispatch path). Tracked object: for a declared generator `G` of
input perturbations, the Boolean (or 2-bit) vector
`sig(impl) = ( [output(perturbed) ≠ output(baseline)] )_{g ∈ G}`.

- *Lossy?* Yes, and the discard is the point: the entire internal state, the control flow, the
  register allocation and the source are gone; only the input/output difference relation
  remains. The fibre over a given signature is the set of all implementations
  observationally equivalent under `G`, which is infinite.
- *Not a change of coordinates.* `sig` depends on `G`. Changing `G` changes the tracked object,
  and **M3-9 is exactly the theorem relating the arity of `G` to which fibres it can separate.**
  That relation is unavailable in the original representation.

### 0.3 The ceilings that bind every entry, stated first

> **CEILING-M3-A (cost side).** `Cost_α = T·M^α` is not a free one-parameter family. For a
> machine laid out in `D` spatial dimensions with `M` cells and bisection bandwidth
> `Θ(M^{(D−1)/D})`, full cost is `T·M^{1/D}` — so **`α = 1/D` exactly**, and physical space
> forces `D ≤ 3`, i.e. `α ≥ 1/3` for a *random-access* workload and `α ∈ [0, 1/3]` once
> locality is credited. `α = 1` is the one-dimensional machine. No entry in this slice may
> quote `α = 1` as "area-time" without saying that it is the `D = 1` charge. This ceiling is
> derived in M3-1 and then used by M3-2, M3-3 and M3-4 without re-derivation.

> **CEILING-M3-B (leakage side).** Nothing computed from `TO-LEAK` is a statement about any
> device. An identifiability number is an upper bound over all decoders under a *stipulated*
> observation law; a real implementation with masking, shuffling and noise attains strictly
> less, and by an amount this slice cannot measure. Consequently **no M3 entry converts an
> identifiability number into a trace count, a query count against a fielded device, or a
> security-level statement.** The one direction that is legitimate is the negative one: a
> capacity floor that says a published query count *cannot* be beaten (M3-5).

> **CEILING-M3-C (conformance side).** A null under a finite generator is not a correctness
> proof (`RQ-MLKEM-002` constraint). Each conformance entry therefore delivers *the generator's
> power against a named defect class*, with the class's silent set given in closed form, and
> never "the implementation is correct". Any newly observed conformance defect escalates to the
> Coordinator and is published by no agent.

> **CEILING-M3-D (tier).** Every entry is `control`, `toy` or `medium`. Reduced-dimension sieve
> measurement (M3-2) and single-implementation emulation (M3-7) are `toy` and may not be
> transferred to FIPS 203 parameter sets. Nothing here is `cryptographic`.

## 1. The ten ideas

### M3-1. `T·M^α` is the family of `D`-dimensional machines with `α = 1/D`; physical space caps the charge at `1/3`, and that closes `H-MLKEM-b1300f`'s enumeration cap

**Tracked object / what it discards.** `TO-COST`, restricted to `(T, M, D)`. Discards the
lattice, the vectors, the success probability and all constants; retains the machine's spatial
dimension, which the incumbent exponent pair `(c_T, c_M)` cannot express at all. The discard is
structural: two attacks with identical `(T, M)` on machines of different `D` have different
full costs, so `D` is not recoverable from the pair.

**Mechanism.** Let the machine be a `D`-dimensional mesh of `V` cells, `V ≥ M`. Its bisection
bandwidth is `Θ(V^{(D−1)/D})`, so delivering `T` random operand fetches takes wall time
`t ≥ T / V^{(D−1)/D}`. Full cost is `V·t = T·V^{1/D}`, minimised at `V = M`:
**`FullCost = T·M^{1/D}`.** Hence `α = 1/D` exactly. `D = 3` gives `α = 1/3`; `D = 2` (the
thermally-limited layout, since heat removal is surface-limited) gives `α = 1/2`; `D = 1` gives
`α = 1`. So `H-MLKEM-b1300f`'s `α = 1`, described there as "area-time under the identification
area ~ stored vectors", is the **one-dimensional** machine: it charges as if a memory of `M`
cells served one request per time step, when a 3-D layout of `M` cells serves `M^{2/3}`. Perfect
operand locality gives `α = 0`, so the physically admissible band is `α ∈ [0, 1/3]`.

**Prediction (forced arithmetic on the relayed constants).** With `c_T = 0.292`,
`c_M = 0.2075`, `c_E = 0.04764016`:

| β (illustrative) | sieve α=0 | sieve α=1/3 | sieve α=1/2 | sieve α=1 | enumeration | α needed for enum ≤ sieve |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 400 | 116.800 | **144.467** | 158.300 | 199.800 | 164.718 | **0.5773** |
| 600 | 175.200 | **216.700** | 237.450 | 299.700 | 263.797 | **0.7116** |
| 875 | 255.500 | **316.021** | 346.281 | 437.062 | 407.395 | **0.8366** |

The required `α` exceeds `1/3` at every illustrative block size by a factor of at least
`0.5773 / 0.3333 = 1.732`, and exceeds even the thermally-limited `1/2` at all three.
Therefore **`H-MLKEM-b1300f`'s P3 — "at every ML-KEM-relevant block size and at α = 1 the
cheaper SVP oracle is enumeration" — is false for every physically admissible `D ≥ 2`, and its
headline overstatements of 35.1 / 35.9 / 29.7 bits reverse sign**: at `α = 1/3` the enumeration
"cap" sits 20.25 / 47.10 / 91.37 bits *above* the sieve, so it caps nothing. The surviving,
defender-favourable statement is that memory charging under 3-D wiring adds
`(c_M/3)·β = 27.67 / 41.50 / 60.52` bits to the core-SVP figure. P1 (the shift exponent
`1 + 0.7106α`) is untouched; only the interpretation of `α` changes, and `β*(1/3) = 191.4`
reproduces the ladder's 191.5 to rounding.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Zero compute; algebra plus a table.
(1) Write the bisection/full-cost derivation for general `D` and evaluate the table above.
(2) **Known-answer gate:** the derivation must return, without adjustment, `α = 1` at `D = 1`,
and at `D = 1` the sieve column must reproduce `H-MLKEM-b1300f`'s published 199.8 / 299.7 /
437.1 exactly; and at `α = 0` it must reproduce 116.8 / 175.2 / 255.5 exactly. A derivation
that cannot reproduce the two endpoints it generalises is void.
(3) **Degenerate gate:** set `c_M = 0`. Then `α` must drop out of every cell and all four sieve
columns must coincide; if they do not, the instrument manufactures a dependence.
(4) Report `d(cost)/dα = c_M·β` beside each cell so the sensitivity, not only the optimum, is
visible.

**Falsifier.** F1-a: a machine model in which full cost is *not* `V·t` — e.g. energy-per-bit-hop
with a length-dependent factor — under which the physical `α` exceeds `0.5773`. Then P3
survives and this entry is wrong; the entry must then report which physical quantity the
`V·t` accounting omits. F1-b: the bisection bound is inapplicable because sieve operand fetches
are not random — but that pushes `α` *down*, strengthening the conclusion, so it cannot falsify.
F1-c: `c_M/c_T` is wrong by enough to move the required `α` below `1/3`; the break-even is
`c_M ≥ 3(c_E·log2 β − c_T)`, i.e. `c_M ≥ 0.3594` at `β = 400` — far above the kissing-number
floor 0.2075, so F1-c requires the relayed constants to be wrong by 73%.

**Charged cost.** Implementation: none. Compute: none. The only charged resource is the
author's arithmetic and the two relayed constants, both labelled. No estimator, no container,
no lattice.

**Ceiling.** `medium` — cost-model arithmetic over relayed constants. No FIPS 203 parameter set
is affected or cleared; the illustrative block sizes are labelled illustrative.

**Risk.** `representation_changing` — it replaces a free parameter `α` by a physical parameter
`D` and therefore removes half the family as unphysical.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `memory_charged_SVP_oracle|Cost_alpha=T*M^alpha|
enum_vs_sieve_crossover|shift_exponent_1+0.7106alpha_forced` (`H-MLKEM-b1300f`,
`IDEA-20260805-cdc87d`). **Why it is not that:** that record sweeps `α ∈ [0,1]` and declares the
mapping from a physical model to `α` its "weakest link"; this entry *supplies* the mapping,
finds it two-sided and narrow, and consequently refutes that record's P3 rather than extending

### M3-2. The locality exponent of a real sieve, measured by counting hops rather than by timing

**Tracked object / what it discards.** The **operand-fetch hop histogram**: for a sieve run,
the multiset `{ hop(i,j) }` over every computed inner product, where `hop` is the L1 distance
between the two operands' cells under a fixed bucket-major embedding of the database into a
cubic grid. Discards every coordinate of every vector, every norm, every lattice, the reduction
quality and the output. What survives is one histogram of integers. Not invertible: the same
histogram is produced by any relabelling of vectors within buckets.

**Mechanism.** M3-1 caps the charge at `α = 1/3` for *random* access. A bucketed sieve (bgj1,
BDGL) is not random-access: most inner products are intra-bucket. In a 3-D mesh of `N` cells the
wires deliver `Θ(N)` bit-hops per time step, so the communication full cost is exactly the total
hop count `H = Σ hop(i,j)`, and the compute full cost is `T`. Define
`ρ = mean_hop / N^{1/3} ∈ (0, 1]` and `α_eff = 1/3 + log2(ρ)/(c_M·d)`. `ρ = 1` is the
random-access value; `ρ → 0` is perfect locality. **`ρ` is a counted quantity, not a timed one**,
which is why the unresolved QEMU-versus-native obstruction (`EV-MLKEM-d8f627`,
`EV-MLKEM-4a9cfe`) cannot bind this measurement: an emulator perturbs wall-clock, not counters.

**Prediction.** For bgj1 with its declared bucketing, `ρ(d)` decreases in `d`, giving
`α_eff(d) = 1/3 − γ` with `γ > 0` and `γ` stable across `d ∈ [50, 80]`; for gauss_sieve (no
bucketing) `ρ(d) = 1.00 ± sampling error` at every `d`. Pre-registered separation:
`ρ_bgj1(80) ≤ 0.60`, i.e. `α_eff ≤ 1/3 − 0.0308` at `d = 80`, against `ρ_gauss(80) ≥ 0.95`.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Instrument the sieve to emit, per inner product, the pair of
bucket ids; stream them into a hop histogram (never store the trace).
**Known-answer gates, three, all with analytically fixed answers:**
(G-A) **Random-permutation embedding.** Assign vectors to cells uniformly at random, ignoring
buckets. The mean L1 distance between two uniform points of a cube of side `L` is exactly `L`
asymptotically, so the instrument must return `ρ = 1.000` within sampling error. Any other
value is an instrument defect, not a locality finding.
(G-B) **Single-bucket configuration.** Force bucket count 1. The embedding degenerates to the
random one, so again `ρ = 1.000`.
(G-C) **Synthetic all-within-one-bucket trace of bucket size `B`.** The instrument must return
`ρ = (B/N)^{1/3}` exactly.
Then run bgj1 and gauss_sieve at `d = 50, 60, 70, 80`, three seeds each, and report `ρ(d)` with
its bootstrap interval.

**Falsifier.** F2-a: `|α_eff − 1/3| < 0.01` at `d = 80` on the bucketed sieve ⇒ locality
contributes `< 0.01·c_M·β = 0.002β` bits, i.e. `< 0.83` bits at `β = 400`; the lever is CLOSED
with a number and M3-1's cap is tight. F2-b: any gate returns a value outside its analytic
answer ⇒ instrument failure, no disposition (AGENTS.md rule 5). F2-c: `ρ` shows no trend in `d`
over four dimensions on either sieve ⇒ the quantity is dimension-independent and cannot be an
exponent; report as such.

**Charged cost.** Instrumentation: ~200 lines against g6k's bucketing hooks, plus a streaming
histogram. Compute: sieving at `d ≤ 80` is minutes to hours per seed; 24 runs. **Memory is the
binding resource and must be charged:** the hop trace is `Θ(T)` pairs and must be histogrammed
online — storing it is a defect, not a saving. Container: `KN-TECH-14efa5` / `KN-TECH-797223`
ephemeral rebuild; declared infrastructure risk if fpylll/g6k cannot be built.

**Ceiling.** `toy`. `d ≤ 80` is reduced dimension; no statement about FIPS 203 block sizes may
be made from it (`RQ-MLKEM-003.claim_tier_ceiling`). What transfers is a *measured value of a
model parameter*, reported with its dimension range on its face.

**Risk.** `high_risk` — the prediction that `ρ` decays is a genuine bet, and the honest prior is
that a bucketed sieve's cross-bucket traffic dominates precisely because bucketing is done by
*sorting the whole database*.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `cross_run_T_N_variance|bgj1_constant_N_vs_gauss_sieve_variable_N|
N_confound_r=0.82|QEMU_artifact_blocking` (`EV-MLKEM-cca527`, `EV-MLKEM-d8f627`,
`EV-MLKEM-4a9cfe`). **Why it is not that:** that lane measured *timing variance* and died on an
emulation confound; this measures *counted hops*, which the confound cannot reach, and it feeds
a declared parameter of the cost model rather than a variance.

### M3-3. Charge the QRAM and Grover loses: `11/6` against `4/3` in the full-cost exponent, and the `2^40` depth cap at ML-KEM-512's illustrative block size

**Tracked object / what it discards.** `TO-COST` restricted to the triple
`(hardware cells, sequential depth, per-access depth)` for the *near-neighbour search
subroutine* of a sieve. Discards the quantum state, the gate set, the error-correction scheme,
the algorithm's correctness and its success amplitude. Structural: two circuits with the same
triple can differ in every gate; the triple is a three-integer summary of a
`2^Θ(β)`-gate object, and the fibre over it is astronomically large.

**Mechanism, in three lines, each independent of `c_M` and `β`.** Let `M = 2^{c_M β}` be the
sieve database.
- *Classical parallel search:* hardware `M`, time = routing/reduction `M^{1/3}` in 3-D, full
  cost `M^{4/3}`.
- *Grover with a 3-D QRAM:* hardware `M` (the QRAM cells are charged), sequential depth
  `M^{1/2}` Grover iterations each of access depth `M^{1/3}`, full cost `M^{1 + 1/2 + 1/3} = M^{11/6}`.
- *Grover with a polylog-depth QRAM (the conventional idealisation):* full cost `M^{3/2}`.

**Prediction.** `11/6 ÷ 4/3 = 1.375` and `3/2 ÷ 4/3 = 1.125`. **Grover's full-cost exponent for
sieve near-neighbour search exceeds the classical parallel search's by 37.5% with a physically
charged QRAM and by 12.5% even with a free-access QRAM — at every block size, independently of
`c_M`, `β` and the gate set.** The break-even QRAM hardware exponent is `4/3 − 1/2 = 5/6`: a
quantum advantage in this subroutine requires a QRAM of `M` cells to cost less than `M^{5/6}`
hardware. In bits at the illustrative block sizes: classical `110.67 / 166.00 / 242.08`, Grover
3-D `152.17 / 228.25 / 332.86`, gap `41.50 / 62.25 / 90.78` — against a headline quantum sieve
gain of only `(0.292 − 0.265)·β = 10.8 / 16.2 / 23.6` bits (the `0.265` is
RECALLED-UNVERIFIED and the conclusion does not depend on it, since the gap exceeds the gain by
`≥ 3.8×` at every listed `β`).

A second, independent and sharper observation from the same triple: the Grover *depth* alone is
`2^{0.5·c_M·β} = 2^{41.50} / 2^{62.25} / 2^{90.78}`, against NIST's stated MAXDEPTH convention
of `2^{40} / 2^{64} / 2^{96}` for categories 1/3/5. **At the category-1 illustrative block size
the required depth exceeds the convention's own cap by 1.50 bits, while categories 3 and 5 sit
1.75 and 5.22 bits under theirs.** So the depth convention binds at exactly one of the three
and the quoted quantum figure is not simultaneously claimable with depth compliance there.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Zero compute; algebra plus a table.
**Known-answer gates:** (a) set per-access depth to `M^0` and QRAM hardware to `poly` — the
derivation must return the textbook `M^{1/2}` speedup exactly, i.e. reproduce the convention it
generalises; (b) set the Grover iteration count to `1` — the quantum and classical rows must
coincide; (c) set `c_M = 0` — every exponent must collapse to the compute term and the ratio
`1.375` must become `1`. Then tabulate the three conventions × three illustrative `β`, with the
MAXDEPTH comparison in a fourth column and the sign of every gap.

**Falsifier.** F3-a: a QRAM architecture whose hardware scales below `M^{5/6}` for `M` cells —
then the quantum row wins and the entry is wrong; the entry names this as the single quantity
that decides it. F3-b: a classical parallel near-neighbour search that cannot achieve `M^{1/3}`
reduction time in 3-D (e.g. because the reduction is not a tree) — then the classical exponent
rises and the ratio shrinks; the break-even classical time exponent is `M^{1/2}`, at which the
two coincide. F3-c: the MAXDEPTH numbers are misremembered; the *structure* of the claim (one of
three categories binds) survives only if the cap triple is `2^{40}/2^{64}/2^{96}`, so this is a
retrieval-blocked check and must be labelled RECALLED-UNVERIFIED until a primary read is
possible.

**Charged cost.** None beyond arithmetic. The entry exists partly to charge a resource nobody in
this program has charged: **QRAM cells are hardware occupied over time and this repository has
never priced them.**

**Ceiling.** `medium`. A convention-comparison result over relayed constants. It makes the
defender's number larger, which is still a result, and it is not a statement about any FIPS 203
parameter set.

**Risk.** `conservative` — the arithmetic is forced once the three conventions are written down.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `memory_charged_re_derivation|c_star_erasing_the_undercut|
c_star<1/3_invariant|memory_exponent_sweep_0.05_0.40` (`EV-MLKEM-020`). **Why it is not that:**
that record charges *classical* memory to a *classical* cost margin and never contains a
quantum term, a depth coordinate, or a QRAM; this entry's whole content is the two coordinates
that record does not have.

### M3-4. Charge the dual attack's own declared data structures — `N` stored vectors and the `q^{k_fft}` accumulator table — instead of a borrowed sieve-memory literal

**Tracked object / what it discards.** The **peak live-set timeline**: a step function over the

### M3-5. The channel-capacity floor on chosen-ciphertext oracle query counts, and the pre-attack test it hands `H-MLKEM-008`

**Tracked object / what it discards.** `TO-LEAK`, projected to a single scalar: the **per-query
channel capacity** `C = log2|response alphabet| − H(response | correct answer)`, together with
the secret's Shannon entropy `H(s)`. Discards the decoder, the lattice, the chosen-ciphertext
construction, the adaptivity schedule, the device, and the trace. What survives is a bound that
no decoder can beat. Not invertible: infinitely many leakage laws share a capacity.

**Mechanism.** A plaintext-checking or decryption-failure oracle with binary response and
accuracy `p` is a binary symmetric channel of capacity `C(p) = 1 − h₂(p)`. Key recovery must
transmit `H(s)` bits through it, so `queries ≥ H(s) / C(p)` — unconditionally, for every
adaptive strategy, every decoder, and every amount of offline lattice post-processing that does
not itself query. `H(s)` is computable exactly from FIPS 203's CBD parameters.

**Prediction (exact, parameter-set-specific).** `H(CBD₂) = 2.030639` bits/coefficient and
`H(CBD₃) = 2.333362`:

| parameter set | `H(s)` | floor at `p=1.00` | at `p=0.99` | at `p=0.95` | at `p=0.90` | at `p=0.75` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML-KEM-512 (`k=2, η₁=3`) | 1194.68 | 1195 | 1300 | 1674 | 2250 | 6330 |
| ML-KEM-768 (`k=3, η₁=2`) | 1559.53 | 1560 | 1697 | **2185** | 2937 | 8264 |
| ML-KEM-1024 (`k=4, η₁=2`) | 2079.37 | 2080 | 2262 | 2914 | 3916 | 11018 |

`ORS-004`'s recorded 2950 queries for ML-KEM-768 at a stipulated 95% DF oracle sits at
**1.350× the floor**, so the published figure is within 0.43 bits of information-theoretic
optimality and there is no room for a further factor-2 query reduction at that accuracy. Second
prediction, the operationally useful one: for the soft-versus-hard comparison `H-MLKEM-008`
proposes to *measure*, the achievable ratio is bounded by `C_soft / C_hard`, where
`C_soft = I(score ; correct answer)` is computable **from the profiling set alone, before any
attack is run**. So `H-MLKEM-008`'s "factor of two" is reachable only if `C_soft/C_hard ≥ 2` in
at least one configuration, and that is a one-line check on a histogram.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Pure computation, no device.
(1) Compute `H(CBD_η)` and the floor table.
(2) Compute `C_soft/C_hard` for a *stipulated* score law (e.g. Gaussian scores with the stated
marginal accuracy) across the three declared noise configurations.
**Known-answer gates:** (a) `p = 0.5` ⇒ `C = 0` ⇒ floor `= ∞`; an instrument returning a finite
floor for a useless oracle is broken. (b) `p = 1.0` ⇒ floor must equal `H(s)` to the last digit
(1194.68 / 1559.53 / 2079.37). (c) a degenerate secret distribution with `η → 0` ⇒ `H(s) = 0`

iff the score is already binary — a data-processing-inequality check that catches a sign error
in the mutual-information code.

**Falsifier.** F5-a: a published query count below the floor at its own stated accuracy ⇒ either
the response alphabet is larger than binary (in which case the floor must be recomputed with
`log2|alphabet|`), or `H(s)` is not the right transmitted quantity (partial recovery plus
lattice post-processing transmits less), or the reported accuracy is not the operative one.
Each branch is a separate, nameable defect and the entry must say which. F5-b:
`C_soft/C_hard < 1.2` in all three stipulated configurations ⇒ `H-MLKEM-008`'s separation is
unreachable *by construction* and its measurement campaign cannot succeed; that is a decisive,
free result about an existing hypothesis. F5-c: `C_soft/C_hard ≥ 2` in some configuration ⇒ the
campaign is worth running and this entry has priced it.

**Charged cost.** Implementation: ~60 lines. Compute: seconds. **Charging note:** the floor
charges *queries only*. Profiling traces, per-query physical executions, retries and wall-clock

omitting them — an uncharged resource is a defect, not a saving.

**Ceiling.** `medium`. It is a lower bound on the attacker's queries, i.e. it can only make the
defender's number larger; it says nothing about whether any oracle exists (FIPS 203 exposes

**Risk.** `representation_changing` — it replaces "queries" as the tracked coordinate by "bits
through a channel", in which the comparison between hard and soft models becomes an identity

### M3-6. Intrinsic identifiability of ML-KEM intermediates under an abstract Hamming-weight law: a representation-forced ranking computed with zero traces

**Tracked object / what it discards.** `TO-LEAK` for one named intermediate: the exact
conditional law `P(HW(rep(x)) | class(x))` where `rep` is the FIPS 203 representation actually
stored (12-bit residue, `d_u`-bit compressed, `d_v`-bit compressed) and `class` is the
security-relevant partition (message bit; CBD secret pair). Discards the device, the noise, the
time axis, the number of shares, and every constant of the leakage function; retains only how
many bits about `class` an *ideal noiseless* observer of `HW` could obtain. Structurally lossy:
`HW` is a 4-bit summary of a 12-bit value, and the fibre over a Hamming weight has up to
`C(12,6) = 924` elements.

**Mechanism.** Identifiability is a property of the **representation**, not of the code, and it
is exactly computable by enumerating the 3329 residues. The interesting consequence is that
FIPS 203's *compression* — a bandwidth measure — is also the dominant leakage-amplification
step, because it maps the message-bit decision boundary onto the top bit of a short word.

**Prediction (exact, computed over `q = 3329`).** For the message-bit decoder
`bit(v) = round(2v/q) mod 2` (`|bit=0| = 1665`, `|bit=1| = 1664`):

| representation | `TV( HW | bit=0 , HW | bit=1 )` | `I(HW ; bit)` (bits) |
|---|---|---|
| 12-bit residue | 0.081566 | **0.010259** |
| `d = 11` compressed | 0.097790 | 0.015402 |
| `d = 10` compressed | 0.106730 | 0.016748 |
| `d = 5` compressed | 0.312782 | 0.115535 |
| `d = 4` compressed (ML-KEM-512/768 `d_v`) | **0.375263** | **0.207400** |

So the `d_v = 4` compressed representation is **20.2× more identifiable than the 12-bit residue**
for the same decision, and the ranking is forced by `d` alone. Second computation, on the first
NTT layer (twiddle `17^64 mod 3329 = 1729`, butterfly output `s_j + ζ·s_{j+128}`):
`I(HW(out) ; (s_j, s_{j+128})) = 3.0335` bits of `H = 4.0613` for `η = 2` (74.69%) and
`3.1945` of `4.6667` for `η = 3` (68.45%). **Prediction: the first NTT layer is the maximally
identifiable point of key generation under this law, and its identifiability is a function of
the twiddle and the CBD parameter only** — so it cannot be reduced by any implementation choice
short of changing the representation or adding randomness. Aggregate model residual, stated as a
model quantity and emphatically not as an attack: `128k` layer-1 butterflies at ML-KEM-768 leave
`1559.53 − 384 × 3.0335 = 394.6` bits of `H(s)` under a *noiseless* observer that no device
realises.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Exact enumeration; no sampling, no device, seconds of compute.
**Known-answer gates:** (a) identity leakage `ℓ = v` must return `I = H(bit) = 1.000000` exactly
and `I(v ; pair) = H(pair) = 4.0613` exactly; (b) constant leakage must return `0.000000`
exactly; (c) the decoder partition must have cardinalities `1665 / 1664` summing to `q` — an
off-by-one in the rounding convention is caught here and nowhere else; (d) the data-processing
inequality `I(HW(compress(v)) ; bit) ≤ I(v ; bit)` must hold at every `d`.

**Falsifier.** F6-a: `I(HW ; bit)` for `d_v = 4` fails to exceed the 12-bit value by at least
`5×` ⇒ the "compression amplifies identifiability" mechanism is not doing the claimed work and
the ranking is not representation-forced. F6-b: the NTT layer-1 fraction falls below 0.5 of the
pair entropy ⇒ the "first layer is the maximal point" claim fails and the ranking must be
recomputed across all seven layers before anything is said. F6-c: any gate misses its exact
value ⇒ instrument failure, no disposition.

**Charged cost.** Implementation: ~80 lines. Compute: `O(q)` and `O((2η+1)²)` — seconds.
**Charging note:** this bound charges *nothing physical*. Traces, alignment, profiling and
masking order are outside it, and the entry must print them as uncharged. Under CEILING-M3-B no
number here becomes a trace count.

**Ceiling.** `toy` — a statement about a stipulated abstract observation law, not about a
device. Its legitimate use is to *rank* countermeasure placement, not to claim any leak.

**Risk.** `conservative`.

### M3-7. Fault observability against implicit rejection as an influence-set difference `A \ B`, and the zero-cost reordering that empties it

**Tracked object / what it discards.** The pair of **influence sets** over an emulated
decapsulation: `A` = the set of dynamic instructions whose result influences the comparison
outcome `b = (c′ == c)`; `B` = the set whose result influences the KDF input on the accept path.
Tracked quantity: `|A \ B|` and its stage decomposition. Discards every value, every address,
the secret, the ciphertext, and the timing — retains a set of program points. Structurally
lossy: an implementation is not recoverable from two subsets of its instruction stream, and
whole families of implementations share the same pair.

**Mechanism.** Under implicit rejection the KEM returns 256 bits in both cases, so a fault that
merely flips the *decision* is useless unless it leaves the *returned value* computable by the
attacker. Declare the observation model explicitly: the attacker submits `c` built from a
guessed message `m*`, knows `KDF(m*, ·)`, and observes only the returned `K`. A useful fault
must therefore force `b := accept` **without** perturbing `m̂` or the KDF input — i.e. it must
land in `A \ B`. So the fault-attack surface for this class is exactly a set difference between
two dataflow influence sets, and it is computable by dynamic taint over a software emulation of
public source, with no glitching and no device (OL-2 respected).

**Prediction.** `|A \ B|` is determined by the *ordering* of Algorithm 18's final steps, not by
the strength of the comparison. Specifically: for implementations that compute `K` from `m̂`
**before** evaluating `b` and then `cmov`, `A \ B` is non-empty and contains the whole
comparison-and-select chain; for an ordering in which `b` is folded into the KDF input (so that
every instruction influencing `b` also influences the returned value), **`A \ B = ∅` and the
class has no useful fault site at all** — a reordering with zero arithmetic cost. Pre-registered
separation: `|A \ B| ≥ 32` for the reference ordering (one per compared 32-byte block in a
`cmov` design), `|A \ B| = 0` for the folded ordering.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Instrument one decapsulation under a byte-level taint engine
over an emulator or an instrumented build; compute `A`, `B`, `A \ B`; repeat for three orderings
(reference, `cmov`-after-KDF, folded).
**Known-answer gates:** (a) a synthetic build in which the comparison result is XORed into the
KDF input by construction must return `|A \ B| = 0` exactly — `A ⊆ B` holds by construction, so
any non-zero answer is a taint-engine defect; (b) a synthetic build with an early
`return K_reject;` must return a non-empty `A \ B` containing the branch instruction, and the
tool must name that instruction; (c) tainting a constant must produce the empty set; (d)
`A ∪ B` must contain every load of `c`, which is independently checkable against the
ciphertext's byte length.

**Falsifier.** F7-a: `|A \ B| > 0` for every ordering of a conforming Algorithm 18, including
the folded one ⇒ the reordering countermeasure does not exist, the class is irreducible, and the
entry closes with that statement (a legitimate CLOSED verdict). F7-b: gate (a) or (c) fails ⇒
instrument failure, no disposition. F7-c: `A` and `B` are found to be equal for the *reference*
implementation ⇒ the reference already has the property and there is nothing to propose.

**Charged cost.** Emulation and taint over `~10⁶–10⁷` dynamic instructions per decapsulation ×
3 orderings × 3 parameter sets. **Memory must be charged:** a byte-granular taint map over the
whole address space is the binding resource, and a shadow-memory design that stores per-byte
taint sets is `O(instructions × set size)` — it must be reported, not assumed free. **No
physical fault injection, no glitching, no device.** Any newly observed defect escalates to the
Coordinator (CEILING-M3-C).

**Ceiling.** `toy` — one implementation, one emulator, one fault model (instruction-level
value corruption). It is not a statement about masked, shuffled or redundant builds, and it is
not a claim that any implementation is faultable.

**Risk.** `high_risk` — the `A \ B = ∅` prediction is a real bet, and the honest prior is that
the KDF input is fixed before `b` exists in every conforming ordering, in which case F7-a fires.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `multi_class_generator_G1_G2_G3|
multibyte_alignment_lane_malformed_length|Algorithm_18_decap_boundary_attestation`
(`EV-MLKEM-006`, `KN-TECH-054`). **Why it is not that:** that work perturbs *inputs* at the
Algorithm-18 boundary and observes outputs; this computes a *dataflow* relation inside the
boundary and never perturbs an input at all.

### M3-8. The decapsulation-key field-integrity defect class and its forced two-bit signature

index `i` of `dk`, the 2-bit signature `(a_i, r_i)` where `a_i = [output changes on a valid

### M3-9. Comparison surrogates with a nontrivial kernel, and the exact generator arity that reaches them

**Tracked object / what it discards.** The **kernel invariants of the comparison surrogate**:
for an implementation whose accept decision is `φ(c) == φ(c′)` for some map `φ`, the tracked
object is the pair `( d_φ , Sym(φ) )` where `d_φ` = minimum number of *byte positions* supported
by a nonzero element of `ker φ` (for linear `φ`), and `Sym(φ)` = the group of byte-index
permutations under which `φ` is invariant. Discards `φ` itself, the code, the output values, and
the ciphertext. Structurally lossy: `d_φ = 2` is shared by XOR-folding, sum-mod-256,
mod-`p` checksums and countless others; the fibre is infinite.

**Mechanism, and the theorem.** `GOAL-MLKEM-002`'s class is *truncation* — bytes never
compared. A **different defect mechanism class** is *surrogate comparison*: bytes are all read
and folded into a short digest that is then compared. `acc |= c[i] ^ cc[i]` is exact;
`acc ^= c[i] ^ cc[i]` and `acc += c[i] − cc[i]` look nearly identical, read every byte, pass
every single-byte differential test, and admit two-byte cancelling differences. The theorem
that decides instrument power: **a `k`-byte differential generator exhibits a silent input for a
linear surrogate `φ` iff `k ≥ d_φ`**, and by pigeonhole every `m`-bit surrogate over `8L` input
bits with `m < 8L` has `d_φ ≤ 2`. So `k = 1` is provably incomplete and `k = 2` is provably
sufficient *in principle* — but the probe economics decide whether it is sufficient in practice.

**Prediction (forced probe economics).** For a random 2-byte mutation with nonzero deltas, the
probability of landing in the kernel is `1/(2^m − 1)` for a uniformly-behaved `m`-bit surrogate:

| surrogate | `d_φ` | expected random-2-byte probes to exhibit a silent input | probes for 99.9% |
|---|---|---|---|
| exact byte compare | `∞` | never | — |
| XOR-fold to 8 bits | 2 | **255** | 1762 |
| sum mod 256 | 2 | 255 | 1762 |
| 16-bit checksum | 2 | 65,535 | ~4.5 × 10⁵ |
| CRC-32 | ≤ 4 | 4.29 × 10⁹ | infeasible |

and the sharper prediction: for any **permutation-invariant** surrogate (XOR-fold, sum, popcount,
horizontal SIMD reduce, sorted compare), a **transposition probe** — swap two ciphertext byte
positions rather than change any value — is silent with probability 1 whenever the two bytes
differ, i.e. `1 − 1/256 = 0.99609` for random ciphertexts, so the expected probe count is
**1.0039**. The transposition generator is value-preserving and therefore *not* a member of the
`G1/G2/G3` weight-and-alignment families; it is a new generator class `G4` that costs one
execution and separates a class the single-byte generator provably cannot.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Build three synthetic comparators (exact; XOR-fold-to-8;
sum-mod-256), plus the transposition generator `G4` and a random 2-byte generator `G5`.

### M3-10. The XOF block-budget defect class in `SampleNTT`, with two degenerate gates that bracket it

**Tracked object / what it discards.** The **XOF block-consumption count**: for each matrix entry
`(i,j)`, the number of 168-byte SHAKE128 blocks the rejection sampler consumes before it has
accepted 256 coefficients. Discards `Â` entirely — a `256 k²`-element object collapses to one
small integer per entry. Not invertible by any stretch: the count is a function of the seed
through the rejection pattern only, and every `Â` consistent with that pattern shares it.

**Mechanism.** FIPS 203 Algorithm 7 reads 3 XOF bytes, forms two 12-bit candidates, and accepts
each iff it is `< q = 3329`, i.e. with probability `3329/4096 = 0.812744`. A SHAKE128 block of
168 bytes yields 56 groups, hence 112 candidates. **A conforming sampler loops until 256 are
accepted; an implementation that pre-squeezes a fixed number of blocks — a natural and
performance-motivated shape in hardware, in constant-time rewrites, and anywhere the XOF is a
fixed-output accelerator — silently produces a wrong `Â` on the tail of the distribution.** This
is a *third* defect mechanism class: neither comparison truncation nor key-field integrity, but
sampler budget, and it lives in key generation and encapsulation rather than decapsulation.

**Prediction (exact binomial tail, no simulation).** `P(k blocks suffice) = P(Bin(112k, 0.812744) ≥ 256)`:

| blocks | candidates | `P(enough)` | `P(short)` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 224 | 0 | 1 |
| 3 | 336 | **0.9916723075** | **0.0083276925** |
| 4 | 448 | 1 − 2.22 × 10⁻³² | 2.22 × 10⁻³² |

So a "fixed 3 blocks" implementation is wrong on `0.83%` of matrix entries. Per key generation:

| parameter set | `k²` entries | `P(≥1 wrong entry)` | key generations for 99% detection power |
|---|---|---|---|
| ML-KEM-512 | 4 | 0.032897 | **138** |
| ML-KEM-768 | 9 | 0.072501 | **62** |
| ML-KEM-1024 | 16 | 0.125236 | **35** |

**Minimal discriminating test.** Generate `N` key pairs from random seeds against a scalar FIPS
203 reference and the target build, compare `Â` (or, black-box, compare `ek`); `N = 138 / 62 / 35`
for 99% power at the three parameter sets. Total: a few hundred key generations, seconds.
**Known-answer gates, two degenerate parameter settings whose correct output is known in
advance:** (a) set the acceptance bound to `4096` (accept everything). Then 3 blocks always
suffice, so the fixed-3-block build and the reference must agree on **every** entry and the
measured detection rate must be exactly `0.000000` — a non-zero rate proves the harness is
detecting something other than the budget. (b) Set the acceptance bound to `2048`
(`p = 0.5`). Then `P(3 blocks suffice) = 6.5 × 10⁻²³`, so the detection rate must be exactly
`1.000000` on every entry. Between those two brackets the instrument is calibrated by
construction. Third gate: the reference sampler's own empirical block-consumption histogram must
match the binomial tail above to within a chi-squared interval, which validates the 0.812744
acceptance rate as implemented rather than as recalled.

**Falsifier.** F10-a: the measured `P(short at 3 blocks)` departs from `0.0083276925` by more
than its binomial interval over `10⁴` entries ⇒ the acceptance model is wrong (wrong candidate
packing, wrong block rate, or a non-standard XOF rate) and the whole table is void. F10-b:
either degenerate gate misses its exact value ⇒ instrument failure, no disposition. F10-c: no
implementation in the audited neighbourhood uses a fixed block budget ⇒ record the class as
empty in that neighbourhood, exactly as `second_impl_unavailable` was recorded, and never
paraphrase it as evidence that samplers are correct.

**Charged cost.** Implementation: low — a reference `SampleNTT`, a mutated fixed-budget variant,
and a comparison. Compute: seconds. **Charging note:** the black-box variant compares `ek`
rather than `Â`, which costs nothing extra and removes the need to reach inside the target — but
it also loses the per-entry resolution, so the power table above applies to the `Â`-visible
variant and must be recomputed (upward) for the black-box one.

**Ceiling.** `control`. A generator-power result for a named class with an exact tail
probability, plus two bracketing degenerate gates. No implementation is named and no defect is
asserted to exist.

**Risk.** `conservative`.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `baseline_invisible_synthetic_control|
G2_or_G3_marginal_nonempty|precedence_reachability_repair` (`EV-MLKEM-007`). **Why it is not
