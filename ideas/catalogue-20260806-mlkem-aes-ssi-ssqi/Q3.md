### 0.2 Tracked objects and the lossy-projection test

Three objects are shared across entries. Each is checked against
`docs/inventor-protocol.md` §2: a projection that loses nothing is a change of coordinates.

**TO-DISP — the displacement law of an operator.** For a map τ on the vertex set of the
supersingular ℓ-isogeny graph, track the histogram of `d_τ(v) = min{deg ρ : ρ : E_v → E_{τ(v)}}`
over v, and nothing else. For τ = σ (Frobenius, `j ↦ j^p`) this is exactly the law of δ_E, whose
median the source pins at (p/2)^{1/3} (Theorem 1.5, L81).

- **Lossy?** Yes. The fibre over one histogram bin at δ contains every curve with that displacement
  — by the count law `P(δ_E ≤ T) ≈ T^{3/2} p^{-1/2}` that is ≈ p·T^{3/2}p^{-1/2} curves, i.e.
  p^{5/6} curves at the median. The isogeny itself, its kernel, the codomain and the curve are all
  discarded; **the discarded part is precisely what the algorithm must output.**
- **Compatible with the target's operations?** Yes, and this is the load-bearing point. Under
  transport along an isogeny η of degree m, `d_τ(v) ≤ m² d_τ(v')` (compose η^{(p)} ∘ ρ ∘ η̂), so the
  histogram propagates deterministically under exactly the operation Algorithm 3 line 7 performs
  when it pulls a solution back through the re-randomising walk (L207).
- **Not a change of coordinates.** τ is not recoverable from its displacement law; two operators
  with identical histograms can differ everywhere.

**TO-WIT — the arithmetic type of the returned witness.** The map
`α ∈ End(E)\Z ↦ (Trd α, Nrd α)`. Discards the endomorphism; the fibre is the representation count
of that norm by the trace-zero ternary form, which for Nrd ≈ p^{4/3} is p^{Θ(1)} elements. Propagates
deterministically under transport: `α ↦ η̂ α η` sends `(t, n) ↦ (mt, m²n)` for deg η = m. Not
invertible — the algorithm's whole job is to name a point in the fibre.

**TO-STRAT — the stratum signature.** For a predicate Q on curves computable from j(E) alone, track
the triple `(ρ, σ_cost, split-compatibility)` = (density of `{E : Q(E)}`, cost of evaluating Q,
whether the meet-in-the-middle midpoint of a path into the stratum is recognisable). Discards which
curves are in the stratum and every isogeny; keeps three numbers. Propagates: a stratum's density
composes multiplicatively under intersection, and split-compatibility is preserved under composing
with a graph automorphism. Not invertible: the stratum is not recoverable from three numbers.

## 1. The ten ideas

### Q3-1. The self-closing operator census: is Frobenius the only vertex map that closes a path into a non-scalar endomorphism?

**Tracked object + what it discards** — TO-DISP applied to *candidate operators*: for each map τ on
the vertex set of G_ℓ(p), the histogram of `d_τ(v)` plus the fixed-point count `#{v : τ(v) = v}`.
Discards which vertex goes where, every path, and every kernel; two operators with identical
histograms are indistinguishable to this object. The discard is structural because the exponent
identity of §0.3 reads **only** the median of the histogram, never the operator.

**Mechanism** — The incumbent's whole architecture rests on one property of σ : j ↦ j^p that the
source never isolates: σ is a **graph automorphism of G_ℓ(p) for every ℓ simultaneously**, its
displacement is bounded universally (Theorem 1.5, L81), and closing a path against it yields an
endomorphism whose inseparable degree p is not a square, hence non-scalar (L211). Call a map τ
*self-closing* if it has all three. The algorithm is: list from E; for each entry with codomain F,
look up τ(F). Nothing in Algorithm 2 (L167–L175) uses τ = σ except at line L171. **The census asks
whether ⟨σ⟩ exhausts the self-closing maps.** The expected mechanism of closure is that
`Aut(G_ℓ(p)) ⊇ Gal(F_{p^2}/F_p) = ⟨σ⟩` for structural reasons and that equality holds for p above a
small threshold — a self-closing τ must commute with ℓ-adjacency for *all* ℓ at once, and the only
maps with that property that this corpus can name are induced by field automorphisms, of which there
are two.

**Prediction** — For every ℓ ∈ {2,3,5} and every p above a small threshold,
`Aut(G_ℓ(p)) ∩ Aut(G_3(p)) ∩ Aut(G_5(p)) = ⟨σ⟩`, of order exactly 2 (order 1 only in the degenerate
tiny-p cases where σ acts trivially). Sporadic extra automorphisms exist at very small p, where the
graph is too small to be rigid, and must **disappear** as p grows — that decay is the structural
tell (§3 of the protocol): an extra-automorphism count that does *not* decay in p is the signature
of a graph-construction artifact, not of a new operator.

**Minimal discriminating test** — Pure Python, no SageMath. (1) Build G_ℓ(p) for
ℓ ∈ {2,3,5} and p in a ladder of ≈ 12 primes from 100 to ≈ 4000 (the campaign's existing four,
p ∈ {2437,3889,5737,7333}, are reused as the top of the ladder so the graph builder is the *already
exercised* one). (2) Compute the automorphism group of each graph by refinement + backtracking on
the vertex-invariant partition (a 150-line implementation is sufficient at these sizes; the
alternative, calling an external tool, is environment-blocked). (3) Intersect over ℓ. (4) For every
automorphism found beyond ⟨σ⟩, compute its displacement histogram and its fixed-point count, and
test self-closure directly by composing one path and checking the inseparable degree.
**Known-answer gate:** at p ∈ {11,13,17,19} the graphs have ≤ 3 vertices and Aut is computable by
brute-force enumeration of all vertex permutations; σ must appear in every one of them, and the
number of σ-fixed vertices must equal the number of F_p-rational j-invariants, which is
independently computable as the class number h(−p) (or h(−4p)) by counting reduced forms. An
implementation that misses σ, or whose fixed-point count disagrees with the class number, is broken
and its output is void.

**Falsifier** — **F1-a** a non-σ automorphism survives the ℓ-intersection at three consecutive
primes above 1000 → the census fails, the lever **reopens**, and the surviving τ's displacement
median is immediately the number that matters (feed to Q3-2). **F1-b** the extra-automorphism count
does not decay in p → instrument artifact; report as a controlled null, not a finding. **F1-c** σ's
fixed-point count disagrees with the class number at any prime → instrument void.

**Charged cost** — impl ≈ 250 lines (graph builder is reused; automorphism search is new). Compute:
minutes to ≈ 2 CPU-hours; the automorphism search is superpolynomial in the worst case but these
graphs have ≈ p/12 ≤ 620 vertices and rich vertex invariants. **Memory:** O(p) adjacency, negligible.
No preprocessing, no oracle, no QRAM. Machine model: single-core RAM, unit-cost integer ops. The
p ≤ 4000 ceiling is a real limit and is charged as such: **this test cannot see structure that only
appears at cryptographic scale**, and a negative here is evidence about toy graphs only.

**Ceiling** — `toy`. **Risk band** — `conservative` (expected verdict: CLOSED).

**Nearest prior-art neighbour** — C2-4 (`frobenius_orbit_quotient_state_space | sigma:j->j^p
self_claw | canonical min(j,j^p) dedup | exactly 1.0 bit ceiling`). **Why it is not that:** C2-4
takes σ as given and prices the *state-space quotient* it induces, arriving at a hard 1.0-bit
ceiling on memory; the observable there is a deduplication factor. Here σ is not given: the
observable is the **cardinality of the automorphism group** of a family of toy graphs, and the
deliverable is an existence census whose positive outcome would hand Q3-2 a second operator. A
1-bit dedup and an operator census cannot substitute for each other in either direction.

### Q3-2. The target-family exchange rate: k closable targets cost p^{1/3}k^{1/3} unbatched, and batched closability is the entire lever

**Tracked object + what it discards** — TO-DISP for a *family*: the histogram of
`d_T(v) = min_{i≤k} d_{τ_i}(v)` over v, for a family T = {τ_1,…,τ_k} of self-closing maps. Discards
which member achieves the minimum, all k operators, and every path. That discard is exactly the
information a lookup structure would have to reconstruct — which is why the entry's whole weight
lands on the lookup, not on the family.

**Mechanism** — Derived, zero compute, from the count law and §0.3's identity. For a rank-3 target
(the trace-forced case; see the adjacency note) the number of isogenies E → τ_i(E) of degree ≤ r is
`≈ r^{3/2} p^{-1/2}`. With k independent such targets the covering radius solves
`k·r^{3/2}p^{-1/2} = 1`, so

    r(k) = (p^{1/2}/k)^{2/3},   table M = r(k) = p^{1/3} k^{-2/3}.

The table shrinks. But each of the M entries must be tested against all k targets, so the
**unbatched** total is

    TIME(k) = M·k = p^{1/3} · k^{1/3}   — strictly increasing in k, minimised at k = 1.

k = 1 is the incumbent, and this is the first derivation in the campaign that says *why* the
incumbent's single target is optimal rather than merely traditional. Now replace the per-entry cost
k by an amortised `k^{o(1)}`:

    TIME_batched(k) = p^{1/3} k^{-2/3+o(1)},   which reaches p^{1/4} at exactly k = p^{1/8}.

**So the entire p^{1/4} question, inside this architecture, is one data-structure question:** can
the k images {τ_i(F)} of a single table entry F be tested for membership in the table in
`k^{o(1)}` amortised work? For k = 1 it is one hash lookup. For k > 1 it requires the family to
have grouped structure — e.g. an orbit of a group acting on the vertex set with a cheap canonical
form, so that one canonical-form computation answers all k queries at once.

**Prediction** — Pre-registered, both halves falsifiable. (P-a) **Unbatched families are strictly
harmful**, exponent `1/3 + (1/3)log_p k`, with no k > 1 beating the incumbent — so every proposal of
the form "use several targets" is refuted at the whiteboard unless it also exhibits the lookup.
(P-b) The *only* surviving cell is (grouped family, canonical-form lookup) at k ≈ p^{1/8}, and the
expected verdict on it is **CLOSED**, because the trace-vanishing argument confines rank-3 targets
to two-sided ideal structure, and Q3-1's census predicts the group is ⟨σ⟩, i.e. k = 2 at best.
Mixing in generic rank-4 targets is worse still: there the count is `r²/p`, giving
`r = (p/k)^{1/2}` and `TIME = p^{1/2}k^{1/2}`, which at k = 1 reproduces the classical
meet-in-the-middle exponent 1/2 — an internal consistency check the arithmetic must pass.

**Minimal discriminating test** — Zero compute for the exchange rate; one small program for the
lookup. (1) Write the two-parameter surface `TIME(k, c)` for c ∈ {rank-3, rank-4} counts and
k ∈ {1, 2, p^{1/16}, p^{1/8}, p^{1/4}} at log2 p ∈ {256, 384, 512}, under (a) §4.1's one-op
convention and (b) the committed measured law charged at ℓ = B_opt with `c` cited **only** as the
bracket [1.327077, 1.576444]. **Known-answer gate:** the (k = 1, rank-3) cell must return exponent
1/3 and reproduce §4.1's five published rows (L234–L238) within the 2.2309-bit NIST-I
irreproducibility band; the (k = 1, rank-4) cell must return exactly 1/2. Machinery that cannot
reproduce both endpoints is void. (2) For the lookup: on the toy graphs of Q3-1, take the only
family this corpus can actually name — T = {σ} ∪ {any extra automorphisms Q3-1 finds} — and measure
the amortised lookup cost per table entry as k grows, against a **null family** of k random vertex
permutations (which admits no canonical form and must show cost exactly proportional to k). A
measured amortised cost that is not Θ(k) on the random null means the harness is caching, not
batching.

**Falsifier** — **F2-a** any exhibited family with amortised per-entry cost `o(k)` and k > 2 →
prediction P-b fails and the lever **reopens with a construction**. **F2-b** the (k=1) gates fail →
the surface is void. **F2-c** a family whose covering radius beats `(p^{1/2}/k)^{2/3}` — i.e. whose
targets are positively correlated in the right direction rather than independent → the exchange rate
is wrong and must be re-derived with the correlation charged. **F2-d** the random-permutation null
shows sub-linear cost → instrument artifact.

**Charged cost** — Arithmetic: zero. Lookup measurement: ≈ 200 lines, minutes. **Memory is charged
equal to time throughout** (`M = r(k)`), so no cell of the surface may be reported as a time saving
without its memory column; the k = p^{1/8} cell has memory p^{1/4} as well as time p^{1/4}, which is
better than the incumbent on *both* axes and is exactly why the entry must not be read as a claim.
No preprocessing (the family is p-dependent but instance-independent — if it ever has size ≥ p^{1/8}
it becomes advice, and then it is IDEA-20260806-9c2f80's frontier that prices it, not this entry).
No oracle, no QRAM. Machine model: RAM with unit-cost hashing; under a 2-D or 3-D area-time metric
the k = p^{1/8} cell must be re-costed and C2-6 owns that metric.

**Ceiling** — `medium` (a derivation that binds the campaign's cost model; no measurement claim).
**Risk band** — `representation_changing` (it replaces "the target" by "the target family" as the
object the exponent budget is written over).

**Nearest prior-art neighbour** — IDEA-20260806-e4c719 (`three_parameter_exponent_identity |

### Q3-3. The witness ledger: the incumbent returns an endomorphism p^{2/3} larger than the smallest one that exists, and the two minima are nested lattices whose joint law is measurable

**Tracked object + what it discards** — TO-WIT, the pair `(Trd α, Nrd α)` of the *returned* witness,
followed alongside the pair of lattice minima `(m_E, p·δ_E)` where `m_E = min{Nrd α : α ∈ End(E)\Z,
Trd α = 0}` and `p·δ_E = min{Nrd α : α ∈ P_0}`. Discards the endomorphism, the curve and every path;
keeps two integers per curve. Lossy in the load-bearing direction: naming a point in the fibre over
`(Trd, Nrd)` **is** the problem.

**Mechanism** — Two nested trace-zero lattices, both rank 3, with determinants that are pinned:
`O_0` (trace-zero part of a maximal order) has det ≍ p², so `m_E ≍ p^{2/3}`; `P_0` (trace-zero part of
the two-sided ideal of reduced norm p, isometric to Hom(E,E^{(p)}) under Nrd/p — the source's own
§4.2 statement, L244–L246) has det ≍ p⁴ under Nrd, so `min = p·δ_E ≍ p^{4/3}`. The index is
`[O_0 : P_0] = p`. **Therefore the incumbent's output α = ϕ∘φ has Nrd ≍ p^{4/3} while a non-scalar
endomorphism of norm ≍ p^{2/3} exists on the same curve** — the algorithm overshoots the smallest
witness by a factor p^{2/3}, and the overshoot is not slack in the analysis, it is forced by the
architecture. The measurable question is whether the two minima are **coupled**. Null: `P_0` behaves
like a generic index-p sublattice of `O_0`, for which det scales by p² and the minimum by p^{2/3},
giving the deterministic relation

    δ_E ≈ max(1, m_E · p^{-1/3})   — slope 1 in log–log, with a hard floor at δ_E = 1.

**Prediction** — Pre-registered as a three-part shape. (P-a) **Slope**: regressing `log δ_E` on
`log m_E` across sampled maximal orders returns slope 1.00 ± 0.15, not 0. (P-b) **Floor**: a visible
hockey-stick at δ_E = 1 — the spine. (P-c) **A known outlier branch that must appear**: curves on the
F_p-locus have δ_E = 1 by definition but generic `m_E ≍ p^{2/3}` (their extra structure is the
Frobenius π, of norm p, which is *larger* than the generic minimum, not smaller). So the relation
must **fail on a p^{-1/2}-density branch**, and the failure is pre-registered, not an excuse. A fit
that shows no branch has smoothed over the spine and is void. **Consequence if P-a holds:** the
instance class "curves with a known small endomorphism" has proportionally small δ_E, and the class's
exchange rate is computable and negative — density of `{m_E ≤ p^{2/3-η}}` is `p^{-3η/2}` while its
δ_E is `p^{1/3-η}`, so re-randomising into it costs `p^{1/3-η+3η/2} = p^{1/3+η/2}`, **worse than the
incumbent for every η > 0**. The lane closes with a number, not with a shrug.

**Minimal discriminating test** — Entirely on the quaternion side, exactly as the source's own §4.2
experiment (L244–L250), so no curve arithmetic and no SageMath is needed. (1) For p ≡ 3 mod 4 in a
ladder p ∈ {103, 251, 503, 1009, 2003, 4003}, enumerate maximal orders up to conjugacy by generating
left ideals of small norm from the standard order and reducing (Brandt-module style, pure Python
quaternion arithmetic). (2) For each order compute `m_E` (minimum of the rank-3 trace-zero form) and
`δ_E` (minimum of the rank-3 form on P_0 under Nrd/p), both by **exhaustive short-vector enumeration
inside a Fincke–Pohst box**, never by a truncated search — this sidesteps EV-SSIQ-48d274's
upward-biasing truncation confound at the cost of restricting p. (3) Fit slope, floor and branch.
**Known-answer gate, three-fold:** the number of order classes must match the Eichler mass formula
p/12 + correction; the number of classes with δ_E = 1 must equal the class number h(−p) or h(−4p),
computed independently by counting reduced binary forms; and the *minimum over all classes* of δ_E
must be exactly 1 while the *maximum* must not exceed (p/2)^{1/3} (Theorem 1.5, L81) — a violation of

### Q3-9. The two-channel dichotomy that closes L2 / A4: every list restriction factors through the codomain or through the degree, and both channels are already priced

**Tracked object + what it discards** — The **channel decomposition of a restriction**. A table entry
of `L(E,X,B)` is fully specified by the pair `(deg ψ, index of the cyclic kernel among the isogenies
of that degree)`; its codomain `j(E')` is the image of that pair under the key map. Track, for a
proposed restriction R, only the pair `(R_deg, R_cod)` = (whether R's indicator is measurable with
respect to the degree, whether it is measurable with respect to the codomain). Discards R itself,
its definition, its selectivity and everything about which entries it keeps — two bits per
restriction. Not invertible by an enormous margin, and lossy in exactly the direction that matters:
the exchange rate depends only on which channel R lives in, never on what R is.

**Mechanism** — Lever L2 asks for a family `F ⊆ L(E,X,B)` of cardinality `X^{2-δ}` that still
contains the required ψ with probability `p^{-o(1)}`. DEC-20260805-2be965 records both nulls:
independent restriction gives exchange rate `e = 2δ` (worse than 1/3) and aligned restriction gives
**exact break-even at 1/3 for every δ**. The open problem is whether anything beats aligned
break-even. **The dichotomy closes it.** The required ψ is determined by two things and only two:
the factorisation of δ_E (which divisor of it lands in the split window) and the kernel index of the
minimal isogeny. Hence:

- **Degree channel** (`R` measurable w.r.t. `deg ψ` — this is C1's TO-MULT): R can be *aligned* with
  the required ψ's degree, and the best achievable is exactly the aligned null — break-even. C1-6
  already measured the archetype (squarefree / ω-band families) and killed it.
- **Codomain channel** (`R` measurable w.r.t. `j(E')`): by the equidistribution of the listing walk
  the codomain of the required ψ is independent of R's indicator, so the exchange rate is the
  independent null, `e = 2δ` — strictly worse than 1/3.
- **There is no third channel**, because the entry is the pair and the codomain is the image of the
  pair. A restriction measurable w.r.t. neither coordinate must be measurable w.r.t. data outside
  the entry — and the only such data that could correlate with the required ψ is `End(E)`, which is
  the answer. **Forward guidance:** the classes that remain open are precisely the ones that leave
  the single-entry σ-algebra — multi-entry restrictions (correlations *between* entries, which is
  A2's relation-family lane) and adaptive restrictions that use partial output.

**Prediction** — (P-a) For any restriction, the measured exchange rate is `≤ max(aligned, independent)
= break-even`, with equality iff R is degree-measurable and aligned. (P-b) A **mixed** restriction
(a conjunction of one degree-channel and one codomain-channel condition) does **not** beat the max
of its parts — its rate is the aligned rate degraded by the codomain part. This is the sharp,
falsifiable content, because "conjunctions might help" is the intuition the dichotomy denies.
(P-c) Consequently L2/A4 is **CLOSED for single-entry restrictions** and open only in A2's lane.

**Minimal discriminating test** — On the toy graphs, with the exchange rate measured directly rather
than argued. (1) For p ∈ {2437,3889,5737,7333} and modest X, materialise `L(E,X,B)` exhaustively.
(2) Apply a pre-registered closed set of nine restrictions: three degree-channel (squarefree degree;
ω(deg) in a dyadic band; deg in a residue class mod a small prime), three codomain-channel
(`j(E') ∈ F_p`; low bits of `Tr(j(E'))`; a hash of `j(E')` into a 1/2-density bucket), and three
mixed conjunctions. (3) For each, measure `δ_measured = -log_X(|F|/|L|)` and the hit probability
against the actual required ψ over ≥ 10³ instances, and report the exchange rate. **Known-answer
gate, two-sided:** the trivial restriction `R ≡ true` must return exchange rate exactly 0 with hit
probability exactly 1; and the restriction "deg ψ = the actual divisor of δ_E in the window" — an
oracle restriction, deliberately unimplementable in practice and used here only as a calibration
arm — must return the aligned break-even rate exactly. An instrument that does not reproduce both
endpoints is void, and the oracle arm is charged as an oracle, never reported as an achievable
restriction.

**Falsifier** — **F9-a** any restriction in the battery measures an exchange rate strictly better
than aligned break-even, reproducibly across ≥ 3 primes → **the dichotomy is false and L2 reopens
with a construction in hand.** **F9-b** a mixed conjunction beats both of its parts → prediction P-b
fails and conjunctions become a live lane. **F9-c** the codomain-channel arms do not reproduce
`e = 2δ` within measurement error → the independent null is mis-stated (it is committed, so this
would be an upstream correction). **F9-d** either gate fails → void.

**Charged cost** — impl ≈ 300 lines (list materialisation reuses the committed builder). Compute:

### Q3-10. The extension-closure neutrality theorem: because the table is closed under isogeny extension, every trade between table size and match radius is exactly neutral

**Tracked object + what it discards** — The **reachability profile** of a search configuration: the
map `(X, t) ↦ (cost, covered degree)` where X is the half-degree bound of the table and t the graph
radius within which a match is accepted. Discards the table, the curves, the isogenies and the
matching procedure; retains a two-dimensional surface. Not invertible: infinitely many search
procedures share a profile. Compatible with the operations because both coordinates compose
multiplicatively under isogeny extension, which is the theorem's engine.

**Mechanism** — A whole family of proposals in this space has the shape "relax the exact match". The
incumbent's step L171 tests whether `(E')^{(p)}` is a key of the table. Relax it to: accept if some
curve within graph distance t of `(E')^{(p)}` is a key. This multiplies the lookups per entry by
`≈ ℓ^t` and raises the covered degree from `X²` to `X²ℓ^t` (the connecting path contributes degree
`ℓ^t`). Compare with simply enlarging the table to `X' = X·ℓ^{t/2}`: cost `M' = X'² = M·ℓ^t`,
covered degree `X'² = X²ℓ^t`. **Identical on both axes.** Hence

> **Neutrality theorem (derivation tier).** In the list-and-close architecture, the reachability
> profile depends on (X, t) only through the product `X²ℓ^t`. Every trade between table size and
> match radius is exactly neutral — not approximately, not up to constants inside the o(1), but
> identically — because the table is already closed under isogeny extension, so a match at radius t
> is a match by a table the algorithm could have built instead.

The same argument decides a second family in one line: **anchor sets**. Suppose the attacker
precomputes T curves with known endomorphism ring and expands a ball of size M around each, plus a
ball of size M around the target. A hit needs `T·M²/p ≈ 1`, so `M = (p/T)^{1/2}` and total cost
`T·M = T^{1/2}p^{1/2}`, minimised at `T = 1` giving `p^{1/2}` — **independent of T in the sense that
no T improves it.** Anchor-set enlargement is neutral-to-harmful for the same structural reason.
**Forward guidance:** the only non-neutral moves left are the ones that change `c` (the degree-count
exponent — e4c719's axis) or the **guaranteed** covering radius r (the covering-theorem axis, Q3-2 /
Q3-7). Everything that trades one already-charged resource for another is neutral by this theorem,
and a future proposal can be screened against it in minutes.

**Prediction** — (P-a) The measured `(X, t)` surface on toy graphs is a function of `X²ℓ^t` alone, to
within the discreteness of t. (P-b) No point of the surface beats the incumbent's `t = 0` column at
equal cost. (P-c) The anchor-set cell measures `T^{1/2}p^{1/2}` with the exponent on T equal to
`0.50 ± 0.08`, and never negative.

**Minimal discriminating test** — (1) On p ∈ {2437,3889,5737,7333}, run the list-and-close search for
`X ∈ {2,4,8,16,32}` × `t ∈ {0,1,2,3}` and record, for each cell, (a) total lookups, (b) peak table
entries, (c) the maximum degree of an isogeny `E → E^{(p)}` the configuration can certify. Plot (c)
against `X²ℓ^t` and against cost. (2) Anchor-set arm: `T ∈ {1,2,4,8,16}` with the corresponding
balanced M, measuring the success rate over ≥ 500 instances and fitting the exponent on T.
**Known-answer gate:** the `t = 0` column must reproduce the incumbent exactly — covered degree `X²`,
cost `Θ(X²)` — and the `X = 1, t = k` row must reproduce a pure breadth-first search of radius k with
covered degree `ℓ^k`. A surface whose `t = 0` column disagrees with the incumbent is void. Second
gate: the `T = 1` anchor cell must reproduce the meet-in-the-middle rate.

**Falsifier** — **F10-a** the surface is not a function of `X²ℓ^t` — i.e. two cells with the same
product show different covered degree at more than discretisation error → the theorem is false and
the radius axis becomes a real lever. **F10-b** any (X,t) cell with t > 0 beats the `t = 0` column at
equal measured cost → neutrality fails in the direction that matters. **F10-c** the anchor-set
exponent on T comes out negative → anchors help and the second half of the mechanism is wrong.
**F10-d** either gate fails → void.

**Charged cost** — impl ≈ 250 lines on the committed builder. Compute: ≈ 1–3 CPU-hours, dominated by
the anchor arm's 500 instances × 5 cells. **Memory is charged per cell and is the reason the theorem
is a theorem**: the t-axis appears to save memory and does not, because the lookups it adds are
lookups into a table that must exist. Peak entries must be reported for every cell; a report that
gives lookups without entries has hidden the entire content of the theorem. Preprocessing: the
anchor arm's T known-End curves are preprocessing and are charged **separately and additively**,
never amortised away — if T ever reaches p^{1/8} it is advice, and IDEA-20260806-9c2f80 prices it.
No oracle, no QRAM. Machine model: single-core RAM with unit-cost hashing.

**Ceiling** — `medium` for the theorem, `toy` for the surface measurement.
**Risk band** — `conservative`.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour** — C1-13 (`listable_set_functional cost(R)/hit(R) | AM-GM symmetric
split closure | threshold monotonicity closure | scoping failure on a nearby object`).
**Why it is not that:** C1-13's functional closes **the split shape** — it shows the symmetric split
is optimal by AM-GM on X₁² + X₂² at fixed product, i.e. it varies *how the degree budget is divided
between two lists*. This entry holds the split fixed and varies a coordinate C1-13 does not have:
the **match radius**, a graph-theoretic quantity that lives in the matching step, not in the listing
step. AM-GM cannot state the neutrality theorem because the radius does not enter as a term in the
sum; conversely neutrality says nothing about split asymmetry.
