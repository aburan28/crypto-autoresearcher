# M2 — ML-KEM scheme layer: the FO transform, decryption-failure geometry, multi-target, key reuse, composition

Slice: everything **above** the Module-LWE instance. The Fujisaki–Okamoto / implicit-rejection
construction and its proof surface; the geometry of the decryption margin and failure boosting;
multi-target and multi-key accounting and where the standard reductions lose factors; key reuse,
re-encoding and re-encryption; domain separation and the hash/KDF layer including the `H(ek)`
binding; ciphertext and key malleability / non-binding properties; KEM combiners and hybrid
composition; seed expansion and `(rho, sigma)` derivation; and **what FIPS 203 pins versus what it
delegates to the implementer or to the protocol**.

**No entry below claims a break, a key recovery, an advantage against a fielded parameter set, or
an asymptotic improvement.** Claim ceilings are `control`, `toy` or `medium`. Three of the ten are
ceiling arguments whose expected verdict is CLOSED; per house rule 8 that is the point of them, not
an apology for them. `sota_delta: 0` and `novelty_status: unverified` on all ten: eprint, arXiv and
nist.gov are unreachable from this environment, so nothing here is asserted new and nothing is
dismissed as known.

### 0.3 Tracked objects and the lossy-projection test

Three objects are shared across the slice. Each names the map, what it discards, and why the
discard is structural rather than a change of coordinates.

**TO-MARGIN — the direct-sum split of the decryption margin.**
The real object is the per-coefficient decryption margin
`E = e^T r − s^T e_1 + e_2 + c_v − s^T c_u`, where `c_u, c_v` are the compression errors
(SPEC-CHECK SC-4). The map is the **direct-sum projection** `E ↦ (D, U)` with
`D = e_2 + c_v` (everything the encapsulator computes exactly from `m` alone, since `r` is pinned
to `m` by SC-1) and `U = e^T r − s^T e_1 − s^T c_u` (everything that touches `s` or `e`).
*Lossy?* Yes, and structurally. `D` is by construction **statistically independent of the
decapsulator's secret**: it is a deterministic function of the encapsulator's own coins, so the
entire secret-dependence of the margin is discarded. This is not a change of basis — the
decomposition is by *who can compute the summand*, an information partition with no inverse.
`D` cannot be recovered from `U` and `U` cannot be recovered from the ciphertext.
*Used by:* M2-2, M2-3, M2-4 (and as the null in M2-1).

**TO-SIGMA — the per-key noise-variance functional.**
`sk = (s, e) ↦ Σ(sk) = (η₁/2)·||e||² + (η₂/2 + Var c_u)·||s||² + Var e_2 + Var c_v`.
*Lossy?* Yes. `Σ` is invariant under every negacyclic isometry, under coordinate permutation of
`s` and of `e`, and under sign flips; each level set of `Σ` is a union of orbits of size at least
`2n·φ(2n) = 2^17` times the permutation group. The **entire failure geometry** (which coefficients
are large, how the mass is distributed) is discarded and only a second moment retained. That the
discard is *structural* is exactly what M2-1's matched-Σ arm tests: if the per-key DFR were a
function of `Σ` alone the map would be effectively invertible on the quantity of interest and the
entry would be a repackaging.
**Symbol warning:** the letter `V` is already bound in this repository to the BKZ frame statistic
`V = Σ_a (P_aa − β/d)²` of `EV-MLKEM-94c773`, which is a *refused adjudicator*. `Σ` here is an
unrelated object; never write `V` for it.

**TO-WIRE — the encoding fibre.**
`(mathematical object) ↦ (set of byte strings that decode to it)`. For an encapsulation key the
fibre is non-trivial; for a ciphertext it is a singleton (SC-4). The tracked object is the
**fibre's cardinality**, i.e. a single integer per key.
*Lossy?* Yes: it discards which encoding, and it discards the key entirely — an integer drawn from
`Binomial(256k, (2^12 − q)/q)` retains nothing about `t̂` beyond a count of small coefficients.
It is nonetheless the exact quantity that decides whether `H(ek)` names one KEM identity or many.
*Used by:* M2-5, and as a row generator in M2-10.

### 0.4 Ceilings that bind every entry in this slice

- **CEIL-1 (the oracle ceiling).** FIPS 203 exposes no decryption-failure and no plaintext-checking
  bit (`EV-MLKEM-002`). Every failure-side entry here is therefore an **accounting** or a
  **ceiling**, never an attack; an entry that needed the oracle to exist would be OL-3 anyway.
- **CEIL-2 (the selection ceiling, restated for two streams).** For best-of-`M` selection from an
  i.i.d. stream, two laws bind and they are different laws:
  (a) on a **tail exponent**, the gain is `≤ log₂ M`, by convexity of `f(β) = −log₂ p(β)`
  (`GOAL-MLKEM-005.ceiling_known_in_advance`);
  (b) on a **second-moment functional**, the gain is `≈ sd·sqrt(2 ln M)`, i.e. `Θ(sqrt(log M))`,
  which is *weaker still*.
  M2-1 and M2-6 both live under (b). Neither becomes an exponent unless `ln M = Θ(β)`.
  **Where this slice differs from the closed lane:** `M = 1` is normative for the *attacker's
  ciphertext stream* (`EV-MLKEM-d146a5`). It says nothing about a stream selected by the **victim's
  own key generator** (M2-6) or by the **population of victims** (M2-1). Naming which actor holds
  `M` is the whole content of the distinction.
- **CEIL-3 (no fresh samples).** Nothing here manufactures Module-LWE samples; the 17-bit algebra
  closure is respected by construction.
- **CEIL-4 (no toy transfer).** `toy`-ceiling entries stop at the instrument. Numbers quoted at
  ML-KEM-512/768/1024 shapes in this file are **model arithmetic on published parameters**, carrying
  no claim tier, in the OBS-M discipline of `EV-PEC-857664` (an ECDLP-lane record, cited here only
  for its reporting convention, not for its content).
- **CEIL-5 (the Gaussian surrogate is disallowed as an adjudicator).** `EV-MLKEM-003` /
  `EV-MLKEM-004` already established that a Gaussian surrogate misprices this tail (second-moment
  ratio `4/σ²`). Every Gaussian number below is written as a **pre-registered prediction to be
  falsified by exact convolution**, never as a result.

### 0.5 Committed state this slice must not redo

- The deployment census of `M` with sourced rows and retrieval dates: **done**

- The norm-ratio→block-size conversion instrument (`EV-MLKEM-d146a5` T3) exists and **produced no

- `KN-TECH-1a5b7e`'s five obligations (object / statistic / sensitivity criterion with a named

## 1. The ten ideas

### M2-1. Per-key DFR dispersion: the decryption failure rate is a random variable over keys, and multi-target selection reads its left tail

**Tracked object / what it discards.** TO-SIGMA: the scalar `Σ(sk)`, plus the per-key failure rate
`δ(sk) = Pr_{m}[Decaps fails]` it is claimed to control. Discards the direction and shape of
`(s, e)` — every `2^17`-sized isometry orbit collapses to one point. Published DFR is an average
over keys *and* messages; this entry refuses the average over keys and keeps only its dispersion.

**Mechanism.** For fixed `(s, e)` the margin's per-coefficient variance is an explicit function of
`||s||²` and `||e||²` alone (TO-MARGIN, SC-4):
`Σ = (η₁/2)||e||² + (η₂/2 + Var c_u)||s||² + Var e₂ + Var c_v`.
`||s||²` is a sum of `256k` i.i.d. squared CBD draws, so it fluctuates: `Var(x²) = 3.75` for
`CBD(3)` and `1.5` for `CBD(2)`. Model arithmetic at published shapes (CEIL-4), with the uniform
approximation for compression error:

| set | `E[Σ]` | `sd(Σ)` | `sd(Σ)/E[Σ]` | linearised `sd(log₂ δ)` | best-of-`2^40` keys |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML-KEM-512 | 6204.9 | 105.4 | **1.699 %** | ≈ 2.36 bits | ≈ 17.6 bits |
| ML-KEM-768 | 5820.9 | 72.3 | **1.242 %** | ≈ 2.04 bits | ≈ 15.2 bits |
| ML-KEM-1024 | 3176.3 | 61.8 | **1.947 %** | ≈ 3.39 bits | ≈ 25.2 bits |

The linearisation is `d log₂ δ / (dΣ/Σ) ≈ |log₂ δ|` under a Gaussian tail, using the reported
`δ ≈ 2^-139 / 2^-164 / 2^-174` (`KN-LIT-7618`, to be re-read). Under CEIL-5 these are predictions,
not results: the true tail is thinner, so the sensitivity coefficient is the thing at issue.

**Prediction.** (i) `sd(log₂ δ)` computed by **exact convolution** lies within a factor 2 of the
linearised column. (ii) The best-of-`N` gain follows `sd·sqrt(2 ln N)` — the CEIL-2(b) law — and is
therefore `O(sqrt(log N))`, not an exponent: even `N = 2^40` targets buys `< 26` bits of DFR at
every set, leaving `δ` below `2^-113` everywhere. (iii) `δ` is **not** a function of `Σ` alone.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Reduced parameters `(n, k, q, η₁, η₂, d_u, d_v)` with the same
compression semantics, sized so `δ` is exactly computable by the `EV-MLKEM-004` convolution
instrument. Draw `10^4` keys, compute `(Σ, δ)` per key. Three arms:
(a) the scatter and `sd(log₂ δ)`; (b) **matched-Σ control** — pairs of keys with `|ΔΣ|/Σ < 10^-4`
but different coefficient multisets, reporting `Δ log₂ δ` within pairs (design pattern borrowed
from `EV-MLKEM-94c773`'s matched-`V` arm, on an unrelated object); (c) the best-of-`N` order
statistic for `N` up to `2^12` measured directly, against the `sqrt(2 ln N)` law.
**Known-answer gate.** Set `s = 0` and `e = 0`. Then `Σ` collapses to `Var e₂ + Var c_v` for every
key, `sd(log₂ δ)` must be **exactly 0**, and `δ` must equal the pure-`(e₂, c_v)` marginal already
computable in closed form. Any nonzero dispersion in this degenerate setting is an instrument
failure, and the run is void.

**Falsifier.** F1-a `sd(log₂ δ) < 0.25` bits at every reduced set → the key-to-key dispersion is
negligible and multi-target key selection on DFR is **CLOSED**. F1-b the matched-Σ arm shows
`|Δ log₂ δ| < 0.05` bits within pairs → `δ` *is* a function of `Σ`, TO-SIGMA is invertible on the
quantity of interest, and this entry is a repackaging: drop it. F1-c the measured best-of-`N` curve
grows faster than `sd·sqrt(2 ln N)·1.2` over the swept range → CEIL-2(b) is misapplied here and the
ceiling section is wrong, which is the most interesting outcome available.

**Charged cost.** Machine model: single CPU core, no accelerator, no QRAM, quantum depth 0.
Convolution over the margin's support at reduced `n`: `O(n k · S log S)` with `S` the support size;
peak memory dominated by one length-`S` float64 array per arm, `< 2 GB` at the sizes named.
`10^4` keys × 3 arms ≈ low tens of CPU-hours; **preprocessing charged in full** (the CBD product
distributions are tabulated once, ~minutes). Zero oracle calls: the failure rate is *computed*, not
queried (CEIL-1, OL-3).

**Ceiling.** `medium`. The dispersion arithmetic is exact at standardized shapes; but any statement
about a fielded parameter set is confined to the **DFR-model layer** and carries no security claim.

**Risk band.** `conservative`.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.**
`multi_target_best_of_N_keys|uniform_public_key_TV_2^-39.8|norm_shortfall_left_tail|gamma_below_gamma_crit`
(`H-MLKEM-dc51f5`, `IDEA-20260805-1d76e9`).
**Why it is not that:** that entry selects keys on the **public key's norm shortfall**, a lattice
quantity read off `ek`; this one selects on the **per-key decryption failure rate**, a quantity
that lives entirely in the FO layer, is not computable from `ek`, and whose ceiling law is
`sqrt(log N)` rather than a tail-exponent convexity bound.

`sota_delta: 0` / `novelty_status: unverified`

### M2-2. The encapsulator-known half of the failure margin: a compression-driven boosting ceiling that needs no oracle to compute

**Tracked object / what it discards.** TO-MARGIN, keeping **only `D = e₂ + c_v`** — the summand the
encapsulator computes exactly. Discards `U`, i.e. **all** secret dependence. The entry deliberately
tracks the non-informative half and asks how much *leverage* it gives.

**Mechanism.** Failure boosting (`KN-TECH-048`) is described in the corpus as a search for
ciphertexts with elevated failure probability, costed as an abstract precomputation. In ML-KEM the
search decomposes exactly: because SC-1 pins `r` to `m`, the attacker may not vary `r` freely — but
`D` is *fully determined and fully computable* from `m`, so the grinding is over `m` and the
objective function is public. The share of the margin variance that `D` carries is large and
parameter-set dependent (model arithmetic, uniform compression-error approximation):

| set | `e^T r` | `s^T e₁` | `s^T c_u` | `e₂` | `c_v` | total | **`f = Var(D)/Var(E)`** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML-KEM-512 | 1152.0 | 768.0 | 676.4 | 1.0 | 3607.5 | 6204.9 | **0.582** |
| ML-KEM-768 | 768.0 | 768.0 | 676.4 | 1.0 | 3607.5 | 5820.9 | **0.620** |
| ML-KEM-1024 | 1024.0 | 1024.0 | 225.5 | 1.0 | 901.9 | 3176.3 | **0.284** |

So at ML-KEM-512/768 **the majority of the decryption-margin variance is contributed by the `d_v`
compression of `v`, and it is a public function of the message.** That is the mechanism: grinding
`2^t` messages and keeping the one maximising `max_j |D_j|` costs `2^t` hash-and-encrypt operations
and needs no key, no oracle, and no lattice work.

**Prediction.** Define `α(t) = log₂ Pr[fail | best of 2^t] − log₂ Pr[fail]`. Boosting is a *gain*
only if `α(t) > t`. Prediction: **`α(t) < t` for all `t` in the swept range at every parameter
shape**, i.e. deterministic-component boosting is a loss, and the margin by which it is a loss
grows with `t`. Secondary prediction: `α(t)` scales as `Θ(sqrt(t))`, not `Θ(t)`, because the
selection acts on a second moment (CEIL-2(b)).

**Minimal discriminating test.** At reduced parameters with FIPS compression semantics, enumerate
or sample `2^t` messages for `t = 0..20`, compute `D(m)` exactly, and evaluate
`Pr[fail | D]` by **exact convolution of `U` only** (the `EV-MLKEM-004` instrument, conditioned on
the observed `D`). Plot `α(t)` against the line `y = t`.
**Known-answer gate.** Two degenerate settings, both with answers fixed in advance.
(1) Set `d_v = 12` (no `v`-compression) and `η₂ = 0`: `D ≡ 0`, so `α(t)` must be **exactly 0 for
every `t`**, and the computed `Pr[fail]` must equal the corpus's own unconditional exact marginal
bit-for-bit. (2) Set `U ≡ 0` (`s = e = 0`): the failure indicator becomes a deterministic function
of `m`, so `α(t)` must equal `min(t, −log₂ Pr[fail])` exactly. An instrument that misses either is
broken and the run is void.
*(Gate (1) is forced by construction and is therefore labelled a gate, per `KN-TECH-1a5b7e`
obligation 4; the headline `α(t)` vs `t` comparison is not forced.)*

**Falsifier.** F2-a `α(t) ≥ t` at any `t ≥ 4` at any shape → the ceiling claim is false and the
deterministic component **is** an amplifier; that is the reportable outcome and it is reachable.
F2-b `α(t)` fits `a + b·t` with `b > 0.9` → the `sqrt(t)` law is wrong. F2-c the exact-convolution
`f` differs from the uniform-approximation column by more than `0.05` in absolute share → the
variance table above is misleading and must be replaced before any use.

**Charged cost.** Classical single core; no oracle, no key, no lattice reduction, quantum depth 0,
no QRAM. `2^20` message evaluations × one keccak permutation chain and one poly-mult each at
reduced `n`: order `10^6` cheap operations, minutes. The conditional convolution is the dominant
cost, `O(nk · S log S)` per distinct `D` profile; **memory charged** at one `S`-array per profile,
with profiles bucketed to bound peak memory. Preprocessing (the `U` product tables) charged once.

**Ceiling.** `medium` for the variance-share arithmetic (exact at standardized shapes, model layer
only); `toy` for the `α(t)` curve.

**Risk band.** `high_risk` — the headline may be forced to a null by CEIL-2(b) before it is
measured, which would make the entry a ceiling argument. That is an acceptable outcome (rule 8),
not a failure.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.**
`decryption_failure_boosting|failure_oracle_query_budget|FIPS_203_exposes_no_DF_or_PC_bit`
(`KN-TECH-048`, `EV-MLKEM-002`).
**Why it is not that:** `KN-TECH-048` records boosting as a *reported technique* with an abstract
precomputation cost and locates its death at the query budget. This entry names the exact summand
that boosting can act on in ML-KEM specifically (`e₂ + c_v`), computes its variance share
(0.582/0.620/0.284), and supplies a numeric criterion `α(t)` vs `t` that decides the question
**without any oracle at all**.

`sota_delta: 0` / `novelty_status: unverified`

### M2-3. The accumulation rectangle: under `M = 1` the failure chain dies at stage 3, not stage 2

**Tracked object / what it discards.** The attacker's transcript across a population, projected to
the **integer rectangle `(N, Q)`** — number of distinct decapsulation keys reached, and queries per
key — together with the derived scalar `H(Δβ)` = number of failure-hints against **one fixed
secret** required to move the primal block size by `Δβ = 1`. Discards which ciphertexts, which
keys, and the content of every hint. The discard is structural: the rectangle is a resource
accounting object with no inverse to a transcript, and `H` is a property of the lattice-hint
calculus, not of any particular run.

**Mechanism.** `KN-TECH-048`'s chain is (1) boost, (2) obtain failures, (3) accumulate and exploit.
The corpus records the chain as dying at (2), the query budget. `EV-MLKEM-d146a5` established
something stronger that has never been carried into the failure lane: **`M = 1` is normative in SSH
(R20) and IKEv2 (R21) and is the per-handshake reality for the TLS 1.3 client key share.** With
`Q = 1`, every failure obtained is a hint about a *different* secret. Stage (3) requires many hints
about *one* secret. So the chain does not die at the query budget; it dies **one stage later and
much harder**, at an accumulation step that is structurally unavailable — `N` is unbounded and
buys nothing, because hints do not compose across keys.

**Prediction.** `H(Δβ = 1) ≫ 1`: order tens of independent inequality hints are needed to move the
block size by one, at every reduced dimension tested, with `H` growing with dimension. Combined
with `Q = 1` this gives an **exact zero**, not a large-but-finite cost — a qualitatively different
closure from a query-budget bound.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Two cheap parts, no new instrument.
(a) *Rectangle derivation*: write `Q(deployment)` for the census's 24 rows, distinguishing
normative single-use, single-use-with-caveat, reuse-permitted, and silent; report the maximum `Q`
achievable in each class. Pure text work on `EV-MLKEM-d146a5`'s own `census.json`, no re-census.
(b) *`H` measurement*: at reduced dimension, plant `h = 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32` exact inequality
hints of the failure form `⟨(rot_j r, −rot_j e₁, −rot_j c_u), (e, s, s)⟩ > τ` against a fixed
secret, integrate each hint into the basis by the standard hint calculus, and read the resulting
required block size off the **`EV-MLKEM-d146a5` T3 norm-ratio→block-size instrument** — its first
use.
**Known-answer gate.** Feed T3 the **perfect-hint** degenerate case: `k·n` exact *equality* hints
revealing `s` coordinate by coordinate. After `d` such hints the required block size must fall to
exactly `2` (the lattice is solved), and after `0` hints it must reproduce the instrument's
archived `primal_bdd` KAT to `< 0.1` bits. An instrument that does not hit both endpoints is void.

**Falsifier.** F3-a `H(Δβ=1) ≤ 1` at any tested dimension → one failure moves the block size by a
full unit, `Q = 1` is not a closure, and the lane reopens. F3-b the census re-read finds any row
with a normative `Q > 1` that the census classified as single-use → the input is wrong and (a) must
be redone before (b) is meaningful. F3-c the T3 gate misses either endpoint → instrument defect,
report it as such and stop.

**Charged cost.** Text work: hours, zero compute. Hint integration: `O(d³)` per configuration in
exact rationals or high-precision floats; `7` hint counts × `4` dimensions × `20` seeds ≈ a few
CPU-hours, memory `O(d²)` words. No oracle calls (CEIL-1: the failure oracle is *assumed* for the
accounting and never constructed, OL-3). No QRAM, quantum depth 0. The T3 instrument's own
estimator calls are charged at their archived cost.

**Ceiling.** `medium`. The rectangle argument is exact and parameter-independent; `H` itself is
measured at reduced dimension and is quoted only as a trend (CEIL-4).

**Risk band.** `conservative`. Expected verdict **CLOSED**, and that is the deliverable: it
re-locates the death of the failure lane from stage 2 to stage 3 and makes the closure independent
of any query-count arithmetic.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.**
`deployment_census_of_M|spec_section_and_retrieval_date|ephemeral_share|M_equals_1_SSH_IKEv2`
(`EV-MLKEM-d146a5`).
**Why it is not that:** the census produced `M = 1` as a *bound on ciphertext selection* against
one shared reduced basis; this entry consumes that same integer as the `Q` axis of a completely
different mechanism — failure-hint accumulation — where its consequence is not a capped gain but a
structural unavailability, and it supplies the missing scalar `H` that the census never needed.

`sota_delta: 0` / `novelty_status: unverified`

### M2-4. What one failure is worth: the concentration of the failure-conditioned hint direction

**Tracked object / what it discards.** The posterior on `(s, e)` given one decryption failure,
projected to the **mean resultant length `ρ` of the unit hint directions** and the derived scalar
`I = −½ log₂(1 − ρ²)` bits. Discards the joint posterior entirely: all dependence between failures,
all higher moments, and the identity of the failing coefficient. Structural, because the posterior
is not a von Mises–Fisher law and the summary map is many-to-one on the space of posteriors — two
different posteriors with the same first moment are indistinguishable under it.

**Mechanism.** Conditioned on a failure at coefficient `j`, the encapsulator's own known vector
`h_j = (rot_j r, −rot_j e₁, −rot_j c_u)` satisfies `⟨h_j, (e, s, s)⟩ > τ − D_j` with `D_j` known
exactly (TO-MARGIN). So a failure is **not one bit**: it is a *directed* inequality hint with a
fully known coefficient vector and a known threshold offset. The corpus records the accumulation
step as "machinery exists" (`KN-TECH-048` → `KN-TECH-047`) but has never put a number on the
per-failure content for ML-KEM's specific noise and compression semantics. The concentration `ρ` is
that number.

**Prediction.** (i) `I` is `O(10^-2)` bits per failure, not `O(1)` — because the hint lives in
dimension `3·256k ≈ 1536..3072` and a single one-sided inequality at a `~10σ` threshold moves the
posterior barely. (ii) `I` is **larger at ML-KEM-512 than at ML-KEM-1024** by a computable factor,
tracking the `s^T c_u` share of the margin, because the compression term is the part of the hint
with the largest known coefficient. (iii) Conditioning on the known `D_j` (M2-2's object) raises
`I` measurably — a failure whose deterministic part was already large carries *less* information
about the secret than one whose deterministic part was small.

**Minimal discriminating test.** At reduced `n` where the failure set can be enumerated or sampled
to `10^5` events, collect failing `(h, D)` pairs, normalise `h`, and measure
`ρ = ||E[ĥ]|| / ||(e,s,s)||`-aligned resultant against the true secret direction; report `I`, and
report `I` stratified into deciles of `D_j`.
**Known-answer gate.** Move the threshold to `τ = 0`, so "failure" means the hint's inner product
is merely positive — a pure sign hint on a near-Gaussian. The conditional variance along the secret
direction must then equal `1 − 2/π = 0.36338` and `ρ` must equal `sqrt(2/π) = 0.79788`, both to
three decimals. Any deviation is an instrument failure, and the entry's headline numbers are void.
**Second gate (null sufficiency, `KN-TECH-1a5b7e` obligation 4).** Replace the real secret by an
independent freshly drawn secret of the same norm and recompute `ρ`: the forced value is `0` to
sampling noise. If the pipeline returns a nonzero `ρ` on an independent secret, the measurement is
about the apparatus, not the scheme.

**Falsifier.** F4-a `I < 0.005` bits per failure at every reduced set → the directional content is
negligible, failure exploitation is a *counting* problem and not a geometry problem, and the
geometry lane is **CLOSED**. F4-b `I` shows no monotone trend across `D_j` deciles → prediction
(iii) is wrong and the `D`/`U` split does not organise the information. F4-c `I > 0.5` bits per
failure → the per-failure content is far larger than the dimension argument allows; re-audit the
instrument before reporting anything.

**Charged cost.** Classical, single core. Enumeration/sampling of `10^5` failures at reduced `n`
requires inflating the failure rate to `~10^-3` by shrinking `q/4`, which must be declared as a
regime change and swept. Cost `O(10^8)` polynomial-coefficient operations, hours; memory `O(10^5 ·
3nk)` floats ≈ a few GB at the largest reduced size, **charged**, with streaming as the fallback.
Zero oracle calls (failures are generated with the secret in hand — this is a simulation, not an
attack). Quantum depth 0, no QRAM.

**Ceiling.** `toy`. Everything is measured at reduced dimension and inflated failure rate; no
transfer to FIPS 203 parameter sets is permitted (CEIL-4, OL-7).

**Risk band.** `high_risk`.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.**
`exact_CBD_negacyclic_convolution_marginal|FIPS_compression_and_rounding_semantics|estimator_n_fold_union_bound`
(`EV-MLKEM-004`, `H-MLKEM-001`).
**Why it is not that:** that work computes the **marginal probability** of the failure event and
stops; this entry computes the **conditional geometry** of the event — the direction, its
concentration, and its stratification by the encapsulator-known offset — which the marginal
integrates away by construction.

### M2-5. The encoding fibre of an encapsulation key: `H(ek)` binds bytes, and the byte fibre has `~2^118` elements

**Tracked object / what it discards.** TO-WIRE: the integer `|fibre(ek)|` = number of byte strings
that `ByteDecode_12` maps to the same `t̂`, together with the corresponding integer for a ciphertext.
Discards the key, the encoding, and everything else; it is a count drawn from a binomial law.

**Mechanism (SPEC-CHECK SC-4, SC-5, SC-1).** `ByteDecode_12` reduces mod `q`, and `2^12 − q = 767`,
so a coefficient whose canonical value `a` satisfies `a ≤ 766` has **two** valid 12-bit encodings
(`a` and `a + q`); larger coefficients have one. `rho`'s 32 bytes have no alternative encodings.
Hence `log₂|fibre(ek)| ~ Binomial(256k, 767/3329)`:

| set | `E[log₂|fibre|]` | `sd` |
|---|---|---|
| ML-KEM-512 | **117.96 bits** | 9.53 |
| ML-KEM-768 | **176.95 bits** | 11.67 |
| ML-KEM-1024 | **235.93 bits** | 13.47 |

Every element of the fibre is a functionally identical encapsulation key with a **different
`H(ek)`**, hence — by SC-1 — a different shared secret for the same message. The FIPS 203 modulus
check `ek == ByteEncode_12(ByteDecode_12(ek))` collapses the fibre to exactly `1`; it is required of
the **encapsulator**. The **decapsulator** stores its own `H(ek)` inside `dk` and never re-derives
it, so it is consistent with whichever encoding it generated, whatever a third party did in
between. The contrast is the point: for ciphertexts, `d_u ∈ {10,11}` and `d_v ∈ {4,5}` are all
`< 12`, decoding is mod `2^d`, every bit pattern is reachable, and the fibre is a **singleton** —
`|c|` bytes of ciphertext carry *zero* encoding redundancy while `ek` carries `~118..236` bits of it.

**Prediction.** (i) The measured mean `log₂|fibre|` matches `256k · 767/3329` to `< 0.01` bits over
`10^5` sampled keys. (ii) Encapsulating the same `m` under a non-canonical sibling `ek'` and under
canonical `ek` yields **both** a different shared secret **and** a different ciphertext — because
`H(ek') ≠ H(ek)` changes `r` as well as `K` (SC-1) — and a decapsulator holding the canonical `dk`
implicitly rejects the sibling's ciphertext. The two encodings therefore behave as **two distinct
KEM identities over one secret key**, which is an interoperability and identity-binding statement,
not a confidentiality one. (iii) No ciphertext byte string of the correct length fails to decode.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Pure implementation of FIPS 203 encode/decode at real parameters,
no lattice work: (a) sample `10^5` keys, count coefficients `≤ 766`, compare the histogram to
`Binomial(256k, 767/3329)` by exact chi-square; (b) for `10^3` keys construct one random
non-canonical sibling and check the modulus check rejects it and the canonical one passes;
(c) enumerate `10^6` random ciphertext-length byte strings and confirm all decode.
**Known-answer gate.** A key with **every** coefficient `≥ 767` has fibre size exactly `1` and must
be reported as such; a key with every coefficient `≤ 766` has `log₂|fibre| = 256k` exactly. Both are
constructible by hand and both must come back exact. An instrument off by one on either is void.

**Falsifier.** F5-a the measured mean deviates from `256k · 767/3329` by more than `0.05` bits →
SC-4 is misread and the whole entry is void. F5-b any correct-length ciphertext string fails to
decode → the singleton-fibre claim is wrong. F5-c the modulus check as specified does **not**
collapse the fibre to 1 (some non-canonical string survives) → a materially different and more
interesting finding, reportable immediately.

**Charged cost.** Trivial: `10^5` keygens and `10^6` decodes, single core, minutes, memory `< 1 GB`.
No estimator calls, no oracle, no QRAM, quantum depth 0. Preprocessing: none.

**Ceiling.** `medium`. The count is exact at standardized parameters, but the claim is about
**identity and interoperability semantics**, not about confidentiality; no security claim is made
or implied.

**Risk band.** `conservative`.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.**
`silent_set_closed_forms_C-BND_C-COV_C-RED|32_divides_every_ciphertext_length|identifiability_collision`
(`H-MLKEM-d9062d`, `IDEA-20260805-3c957e`).
**Why it is not that:** those entries characterise which ciphertext **byte indices a comparison
routine can skip** in an implementation. This one characterises the **encoding fibre of the
standard's own key format** and the fact that `H(ek)` names a byte string rather than a
mathematical object; it involves no comparison primitive, no library, and no differential gate
(OL-2).

### M2-6. Best-of-`2^t` seeds: moving the selector from the attacker's ciphertext stream to the victim's key generator

**Tracked object / what it discards.** The **grinding advantage curve** `g(t)` = expected best value
of the functional `||(s,e)||²` over `2^t` independently drawn 32-byte seeds `d`. A single
real-valued curve; it discards which seed, which key, and the entire structure of `(s, e)` except a
squared norm. Structural, because the seed→key map is a hash: no inverse exists, and the curve is a
property of the *distribution*, not of any key.

**Mechanism.** `EV-MLKEM-d146a5` closed best-of-`M` ciphertext selection with a normative `M = 1`.
That closure binds the **attacker's** stream. It says nothing about the stream generated by the
victim's own key generator, where `M = 2^t` is bounded only by the device's compute budget and
where nothing on the wire attests that `d` came from an approved RBG. A generator that grinds `d`
for a small `||(s,e)||` produces a key that is (SPEC-CHECK) indistinguishable from an honest key by
any public check that does not itself solve Module-LWE. The governing law is CEIL-2(b): the gain is
an order statistic of a sum of `512k` i.i.d. bounded terms, so it grows as `sqrt(t)`, not `t`.
Model arithmetic (Gaussian extreme-value approximation, CEIL-5 — a prediction, not a result):

| set | `E||(s,e)||²` | `sd` | `t = 20` ratio | `t = 40` ratio | `t = 64` ratio | `t` to halve `||·||²` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML-KEM-512 | 1536 | 61.97 | 0.888 | **0.836** | 0.787 | ≈ 111 |
| ML-KEM-768 | 1536 | 47.99 | 0.914 | **0.876** | 0.840 | ≈ 185 |
| ML-KEM-1024 | 2048 | 55.43 | 0.926 | **0.894** | 0.863 | ≈ 246 |

("ratio" = achieved `||(s,e)||` / expected `||(s,e)||`.)

**Prediction.** (i) The exact left-tail order statistic, computed by convolution rather than by the
Gaussian surrogate, gives a **smaller** shift than the table — CEIL-5 predicts the surrogate
over-rewards the tail. (ii) The norm ratio at `t = 40`, fed to the `EV-MLKEM-d146a5` T3
norm-ratio→block-size instrument, produces a `Δβ` that is positive but bounded, and its growth in
`t` is `Θ(sqrt(t))`. (iii) There is no efficient public predicate on `ek` that separates ground keys
from honest keys at `t = 40`.

**Minimal discriminating test.** (a) Compute the exact distribution of `||(s,e)||²` by convolution
of `512k` squared-CBD terms (a one-dimensional exact convolution, cheap), read off the
`2^-t`-quantile for `t = 0..64`, and compare against the Gaussian column with a pre-declared
threshold of `0.01` in the norm ratio. (b) Feed both the exact and surrogate ratios to T3 and report
`Δβ` side by side. (c) *Detectability arm*: train the cheapest available public predicate — a
threshold on `||t̂||` and on the empirical coefficient histogram of `t̂` — on `10^4` honest vs `10^4`
simulated-ground keys at reduced dimension, and report AUC.
**Known-answer gate.** At `t = 0` the "best of `2^0`" is a single draw, so the exact quantile must
equal the mean `E||(s,e)||²` and the ratio must be `1.0000` to four decimals; and at
`t = log₂|support|` (grinding the whole space at a tiny reduced parameter) the ratio must equal the
true minimum norm, computable by exhaustion. Both endpoints exact or the run is void.

**Falsifier.** F6-a the exact quantile matches the Gaussian column within `0.005` in ratio at every
`t` → CEIL-5 does not bind here and the surrogate was adequate (a useful negative about the
surrogate, reportable). F6-b `Δβ` from T3 at `t = 64` is below `1.0` at every set → grinding buys
less than one block-size unit even at `2^64` and the lane is **CLOSED** with a number.
F6-c the detectability arm reaches `AUC > 0.6` → ground keys *are* publicly detectable, which
inverts the entry into a defensive result and is the more valuable outcome.

**Charged cost.** (a) is one 1-D convolution over a support of size `~9·512k`, seconds, memory
`< 100 MB`. (b) is `~10` T3 estimator calls, charged at their archived cost. (c) is `2·10^4` reduced
keygens plus a logistic fit, minutes. **The grinding itself is never performed** — `2^40` keygens is
not run; only its *order statistic* is computed, and that substitution is declared, not hidden.
No oracle, no QRAM, quantum depth 0. A quantum grinder would reach the same quantile at `2^{t/2}`
hash evaluations with Grover, at circuit depth `2^{t/2}`; that variant is **named and charged here,
and not claimed**, since amplitude amplification over a max-finding objective needs a fixed
threshold and a full accounting this entry does not perform.

**Ceiling.** `toy`. The detectability arm and any `Δβ` reading are at reduced dimension; the
standardized-shape table is model arithmetic carrying no claim tier (CEIL-4).

**Risk band.** `representation_changing` — it relocates the `M` of the repository's own binding
ceiling from the attacker to the victim's generator, which changes what the closed census means
rather than adding to it.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.**
`shared_BKZ_basis|best_of_M_ciphertext|projected_error_ratio_R|convexity_capped_by_log2M`
(`GOAL-MLKEM-005`).
**Why it is not that:** that lane selects **ciphertexts** from a stream the attacker controls, is
capped by `log₂ M` on a tail exponent, and is closed by `M = 1`. This one selects **keys** from a
stream the *victim's generator* controls, is capped by `sqrt(log M)` on a second moment (a
different and weaker law), and `M` is bounded by device compute rather than by any protocol clause.

### M2-7. Instrument the reduction, do not read it: measuring the multi-target FO loss slope in `N`

**Tracked object / what it discards.** The multi-target FO/ROM reduction as a black box, projected
to **one number: the slope `σ_N = d log₂ Pr[extract] / d log₂ N`** at fixed `(q_G, q_D, δ)`.
Discards the entire proof — its case analysis, its constants, its hybrid structure, its bad-event
decomposition. Structural: a slope is a derivative of an empirical success rate, and no proof can be
reconstructed from it.

**Mechanism.** The corpus has never touched the FO proof surface, and this environment cannot fetch
the published bounds, so *reading* them is not an option. But a reduction is a **program**, and a
program can be run. Implement the ML-KEM-shaped FO reduction at toy parameters with `G`, `H`, `J`
as explicit tables, simulate the decapsulation oracle **without** the secret (answer from the
`G`-table when a queried `m` re-encrypts to `c`, else return `J(z ‖ c)`), plant a synthetic
adversary with a *tunable, known* advantage against the multi-target KEM game, and measure what the
simulator extracts. The discriminating question is binary and structural: does `H(ek)` inside `G`
(SPEC-CHECK SC-1) remove the factor `N`, or not?

**Prediction.** `σ_N = 0.00 ± 0.10` with the `H(ek)` salt present — each `G` query is useful for at
most one target, so `q_G` queries cover `q_G` (target, message) pairs, not `q_G · N`. With the salt
removed (`(K, r) ← G(m)`), `σ_N = −1.00 ± 0.10`. That contrast is the entry.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Toy `n = 16..32`, `q = 97..257`, `k = 1`, small `η`, message space
`2^12..2^16` so tables fit. Sweep `N ∈ {1, 2, 4, ..., 256}` at fixed `q_G`, `q_D`, with `δ` driven to
`0` by inflating `q`; measure `Pr[extract]` over `10^4` trials per cell; fit the slope.
**Arm B (the sensitivity arm, `KN-TECH-1a5b7e` obligation 3, comparator and threshold declared
before the run):** identical pipeline with `H(ek)` deleted from `G`'s input. Comparator = the salted
arm; threshold = the two slopes must differ by at least `0.5`.
**Known-answer gate.** A degenerate adversary that simply outputs the correct bit it was handed has
advantage `1`; the reduction must extract with probability `1 − o(1)` at `N = 1`, and
`Pr[extract]` must be **exactly 0** for an adversary that outputs a uniformly random bit. Both
endpoints exact, or the instrument is void and neither slope is reportable.

**Falsifier.** F7-a the two slopes differ by less than `0.5` → the `H(ek)` salt is not what carries
the multi-target separation in this reduction, and the mechanism as stated is wrong.
F7-b the salted arm shows `σ_N < −0.3` → an `N` loss survives the salt, which is the reportable
outcome and would locate a term worth naming. F7-c the ablated arm does **not** reach `−1` → the
instrument cannot see the effect it was built to see, so its null result in arm A carries no
information (`KN-TECH-1a5b7e` mode 4).

**Charged cost.** Tables of size `|M| ≤ 2^16` for `G`, `H`, `J` per target: `N = 256` targets ×
`3 × 2^16 × 32 B` ≈ `1.6 GB` at the top of the sweep — **memory is the binding resource here and is
charged**, with per-target lazy tables and a fixed RNG the declared mitigation. Compute:
`9 × 10^4` game executions × (one encapsulation + `q_D` simulated decapsulations) at toy `n`,
order `10^9` coefficient operations, hours on one core. No estimator calls, no oracle beyond the
simulated one, no QRAM, quantum depth 0 (the reduction instrumented is the classical ROM one; a QROM
variant is explicitly **out of scope and not claimed**).

**Ceiling.** `toy`. A slope measured at `n = 16` says nothing about the constant in any bound at
FIPS 203 parameters; the deliverable is the presence or absence of an `N` factor in a specific
reduction as implemented, and the entry must say so in those words.

**Risk band.** `representation_changing` — it replaces "read the bound" with "run the reduction",
which changes the kind of object the program treats as evidence about a proof.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.**
`MATZOV_Nf_internal_constants_audit|single_representative_length_model|mixed_bits_nats_union_bound_term`
(`IDEA-20260805-a102ec`).
**Why it is not that:** that entry audits an **analysis's internal constants by re-deriving them
symbolically**. This one refuses symbolic re-derivation (the sources are unreachable) and instead
measures a **slope of an executed reduction**, with an ablation that forces the alternative slope —

### M2-8. The fibre census of the decapsulation output map, with an explicit-rejection ablation that must flip exactly the predicted rows

**Tracked object / what it discards.** The map `(dk, c) ↦ K` and its fibres, projected to a
**boolean table**: rows = `{honest-key, malicious-key} × {K-collision, CT-collision, PK-collision} ×
{accept branch, reject branch}`, cells = "witness exists / provably none at toy scale". Discards the
witnesses' content, their cost, and every quantitative aspect. Structural: a boolean table cannot
reconstruct a witness, and existence is not recoverable from the map by any inverse.

**Mechanism.** ML-KEM's decapsulation has two branches (SPEC-CHECK SC-1, SC-2, SC-3), and they have
*structurally different* fibres. The accept branch outputs `G(m ‖ H(ek))[0:32]`, which is a function
of the message and the **byte-encoded** key. The reject branch outputs `J(z ‖ c)`, which is a
function of a secret-key component and the ciphertext, and **not** of the public key at all. Any
statement of the form "the shared secret determines the public key" must therefore be evaluated
branch by branch, and a malicious key generator chooses `z`. The entry's content is not a claim
about which cells are which — it is a **machine-checked table with witnesses or exhaustion proofs**,
plus an ablation that predicts, in advance, exactly which rows an explicit-rejection variant flips.

**Prediction.** (i) The accept-branch `K`-collision rows are empty at toy scale by exhaustion.
(ii) The reject-branch rows behave differently from the accept-branch rows in the malicious-key
column. (iii) Replacing implicit rejection by explicit rejection (return `⊥`) flips **exactly** the
reject-branch rows and **no** accept-branch row. Prediction (iii) is the falsifiable one: it is a
claim about the *structure* of the fibre decomposition, and a single unexpected flip refutes it.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Toy ML-KEM (`n = 16`, `q = 97`, `k = 1`, message space `2^12`) with
real SHA3/SHAKE at real output lengths. For each cell, either exhibit a witness by construction or
certify emptiness by exhausting the `2^12` message space and `10^6` random ciphertexts. Then run the
whole table again on the explicit-rejection variant and diff.
**Known-answer gate.** Two forced cells. (1) Accept-branch `K`-collision under **honest** keys must
be certified empty by exhaustion — it reduces to a `G` collision on `2^12` inputs, which the
instrument must confirm by direct enumeration. (2) A deliberately *seeded* collision — patch `G` to
map two specific `(m, h)` pairs to the same output — must be **found** by the instrument in the same
cell. A census that cannot find a planted witness cannot certify an empty cell.

**Falsifier.** F8-a the explicit-rejection diff flips any accept-branch row → the branch
decomposition is wrong and the table's organising principle fails. F8-b the planted-collision gate
is missed → instrument void. F8-c the table comes back with *every* cell identical between the two
variants → implicit rejection is not the discriminating feature and the entry has no content.

**Charged cost.** Toy exhaustion: `2^12` messages × `10^3` key pairs × 2 variants ≈ `10^7`
encapsulations at `n = 16`, minutes. Memory `O(2^12)` hash outputs per key, `< 1 GB`. No oracle, no
estimator, no QRAM, quantum depth 0. Preprocessing (SHA3/SHAKE tables) charged and negligible.

**Ceiling.** `control`. The deliverable is a certified table and a working census instrument. **No
cell of this table is a break, a key recovery, or a confidentiality claim**, and the write-up must
say so in those words: binding/fibre properties are orthogonal to IND-CCA confidentiality, which is
untouched here.

**Risk band.** `conservative`.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.**
`multi_class_generator_G1_G2_G3|multibyte_alignment_lane_malformed_length|Algorithm_18_decap_boundary_attestation`
(`EV-MLKEM-006`, `KN-TECH-054`).
**Why it is not that:** that work builds a **differential generator** to attest an implementation's

statement about the construction, not about any code (OL-2).

### M2-9. The combiner binding bit-vector, tested against a component KEM with a planted defect

**Tracked object / what it discards.** A hybrid/combiner construction, projected to a
**bit-vector over `{ss_A, ct_A, pk_A, ss_B, ct_B, pk_B, label}`** recording which values enter the
final KDF call. Discards the KDF itself, the ordering, the length encodings, the domain-separation
labels' content, and every security proof. Structural: infinitely many concrete combiners share a
bit-vector, and the KDF cannot be recovered from seven bits.

**Mechanism.** `EV-MLKEM-d146a5` established that `draft-ietf-tls-hybrid-design-16` §2 **delegates**
the key-reuse bound to the KEM specification, which in turn states none — a normative dead end. The
same delegation structure exists for **binding**: a hybrid's binding properties are not properties
of ML-KEM but of the combiner, and the standards stack does not say which combiner shape is
required. The testable hypothesis is that **the bit-vector alone predicts inheritance of a component
defect** — that is, the projection is not *too* lossy. Instrument: build an "evil" component KEM
whose ciphertexts are deliberately non-binding (two distinct ciphertexts, or two distinct public
keys, yielding one shared secret), plug it into each combiner shape, and see which shapes inherit
the defect.

**Prediction.** For each planted defect `X ∈ {ct, pk, ss}` of component `A`, a combiner inherits the
defect **iff** its bit-vector omits `X_A`. Zero exceptions across the shape catalogue.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Catalogue at least six combiner shapes covering the reachable
bit-vector space (from "hash both shared secrets only" through "hash both shared secrets, both
ciphertexts, both public keys and a label"). Build three evil component KEMs, one per defect.
Cross-product: `6 × 3 = 18` cells, each testing whether a collision witness survives the combiner.
**Known-answer gate.** Two forced cells. (1) The all-inputs combiner must be reported **binding**
against all three defects — if it is not, the instrument is producing spurious collisions.
(2) The shared-secrets-only combiner must be reported **non-binding** against the `ss` defect, since
the defect is passed through verbatim. Both forced; both are gates, not results.
**Sensitivity criterion (comparator and threshold declared in advance):** comparator is the
all-inputs combiner; the instrument must separate it from the shared-secrets-only combiner on at
least `2` of the `3` defects, or the census carries no information.

**Falsifier.** F9-a any cell where a combiner *omitting* `X_A` nonetheless resists the `X_A` defect →
the bit-vector is too lossy to be the tracked object, and the entry must be dropped or the object
refined (this is the entry's own rule-4 self-check, and it is reachable). F9-b any cell where a
combiner *including* `X_A` inherits the defect → either the inclusion is not in the hashed input as
catalogued, or the KDF is not collision resistant; audit before reporting. F9-c fewer than `2` of
`3` defects separate the two gate combiners → instrument void.

**Charged cost.** Trivial compute: the evil KEMs are ~20 lines each, the combiners ~10 lines each,
witnesses are constructed not searched. Minutes on one core, memory `< 100 MB`. **The expensive

### M2-10. The domain-separation ledger: every hash call site in the ML-KEM family as an `(function, input length, role)` tuple, with the `k`-byte ablation as its gate

**Tracked object / what it discards.** Every invocation of a Keccak-based function anywhere in
ML-KEM keygen/encaps/decaps and in the composition layer, projected to the tuple
`(primitive, input length in bytes, output length, role, secret-carrying?)`. Discards every byte
value, every key, and every message — the ledger is pure metadata. Structural: no input can be
recovered from a length and a role name.

**Mechanism.** `EV-MLKEM-021` established a discipline this program paid for: **verify that the
callable you invoked is the callable you named** (`lwe.dual_hybrid is lwe_dual.matzov` was True while
the run used a different function, and the correction is still blocked). Domain separation is the
same discipline applied to hash call sites: a scheme is separated only if no two roles can present
the same primitive with the same input. ML-KEM's separation is carried partly by **distinct
primitives** (SHA3-256 / SHA3-512 / SHAKE128 / SHAKE256), partly by **input length**, and — for the
three parameter sets — by a **one-byte module rank `k` inside `G(d ‖ k)`** (SPEC-CHECK SC-5). The
ledger asks whether any two roles collide, and it extends to composition: a deployment holding one
32-byte master secret and deriving ML-KEM, ML-DSA and hybrid material from it is exactly the case
where a collision would matter, and no single document owns that surface.

**Prediction.** (i) Zero `(primitive, input-length, secret-carrying)` collisions across distinct
roles **inside** ML-KEM, at every parameter set. (ii) Exactly one **length coincidence** in the ML-KEM
wire-object census: `|ek| = |c| = 1568` bytes at ML-KEM-1024, and no other pair among
`{800, 1184, 1568}` and `{768, 1088, 1568}` — a parsing hazard for any length-dispatched
container, and not a cryptographic one. (iii) Extending the ledger across the FIPS 203/204/205
family and the named hybrid groups yields at least one further length coincidence.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Build the ledger from the specification text (SPEC-CHECK) as a CSV,
one row per call site per parameter set, with a section reference and a retrieval date per row.
Then run a mechanical collision search on the `(primitive, input length, secret-carrying)` key.
Separately, compute the wire-length census and enumerate coincidences.
**Known-answer gate (forced by construction — a gate, not a result).** Delete the `k` byte from
`G(d ‖ k)` and re-run keygen for the same `d` at all three parameter sets: `rho` must come back
**bit-identical** across ML-KEM-512/768/1024, and with `k` present it must differ in essentially
every byte. The first outcome is forced — it is literally the same hash input — which is exactly why
it is a valid instrument check and an invalid headline. An implementation that does not reproduce it
is not implementing SC-5.
**Alternative exhaustiveness (`KN-TECH-1a5b7e` obligation 5).** The collision search can return
`{collision found, none found, ledger incomplete}`. The third branch is explicit: a row whose
section reference could not be retrieved is marked `unretrieved` and the verdict is
`ledger incomplete`, never `none found`.

**Falsifier.** F10-a a `(primitive, input-length, secret-carrying)` collision between two distinct
roles inside ML-KEM → separation is carried by something other than the ledger's key, and the
tracked object is inadequate. F10-b zero coincidences in a wire-length census of `≥ 40` rows →
the parsing-hazard class is empty and that limb is **CLOSED** (a useful negative). F10-c the
`k`-ablation gate does not produce bit-identical `rho` → SC-5 is misread and every statement above
is void.

**Charged cost.** Documentary work dominates: `~60` ledger rows, each needing a section reference
and a retrieval date, against an environment where nist.gov is unreachable — so a material fraction
of rows will land in the `unretrieved` branch, and that is charged as the expected outcome rather
than treated as a surprise. Compute: three keygens per ablation arm, seconds. Memory negligible. No
oracle, no estimator, no QRAM, quantum depth 0.

**Ceiling.** `control`. A ledger and a collision search; no security claim, no parameter-set claim.

**Risk band.** `conservative`.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.**
`callable_identity_discipline|lwe.dual_hybrid_is_lwe_dual.matzov|EJK_hybrid_confusion|D1_+4.13/+7.42/+11.48`
(`EV-MLKEM-021`).
**Why it is not that:** that record applies identity discipline to a **Python callable in an
estimator run** and quantifies the bit cost of getting it wrong. This one applies the same discipline
