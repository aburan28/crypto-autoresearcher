### 0.2 Tracked objects and the lossy-projection test

Two objects are shared across the slice. Both are stated with the map, the discard, and the
argument that the discard is structural rather than a change of coordinates.

**TO-KS — the σ-relaxed round-key variety.**
Map: `K ↦ RK(K) = (RK_0, …, RK_{Nr})`, then **relax**: treat the SubWord output words
`σ = (σ_1, …, σ_m)` (m = 10 / 8 / 13 for AES-128/192/256, 4 bytes each) as **free variables**
rather than as functions of K. The tracked object is the GF(2)-affine image
`A(K, σ) + c ⊆ GF(2)^{1408 / 1664 / 1920}`, and derived quantities of it (its annihilator space,
its coefficient matrices `A_K` and `A_σ`).

- **Lossy?** Yes. The relaxation **discards the S-box entirely** — the map from K to σ is thrown
  away and replaced by "anything". The relaxed image has GF(2)-dimension ≤ 448 / 448 / 672 while
  the true image is a 128 / 192 / 256-parameter subset of it; the discarded information is
  exactly the graph of SubWord, i.e. exactly 320 / 256 / 416 bits of constraint.
- **Not a change of coordinates.** From TO-KS you cannot recover K, cannot recover any round
  key, and cannot recover the S-box: the same relaxed variety is produced by *every* 8-bit
  bijection substituted for SubWord and by *every* Rcon sequence (the constants move only `c`).
  That invariance is the discard, and A2-2 turns it into a falsifiable prediction.
- **Contrast with LP-1, which correctly rejected the adjacent object.** LP-1 asked whether
  `(K, σ)` is a *lossy re-coordinatisation of K* and answered no, because σ determines K. That
  is right and this is a different question: TO-KS is not the pair `(K, σ)`, it is the **image**
  of the relaxed map, and what it retains is exactly the part of the schedule that is
  S-box-independent. The relaxation LP-1 declined to use as a coordinate change is used here as
  a **projection**, in the opposite direction.

**TO-COMB — the output combiner of a mode.**
Map: a mode of operation `M` built on a block cipher `E` ↦ the algebraic class of the function
that assembles the adversary's transcript out of the block-cipher outputs. Three classes:
**(i)** GF(2)-affine in the block outputs (CTR/GCM keystream, CBC's per-block emission, XOR of
two keyed permutations, truncation, OCB-style XOR offsets); **(ii)** GF(2^128)-linear with
**non-constant** weights (GHASH: block `c` is weighted by `H^{n−c}`); **(iii)** neither.

- **Lossy?** Yes, brutally. Nonces, lengths, associated data, padding, ordering, the tag length,
  the nonce-derivation rule and the entire key-derivation structure are discarded. Only the
  algebra of the combiner survives.
- **Not a change of coordinates.** Infinitely many distinct modes share a combiner class; you
  cannot reconstruct GCM from "class (i) keystream plus class (ii) digest". The fibre is
  unbounded.
- **Why it earns its place.** A2-4 shows this projection is **exactly** the right granularity
  for set-aggregate transfer: whether a reduced-round balance property survives a mode depends
  on the combiner class **and on nothing else** in the projection. A2-9 shows class (ii) blocks
  the transfer that class (i) preserves, and locates the exact condition under which it would
  not.

### 0.5 Two exact derivations this slice rests on, and their known-answer gate

Both were re-derived and machine-checked in pure Python during ideation against the **FIPS 197
Appendix A.1 key-expansion vector** (`2b7e151628aed2a6abf7158809cf4f3c` ⟹ `W[4] = a0fafe17`,
`W[43] = b6630ca6`, both reproduced exactly). They are stated here as derivations, not as
evidence, and every test below re-checks them before believing anything.

- **KS-1.** For AES-128, of the 40 words `W[4..43]`, exactly 30 satisfy the pure-XOR recurrence
  `W[i] = W[i−1] ⊕ W[i−4]`. This gives **960 = 30 × 32 exact GF(2)-linear relations satisfied by
  every expanded key**. Independently: the σ-relaxed domain has 128 + 320 = 448 bits and the
  expanded key has 1408, so the annihilator has dimension ≥ 1408 − 448 = **960**. The two counts
  agree exactly. AES-192: 38 linear words ⟹ **1216**, and 1664 − (192 + 256) = 1216. AES-256:
  39 linear words ⟹ **1248**, and 1920 − (256 + 416) = 1248. S-box byte counts 40 / 32 / 52
  reconcile with the campaign's `N_S = 200 / 224 / 276` as 160+40, 192+32, 224+52.
- **KS-2.** Put `K' = RK_s(K)` (the s-th round key of K, used as a fresh master key). Then
  `RK_1(K') ⊕ RK_{s+1}(K) = (Rcon_1 ⊕ Rcon_{s+1}) ‖ 00 ‖ 00 ‖ 00`, **replicated identically in
  all four words, key-independently, for every K**. Machine-checked over 100–500 random keys per
  shift: the defect set has size exactly 1 at every s ∈ {1,…,9}, with values
  `03, 05, 09, 11, 21, 41, 81, 1a, 37`, matching `01 ⊕ x^s` byte-for-byte.

## 1. The ten ideas

### A2-1. The σ-relaxed annihilator: 960 / 1216 / 1248 exact GF(2)-linear relations on the expanded key, and the open-key freedom count they fix

**Tracked object + what it discards.** TO-KS, and specifically its **annihilator space**
`Ann = { L ∈ (GF(2)^{1408})^* : L(RK(K)) is constant over all K }`. Discards: the S-box
completely (σ is relaxed to free), the round constants (they move only the affine offset), the
key, and every round key. Structural, not a coordinate change — `Ann` is invariant under
replacing SubWord by *any* 8-bit bijection and Rcon by *any* sequence, so the fibre over a given
`Ann` contains a 256!-sized family of schedules, and no key is recoverable from it.

**Mechanism.** The expanded key is GF(2)-affine in `(K, σ)`. The image dimension is at most
`|K| + |σ|`, so the annihilator dimension is at least `(round-key bits) − |K| − |σ|`. That bound
is **met with equality by an explicit basis**: every word whose recurrence has no SubWord term
contributes the relation `W[i] ⊕ W[i−1] ⊕ W[i−Nk] = 0`. Counting those words gives 30 / 38 / 39,
i.e. **960 / 1216 / 1248 bits of exact relation, for every key, at full schedule length**
(KS-1). The relation D8 used by CAND-ND-4 — `σ(RK_r) = RK_{r−1}[1] ⊕ RK_{r−1}[3]` — is a
**corollary**: XOR the three AES-128 relations of round r and the W[4r] terms cancel in pairs.
D8 accounts for 32 bits per round key, i.e. **320 of the 960**; this entry's content is the other
**640**, which the record has never written down.

**Prediction.** (i) `dim Ann` is **exactly** 960 / 1216 / 1248, not more — i.e. `rank(A) = 448 /
448 / 672` exactly, with no rank deficiency hiding extra relations. (ii) All of them hold on
100% of real schedules, at every key size, with zero exceptions. (iii) The **open-key freedom
count** follows and is the number a chosen-key or known-key analysis of AES-based hashing
actually needs: the round-key tuple has exactly **448 bits of affine freedom** for AES-128 and
the 40 S-box constraints remove exactly **320** of them, leaving 128. A chosen-key strategy that
respects only the linear relations therefore **over-counts its own freedom by exactly 320 bits**
(AES-192: 448 → 256 removed → 192; AES-256: 672 → 416 removed → 256).

**Minimal discriminating test.** Pure Python, seconds, no AES-NI needed.
(1) Build `A_K` (1408 × 128) and `A_σ` (1408 × 320) by evaluating the schedule with SubWord
replaced by a formal symbol; compute `rank([A_K | A_σ])` over GF(2) by bitset elimination and
report `1408 − rank`. (2) Emit the explicit 30-relation basis and check it is contained in the
computed annihilator and spans it. (3) Evaluate all 960 functionals on 10^5 random real
schedules per key size. **Known-answer gate (a):** the FIPS 197 A.1 vector must reproduce
`W[4] = a0fafe17` and `W[43] = b6630ca6` before any rank is reported — an instrument that cannot
expand a published test key decides nothing. **Known-answer gate (b), degenerate parameter
setting:** run the identical code on the **fully linearised schedule** (SubWord deleted, so
`|σ| = 0`); the annihilator dimension there is known in advance to be exactly `1408 − 128 =
1280`, and any other answer falsifies the instrument rather than the idea.

**Falsifier.** F1-a `dim Ann ≠ 960` for AES-128 (or ≠ 1216 / 1248) → the two independent counts
disagree and the derivation is wrong; withdraw, do not repair. F1-b **any** of the 10^5 × 960
evaluations is non-constant → the relations are not identities and everything downstream is void.
F1-c the explicit basis fails to span the computed annihilator → there are relations beyond the
XOR recurrences, which is a *stronger* and more interesting outcome and reopens the entry with
the residual dimension as the object. F1-d the degenerate gate returns anything but 1280 →
instrument failure, no disposition.

**Charged cost.** Preprocessing: none. Memory: a 1408 × 448 GF(2) matrix = 79 kB; peak RSS under
50 MB. Memory access: one pass, no random access, no table. Compute: < 10 s single core, pure
Python. Data: 0 plaintexts. Oracle calls: 0. QRAM/quantum depth: none.

**Ceiling.** `medium` — an exact identity holding at full schedule length for all three key
sizes, verified against 100% of samples with a null probability of 2^-960 per vector for a random
1408-bit string. It is **not** a statement about the block cipher and carries no round-count
claim.

**Risk band.** `representation_changing` — it replaces "the key schedule is a bijection" with
"the round-key space is a 448-dimensional affine variety carrying 960 known annihilators plus 40
S-box constraints", which is the representation an open-key analysis has to work in.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `sigma_K_key_schedule_linearisation | SubWord_RotWord_bijective |
sigma_determines_K | not_lossy_change_of_coordinates` (closure LP-1).
**Why it is not that:** LP-1 tested whether `(K, σ)` is a lossy re-coordinatisation of K and
correctly answered no; A2-1 tracks the **annihilator of the σ-relaxed image**, which exists
*because* of the relaxation LP-1 declined, is lossy in the required direction (the S-box is
discarded), and yields a count — 960, with D8 as 320 of it — that LP-1's argument does not
produce.

`sota_delta: 0` / `novelty_status: unverified`

### A2-2. Is MEAS-RT-D's 3584-cell permanent-zero pattern S-box-independent? Predict the set, don't sample the density

**Tracked object + what it discards.** The **permanent-zero incidence set**
`Z_r = { (b, b') : flipping master bit b never flips round-key bit b' of RK_r }` — a *set of
cells*, not a density. Discards: the magnitude of every non-zero cell, the key, all round-key
values, and the direction of every flip. Structural: `Z_r` is a property of the schedule as a
family of functions and 2^128 keys map to one `Z_r`; nothing about any key or any round key is
recoverable from it.

**Mechanism.** MEAS-RT-D measured the *density* (0.78125 = 25/32 for AES-128 from r = 5, i.e.
**3584 of 16384 cells permanently zero over 200000 keys**; 0.8750 for AES-192, 0.78125 for
AES-256) and explicitly **retracted** its own symbolic derivation, because an OR-based dependency
closure cannot represent XOR cancellation and returned support 1.0 at round 5. So the zeros are
**cancellations**, not missing paths, and nobody knows which cells they are or why. TO-KS gives a
route that OR-closure cannot: in the σ-relaxed model the flip is
`ΔRK = A_K e_b ⊕ A_σ Δσ`, so a cell is **certifiably** permanently zero when `(A_K)_{b',b} = 0`
**and** row `b'` of `A_σ` is zero on every σ-coordinate that `b` can reach. That yields a
**certified subset** `Z_pred ⊆ Z_true` computable by GF(2) algebra alone, with **no S-box and no
Rcon anywhere in the computation**.

**Prediction.** `|Z_pred| = 3584` **exactly** for AES-128 at r ≥ 5 — i.e. the entire measured
zero set is σ-relaxed-linear, hence provably S-box-independent and Rcon-independent. Consequences
that then must hold and are separately measurable: replacing the S-box by a uniformly random
8-bit bijection (real wiring, real Rcon) leaves the measured density at 0.78125 within the
binomial band at 200000 keys; replacing Rcon by all-zeros, or by a shuffled sequence, does the
same. AES-192's 0.8750 and AES-256's 0.78125 must be reproduced by the same predictor with no
per-key-size tuning.

**Minimal discriminating test.** (1) Compute `Z_pred` from `A_K`, `A_σ` and a conservative
reachability relation (assume `b` reaches every σ-step ≥ its first, which makes `Z_pred ⊆ Z_true`
by construction — the inequality direction is stated because it is what makes equality
informative). (2) Re-run MEAS-RT-D's sampler at 200000 keys in three arms: **real AES**, **random
bijective S-box / real wiring / real Rcon** (sibling null isolating the S-box), **real S-box /
Rcon ≡ 0** (sibling null isolating the constants). Report the *cell-by-cell* zero set in each
arm, not just the density. **Known-answer gate:** at r = 0 the map is the identity, so `Z_0` is
known in advance — exactly the 16256 off-diagonal cells of a 128 × 128 identity pattern, zero
diagonal cells; the sampler and the predictor must both return exactly that, in every arm.
**Second gate:** the r = 1 zero set is hand-computable for the bits of `W[0]`, `W[1]`, `W[2]`
(they reach `W[4]` only through the fixed XOR chain) and must match.

**Falsifier.** F2-a `|Z_pred| < 3584` and the residual cells are **not** reproduced by the
random-S-box arm → part of the zero set is a genuine S-box-specific cancellation, the σ-relaxed
explanation is incomplete, and the residual is a new and much more interesting object.
F2-b `|Z_pred| > 3584` → the reachability relation is not conservative, i.e. the instrument is
wrong, not the idea. F2-c the random-S-box arm's density differs from 0.78125 by more than
±3 binomial sd at 200000 keys → the pattern is S-box-specific and prediction (i) is dead.
F2-d the r = 0 gate fails in any arm → instrument failure, nothing may be reported.

**Charged cost.** Preprocessing: the same `A_K`/`A_σ` build as A2-1, reused, not rebuilt.
Memory: 128 × 128 bit matrices per round per arm ≈ 2 kB each; the sampler needs 16384 counters
per (round, arm) = 64 kB; peak RSS under 100 MB. Memory access: sequential; the sampler is
200000 keys × 128 flips × 11 rounds ≈ 2.8e8 key expansions per arm, ≈ 3 arms — **charged at
roughly 10–30 minutes on 4 cores**, well inside a 1600 s task. Data: 0 plaintexts (this is the
schedule alone; no encryption is performed). Oracle calls: 0. QRAM: none.

**Ceiling.** `toy` — scoped to the exact instrument, key sizes, sample count and seeds; an
empirical zero over 200000 keys is not a proof of exact independence, and the entry says so in
the same sentence as every density it reports.

**Risk band.** `conservative`.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `key_schedule_as_standalone_object |
bit_influence_support_density | saturates_at_25_over_32=0.78125 | invertible_from_one_round_key`
(MEAS-RT-D / NC-4).
**Why it is not that:** MEAS-RT-D sampled a **density** and retracted its symbolic derivation as
wrong in the unsafe direction; A2-2 tracks the **incidence set**, predicts it from GF(2) algebra
that contains no S-box, and adds the two substitution arms that decide whether the pattern is
about AES or about wiring — a question a density over one arm cannot answer.

### A2-3. Rcon's only related-key job is cross-round *value* decorrelation: the marginals are Rcon-invariant by theorem, the joint is not

**Tracked object + what it discards.** The **SubWord input-difference trajectory**
`T(ΔK, K) = (δ_1, …, δ_m)`, `δ_i ∈ GF(2)^{32}` the input difference to the i-th SubWord call of
the schedule, together with its coarsening to the **activity pattern** `a ∈ {0,1}^m`,
`a_i = [δ_i ≠ 0]`. Discards: every SubWord input *value*, every round key, and K itself.
Structural: 2^128 keys share a trajectory family, and the trajectory does not determine the round
keys — only their differences up to the unknown σ-differences. Not invertible in any direction.

**Mechanism.** Rcon is added identically in both members of a related-key pair, so it **cancels
in the difference at the point where it is added**. Consequence, provable in one line and used as
this entry's own gate: the **marginal** law of each `δ_i` is *exactly* Rcon-invariant, because a
DDT row probability `Pr_x[S(x⊕δ) ⊕ S(x) = γ]` does not depend on `x`. But Rcon *is* XORed into
`W[Nk·i]`, which feeds the **input value** of later SubWord calls; the calls are therefore
functions of the same 128 bits of entropy, and **Rcon is the only thing decorrelating their
values across rounds**. So the *joint* law of `T` — concretely `Pr[a = pattern]` for the 2^m
patterns — is where Rcon can show up at all, and nowhere else. Model, named: this is a
**related-key** statement about the key schedule alone; it is a model strictly weaker than
single-key security and it is not a statement about single-key AES at any round count.

**Prediction.** (i) **Marginals identical to the last bit** across arms {real Rcon, Rcon ≡ 0,
shuffled Rcon, uniformly random Rcon} at every i and every ΔK — this is the known-answer gate,
not the finding. (ii) The **joint** pattern histogram departs: specifically, `Pr[a_i = 0 for two
or more consecutive i]` (the local-collision-shaped patterns) is **strictly larger under
Rcon ≡ 0** than under real Rcon, at r ≥ 3, by a margin exceeding 5 sd at 2^24 sampled keys per
(ΔK, arm). (iii) The departure **grows** with the number of steps and is absent at m = 1 by
construction.

**Minimal discriminating test.** For each of 32 pre-registered master differences ΔK (single
active byte in each of the 16 positions, plus 16 multi-byte patterns), each of 4 Rcon arms, and
2^24 random keys: expand the schedule twice, record `a`, histogram the 2^m patterns (m = 10 for
AES-128, so 1024 bins). Compare arms by an order-statistic difference on the sorted bin counts
(no numpy). **Known-answer gate (a):** ΔK = 0 must give the all-zero pattern with probability
exactly 1.000000 in every arm and every trial — any exception is a harness bug.
**Known-answer gate (b), degenerate setting:** `Pr[a_1 = 1]` is combinatorially determined by ΔK
alone (it is 1 if ΔW[Nk−1] ≠ 0 and 0 otherwise) and must be reproduced **exactly**, in every
arm, before any joint statistic is read. **Sibling null:** real Rcon with the S-box replaced by a
random bijection — if the joint departure appears there too, the effect is about the *presence*
of a nonlinear layer and not about the AES S-box, and the attribution is withdrawn.

**Falsifier.** F3-a any marginal differs across Rcon arms → the one-line derivation is wrong and
the whole entry is void. F3-b the joint histograms agree across all four arms within the
order-statistic band at every ΔK → **Rcon does no measurable decorrelation work at this
resolution**; expected-verdict CLOSED, and the closure is the deliverable: it says the schedule's
difference behaviour is *constant-free* and any related-key statement attributing structure to
specific Rcon values is misattributed. F3-c the departure is present but **flat** in the number
of steps → artifact tell, arm voided. F3-d the effect survives S-box substitution → re-attributed
to nonlinearity in general, and the AES-specific reading is withdrawn.

**Charged cost.** 32 ΔK × 4 arms × 2^24 keys × 2 expansions = 2^31.0 key expansions ≈ 2^28
"AES-equivalent" units; at the measured envelope this is minutes on 4 cores, **inside one 1600 s
task with room to spare**. Memory: 1024 counters × 128 (ΔK, arm) cells = 512 kB; peak RSS < 200
MB. Memory access: sequential, no table lookup beyond the 256-byte S-box. Data: 0 plaintexts, 0
encryptions — the data path is never touched. Oracle calls: 0; **both schedules are computed
offline by the analyst, so no related-key oracle is queried, and the "related-key" label refers
to the model the result would live in, not to a query made**. QRAM: none.

**Ceiling.** `toy`, and additionally capped by CEILING-A2-M: a related-key-model statement about
the key schedule in isolation.

**Risk band.** `high_risk` — the joint departure may sit below the instrument's resolution at
2^24 keys, in which case the honest outcome is F3-b and the entry converts to a closure. The
resolution is stated up front: at 2^24 samples the per-bin sd is 2^-12 relative, so any joint

### A2-4. Mode-transfer identity: a GF(2)-affine output combiner preserves set-aggregate death rounds **exactly**, and that is a property of the combiner, not of AES

**Tracked object + what it discards.** TO-COMB, evaluated on the set-aggregate statistic. Concretely:
for a text set `X` and a mode `M`, the object is the pair (combiner class of `M`, the aggregate
`⊕_{x∈X} transcript_M(x)`), with everything else about the mode discarded — nonce, lengths,
associated data, ordering, tag, key derivation. Not invertible: the aggregate is 128 bits from an
unbounded transcript, and the combiner class is one of three labels from an infinite family of
modes.

**Mechanism.** REF-C's measured death rounds — 1-byte delta-set balance exact through r = 3 and
at the null from r = 4; full-diagonal 2^32 integral **exactly** 16/16 balanced through r = 4 and
0/16 at r = 5 on two keys — are statements about `⊕_{x∈X} E^r_K(x)`. Any mode whose combiner is
GF(2)-affine in the block outputs satisfies `⊕_x C(E(x)) = C'(⊕_x E(x))` up to a constant that
vanishes for even |X|. **Therefore the death round transfers verbatim: neither better nor worse.**
This is an *identity*, not a measurement, and its sharp content is the negative half: the
beyond-birthday PRF construction "XOR of two independently keyed permutations", the CTR/GCM
keystream, truncation, and OCB-style XOR offsets **buy exactly zero resistance** against
set-aggregate distinguishers, because balance is preserved under affine recombination.

**Prediction.** At r = 4 and r = 5, with the same 2^32 diagonal coset and the same code path:
(i) `E^r_{K1} ⊕ E^r_{K2}` (XOR of two keyed permutations, independent keys) reproduces REF-C
**cell for cell**: 16/16 balanced at r = 4, 0/16 at r = 5. (ii) CTR keystream over the same coset
does the same. (iii) A **non-affine** combiner — the GF(2^128) product `E^r_{K1}(x) · E^r_{K2}(x)`
under the GCM reduction polynomial — **destroys balance already at r = 4**, giving 0/16.
(iv) Truncation to any byte subset preserves the r = 4 balance on the retained bytes exactly.

**Minimal discriminating test.** One 2^32 sweep per arm at the campaign's measured 53 s per
sweep; six arms (diagonal AES baseline, XOR-of-two-keys, CTR keystream, GF(2^128)-product,
truncated, PRP control) × two round counts. **Known-answer gate:** the diagonal AES baseline arm
must reproduce REF-C exactly — 16/16 at r = 4 and 0/16 at r = 5 — **in the same binary, on the
same two keys**, before any other arm is read. An instrument that cannot reproduce the
program's own committed measurement decides nothing about combiners. **Sibling null:** the
GF(2^128)-product arm exists precisely to show the transfer is about *affineness* and not about
"modes generally"; if it also balances at r = 4 the mechanism named is wrong.
**Decay control:** every arm at r = 10 must read at the PRP null.

**Falsifier.** F4-a any affine arm departs from REF-C at r = 4 or r = 5 → the identity is false
as stated (or the instrument is broken; the known-answer gate separates the two).
F4-b the non-affine arm **also** balances at r = 4 → affineness is not the load-bearing property
and the projection TO-COMB is at the wrong granularity. F4-c any arm reads non-null at r = 10 →
artifact tell, all arms voided.

**Charged cost.** 6 arms × 2 round counts × 2^32 encryptions = 2^35.6 AES evaluations, ≈ 2^35.6 /
(4 × 2^26.17) ≈ 10 minutes wall clock on 4 cores — **inside one 1600 s task**, and 2^35.6 is
3.2 bits under the 2^38.81 per-task reach. Memory: 16 × 256 accumulators per arm; peak RSS under
100 MB (the sweep is streamed, nothing is stored). Memory access: strictly sequential, no
table beyond the AES-NI path. Data: 2^32 chosen plaintexts per arm, all self-generated offline.
Oracle calls: 0. QRAM: none.

**Ceiling.** `medium` for the identity (it is exact and mode-general); `toy` for each measured
arm, scoped to the round counts, keys and seeds. **Explicitly a MODE/COMPONENT result** and not a
statement about the block cipher (CEILING-A2-C).

**Risk band.** `representation_changing` — it moves "at what round does the integral die" from
being a property of AES to being a property of (AES, combiner class), which changes which
mode-level questions REF-C already answers and which it does not.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `raw_delta_set_integral | 256_texts_one_active_byte |
balance_exact_through_r3 | at_random_null_from_r4` (MEAS-GOAL-AES-002-002, depth_wall_receipt).
**Why it is not that:** that record measures the balance statistic **on the block cipher**;
A2-4 asserts and tests an identity about **output combiners** which predicts, in advance, that

### A2-5. GCM's counter sits on a **column**, not a diagonal — an exactly-one-round integral deficit, and a message-limit charge that caps the coset at 2^30 (2^31 with the tag mask)

**Tracked object + what it discards.** The **byte-placement class of the varying input bytes**:
a mode determines *which* state positions its adversary-visible variation occupies, and this
object retains only that position set (diagonal / column / other) plus the coset dimension `d`.
Discards: the nonce, the key, all values, and the entire remaining state. Not invertible — a
position set and a dimension say nothing about any text.

**Mechanism, derived from ShiftRows index arithmetic.** With the campaign's pinned column-major
layout (`state[t][c] = byte[4c+t]`, `SR(x)[t][c] = x[t][(c+t) mod 4]`): the diagonal
`D_0 = {0,5,10,15}` maps under ShiftRows **onto column 0**, so after round 1 exactly one column
is fully active. GCM (SP 800-38D, 96-bit nonce) fixes the counter block as `N ‖ ctr` with the
counter in **bytes 12–15 = column 3**; ShiftRows sends column 3 to `{12, 9, 6, 3}` — **one byte
in each of the four columns** — so MixColumns makes **all four columns fully active after round
1**. The column coset is therefore **exactly one round further along in diffusion** than the
diagonal coset at the same data cost. Two additional charges follow from the mode's own limits:
GCM caps the plaintext at 2^32 − 2 blocks with counters 1 … 2^32 − 2, so the largest **aligned**
(hence GF(2)-coset) run inside the available counters is **2^30**; the missing element of the
aligned 2^31 run `[0, 2^31−1]` is counter 0, whose block `E_K(J_0)` is the **tag mask** and is
therefore recoverable from any known-plaintext message with its tag — restoring the aligned
**2^31** coset at the cost of one recovered block, and never 2^32 inside GCM's own limit.

**Prediction.** (i) The full 2^32 **column** coset balances exactly through **r = 3** (16/16) and
is dead at **r = 4** (0/16) — one round earlier than REF-C's diagonal 16/16-through-r = 4.
(ii) On a dimension ladder `d ∈ {8, 16, 24, 30, 31, 32}` the column-coset depth is
non-decreasing in `d` and reaches its maximum by `d = 24`, because balance needs only *even*
multiplicities, not uniform ones. (iii) Consequently the largest set-aggregate depth available to
a GCM adversary from **ciphertext alone** is the `d = 30` column reading, and adding the
tag-mask block moves it to `d = 31` and **no further**.

**Minimal discriminating test.** One 53 s sweep per (position set, d, r) cell. Arms: diagonal
(baseline), column 3 (the GCM placement), a **third position set that is neither** (e.g.
`{0,1,2,3}` — a row) as a sibling null isolating "column" rather than "any 4 bytes", and a PRP
control. **Known-answer gate:** the diagonal arm at d = 32 must return **16/16 at r = 4 and 0/16
at r = 5**, reproducing REF-C in the same binary on the same two keys, before any column reading
is believed. **Second gate, degenerate:** at d = 8 (single active byte) every arm must reproduce
the committed 1-byte result — exact through r = 3, at the null from r = 4 — which is known in
advance. **Decay control:** every arm at r = 10 reads the PRP null.

**Falsifier.** F5-a the column arm balances through r = 4 → the one-round deficit is wrong and
the ShiftRows argument has an error; withdraw. F5-b the row arm behaves identically to the column
arm → "column" is not the load-bearing description and the attribution is withdrawn in favour of
"any 4 bytes not forming an inverse-SR diagonal". F5-c the d-ladder shows depth *decreasing* with
d → instrument failure (balance cannot get worse with a larger even-multiplicity set).
F5-d the diagonal gate fails to reproduce REF-C → nothing may be reported.

**Charged cost.** 4 arms × 6 ladder points × 3 round counts, dominated by the four d = 32 sweeps:
≈ 2^34 AES evaluations ≈ 4 minutes on 4 cores, 4.8 bits under the per-task reach. Memory: 16 ×
256 accumulators; peak RSS < 100 MB, everything streamed. Memory access: sequential. Data: up to
2^32 chosen counter blocks per arm, generated offline; **note that in a real GCM setting this is
2^32 blocks = 64 GiB of ciphertext under one nonce, which is why the mode's own 2^32 − 2 limit is
the binding charge and is stated as part of the result rather than as a footnote.**
Oracle calls: 0. QRAM: none.

**Ceiling.** `toy` — scoped to the tested rounds, keys, seeds, position sets and the pinned
final-round convention (no final MixColumns). **Explicitly a MODE result about AES-GCM/AES-CTR
byte placement, not a statement about AES.** It exhibits no attack: a reduced-round depth deficit
in a mode is not a property of the full-round mode and is not claimed to be.

**Risk band.** `conservative` — the ShiftRows argument is index arithmetic re-checkable in
seconds, and the ladder's only real uncertainty is where between d = 8 and d = 24 the depth
saturates.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `full_diagonal_2_pow_32_integral | AES_NI_53s_sweep |
16_of_16_balanced_through_r4 | 0_of_16_full_entropy_at_r5` (MEAS-GOAL-AES-002-002 measurement_2 /
REF-C).
**Why it is not that:** REF-C measures the **diagonal** coset, chosen because it maximises depth;

### A2-6. The key-schedule byte-activity automaton is exactly solvable at 2^16 states; the joint key+data automaton is 2^32 states and 64 GB, and that boundary is exactly where MILP was doing the work

**Tracked object + what it discards.** The **byte-activity state** of the key schedule: the
16-bit vector recording which of the 4 bytes of each of the last `Nk` difference-words is
non-zero. The tracked object is the labelled transition system on these 2^16 states, and the
derived quantity is `A_min(Nr)`, the **minimum number of active SubWord bytes** over all non-zero
master differences and all `Nr` steps. Discards: every difference *value* (only "zero / non-zero"
per byte survives), every key, every probability, and the entire data path. Structural: each
activity pattern has ≈255^{weight} realising differences, and nothing about a difference is
recoverable from its pattern.

**Mechanism.** SubWord acts bytewise and bijectively, so it **preserves byte activity exactly**;
RotWord permutes it; XOR of two active bytes is *non-deterministically* active or cancelled, which
is the only branching in the system. The whole key-schedule activity propagation is therefore a
non-deterministic finite automaton on `(activity of last Nk words) = 4·Nk` bits — **16 bits for
AES-128, 24 for AES-192, 32 for AES-256** — and `A_min` is a shortest-path over
`2^{4Nk} × (Nr+1)` nodes with branching bounded by the cancellation choices. That is **explicit
enumeration, not MILP**: for AES-128 it is 65536 states × 11 layers × 4 bytes = 2.8 MB of DP
table. Model, named: this is a **related-key** quantity — the number of active S-boxes on a
master-key difference trail — and it is a statement about the key schedule in a model strictly
weaker than single-key security, not about single-key AES.
**The charged boundary is the point.** The *joint* key-plus-data automaton has
`(16 data-byte activity bits) + (4·Nk key bits) = 32` bits for AES-128, i.e. 2^32 states ×
15 layers × 1 byte ≈ **64 GB — out of the 15 GB envelope by 2.1 bits**. This entry therefore
delivers the key-side quantity exactly and **declares the joint quantity out of envelope with its
byte count**, rather than estimating it into a result.

**Prediction.** (i) `A_min` is a specific small integer for each key size, computed exactly, with
`A_min(AES-128) ≥ 1` trivially and a predicted value in single digits over 10 steps.
(ii) The **no-cancellation** restriction of the automaton (XOR of two active bytes always active)
returns a value `≥ A_min`, and the gap between them is exactly the cancellation credit — a number
nobody in this repo has written. (iii) The AES-192 automaton's `A_min` is **strictly smaller per
round** than AES-128's, because AES-192 supplies 32 S-box bytes over 13 round keys against
AES-128's 40 over 11 (0.154 vs 0.227 nonlinear bytes per round-key byte).

**Minimal discriminating test.** Pure C, seconds. Build the transition relation from the FIPS 197
word recurrence; run a layered Bellman-Ford / BFS over 2^{4Nk} states for `Nr` layers; report
`A_min`, the minimising activity trajectory, and the no-cancellation variant.
**Known-answer gate (a):** the all-zero master difference must return `A_min = 0` and the all-zero
trajectory, in every variant. **Known-answer gate (b), degenerate:** restrict to `Nr = 1`; the
answer is hand-computable from `ΔW[Nk−1]` alone (0 if that word is inactive, else the number of
its active bytes) and must be reproduced exactly for all 2^{4Nk} start states before any
multi-step number is read. **Gate (c):** the no-cancellation variant must be **≥** the full
variant at every `Nr`; a violation is a DP bug.

**Falsifier.** F6-a gate (b) mismatches on any start state → instrument void.
F6-b `A_min` is not monotone non-decreasing in `Nr` → DP bug (activity cannot be un-spent).
F6-c the no-cancellation variant is **below** the full variant → sign error in the relaxation.
F6-d the joint automaton is found to fit in 15 GB after all (e.g. by a reachability argument
pruning below 2^28 states) → the out-of-envelope declaration is withdrawn **and the entry gets
strictly better**, which is the one outcome that reopens rather than closes it.

**Charged cost.** Memory: 2^16 × 11 × 1 byte = 0.7 MB (AES-128), 2^24 × 13 = 218 MB (AES-192),
2^32 × 15 = **64 GB (AES-256 joint variant: declared OUT OF ENVELOPE and not run)**. The AES-256
*key-only* automaton at 2^32 states × 15 layers × 1 byte is likewise 64 GB and is **also declared
out of envelope**; only AES-128 (2^16) and AES-192 (2^24) are executable here, and that limitation
is part of the result. Memory access: random access into the DP table — charged, and it is why
the 2^24 case is minutes rather than seconds. Compute: < 1 s (AES-128), minutes (AES-192).
Data: 0. Oracle calls: 0. QRAM: none. **No MILP, no SAT, no solver of any kind.**

**Ceiling.** `toy`, and additionally capped by CEILING-A2-M.

**Risk band.** `conservative` — the automaton is small and its answer is exact; the risk is that
`A_min` turns out uninformative, which is a legitimate closure.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `key_schedule_as_layered_boolean_circuit | SubWord_layer_count_a(k) |
degree_bound_delta^a(k) | cap_127_by_bijectivity` (H-AES-b02749).
**Why it is not that:** H-AES-b02749 bounds the **algebraic degree** by counting nonlinear layers;
A2-6 counts **active bytes along difference trajectories** with an explicit finite automaton,
reports an exact minimum plus the cancellation credit, and — the part with operational value —
locates the exact state-count at which the joint problem leaves this machine's envelope.

`sota_delta: 0` / `novelty_status: unverified`

`KS_θ`, θ ∈ {0, 1, …, Nr}, in which θ pairs of round keys are forced equal (θ = 0 is the real

### A2-8. The cross-key shift defect is exactly `(01 ⊕ x^s) ‖ 00 ‖ 00 ‖ 00` in all four words, for every key: the slide window is exactly 1, derived rather than sampled

**Tracked object + what it discards.** The **shift-defect trajectory**
`Δ_i^{(s)}(K) = RK_i(RK_s(K)) ⊕ RK_{i+s}(K)`, i.e. the difference between the schedule of the
"slid" master key `K' = RK_s(K)` and the shifted tail of K's own schedule. Discards: K, both full
schedules, and all round-key values — only a difference sequence survives, and 2^128 keys map to
one trajectory prefix. Not invertible: no key, no round key and no S-box value is recoverable
from the trajectory.

**Mechanism, derived and machine-checked (KS-2).** `Δ_0^{(s)} = 0` by construction. At the next
step the two schedules apply the *same* recurrence to the *same* words and differ **only in the
round constant used**: `Rcon_1` on the slid side versus `Rcon_{s+1}` on the original side. The
SubWord terms are therefore **identical**, not merely equal in distribution, and they cancel:
`Δ_1^{(s)} = (Rcon_1 ⊕ Rcon_{s+1}) ‖ 00 ‖ 00 ‖ 00`, and because `W[4r+k] = W[4r+k−1] ⊕ W[4r+k−4]`
(KS-1) the same 4-byte defect is **replicated in all four words**. Checked over 100–500 random
keys at every `s ∈ {1,…,9}`: the observed defect set has size **exactly 1** at each s, with values
`03, 05, 09, 11, 21, 41, 81, 1a, 37` — byte-for-byte `01 ⊕ x^s`. Since `ord(x) = 51` in GF(2^8)
under the AES polynomial, `x^s ≠ 1` for every `s ∈ {1,…,9}`, so **the defect is non-zero for every
shift and every key, without exception and without sampling**. At step 2 the defect
`03 ‖ 00 ‖ 00 ‖ 00` is rotated by RotWord to `00 ‖ 00 ‖ 00 ‖ 03`, giving **exactly one active
S-box**, so the trajectory continues at a per-step DDT ceiling of 4/256 = 2^-6.

**Prediction.** (i) The **cross-key slide window is exactly 1** for AES-128: `RK_0(K') = RK_s(K)`
holds by definition and `RK_1(K') = RK_{s+1}(K)` **fails for every K and every s**, with a
key-independent defect. (ii) The per-step active-S-box count of the shift family is **exactly 1**
for the first several steps, which is the input A2-6's automaton consumes. (iii) The analogous
computation for AES-192/256 gives a defect determined by `Rcon_1 ⊕ Rcon_{s+1}` in the same way,
and it is likewise never zero. Model, named: this is a **related-key** relation of a non-XOR type
(`K' = KS_s(K)`), which an adversary cannot even instantiate without K — and that is the reason
the entry's verdict is CLOSED rather than a candidate.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Pure Python, seconds. For each key size and each admissible s:
expand K, take `K' = RK_s(K)`, expand K', and record the defect at steps 1, 2, 3 over 10^4 random
keys; report the **size of the observed defect set** at each step. **Known-answer gate:** the
FIPS 197 A.1 vector must expand correctly (`W[4] = a0fafe17`, `W[43] = b6630ca6`) before any
defect is reported. **Degenerate gate:** replace Rcon by the constant sequence
`Rcon_i ≡ Rcon_1`; the step-1 defect must then be **exactly zero for every key and every s**, and
the slide must extend to step 2 — a setting where the correct answer is known in advance and a
silently broken instrument is caught.

**Falsifier.** F8-a the defect set at step 1 has size > 1 for any s → the derivation is wrong,
withdraw. F8-b the constant-Rcon gate does **not** give a zero defect → the instrument is broken
and nothing may be reported. F8-c the step-2 active-S-box count exceeds 1 → the RotWord analysis
is wrong. F8-d the defect is zero for some s at some key size → `x^s = 1` at that s, which would
contradict `ord(x) = 51`; this is the one falsifier that would be a real discovery about the
constant sequence rather than about the instrument.

**Charged cost.** 3 key sizes × 9 shifts × 10^4 keys × 2 expansions ≈ 5.4e5 expansions,
milliseconds. Memory: < 10 MB. Memory access: none beyond a 256-byte S-box. Data: 0 plaintexts.
Oracle calls: 0 — **and the reason matters: the relation `K' = KS_s(K)` is not a relation any
adversary can request, so this is a derivation about the schedule and not an attack model that
can be instantiated.** QRAM: none.

**Ceiling.** `control` — its expected verdict is CLOSED and it is written to be valuable as a
closure: it replaces NC-5's *sampled* "0 repeated round keys in 200000 schedules" with an
**exact, key-independent, derived defect value** covering the cross-key shift family that NC-5's
within-schedule statistic never examined.

**Risk band.** `conservative`.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `single_key_slide_self_similarity | repeated_round_key_precondition |
0_of_200000_schedules_per_key_size | Rcon_differs_per_round` (NC-5 + MEAS-RT-D).
**Why it is not that:** NC-5 sampled **within-schedule** round-key repetition and asserted "Rcon

### A2-9. GHASH's power weighting blocks set-aggregate transfer into the tag — and the exact condition under which it would not is weight-constancy on the round-3 byte fibres

**Tracked object + what it discards.** TO-COMB restricted to class (ii): the **weight sequence**
`(w_c)` of a GF(2^128)-linear digest `Σ_c w_c · B_c` over block outputs `B_c`, together with the
**level-set partition** of the round-3 byte-value map on the counter coset. Discards: the digest
value, the key, H, the ciphertext, the nonce, and all block values — only "which blocks share a
byte value" and "what weight each block carries" survive. Not invertible: a weight sequence and a
partition determine nothing about any message.

**Mechanism.** A2-4's identity gives transfer through **affine** combiners. GHASH is not affine
in the block outputs with constant coefficients: it is `Σ_c C_c · H^{n−c}`, i.e. GF(2^128)-linear
with **key-derived, block-position-dependent** weights. The classical balance argument works
because after three rounds each active byte takes each of its values the **same number of times**;
a *weighted* aggregate over that multiset is `Σ_v v · (Σ_{c : value(c) = v} w_c)`, which vanishes
for **every** value distribution **iff the weight function is constant on each level set of the
byte-value map**. GHASH's weights `H^{n−c}` are distinct for distinct c whenever `H ∉ {0, 1}`,
so the condition fails and the aggregate does not vanish. The degenerate case is exactly
`H = 1`: then every weight is 1, GHASH collapses to the plain XOR of all blocks, and the transfer
**does** occur — which makes `H = 1` a free, exact known-answer gate rather than a curiosity.

**Prediction.** (i) With `H = 1` forced, the r = 4 balance is visible **in the digest alone**,
exactly: the digest over a balanced coset is `0^{128}` in 16/16 byte positions, in 100% of trials.
(ii) With `H` random, the digest over the same coset is uniform: 0/16, with a per-trial null
probability of 2^-128 for the all-zero reading. (iii) The **threshold is sharp in the weight
structure, not in H's size**: any weight sequence taking ≤ 2 distinct values, each on an even-size
block of the coset, restores the transfer, and any sequence with all-distinct weights destroys it —
so the discriminator is the *partition induced by the weights*, not `H` itself.
(iv) Consequently a reduced-round set-aggregate property of AES **does not** become a tag-only
distinguisher on AES-GCM, and the block-by-block transfer A2-4 licenses stops at GHASH.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Reuse A2-5's counter coset. For `H ∈ {1, x, a random field
element, a 2-valued weight sequence, an all-distinct weight sequence}` and `r ∈ {3, 4, 5, 10}`:
compute both the **unweighted** aggregate `⊕_c B_c` and the **weighted** digest `Σ_c w_c B_c`,
and report both.
**Known-answer gate (a):** at `H = 1` the weighted digest must equal the unweighted aggregate
**bit for bit** in 100% of trials — the two code paths must agree exactly, or the field arithmetic
is wrong. **Known-answer gate (b), degenerate:** at r = 3 with the column coset (A2-5's predicted
balanced depth) the unweighted aggregate must be exactly zero, in the same binary; if it is not,
neither reading may be reported. **Sibling null:** the 2-valued-weight arm exists to isolate
*weight-constancy on fibres* rather than "H is not 1"; if it fails to restore the transfer, the
mechanism named is wrong. **Decay control:** r = 10 must be uniform in every arm.

**Falsifier.** F9-a the `H = 1` arm does **not** reproduce the unweighted balance → the collapse
of GHASH to plain XOR at H = 1 is misstated, or the field arithmetic is wrong; the gate separates
the two. F9-b the random-`H` arm reads all-zero in more than a 2^-128-consistent fraction of
trials → the weighting does not destroy the aggregate and prediction (iv) is dead — the strongest
and most interesting failure available here. F9-c the 2-valued-weight arm behaves like the
all-distinct arm → the fibre-constancy condition is not the right condition and the projection is
at the wrong granularity. F9-d gate (b) fails → nothing may be reported.

**Charged cost.** Rides A2-5's sweeps: one extra GF(2^128) multiply-accumulate per block over
2^32 blocks per arm — carry-less multiplication is not on the pinned AES-NI path, so this is
charged at **≈15–40 cycles per block in software**, i.e. roughly 2–5× the AES cost per arm, and
5 weight arms × 4 round counts ≈ **30–45 minutes on 4 cores**. That is the dominant charge in the
slice and is stated rather than absorbed. Memory: one 128-bit accumulator per arm plus the
streaming coset; peak RSS < 100 MB. Memory access: sequential. Data: 2^32 chosen counter blocks
per arm, generated offline. Oracle calls: 0. QRAM: none.

**Ceiling.** `medium` for the algebraic condition (it is exact and holds for any weighted digest,
not only GHASH); `toy` for each measured arm. **Explicitly a MODE/COMPONENT result about
GHASH/AES-GCM and not a statement about AES** (CEILING-A2-C). It exhibits no forgery, no
authentication break and no weak-key claim; `H = 1` is used as an instrument setting, never as an
attack.

**Risk band.** `high_risk` — prediction (iii)'s sharp fibre-constancy threshold is the part most
likely to be wrong, because the round-3 level sets are only *approximately* uniform at coset
dimensions below 32 and the 2-valued arm may sit in a grey zone. The entry says "grey zone",
never "absent".

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `univariate_GF256_interpolation_coefficients |
d_byte_plaintext_coset_restriction | a_255_IS_the_integral_balance | object_contains_integral_family`
(H-AES-ecf3ad).
**Why it is not that:** H-AES-ecf3ad tracks GF(2^8) interpolation coefficients over a plaintext
coset **inside the block cipher**, and finds the integral balance sitting inside that object; A2-9
tracks a GF(2^128) **weighted** aggregate imposed by a mode's digest, and its content is a
condition on the **weights** and their level sets — an object with no counterpart inside the
cipher.

`sota_delta: 0` / `novelty_status: unverified`

### A2-10. Davies–Meyer / Miyaguchi–Preneel makes the message the key: the zero-in/zero-out cancellation chain forces `δRK_i` to equal the data difference **exactly**, and that yields a conservative active-S-box lower bound to compare against 2^64

**Tracked object + what it discards.** The **cancellation-feasibility chain**: for a
compression-function collision with zero chaining-value difference, the per-round pair
`(data difference entering AddRoundKey_i, δRK_i)` reduced to the single bit
`[the round is data-inactive]`, plus the byte-activity vector of `δRK_i`. Discards: every
difference value, every message, every chaining value, every key, and all probabilities.
Structural: the chain is a property of the mode's wiring plus the schedule's activity automaton,
and no message or chaining value is recoverable from it.

**Mechanism.** In Davies–Meyer `h_i = E_{m_i}(h_{i−1}) ⊕ h_{i−1}` the **message is the key** and
the chaining value is the plaintext; in Miyaguchi–Preneel the chaining value is the key. Either
way, a compression collision with `Δh_{i−1} = 0` is exactly a differential with
`ΔP = 0`, `ΔK = Δm ≠ 0`, `ΔC = 0` — i.e. a **related-key/open-key object** whose model must be
named: this is a statement about the **AES-based compression function**, in a model strictly
weaker than single-key security, and it is not a statement about AES as a block cipher.
The chain is forced and short to state: `ΔP = 0`, so the difference after `ARK_0` is `δRK_0`,
which for AES-128 is `ΔK ≠ 0`; a round with **zero** active data S-boxes requires the difference
entering its SubBytes to be zero, which requires `δRK_i` to equal the incoming data difference
**exactly as a value**, not merely in activity pattern; and once a round is fully cancelled the
next round's pre-`ARK` difference is zero, so the **next** round can be inactive only if
`δRK_{i+1} = 0`. Chaining these gives a rigid alternation that a byte-activity automaton can
enumerate, and the resulting count is a **valid lower bound** on total active S-boxes (key side +
data side) because every relaxation used enlarges the feasible set.

**Prediction.** (i) The chain forbids two consecutive fully-inactive data rounds unless the
corresponding round-key differences are zero, and A2-8/KS-1 show a zero round-key difference at
step i forces zero at every subsequent step for AES-128 — so **at most one fully-inactive data
round is available**, and the rest of the trail pays. (ii) The conservative lower bound
`L = A_min(key side, from A2-6) + D_min(data side under the cancellation constraint)` is a
computable integer, and the decision the entry exists to make is whether
`6 · L` — the DDT ceiling in bits, `max DDT = 4/256 = 2^-6` per active S-box — **exceeds 64**, the
generic collision cost of a 128-bit DM/MP hash. (iii) Predicted outcome: `6L > 64` comfortably,
i.e. the lane is **CLOSED at the level of this bound**, and the entry's value is the number plus
the explicit statement of how loose the bound is.

**Minimal discriminating test.** Extend A2-6's automaton with the DM/MP boundary conditions
(`ΔP = 0` forced, `ΔC = 0` forced) and the cancellation rule above, keeping the **key-side state
at 2^{4Nk} bits** and treating the data side only through the conservative per-4-round
wide-trail credit (`≥ 25 active S-boxes per 4 rounds minus the bytes a round-key difference can
cancel, capped by that difference's own active-byte count`). Report `L`, `6L`, and the
minimising trajectory. **Known-answer gate (a):** with `ΔK = 0` the bound must return `L = 0` and
the trivial trajectory. **Known-answer gate (b), degenerate:** delete the key schedule and let
every `δRK_i` be **free and independent**; the bound must then collapse to `L = 1` (only the first
round is forced), because a free round-key difference can cancel anything — a setting whose
correct answer is known in advance and which catches a bound that is secretly ignoring the
schedule. **Gate (c):** the bound computed with cancellation forbidden must be `≥ L`.

**Falsifier.** F10-a gate (b) does not return `L = 1` → the bound is not actually using the
schedule constraint and every number it produces is meaningless. F10-b `6L ≤ 64` → the bound is
too loose to close the lane, which is a legitimate and *reportable* outcome: it says the lane
stays open **at this bound's resolution**, and it names the tightening needed (the full joint
automaton, which A2-6 charges at 64 GB and declares out of envelope). F10-c `L` is not monotone in
the round count → DP bug. F10-d the cancellation rule is shown to admit a trail this bound
excludes → the bound is not conservative, i.e. invalid in the unsafe direction; withdraw
immediately rather than repair.

**Charged cost.** Same automaton as A2-6 plus a per-round credit table: AES-128 at 2^16 key-side
states × 11 layers, < 1 s and < 1 MB. Memory access: random into a 0.7 MB table. Data: 0.
Oracle calls: 0. Compression-function evaluations: 0 — **this is a counting bound and evaluates
nothing**, which is stated because a bound that is never tested against a search is exactly the
kind of number this repo has learned to distrust; the honest limitation is that no matching trail
search is run and none is claimed. QRAM: none. **No MILP, no SAT.**

**Ceiling.** `toy`. **Explicitly a COMPONENT result about the AES-based compression function in a
named open-key/related-key model** (CEILING-A2-C and CEILING-A2-M jointly), not a statement about
AES, not a collision, and not a claim about any deployed hash function.

**Risk band.** `high_risk` — the conservative relaxations may make `L` so small that `6L ≤ 64`
and the entry returns F10-b, i.e. an uninformative bound. That outcome is pre-declared as the
likely one and is reported as "the lane stays open at this resolution", never as "no bound
exists" (a fatigue report, barred).

**Nearest prior-art neighbour.** `F_linear_round_independent_per_word_projection |
one_super_box_interface_ARK.MC.SR | kernel_0<dim K<4 | per_word_branching_bound_B`
(Proposition 806-1, scoped).
**Why it is not that:** 806-1 bounds per-word branching across a single super-box interface in the
**single-key data path**, where the round key is a translation and drops out; A2-10's counting
object is the **cancellation chain forced by the feed-forward**, in which the round-key difference
must equal the data difference *as a value* — a constraint with no counterpart in the single-key
data path and which a per-word branching bound does not see.

`sota_delta: 0` / `novelty_status: unverified`
