### 0.2 Tracked objects and the lossy-projection test

C1 tracks **TO-MULT** — `(E, φ_min : E → E^{(p)}) ↦ multiset of prime factors of deg(φ_min)` —
and C2 tracks the fibre over it. This slice needs three *different* projections. Each is stated
with what it discards and why the discard is structural rather than a change of coordinates.

**TO-OVH — the overhead functional.** The map
`(algorithm A, parameter path B(·)) ↦ F_A(p) := log₂ T_A(p) − (1/3)·log₂ p`,
regarded as a *function of p along a path*, together with its asymptotic normal form
(the pair (a, b) in `ln B_opt ≍ κ (ln p)^a (ln ln p)^b`).

- **Lossy?** Yes, and structurally. It discards the curve, the isogeny, every constant, every
  polylog cofactor, and the entire concrete-cost model; two algorithms with the same normal form
  can differ by any number of bits at every finite p. The fibre over a given F is the whole class
  of cost functions agreeing to second order.
- **Compatible with the target's operations?** Yes: composition of cost stages is addition on
  TO-OVH, and the source's own assembly (L210–L218) is exactly a sum in this coordinate.
- **Not a change of coordinates.** T is not recoverable from F: the source's five published rows
  (L234–L238) are *not* determined by "p^{1/3+o(1)}", which is precisely the complaint the
  abstract itself lodges (L13: "a superpolynomial overhead hiding in the o(1)").
- **Why it is a different object from TO-MULT.** TO-MULT is where *correctness* lives; TO-OVH is
  where the **exponent budget's second order** lives. A statement about TO-MULT's hit probability
  is invisible in TO-OVH unless it changes the normal form, and vice versa.
- Used by **Q1-1, Q1-4, Q1-6, Q1-7**.

**TO-NORMSET — the truncated norm multiset of the rank-3 lattice.** The map
`E ↦ {Nrd(v)/p : v ∈ Λ_E, Nrd(v)/p ≤ S}` as a **multiset**, where Λ_E is the rank-3 trace-zero
sublattice of Hom(E, E^{(p)}) (disc p/4; committed anchor `p/4 ≤ N₁N₂N₃ ≤ p/2`) and S is the
ceiling the algorithm has **already paid for**, S = X² = B·D.

- **Lossy?** Yes. It discards which vector realises each norm, the lattice basis, the Gram
  matrix, the curve, and the isogenies themselves. The fibre over a norm multiset is the whole
  genus-plus-automorphism orbit; and the *ordering* information that C2 tracks is gone.
- **Not TO-MULT.** TO-MULT is the factorisation of **one** element of this multiset — the
  minimum. TO-NORMSET keeps the whole truncated multiset and *forgets* the factorisations.
  Neither is recoverable from the other: you cannot get the multiset from the minimum's
  factorisation, and you cannot get any factorisation from the multiset's norms without factoring.
- **Not a change of coordinates.** Recovering Λ_E from the norm multiset would solve the
  representation problem for the ternary form, which is the object the whole goal turns on.
- Used by **Q1-2, Q1-8, Q1-10** (Q1-10 through a further projection, TO-LOGSPEC, defined there).

**TO-LPF — the cost-attribution projection.** The map `entry ψ ↦ P(deg ψ)`, the **largest prime
factor** of the entry's degree.

- **Lossy?** Yes, brutally: the fibre over ℓ is Ψ(X/ℓ, ℓ)-many degrees, each with its own
  isogeny fibre. It discards the entire factorisation except its top.
- **Why it is the right object for cost and the wrong one for correctness.** Algorithm 1's loops
  run `for ℓ ≤ B` ascending (L143), and the POC's enumerator uses `prime_range(prev_deg, …)`
  (POC:L48), so **an entry of degree d is created exactly once, during the pass of ℓ = P(d)**,
  and its marginal creation cost is one Φ_{P(d)}-root-finding share. Correctness, by contrast,
  factors through TO-MULT and is blind to P(d). So cost and correctness factor through *different*
  projections — that is the structural content, and it is why TO-LPF is not a repackaging of
  TO-MULT: TO-LPF is strictly coarser and is the unique coarsening the *cost* respects.
- Used by **Q1-3, Q1-9**.

## 1. The ten ideas

### Q1-1. The o(1) has a saddle-point normal form, and the paper's fixed B is not on it

**Tracked object** — TO-OVH along the one-parameter family `B = exp(c·√(log p))`, projected to
the pair (a, b) in `ln B_opt ≍ κ·(ln p)^a·(ln ln p)^b` and to `F(p) = log₂T − (1/3)log₂p`.
**Discards:** the curve, the isogeny, the fibre, every constant and every polylog; two cost
functions differing by 40 bits at every finite p have the same TO-OVH. The discard is structural
because F is a *limit* object: no finite table of rows determines it, and it does not determine
any row. That is exactly why the source can assert p^{1/3+o(1)} and simultaneously warn that the
o(1) hides a superpolynomial overhead (L13, L39) without contradiction.

**Which factor** — the o(1) itself, i.e. the residual after F1–F8 are fixed. Exponent 1/3 is
untouched throughout; CEILING-SLICE is respected by construction.

**Mechanism** — Write the source's own assembly with B free. With `D = (p/2)^{1/3}`,
`X = B^{1/2}D^{1/2}`, `w = log X/log B = 1/2 + u/2`, `u = log D/log B`:
`M = Ψ(X,B)·X = X²·ρ(w)` and `T = M/ρ(u)`, so with `L = ln p`,
`ln T − (1/3)ln p = ln B/2·(1) + [w ln w − u ln u]·(−1) + o(...)`, whose leading behaviour along
`B = e^{c√L}` is

> `f(c) ≈ √L · ( ln L/(12c) + c )`,

a textbook saddle in c. The minimiser is `c* = √(ln L/12)` — **not a constant** — giving
`ln B_opt ≈ √(L·ln L/12)` and overhead `exp((1+o(1))·√(L·ln L/3))`. The paper's fixed
`B = e^{(1/3)√log(p/2)}` (L193) pins c = 1/3 and gives overhead `exp(Θ(√L·ln L))`. The ratio of
the two overheads is `Θ(√(ln ln p)) → ∞`. Both are p^{o(1)}, so **Theorem 1.1 is untouched**;
what moves is the *shape* of its o(1). The source itself flags the practical version of this at
L200 ("in practice, one may instead precompute an optimal choice of B"); what has never been
written down here or there is the **asymptotic law** of that optimum.

**Prediction (MODELED-HERE, numbers in §0.4)** —
(a) `ln B_opt/√(L ln L)` is constant to within ±7 % over log₂p ∈ [2^8, 2^20] (0.3420 → 0.3178,
drifting toward the predicted 1/√12 = 0.2887), while `ln B_opt/√L` drifts by +50 % (0.7783 →
1.1674) over the same range. (b) The overhead over p^{1/3} at the optimum is 23.47 bits at
NIST-I versus 38.66 bits at the paper's B — a **15.19-bit gap at NIST-I**, and the ratio of the
two overheads rises monotonically 1.647 → 2.104 across the ladder. (c) The optimal u stays inside
both uniformity windows: u = 5.68 at NIST-I, and `u ≍ (2/√3)√(L/ln L)` sits strictly between

### Q1-2. The success probability is a lattice integral at the threshold already paid for, not ρ(u) at the minimum

**Tracked object** — TO-NORMSET at the ceiling `S = X² = B·D`: the multiset of norms
`{Nrd(v)/p ≤ B·D}` of the rank-3 trace-zero lattice Λ_E, jointly with the *B-smoothness
indicator* and the *window-catch indicator* of each norm.
**Discards:** which vector, the Gram matrix, the curve, the isogenies, and every factorisation.
Structurally lossy: recovering Λ_E from the multiset is the representation problem for the
ternary form. **Not TO-MULT:** TO-MULT is the factorisation of the multiset's *minimum*; here the
minimum is one of ≈ 1.5·10^7 elements (NIST-I, B = 2^{14.2}) and the factorisations are forgotten.

**Which factor** — F4 (1/P0) at its interface with F2 (the split). Exponent 0 by CEILING-SLICE;
the entire deliverable is a **signed bit count on 1/P0**, which multiplies every margin row
identically.

**Mechanism** — Lemma 3.5 (L187) lower-bounds P0 by the smoothness probability of the **single
smallest** isogeny, and Remark 1 (L191) records that multiplicity exists, that Panny observed it,
and that it "is absorbed in the hidden term". But Algorithm 2's table catches **every** cyclic
B-smooth isogeny E → E^{(p)} of degree n ≤ X² that admits a divisor a with n/X ≤ a ≤ X — the
proof of Lemma 3.4 (L177–L185) merely *guarantees* such a divisor when n ≤ D, by the greedy
argument. Degrees between D and X² are therefore **already paid for**: the table is built to X
regardless, and catching them costs nothing extra. The honest success probability is

> `P0 = Pr[ ∃ n ∈ TO-NORMSET(X²) : n is B-smooth, primitive, and has a divisor in [n/X, X] ]`,

whose first moment is `∫₁^{X²} q(n)·ρ(log n/log B) dN(T)` with
`N(T) = (8π/3)·T^{3/2}/√p` the Siegel–Rogers count for the disc-p/4 rank-3 lattice, and q(n) the
divisor-window density at *relative* window width `log(X²/n)/log n`. The incumbent's ρ(u) is
exactly the `n ≤ D, q ≈ 1` term of this integral. Separability is free: every n ≤ X² = p^{0.39}
is below p, so no vector in the range is inseparable.

**Prediction (MODELED-HERE)** — **Undiscounted**, the integral gives
E[# B-smooth norms ≤ X²] = **17.85** at NIST-I with B = 2^{14.2} (cumulative 1.5·10^{−4} at
n ≤ D; 0.98 at n ≤ D·B^{0.75}; 17.85 at n ≤ X²), i.e. **19.73 bits above ρ(u)** — while the
committed model asserts 1/P0 = 15.45 bits. Since P0 ≤ 1, the correction is bounded:
**the free-multiplicity credit lies in [0, 15.45] bits at NIST-I**, and the entire budget is
carried by q. Two readings with opposite consequences, and the entry exists to separate them:
*(R-tiny)* q decays fast enough that ρ(u) is accidentally right — but then C1-1's own claim that
q(λ) is Θ(1) at λ = O(1) is **inconsistent with the committed 1/P0**, an internal contradiction
this catalogue must surface rather than inherit; *(R-large)* q is Θ(1) over a range of n, and
§4.1's 1/P0 factor largely evaporates, moving every margin row **in the attack's favour** by up
to 15.45 bits. At NIST-V and 768 the undiscounted headroom is 31.43 and 39.83 bits, so the
tension grows with p.

**Minimal discriminating test** — Pure integer arithmetic; no curve, no isogeny, no SageMath.

**Tracked object** — TO-LPF: `entry ψ ↦ P(deg ψ)`, the largest prime factor of the entry's

### Q1-4. The minimum sufficient distributional hypothesis: Heuristic 1 is exponent-decorative and o(1)-load-bearing

**Tracked object** — TO-OVH composed with a **hypothesis poset**: the map

### Q1-5. Does 1/P0 multiply or add? The attempt-correlation length, and the one lever that acts on A7's open defect

**Tracked object** — the **attempt process**: the sequence of re-randomised curves
`E → E'_1, E'_2, …` produced by Algorithm 3 (L195–L207), projected to the pair
`(graph distance between successive attempts, smoothness indicator of δ_{E'})`.
**Discards:** the curves themselves, the walks, δ_E's *value*, the isogenies, everything except a
distance and a bit. Structurally lossy: from a distance-and-bit sequence one cannot recover a
single curve, and the fibre over "(d, 1)" is the entire set of pairs at distance d with smooth
minimal degree.

**Which factor** — the **composition** F_total = (per-attempt cost) × (1/P0). Red Team O6 records
that no lever acts on this multiplication, that it is an OPEN_DEFECT of the composition, HIGH
severity, and that it has never been repaired. This entry is the one that attacks it.

**Mechanism** — Algorithm 3 pays a fresh table of M entries per attempt and multiplies by
1/P0 ≈ 2^{15.45}. Two structurally different reasons the multiplication could fail to be a
multiplication:
- *(S-share)* **Prefix sharing.** If the next attempt curve is taken **from inside the table just
  built** rather than from a fresh long walk, the two tables share a subtree, and the marginal
  cost of attempt k+1 is only the part of the tree not already present.
- *(S-corr)* **Correlated failure.** The same choice makes the attempts *dependent*: δ obeys the
  near-Lipschitz relation `δ(E'') ≤ δ(E')·deg(ω)²` along a connecting isogeny ω, so nearby curves
  have correlated smoothness indicators and sharing buys fewer *effective* attempts than it buys
  cheap ones.
The entry's whole content is that S-share and S-corr **pull in opposite directions with a
measurable crossover**, and that the crossover is a property of a *correlation length* in the
2-isogeny graph: `C(d) = Pr[δ_{E''} B-smooth | δ_{E'} B-smooth, dist = d] / Pr[δ B-smooth]`.

(predicted d* ≤ 4 at toy scale), because the Lipschitz factor deg(ω)² = 4^d destroys the

subtree fraction. Predicted net saving on 1/P0: **under 1 bit**, i.e. the multiplication stands
and Red Team O6's defect is confirmed as unrepairable by this route — which is a legitimate,
valuable deliverable (house rule 8). The *disconfirming* branch is sharp: if C(d) > 1.5 out to
d ≥ 8 while the shared-prefix fraction at d = 8 is still ≥ 2^{−8}, the composition is not a clean
multiplication and A7 becomes live.

**Discards:** the isogeny, its kernel, the intermediate curves, and the entire meet-in-the-middle
structure — the predicate returns one bit per n. Structurally lossy: from the bit-vector
`(1_{Φ_n = 0})_{n ≤ D}` one cannot recover a single isogeny; recovering one requires a second,
constructive stage. **Not TO-MULT:** correctness here does not factor through any factorisation
at all, which is precisely why the skeleton is different rather than a re-parameterisation.

**Which factor** — F3 (list cardinality) removed entirely, replaced by a per-n arithmetic charge.
This is the "sieving-style relation collection instead of table construction" cell of the slice.

**Mechanism, and the expected closure** — The skeleton is: for n = 1, 2, … up to some ceiling,
evaluate the classical modular polynomial at the single point (j, j^p) and test for zero; the
first zero certifies an n-isogeny E → E^{(p)}, after which a constructive stage recovers it. The
appeal is that the search space is the **degree axis** (D = p^{1/3} values) rather than the
**isogeny axis** (M = p^{1/3} entries with memory p^{1/3}) — the memory drops to O(1), which is
the source's own stated obstacle (L39: "its memory cost is essentially as high as the
complexity"). The closure is a coefficient count: Φ_n has degree ψ(n) = n∏_{ℓ|n}(1+1/ℓ) in each
variable, hence Θ(n²) coefficients, and **no evaluation of a bivariate polynomial can cost less
than reading the data that defines it** unless the polynomial is never materialised. Summing the
materialising cost over n ≤ D gives Σ_{n ≤ D} n² ≈ D³/3 = **Θ(p)** — worse than the
unconditional p^{1/2}·(log p)^{O(1)} baseline (L25) by a full p^{1/2}, and worse than the
incumbent by p^{2/3}. Restricting to B-smooth n does not rescue it: the smooth n near the top of
the range cost n² each, and there are Ψ(X², B) of them.

**Prediction** — Expected verdict **CLOSED at the whiteboard**, with two named escapes stated
precisely so that the closure carries forward guidance rather than a full stop:
- **Escape E1 (the only quantitative one).** A point evaluation of Φ_n mod p in `n^{o(1)}`

  the sieve beats the incumbent iff `Σ_{n ≤ D} eval(n) ≤ p^{1/3+o(1)}`, i.e. iff

### Q1-7. Is the table compressible as a product of two half-tables? The group-action obstruction to a recursive skeleton

**Tracked object** — TO-OVH for the **table representation**: the map
`(E, X, B) ↦ the codomain set S_X = {j(codomain ψ) : ψ ∈ L(E, X, B)}`, tracked together with its
**product-set profile** `π(A, B') = the multiset of multiplicities of the composition map
A × B' → S_X` for half-radius sets A, B'.
**Discards:** the isogenies, the degrees, the kernels, the order of composition — everything
except a set of j-invariants and a multiplicity histogram. Structurally lossy: from the codomain
set one cannot recover a single isogeny, and the fibre over a codomain is exactly the object C2
tracks. **Not TO-MULT and not TO-NORMSET:** it is a set of curves with a composition profile, and
neither degrees nor norms appear in it.

**Which factor** — F3 (list cardinality) and the **memory equality** memory = time. The question
is whether the p^{1/3} table can be *stored implicitly* as a product of two p^{1/6} tables — the
baby-step/giant-step move — which would break memory = time without touching the exponent of time.

**Mechanism, and the expected closure** — Baby-step/giant-step compression works exactly when the
search space carries a **group action**: in a Cayley graph of an abelian group, the distance-k
sphere factors as a product of two distance-k/2 spheres, and membership in the product can be
tested without materialising it. The supersingular ℓ-isogeny graph is **not** a Cayley graph: the
composition of two smooth isogenies from E is not determined by their codomains, so the "product"
A · B' is not a well-defined set operation on codomains at all — the map (ψ, η) ↦ codomain(η∘ψ)
depends on ψ's *codomain-with-basis*, not on its codomain. The expected closure is therefore:
**the codomain set has no product structure, and the multiplicity profile of the composition map
is the Poisson profile of a random map into ≈ p/12 points**, matching an unstructured null exactly
and matching a Cayley null not at all. The value of the entry is that this is *measurable* rather
than asserted, and that it produces the **exact structural predicate** a compressible skeleton
would need — which is the same predicate that separates the in-scope schemes from the
group-action family that the source itself places out of range (L33).

**Prediction** — Expected verdict **CLOSED**, with a measured separation. Predicted at toy scale:
the composition-multiplicity histogram on the supersingular graph is within its Poisson interval
at every bin, while on an oriented crater (a genuine cyclic-class-group Cayley graph of the same
size) it is a delta at multiplicity 1 with zero variance — a separation of many standard
deviations that any working instrument must see. Predicted consequence if the closure holds:
**memory = time is a structural property of the skeleton, not an implementation choice**, and no
recursive/BSGS re-representation of the table exists inside this projection.

**Minimal discriminating test** — Toy, exhaustive, therefore decisive **about the projection**.
(1) At p ≈ 2^{10}–2^{16}, p ≡ 3 mod 4, enumerate the supersingular set (≈ p/12 classes) by
2-isogeny closure from j = 1728. Build S_X for a modest X, and for every pair (A, B') of
half-radius sets measure the multiplicity histogram of the composition map.
(2) Repeat on the **Cayley control**: an ℓ-isogeny volcano crater / oriented cycle of comparable
size where the class-group action is exact.
(3) Repeat on the **random-map control**: a uniformly random function into a set of the same size.
(4) Report the three histograms with χ² against Poisson and against the delta.
**Known-answer gate (a):** on the Cayley control the machinery **must** return the delta at
multiplicity 1 exactly; an instrument that cannot detect the group structure where it certainly
exists is void. **Known-answer gate (b):** on the random-map control it must return Poisson(λ)
with λ = |A||B'|/|S| within its interval. **Known-answer gate (c):** |A · B'| ≤ |A||B'| must hold
identically; a violation is a counting bug.

**Null object / control** — the Cayley arm and the random-map arm **are** the test (steps 2–3);
the design has no arm without a ground truth. Additional **degenerate control:** A = {E} must give
A · B' = B' exactly.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric)** — **F7-a** the supersingular histogram is within its Poisson
interval at every bin at all tested p, while the Cayley arm returns the delta → CLOSED with a
mechanism: no product compression, memory = time is structural inside this projection, and the

compression exponent to chase. **F7-c** the Cayley arm does not return the delta → instrument
failure, no disposition. **F7-d** the departure appears on the random-map arm too → an artifact of
the histogram estimator.

**Charged cost** — impl: medium (~350 lines: supersingular enumerator, X_0(ℓ) neighbour lists,
composition tables, histogram statistics). compute: ≤ 3 CPU-hours; peak memory ≈ |S|² bounded by
choosing p ≤ 2^{16} so |S| ≤ 5500 and the pair table fits in < 1 GB — **the memory of the control
itself is charged**, and it is the binding constraint on p, stated in advance. No SageMath (a

**Declared limitation, charged not hidden:** an exhaustive product-profile measurement exists only

**Ceiling** — `toy`. Exhaustive enumeration exists only at toy p, and the deliverable is a

### Q1-9. Φ_ℓ(j, ·) is totally split on the supersingular locus, so root-finding costs log ℓ powmods — and batching cannot remove them

**Tracked object** — TO-LPF paired with the **splitting type** of the specialised modular
polynomial: `entry ψ ↦ (P(deg ψ), the factorisation type of Φ_{P(ψ)}(j(codomain), x) over F_{p²})`.
**Discards:** the roots themselves, the isogenies, the degrees below the top — it keeps a prime
and a partition of ℓ+1. Structurally lossy: the partition determines the *cost shape* of the step

**Which factor** — F7/F8 and, through them, the **L4-BATCH removable term** (11.502–13.247 bits,

**Mechanism** — Every curve ℓ-isogenous to a supersingular curve is supersingular, and every
supersingular j-invariant lies in F_{p²} (L109). Therefore, for supersingular j,
**Φ_ℓ(j, x) splits completely into ℓ+1 linear factors over F_{p²}** — the maximally degenerate
case for root-finding. Two consequences the record does not carry:
- **(C-gcd) The rational-root shortcut is a no-op.** The standard first move,
  `gcd(x^{p²} − x, f)`, returns **f itself** on this locus. A cost model that charges one modular
  exponentiation and then "finds the roots" has charged the step that does nothing and not the
  step that does the work. The subsequent equal-degree splitting of a totally split degree-(ℓ+1)
  polynomial costs Θ(log ℓ) further modular exponentiations (the standard
  `O(M(n) log n log q)` Cantor–Zassenhaus profile), not O(1).
- **(C-batch) Batching cannot remove them.** The footnote at L156 names batched evaluation of
  modular polynomials as the likely fastest population route, and L4-BATCH prices it. Batching
  amortises the **evaluation** of Φ_ℓ across many j; it does **not** amortise the **splitting**,
  which is a separate polynomial per j and is inherently per-entry. Hence the splitting term is a
  **floor** under the per-entry cost that no amount of batching removes, and it therefore caps
  L4-BATCH's removable fraction from above.

one-powmod convention: at ℓ = B_opt = 2^{14.2} that is **≈ 3.9 bits**, and at Q1-1's optimum
ℓ = 2^{14.96} it is ≈ 4.0 bits. Since the total corrected overhead is 21.11–25.92 bits and
L4-BATCH's removable term is 11.50–13.25 bits, a ≈ 3.9-bit irremovable splitting floor caps the
removable fraction at **≤ 48–56 %** rather than the recorded 48–59 %, and does so *without
building a batched implementation* — which is the only reason this entry is cheap enough to run.
Direction: **anti-attack** (the algorithm is more expensive than the one-powmod convention says),
and it must be carried signed and un-netted against the pro-attack RT3-C1 correction of Q1-3.

### Q1-10. TO-LOGSPEC: the split question is a Poisson–Dirichlet functional, and its universality is a sharp, opposed prediction

**Tracked object** — **TO-LOGSPEC**: the map
`d ↦ (log ℓ₁/log d, …, log ℓ_k/log d)` — the *normalised log-spectrum* of the degree, a point in
the infinite simplex, i.e. the multiset of prime factors rescaled so their logs sum to 1.
**Discards:** the primes themselves, the size of d, the curve, the isogeny. Two degrees with the
same log-spectrum share no prime factor. Structurally lossy: the primes are unrecoverable, and
the fibre over a spectrum is infinite.
**Why it is not a coordinate change of TO-MULT:** TO-MULT determines TO-LOGSPEC but not
conversely, and — the load-bearing point — **the split-window predicate factors through
TO-LOGSPEC while the cost does not**. Q1-3's TO-LPF is the projection cost respects; TO-LOGSPEC is
the projection *correctness* respects, one level coarser than TO-MULT. Separating the three is
what makes each entry's mechanism different rather than a restatement.

**Which factor** — F2 (the split criterion) as a *universality* question, and through it F3.
Exponent 0 by CEILING-SLICE; the deliverable is a p-free, B-free limit law plus a bit count.

**Mechanism** — Lemma 3.4's split (L177–L185) asks: does d admit a divisor in
`[d/X, X]`? Written on TO-LOGSPEC, the question is: **does a sub-multiset of the normalised log
parts have a sum in an interval of relative width `η = log(X²/d)/log d` around 1/2?** That is a
pure random-partition question, and for a *uniform* integer the normalised log-spectrum converges
to the **Poisson–Dirichlet(1)** law; the corresponding conditioned law for B-smooth integers is
its smooth analogue with parameter u. On PD-type spectra, the sub-multiset sums form a set whose
gaps shrink geometrically in the number of parts — the classical number-partitioning phase
transition — so `q(η) ≈ 1 − exp(−Θ(2^{ω}·η))` rather than a log-power. That is a **different
mechanism** from the Erdős–Ford multiplication-table density (which is the answer for uniform
integers at a *dyadic* window and gives `(log y)^{−0.086}(log log y)^{−3/2}`), and it makes a
**different and opposed prediction** about the u-dependence.

**Prediction, stated so that it can disagree with a sibling entry** — C1-1's design registers
"q must increase monotonically in λ and decrease as u increases at fixed λ" as its artifact tell,
and its mechanism section argues q "grows with u". TO-LOGSPEC predicts instead that the *governing
variable is `2^{ω}·η`, not u or λ separately*, and that `q → 1` rapidly once `2^{ω}η ≫ 1`:
at NIST-I parameters (u ≈ 6, ω typically 8–12, η = 1/u at λ = √B) the product is
`2^{10}/6 ≈ 170 ≫ 1`, so **q ≈ 1 at λ = O(1)** and C1-1's gain sits at essentially its maximum.
The two predictions differ measurably at small ω: at ω ≤ 4 the PD law says q collapses while a
u-monotone reading says it should still improve with u. **The discriminator is a collapse of
q(λ, u, ω) onto the single variable `2^{ω}η`.**

**Minimal discriminating test** — Pure integer arithmetic, shared with C1-1's sample so no new
data is generated.
(1) Enumerate/sample B-smooth n at D ∈ {2^{40}, 2^{48}, 2^{56}} across a u-ladder, recording ω(n)
and the exact window-hit indicator at a grid of η.
(2) Plot q against `2^{ω}η` and against (u, λ) separately; fit the collapse and report the
residual under each parameterisation. **The discriminator is which parameterisation collapses the
family.**
(3) Emit the implied λ (hence the implied X) at which q ≥ 0.9, and the resulting bit change in M
and in peak memory.
**Known-answer gate (a):** on **uniform** integers with the full spectrum (no smoothness
conditioning), the machinery must return the **Golomb–Dickman constant**,
`E[log P(n)/log n] = 0.6243299885…`, and `Pr[P(n) ≤ n^{1/2}] = ρ(2) = 0.30685`. Both are exact,
free, and cannot be fitted by accident. **Known-answer gate (b):** at η = 1 (window = everything)
q must be exactly 1.000 at every sampled n. **Known-answer gate (c):** q must be monotone
non-decreasing in η at fixed n.

**Null object / control** — **Uniform-integer arm** at the same size and count through the
identical pipeline: the PD(1) limit is *its* law, so it is the positive control for the
universality claim, and a smooth-vs-uniform difference in the collapse variable is the structural
signal. **Fixed-ω arm:** restrict to n with exactly ω prime factors; the collapse must then be a
one-dimensional function of η alone. A family that does not collapse at fixed ω has not found the
governing variable. **Shuffled-spectrum null:** replace each n's log-spectrum by an independent
PD(1) draw of the same length and re-measure; agreement supports universality, disagreement
localises what is special about smooth integers.

**Falsifier (reachable, numeric)** — **F10-a** the (u, λ) parameterisation collapses the family
better than `2^{ω}η` (residual smaller by ≥ 2×) → the PD framing is the wrong object and the entry
is retracted in favour of C1-1's. **F10-b** q at λ = O(1) and NIST-I-matched u is below 0.5 →
both this entry's optimism and C1-1's 13–15-bit claim are wrong together, and the correct
statement is the Ford log-power. **F10-c** the Golomb–Dickman gate fails → the spectrum
instrument is void and nothing is decided. **F10-d** the fixed-ω arm fails to collapse → ω is not
the right complexity parameter and the mechanism must be re-specified.

**Charged cost** — impl: low (~200 lines: smooth-integer enumeration with full factorisation at
D ≤ 2^{56} by sieve, subset-sum window test, collapse fitting). compute: ≤ 2 CPU-hours; peak
memory < 2 GB with streaming. The subset-sum test is exponential in ω in the worst case and is
charged as such: ω ≤ 20 at the tested D keeps 2^{ω} ≤ 10^6 per n, and the design caps ω rather
than silently dropping the tail — **a dropped tail would bias q upward**, the same direction as
the committed truncation bias, and is therefore forbidden. No isogeny, no curve, no SageMath, no
network, no oracle, no QRAM.

**Ceiling** — `medium`. A limit law plus a bit count at stated parameters. It cannot move the
exponent: `q ≤ 1` forces `M ≥ X² ≥ D = p^{1/3}` whatever q is, exactly as C1-1's method ceiling
already records.

**Risk band** — `representation_changing`. It replaces the integer by its normalised log-spectrum
and re-poses the split criterion as a random-partition functional, which changes what the
divisor-window measurement is a measurement *of*.

**Nearest prior-art neighbour** — C1-1
(`divisor_in_window_split|X=lambda*sqrt(D)_replaces_(BD)^{1/2}|ford_erdos_multiplication_table_
density|smooth_vs_uniform_integer_null|lambda=sqrtB_known_answer_gate|bits_only_exponent_
unchanged`).
**Why it is not that:** C1-1 tracks the integer and its divisor set and reads the window density
off the Erdős–Ford multiplication-table law, with (u, λ) as its variables; Q1-10 tracks the
**normalised log-spectrum** — a strictly coarser object that forgets the primes entirely — reads
the same event off the Poisson–Dirichlet / number-partitioning limit, predicts a **different
governing variable** (`2^{ω}η`), and makes a prediction that **can disagree with C1-1's registered
u-monotonicity tell** on the same data. Two mechanisms, one dataset, opposed collapses: that is
the discriminating structure, and the entry exists because C1-1's own artifact tell would
misread a PD-governed q as an artifact.
