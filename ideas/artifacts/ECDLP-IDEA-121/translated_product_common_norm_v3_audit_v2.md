## 1. Additive-line control versus elliptic divisor identity

For additive-line multisets `Y,Z`, the polynomial identity


is exact. It is an additive-group control, not a literal affine-coordinate formula for
elliptic addition.

On the elliptic curve, the coordinate-free effective divisor


coefficientwise-minimum multiplicity. But local equations for this divisor do not
automatically become the additive-line resultant above. A complete elliptic
realization must supply chart-specific addition-law or source-marked Semaev leaves,
track signs and return branches, and account for compatible line bundles/local
trivializations and their construction cost.

for the stated additive-line/represented-algebra control. The original P1513

## 4. Squarefree Newton and logarithmic-derivative mutation

For squarefree norms, a common simple root gives a double pole of

```text
(N_T'/N_T)*(N_F'/N_F).
```

At a root exclusive to one norm, the product has at most a simple pole and can be
removable when the other logarithmic derivative vanishes there. The producer's phrase
that exclusive roots remain simple is too strong.

The generic short-moment mutation can remain within the squarefree model. Let `M|D`,
`p>D`, and choose `a` with `a!=0,1`:

```text
f_X(U)=U^M-1,
R_0(U)=U^D-1,
R_1(U)=U^D-a.
```

Both `R_0` and `R_1` are squarefree and have identical root power sums through order
`D-1`, while

```text
gcd(f_X,R_0)=f_X,
gcd(f_X,R_1)=1.
```

Their logarithmic derivatives also agree at infinity through the short prefix. This
continues to prove only that generic short Newton/log-derivative data need an
additional special-family theorem; it is not asserted to be an elliptic norm pair.
