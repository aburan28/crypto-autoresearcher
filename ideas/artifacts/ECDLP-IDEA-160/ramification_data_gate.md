# IDEA-160 prime-to-p ramification-data theorem gate

Status:
`SCOPED_NEGATIVE_FUNCTORIAL_FULL_TOWER_RAMIFICATION_IS_GENERATOR_INVARIANT__ORIENTED_BRANCH_OPEN`

This is a theorem-only producer receipt. No contract, local-field tower,
precision experiment, scalar sample, or timing run was executed. It screens
the operation declared by ECDLP-IDEA-160: recovering scalar digits from the
upper/lower ramification breaks or field-of-norms data of a canonical public
tower attached to `Q=[x]P`.

The result is scoped to functorial towers built from the good-reduction
prime-to-residue-characteristic subgroup, its full torsion/division fibers,
or fields canonically generated by those objects. It does not claim that no
nonfunctorial marked branch can depend on `Q`. Such a branch must expose and
charge the orientation information that makes it noncanonical.

## Frozen interface

Let `K` be a complete discretely valued field with residue characteristic
`p`, valuation ring `O_K`, and residue field `k`. Let `E/K` have good
reduction. Let

```text
H = <P> subset E[N]
```

have prime order `N` with `N != p`. For one unknown
`x in Z/NZ`, let

```text
Q = [x]P.
```

The ECDLP case `x=0` is immediately recognizable from `Q=O`, so assume
`x != 0`. Then `x` is a unit modulo `N`, and

```text
<Q> = <P> = H.
```

All fields below are taken inside one fixed algebraic closure of `K`.

## Lemma 1: nonzero generators define the same field

Because `N` is prime and `x != 0`, there is `y` with

```text
x*y = 1 mod N.
```

The multiplication maps are defined over `K`, so

```text
Q = [x]P,
P = [y]Q.
```

Therefore

```text
K(P) = K(Q) = K(H).
```

This equality is exact, not merely an isomorphism. Any field invariant of the
extension generated by one nonzero subgroup point is independent of its
hidden scalar label.

## Lemma 2: good-reduction prime-to-p torsion is unramified

For every prime `ell != p`, the Neron-Ogg-Shafarevich criterion says that good
reduction makes the `ell`-adic Tate module unramified. In particular, inertia
acts trivially on `E[N^m]` for every `m>=1` because `N != p`.

Equivalently, the prime-to-`p` torsion group scheme extends as finite etale
torsion over `O_K`, and reduction identifies its geometric torsion without a
ramified formal-kernel contribution. Hence

```text
K(E[N^m])/K
```

is unramified for every `m`. Every positive upper or lower ramification group
of these finite layers is trivial. The infinite `N`-primary tower has no
scalar-sensitive wild or tame break attached to a chosen generator.

## Lemma 3: full division-fiber fields are generator invariant

Let `a>=1` be any public integer. Because `[a]` is a group homomorphism, a
nonempty full division fiber has the form

```text
[a]^(-1)(Q) = T_Q + E[a]
```

for any one preimage `T_Q`. If `gcd(a,N)=1`, multiplication by `a` is an
automorphism of `H`, so there is a unique `h_Q in H` satisfying

```text
[a]h_Q = Q.
```

Thus the full fiber is

```text
h_Q + E[a]
```

and its generated field is

```text
K([a]^(-1)(Q)) = K(H,E[a]),
```

which is independent of the nonzero generator `Q`.

This includes `a=p^m`: although the `p`-power torsion field may be ramified,
the ramification comes from `E[p^m]`, not from the scalar `x`. Multiplication
by `p^m` is still an automorphism of `H` because `N != p`.

For the `N`-primary fiber, every preimage is `N`-power torsion and lies in the
unramified tower from Lemma 2. Multiplication by `x` maps the full fiber above
`P` bijectively to the full fiber above `Q`; its inverse on `E[N^(m+1)]` is
multiplication by `x^(-1) mod N^(m+1)`. The two generated fields are therefore
equal. More generally, a tower of full fibers under a fixed public sequence of
multiplication maps is generated by `H` and the corresponding torsion fields.
Replacing `P` by any nonzero `[x]P` leaves that tower unchanged layer by layer.

## Theorem: functorial ramification data returns no scalar digit

Let `T(Q)` be a target-uniform tower whose finite layers are canonically
generated by any combination of:

1. the subgroup `H=<Q>`;
2. full torsion group schemes `E[a_m]`;
3. full division fibers `[a_m]^(-1)(Q)`; and
4. field operations or normal closures that are functorial in these data.

For every two nonzero generators `Q=[x]P` and `Q'=[x']P`, the preceding
lemmas give equality, or a canonical layerwise isomorphism, of `T(Q)` and
`T(Q')` over `K`. Therefore all of the following are identical:

- decomposition and inertia groups;
- upper and lower ramification filtrations and their breaks;
- residue degrees, ramification indices, and discriminant/conductor data;
- Herbrand functions; and
- field-of-norms objects and invariants functorially derived from the tower.

Consequently any invariant depending only on this ramification data is
constant on `H minus {O}`. It can distinguish the trivial scalar `x=0` from a
nonzero scalar, but it returns no digit of a generic `x in (Z/NZ)^*`.

## Why p-primary ramification is not an escape

The integer representative of `x mod N` has no canonical `p`-adic digit
expansion compatible with the subgroup law. Since `[p]` is an automorphism of
the order-`N` subgroup, every `Q in H` is uniquely `p^m`-divisible inside
`H`. Full `p^m`-division fibers differ only by translation by that subgroup
point and generate the common field `K(H,E[p^m])`.

Thus ramification in the connected/formal `p`-power torsion direction is
curve/tower data shared by every nonzero `Q`; it does not encode the integer
representative of the scalar.

## The only surviving exception: an oriented branch

A procedure may choose one point from a full division fiber rather than the
full field. A branch can vary with `Q`, but then the theorem obligation moves
to the selector:

- it must be canonical and target uniform;
- it must commute with changes of presentation and subgroup generator;
- it must not use an `N`-torsion basis, scalar label, or post-hoc branch;
- its selected-point field, precision, and ambiguity must be charged; and
- its output must return a typed residue in `Z/NZ`, not merely a local-field
  label whose inversion is another DLP.

If the selector is functorial under the automorphisms `[x]` of `H`, the tower
is again generator-isomorphic and its pure ramification data is unchanged.
If it is not functorial, the missing orientation is the new operation and the
ramification break is only a wrapper around it.

This oriented-branch possibility is not disproved here. No explicit public
selector satisfying its obligations is supplied by IDEA-160.

## Cost disposition

The frozen functorial invariant is constant, so its digit density is zero and
its query exponent is not a useful algorithmic cost. Repeating the same
invariant at greater precision or deeper tower levels cannot create scalar
information absent from the layerwise fields.

Constructing a full `N`-torsion orientation or an `N`-dimensional labelled
tower has time or state exponent at least one in `N`, already above rho. A
typed branch selector with a smaller representation remains an open new
mechanism and must be costed independently; correctness of one lifted point
would not establish full scalar recovery.

## Disposition for ECDLP-IDEA-160

The declared functorial ramification-break operation is scoped negative:

- nonzero `P` and `[x]P` generate the same subgroup and field;
- prime-to-`p` torsion at good reduction is unramified;
- full `N`-primary and `p`-primary division-fiber towers are generator
  invariant; and
- field-of-norms and ramification invariants of those towers are constant on
  nonzero scalar orbits.

Independent review should either accept this scoped rejection or identify one
explicit nonfunctorial but publicly canonical oriented branch with a typed
scalar law. No tower should be implemented or timed before that operation is
written down.

No generic-prime ECDLP recovery, relation campaign, below-rho algorithm,
Shoup-bound improvement, or breakthrough is established.

## Independent review checklist

An independent reviewer should verify:

1. `K(P)=K([x]P)` for every nonzero `x mod N`;
2. good reduction and `N != p` imply unramified `N`-adic Tate-module action;
3. a full `a`-division fiber is a torsor under `E[a]`;
4. for `gcd(a,N)=1`, the fiber field is `K(H,E[a])` independently of the
   generator;
5. the `p`-primary case does not confuse the integer representative of `x`
   with the scalar class modulo `N`;
6. field-of-norms data is used only functorially on the common tower; and
7. the nonfunctorial oriented-branch exception is not silently closed.
