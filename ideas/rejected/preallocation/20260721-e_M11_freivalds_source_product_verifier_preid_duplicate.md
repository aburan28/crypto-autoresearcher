## Falsifiable hypothesis

For generic prime-order `E(F_p)`, one can encode batched source-incidence propagation as a matrix product and use Freivalds random-vector checks to reject invalid blocks, then self-reduce an accepted block to one signed relation. The target-uniform operation supports exact arbitrary restrictions, at least `max(d_FB+32,1000)` verified independent rows of rank `d_FB`, complete factor logs, and 100 fresh blind descents with complete time and live-memory exponents below rho and BSGS.

## Mechanism-new operation

Freivalds verification checks whether supplied matrices satisfy a claimed product using random vectors. It counts only if matrices and the claimed product are endpoint-derived below the caps and verification yields an exact occurrence inverse rather than a relation-only certificate; applying the native method to a supplied graph, sequence, matrix, distribution, score array, or source catalogue is a duplicate/control.

## Assumptions

1. Compact endpoint-derived matrices encode all and only complete signed source transitions.
2. The claimed product is constructed without doing the dense/source-sized computation being verified.
3. Accepted restricted products are biconditional with exact nonempty source fibres at total error below 2^-80.
4. Self-reduction and repeated independent checks recover occurrence labels with fully charged randomness.
5. Matrix construction, verification, output, rank, logs, blind descent, bit time, and memory meet the gates.

## Likely fatal obstruction

Freivalds verifies a claimed product; it neither constructs the matrices/product nor returns a factor/source of that computation. Building faithful transition matrices already encodes source incidence, and repeated restricted verification is only a one-sided relation certificate until an occurrence inverse is supplied. This is explicitly a relation-only certificate/solver control.
