## Falsifiable hypothesis

For a declared family of ordinary prime-field curves, attach to each public point `R` an
object `M_R` in a neutral Tannakian category generated by a bounded public tensor object.
Assume elliptic addition is carried to tensor product and a bounded representation of the
Tannaka group splits `M_R` into one-dimensional weights whose labels are biconditional with
signed factor-base points. A public inverse from weights to points would factor known
`R_j=[r_j]P` and blind `Q+[t]P` into exact five-source tuples in complete query exponent
`alpha<3/2`, yielding full-rank factor logarithms and charged blind descent below rho.

This record is rejected and merged at the weight-inverse boundary. Current Tannakian
reconstruction recovers a group from a supplied tensor category; it does not supply a
scalar-blind point-weight inverse for a prime-order elliptic subgroup.

## Mechanism-new operation

The proposed operation is **reconstruct a bounded Tannaka group from public point-sheaf
tensor data, split the tensor object of a target sum into one-dimensional source weights,
and invert each weight to an exact factor-base point**. The inverse must be defined before
relation collection, preserve signs, multiplicities, repeated points, and infinity, and
expose every weight-kernel ambiguity.

Taking traces or characters after a relation is known, decomposing an explicitly tabulated
incidence representation, replacing factor logs by weights, or using a pairing/isogeny to
orient the same cyclic subgroup is a duplicate or control. Only a public source inverse
that removes the recorded source-generation obstruction would be mechanism-new.

## Assumptions

1. `E(F_p)` contains a public prime-order subgroup `<P>` of order `N=p^(1+o(1))`, target
   `Q=[x]P`, fixed arity five, and target-independent sign-canonical factor base
   `F={F_1,...,F_B}` with `B=L=N^ell`.
2. A neutral Tannakian category, tensor generator, fiber functor, point objects `M_R`, and
   bounded Tannaka-group representation are public and uniform over the declared family.
3. `M_{R+S}` is computed from public point data and is tensor-compatible without knowing
   scalar labels or first enumerating source tuples.
4. The relevant representation splits into source-labelled one-dimensional weights and a
   public inverse maps every accepted weight to exact factor-base points.
5. Weight collisions, nonsemisimplicity, field extensions, torsors, signs, repeated points,
   infinity, and all source ambiguity are enumerated and independently verified.
6. Category construction, tensor operations, decomposition, output, misses, rank, linear
   algebra, blind descent, verification, coefficient bit sizes, and peak memory are charged.
7. All finite observations remain toy, heuristic, model-bound, and novelty-unverified.

## Complete factor-base-to-target-descent path

1. Freeze `E,P,N,F,B=L`, point objects, tensor generator, fiber functor, Tannaka group,
   bounded representation, weight normalization, exceptional branches, and an independent
   elliptic source verifier.
2. Construct the target-independent tensor category and certify that point objects and
   weight labels use public point data only; record all torsors, extensions, kernels, and
   coincident weights.
3. For known public `R_j=[r_j]P`, form `M_{R_j}`, perform the frozen tensor factorization,
   invert every accepted weight word to exact signed factor-base points, and independently
   verify each five-point elliptic sum.
4. Preserve misses, duplicate weight words, and ambiguities; collect exactly `B+sigma`
   verified source rows whose coefficient matrix has rank `B` modulo `N`.
5. Solve every factor-base logarithm and independently verify
   `[log_P(F_i)]P=F_i` for all `i`.
6. Freeze all categorical state, choose fresh public masks `t`, and apply the identical
   factorization and source inverse to blind targets `Q+[t]P`.
7. Substitute verified factor logs, subtract `t`, enumerate every kernel-derived scalar
   candidate, and accept only `x` satisfying `[x]P=Q`.

## Full rho/BSGS cost model

Pollard rho costs `N^(1/2+o(1))` expected group operations with constant-state memory;
BSGS costs `N^(1/2+o(1))` time and memory. Set `B=L=N^ell`. Let category, generator,
representation, and weight-inverse setup cost `L^(s+o(1))` time and `L^(s_m+o(1))`
peak memory. Let one complete point-object construction, factorization, all source output,
kernel enumeration, and verification cost `L^(alpha+o(1))` time and
`L^(m_q+o(1))` memory.

Unless a theorem proves a changed density, use `pi=min(1,L^5/N)`. In the sparse regime,

`T_rel=N*L^(alpha-4+o(1))`

and

`T_desc=N*L^(alpha-5+o(1))`.

Sparse linear algebra costs `L^(2+o(1))` time and at least `L^(1+o(1))` memory. Thus

`lambda=max(s*ell,1+(alpha-4)*ell,2*ell,1+(alpha-5)*ell)`

and

`mu=max(s_m*ell,m_q*ell,ell)`

These are the complete time and peak-memory exponent bounds for the declared path.

For `ell=1/5`, strict time below rho requires `alpha<3/2`; the conservative promotion
gate `lambda,mu<=0.45` requires `alpha<=1.25`, `s,s_m,m_q<=2.25`, and all output to fit
those bounds. Torsor splitting fields, representation dimension, weight multiplicity,
coefficient bit lengths, failed factorizations, emitted sources, `B+sigma` rows, factor
logs, and verifier work are charged rather than treated as free categorical notation.

## Likely fatal obstruction

Tannakian reconstruction recovers an affine group scheme from an already specified tensor
category, but its irreducible weights need not correspond to elliptic source points.
One-dimensional weights see only an abelian quotient and tensor products forget the
ordered source provenance. Any faithful public weight on a prime-order cyclic subgroup is
effectively a character/orientation of that subgroup; evaluating and inverting it can be
the original DLP or require an `N`-scale table. Quotienting enough to obtain bounded
representations is likely to identify distinct source tuples.

## Proof track

Construct the point-sheaf tensor category uniformly, prove addition/tensor compatibility,
bounded Tannaka representation, source-weight biconditional, exact public inverse, and the
complete seven-step rank, factor-log, blind-descent, output, and memory bounds.

## Disproof track

Prove every public one-dimensional weight is trivial or scalar-equivalent, exhibit two
different source multisets with the same complete weight word, show inversion needs the
DLP or `Omega(N)` advice, or derive complete time or peak-memory exponent at least `1/2`.

## Positive and negative controls

- Positive categorical control: a finite diagonalizable group with a known fiber functor,
  labelled one-dimensional representations, and exact public weight inversion.
- Positive EC control: exhaustive tiny curves with planted point-object labels hidden from
  the factorization code and every exceptional elliptic branch enabled.
- Negative controls: nonsemisimple representations, quotient categories that merge point
  objects, and equal characters with different source decompositions.
- Mechanism controls: Weil/Tate pairings, same-field isogenies, Prym transfer, transposed
  factor matrices, generic tensor decomposition, and post-hoc weights on known relations.
- Leakage control: permute hidden scalar labels while preserving point/category data; all
  output must follow points and never the scalar permutation.
- Baseline control: matched Pollard rho and memory-matched BSGS.

## Quantitative promotion and falsification gates

This rejected lane cannot reopen without a theorem proving the point/tensor and
weight/source biconditionals, a public exact inverse, and symbolic `lambda,mu<=0.45`. A
future toy preflight must cover at least 20 ordinary curves per size across four increasing
sizes, exhaustive source truth through 18 bits, at least `1,000` verified relations and
`100` blind descents at each of the two largest sizes, exactly `B+sigma` retained rows of
rank `B`, zero source errors, and upper 95% bounds `lambda<=0.45` and `mu<=0.45` including
all categorical setup and output. Falsify on one stable weight collision without a public
disambiguator, scalar-label leakage, or a proved or lower-95% complete bound
`lambda>=0.50` or `mu>=0.50`.
