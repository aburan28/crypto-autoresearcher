## Falsifiable hypothesis

For a target-independent public factor base `F` of size `B=L=N^ell` in a public
prime-order subgroup `<P>` of order `N`, a density-normalized source indicator admits a
sparse-transference higher-order uniformity excess, such as a nonvanishing `U^4` signal,
with a constructively recoverable bounded-complexity nilspace factor. That factor has an
exact cube-completion inverse: target data and a bounded number of public cube faces
determine all signed factor-base indices of a five-point decomposition without
enumerating the dense pair or triple surfaces. The inverse yields `B+sigma` exact rows of
rank `B`, verified factor logs, and blind `Q+[t]P` descent with fully charged time,
output, and memory exponents below `1/2`.

The present record is rejected and merged because nearby inverse theorems and aggregate
phase observations provide correlation or counting structure, not source unranking. It
could reopen only with the exact inverse operation stated above.

## Mechanism-new operation

The proposed operation is **recover a certified higher-step nilspace factor from sparse
elliptic source incidence and use its unique cube-completion law to unrank exact endpoint
sources**. The completion must be a biconditional: every accepted cube word returns the
complete factor-base tuple, and every returned tuple verifies on `E`.

A large Gowers norm, heavy Fourier coefficient, quadratic phase, nilsequence correlation,
character bucket, approximate sampler, count oracle, or post-hoc selector is an aggregate
control and merges into the recorded no-unranking boundary. Changing phase families or
estimators is not mechanism-new. Only an exact, scalar-blind nilspace-to-source inverse
that removes the membership surface could distinguish this proposal.

## Assumptions

1. `E(F_p)` contains public prime-order `<P>` of order `N=p^(1+o(1))`, target
   `Q=[x]P`, fixed arity five, and public target-independent factor base
   `F={F_1,...,F_B}` with `B=L=N^ell`.
2. A declared sparse-transference measure normalizes the vanishing density of `F` without
   using scalar labels, target-selected samples, or an oracle-injected nilsequence.
3. The higher-order norm and recovered nilfactor are computed with all samples,
   coefficient precision, exceptional branches, and unsuccessful learning attempts
   charged.
4. Cube completion is exact over the finite group and returns source indices, signs, and
   multiplicities rather than only a target sum, count, phase, or distribution.
5. The nilfactor and completion rule are frozen before known-scalar relation collection
   and reused unchanged for blind target descent.
6. Relation independence, factor-log solution, target ambiguity, verification, written
   output, and peak bit memory are fully charged.
7. Every finite observation remains toy, heuristic, model-bound, and novelty-unverified.

## Complete factor-base-to-target-descent path

1. Freeze `E,P,N,F,B=L`, the sparse-transference majorant, norm order, cube sampling,
   nilfactor class, coefficient precision, completion rule, and independent source truth.
2. Certify the higher-order excess on a target-independent training distribution and
   recover one bounded nilspace factor under matched random, shuffled, and linear-phase
   controls.
3. For known public `R_j=[r_j]P`, construct target cube data, apply the exact completion
   inverse, recover factor-base indices/signs, and independently verify every elliptic
   sum; preserve all failed, ambiguous, and aggregate-only completions.
4. Collect exactly `B+sigma` verified sparse rows of rank `B` modulo `N`, with no row
   admitted from a count or phase certificate alone.
5. Solve all factor-base logarithms and independently verify
   `[log_P(F_i)]P=F_i` for every factor-base point.
6. Freeze the nilfactor, choose fresh public masks `t`, and apply the identical completion
   and unranking process to blind targets `Q+[t]P`.
7. Substitute verified logs, remove `t`, preserve all scalar candidates, and accept only
   `x` for which `[x]P=Q`.

## Full rho/BSGS cost model

Pollard rho has time exponent `1/2` and constant-state memory; BSGS has time and memory
exponents `1/2`. Set `B=L=N^ell`. Let sparse-transference/nilfactor setup cost
`L^(s+o(1))` time and `L^(s_m+o(1))` peak memory. Let one complete cube query, including
norm evaluation, nilsequence arithmetic, every completion, source unranking, written
output, and verification, cost `L^(alpha+o(1))` time and `L^(m_q+o(1))` memory. Any
`L^z` emitted candidate list forces `alpha>=z` and contributes `z` to storage unless
streamed and independently verified.

Using the P1476 random-support probability `pi=min(1,L^5/N)`, the sparse-regime costs are

`T_rel=N*L^(alpha-4+o(1))`,

for `Theta(B)` accepted rows, and

`T_desc=N*L^(alpha-5+o(1))`

for one blind target. Dense-regime costs are `L^(1+alpha+o(1))` and
`L^(alpha+o(1))`. Sparse linear algebra costs `L^(2+o(1))` time and at least
`L^(1+o(1))` memory; `B+sigma` rows, factor logs, cube certificates, and every accepted
source are explicit output.

The sparse-regime exponents are

`lambda=max(s*ell,1+(alpha-4)*ell,2*ell,1+(alpha-5)*ell)`

and

`mu=max(s_m*ell,m_q*ell,ell)`.

Conditionally, `alpha<=1` gives optimized time `2/(6-alpha)`; for
`1<alpha<3/2`, `ell=1/5` gives `max(2/5,(1+alpha)/5)<1/2`. Estimating a norm or
learning a nilsequence by `N^(1/2)` samples, storing a dense cube table, or enumerating
all completions is charged at its full exponent.

## Likely fatal obstruction

Inverse theorems conclude correlation with a structured phase, not a unique inverse from
phase values to summand labels. A sparse algebraic factor base may be higher-order
pseudorandom after proper transference, and any useful nilcoordinate on the prime cyclic
group may require the unknown scalar parametrization. Even a genuine excess can therefore
leave exact witness search at the original birthday or dense-incidence cost.

## Proof track

Prove sparse transference, recover the nilfactor constructively below rho, prove a unique
exact cube/source biconditional with bounded output, and prove the complete seven-step
rank, factor-log, blind-descent, time, and memory path.

## Disproof track

Show that all held-out higher-order excess vanishes under matched nulls, construct two
source tuples with identical nilspace data, prove that exact completion requires scalar
coordinates or dense tables, or derive complete time or memory exponent at least `1/2`.

## Positive and negative controls

- Positive higher-order control: planted quadratic and cubic nilsequences with known
  exact cube-completion tables and blinded source labels.
- Positive EC control: exhaustive tiny five-source relations whose tuples are hidden from
  the completion code until verification.
- Negative controls: matched random subsets, shuffled factor labels, linear characters,
  multiplicative-character buckets, and random features with identical density.
- Mechanism controls: aggregate `U^s` estimators, heavy Fourier/phase selectors, count
  oracles, approximate samplers, and explicit source dictionaries.
- Leakage control: independently permute scalar labels while preserving point-coordinate
  and nilsequence marginals.
- Baseline control: Pollard rho and memory-matched BSGS under the same group-operation
  accounting.

## Quantitative promotion and falsification gates

The rejected lane cannot reopen without a theorem proving sparse transference, an exact
source-unranking cube law, and symbolic `lambda,mu<=0.45`. A future toy preflight would
require at least 20 ordinary curves per size across four increasing sizes, exhaustive
truth through 18 bits, `1,000` verified relations and `100` blind descents at each of the
two largest sizes, exactly `B+sigma` retained rows of rank `B`, zero source errors, and
upper 95% bounds `lambda<=0.45` and `mu<=0.45`. Falsify the mechanism on one stable
aggregate-only collision or if the lower 95% bound gives `alpha>=3/2`,
`lambda>=0.50`, or `mu>=0.50`.
