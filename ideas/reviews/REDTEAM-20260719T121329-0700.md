## Frozen interface and complete-path boundary

The weakest sufficient P1553 R4 interface is the subset-stable exact bit represented by
`z_R(T)=gcd(g_I(T),r_R(T))`, or an equivalent endpoint-derived operation, on every
signed identity/infinity/vertical/tangent/secant/repeated/nonreduced stratum. It must be
available under arbitrary dyadic restrictions without assuming `r_R mod g_I` or a
source catalogue. Charged positive-parent/negative-child bisection and singleton
verification may then recover one labelled occurrence tuple; a direct source inverse is
not required.

Every prospect correctly states the conditional downstream path: freeze target-
independent state; answer restricted existence and replay a verified five-source row;
collect at least `B` independent rows; solve factor-base logs; reuse the same state on a
fresh scalar-blind `Q+[t]P`; remove `t`; and verify `[x]P=Q`. None supplies the first
exact operation, so correctness of its native transform or a valid toy relation does not
reach row rank, factor logs, blind descent, or scalar recovery.

For `B=N^beta`, `beta=1/5`, the common model is

`lambda=max(a,beta+delta+q-r+o,ell,delta_t+q+o+u,beta)`

`mu=max(a_m,q_m,beta+o,ell_m,u)`, with `0<=r<=o`.

Setup/advice/state is capped at `B^(9/4+o(1))`; **total** fresh online restriction,
negative-branch, bisection, source replay, and verification work/workspace is capped at
`B^(5/4+o(1))`. Promotion requires `lambda,mu<=0.45`; rho and BSGS remain matched
`0.50` baselines. The explicit routes called out below are cost controls, not
unrestricted lower bounds.

Compatibility edges are the missing incidence, and perfect matchability is not inherently five-way group equality. A natural `2+3` explicit encoding contains a `B^3` side or equivalent target-dependent incidence, already above `B^(9/4)`; decomposition time starts only after that input exists. | Expand one edge predicate to endpoint operations, then use a tiny graph whose matching status persists while the planted five-sum is removed. Charge graph construction, not just DM traversal. |
| `A03` Szemeredi regularity | Reject/merge, high-risk. Regularity preserves dense statistics within an epsilon error, not a rare exact singleton. On `Theta(B)`-sized cells, fixed epsilon permits `Theta(epsilon B^2)` edge uncertainty, asymptotically larger than the exact relation signal; forcing sub-unit error destroys the claimed bounded quotient, while constructing the incidence graph is already source-bearing. | Plant/delete one compatibility edge inside an otherwise epsilon-regular pair and verify that the quotient is unchanged although exact restricted existence flips. |

There is no worst-case `B^(9/4)` node bound or `B^(5/4)` all-negative replay bound. | Freeze one variable order, switch to a fresh blind target, and charge every membership call and node needed to add or remove a single accepting tuple. |

Input construction or dense/no-separator cases consume the omitted cost. | Give the smallest compatibility graph with identical SPQR/triconnected summary before and after one exact-relation edge change, or expand every edge constructor and charge it. |

| `A07` Rees factors | Reject/merge, high-risk. Rees matrix coordinates describe a supplied simple or zero-simple semigroup/factor; they do not construct the partial-transition semigroup, sandwich entries, or labelled ancestry. In the prime-order commutative quotient the group coordinate is either unchanged or loses source occurrence identity; explicit partial-transition tables restore `B^2/B^3` traffic before classification. | Relabel two histories with the same semigroup element and require the same Rees coordinates to return distinct occurrences; charge construction of every multiplication and sandwich entry. |

| `A09` dancing links | Reject/merge, conservative. Dancing links reversibly implements backtracking over a supplied exact-cover matrix. ## Independent primary-literature scope check

The cited primary sources support the native operations but not the missing ECDLP
interface:

- Tverberg's [original theorem](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-41.1.123) concerns
  partitions of supplied points in real Euclidean space whose convex hulls intersect.
- Dulmage and Mendelsohn's [paper](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1958-052-0)
  canonically reduces a supplied bipartite graph through coverings and matchability.
- Szemeredi's *Regular Partitions of Graphs* is a structural approximation theorem for
  a supplied graph. Calling the 1978 result an efficient exact `energy-increment
  regularization` algorithm overstates the cited source; the correction only weakens
  `A03`.
- Minato's [ZDD paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/157485.164890) gives set-family
  manipulation rules and favorable compression, not a worst-case compact constructor
  for an unknown accepting family.
- Hopcroft and Tarjan's [paper](https://doi.org/10.1137/0202012) gives `O(V+E)` time and
  space after a graph is supplied. The cited source is triconnected-component
  decomposition; the modern `SPQR` tree terminology/packaging is later. This does not
  supply `A05`'s graph or biconditional.
- Ferragina and Manzini's [paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/SFCS.2000.892127) indexes an
  explicit text with space tied to that text's empirical entropy.
- Rees's [paper](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100017436) represents supplied
  completely simple/zero-simple semigroups by matrix data. It is not an efficient
  algorithm for constructing a partial-source semigroup or recovering histories.
- George's [paper](https://doi.org/10.1137/0710032) analyzes factorization ordering for
  a structured regular finite-element mesh and its supplied sparse matrix.
- Knuth's [Dancing Links](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0011047) is a reversible data-
  structure implementation of backtracking over a supplied exact-cover instance.
- Clarke et al.'s [CEGAR paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/10722167_15) refines an
  abstraction using counterexamples checked against a supplied concrete model.
- Fredman, Komlos, and Szemeredi's [static-dictionary paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/828.1884)
  supplies constant worst-case access after the sparse key set is given.
- Aho and Corasick's [paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/360825.360855) constructs a finite
  state matcher from supplied keywords and processes a supplied text.

Semaev's [summation-polynomial paper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/031) gives polynomial
endpoint equations and an index-calculus framing; it does not provide the restricted
finite-deck exact predicate or labelled source replay. Shoup's
[generic lower bound](https://www.shoup.net/papers/dlbounds1.pdf) remains only the
matched generic-group baseline. No checked source gives a complete factor-base-to-
blind-target descent for any prospect. Global novelty was not established; every
prospective claim remains `novelty-unverified`, and any finite check remains `toy`,
`heuristic`, and `model-bound` as labelled.
