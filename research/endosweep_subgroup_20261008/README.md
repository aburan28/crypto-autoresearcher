# Subgroup membership and cofactor clearing with chain endomorphisms

**Question.**  The endomorphism sweep ([`../endosweep_curves_20261006`](../endosweep_curves_20261006/README.md))
found a cheap chain endomorphism on CP6-782 `G1`: `α = 4 + ω` with
`D_K = −339`, class number 6, norm 105, evaluated as a walk of 7-, 3- and
5-isogenies for 65 M_eq.  That curve has a 377-bit prime subgroup `G` and a
406-bit cofactor `h`.  Can `α` make the two cofactor operations cheaper?

* **Membership**: is `Q ∈ G`?  The baseline is `[r]Q = O`.
* **Cofactor clearing**: map `E(F_p)` onto `G`.  The baseline is `[h]Q`.

The handed-in idea uses the `O`-module structure of `E(F_p)`.  A test or a
clearing map becomes a fixed element `β = c0 + c1·α` of about half the
bit length, evaluated as a 2-D multi-scalar multiplication plus one chain.
The prediction was 1.40–1.50× fewer operations than `[r]Q` and `[h]Q`.  The
kill criterion was short vectors more than 8 bits over half length, or a
test that fails on points.

Everything here is generated by one command, which takes about 13 minutes
on one core under `nice` (CP6-782's 1 613 points take most of it):

```sh
python -m harness.endosweep.subgroup --out-dir research/endosweep_subgroup_20261008
```

It reads `research/endosweep_curves_20261006/arkworks/curves.json` (CP6-782,
BLS12-381, Bandersnatch) and [`inputs.json`](inputs.json).  `inputs.json`
holds mnt1's constants and the cofactors of the 17 chain curves, copied from
J08nY/std-curves at commit `77fe6e35` (the commit the sweep used), with the
URL and SHA-256 of each file read.  python-flint 0.9.0 is needed for the
torsion counts.

## The algebra (what is used, what is checked)

Lenstra, "Complex multiplication structure of elliptic curves", J. Number
Theory 56 (1996), as handed to us (the paper was not re-read here,
*unverified citation*): if `End(E) = O`, then `E(F_q) ≅ O/(π − 1)` as
`O`-modules.  On CP6-782 the chain `4 + ω` is in `End(E)`, so
`End(E) ⊇ Z[4 + ω] = O_K`, which means `End(E) = O_K`.

Write `I = (π − 1) = 𝔯·J`, where `𝔯` is the prime of norm `r` and `J` is
the cofactor part.  `r ∤ h`, so `𝔯 + J = O`.  The theorem gives three
consequences, which `harness/endosweep/subgroup.py` turns into exact
integer computations:

1. **Kernel size.**  For any `β ∈ O`, `|ker β on E(F_p)| = [O : βO + I]`.
   This index is the gcd of the 2×2 minors of `{β, βω, π−1, (π−1)ω}`.
2. **Membership test.**  `β` is a valid test if and only if `β ∈ 𝔯` and the
   index is exactly `r`: then `ker β = G`.  This holds because `𝔯` is the
   only ideal of index `r` between `I` and `O`.  The brief's condition
   `gcd(N(β)/r, h) = 1` is sufficient but not necessary.
3. **Clearing map.**  `β` is a valid clearing map if and only if the index
   is exactly `h`, which means `βO + I = J`.
   * Then `β(E(F_p)) = J/I = G` (onto), with fibres that are cosets of a
     fixed kernel.
   * On every point, `β(Q) = [c·h⁻¹ mod r]([h]Q)`, where `c` is the scalar
     by which `β` acts on `G`.
   * The least positive integer in `J` is the exponent `e` of the cofactor
     group `O/J`.  It is the smallest integer "effective cofactor".

The rest of the method:

* **Frobenius.**  `π` is written in `O` from `t` and the conductor.  Its
  sign is pinned by requiring that it act as 1 on `G`.
* **Short elements.**  LLL is run on the Z-bases of `𝔯` and `J`, written in
  coordinates `c0 + c1·α` for the evaluated `α`.  The box `|i|, |j| ≤ 4`
  over the reduced basis is searched.  Of the 12 shortest valid elements,
  the cheapest under the cost model is kept.

Every prediction is **checked on points** against the definitions:

* Membership is compared with `[r]Q = O`.
* The clearing map and the integer `e` are compared with
  `[c·h⁻¹]([h]Q)`.  Since `[h]Q ∈ G`, matching it puts the image in `G`, and
  the image is `O` exactly when `Q` has no `G`-component.
* For every small `ℓ | h`, the module's prediction
  `#E(F_p)[ℓ] = [O : ℓO + I]` is compared with an exact count from the
  roots of the ℓ-division polynomial.

Points tested on each curve:

* random points of `E(F_p)`;
* members `[h]R`;
* cofactor-only points `[r]R`;
* mixed points `[h]R + [r]S`;
* points of each small prime order dividing `h`;
* `O`.

**Costs are modelled, not timed.**  They use the cross-curve sweep's
formula counts (`curvesweep`):

* the curve's own Jacobian doubling, plus add-2007-bl and madd-2007-bl;
* interleaved width-w NAF, using the exact digits of each fixed scalar,
  with the best width (2–7) and the better of affine and Jacobian tables;
* the optimised chain evaluator for `α`.

Every figure is in `M_eq = M + S`.

## Findings

1. **The positive control reproduces the known BLS12-381 `G1` test and its
   effective cofactor from the module structure alone.**
   * The arkworks crate at the pinned commit (`bls12_381/src/curves/g1.rs`,
     whose SHA-256 `3e22b089…` is the one recorded in the sweep's
     `arkworks/curves.json`) implements the known test.  It cites "Section 6
     of https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/1130" (Scott) and checks "that
     endomorphism_p(P) == -[X^2]P", with `endomorphism_p(x, y) = (BETA·x, y)`.
     Its `clear_cofactor` multiplies by `1 − x` and cites "Section 5 of
     https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/403.pdf".  These quotes are from the
     source code; neither paper was read here.  No note on either paper, nor
     on Bowe's or El Housni–Guillevic–Piellard's results, was found in
     `knowledge/literature/`, so nothing is quoted from them.
   * **What the derivation gives.**
     * The arkworks `BETA` acts on `G1` as `−u²`.
     * `u² + φ` lies in `𝔯` and has norm exactly `r = u⁴ − u² + 1`, so its
       kernel on `E(F_p)` is exactly `G1`.
     * The cheapest valid membership element found by LLL is `−(u² + φ)`.
       The shortest by height, `−1 + (u² − 1)φ`, is a unit multiple of it.
     * `J` contains `1 − u`, and `1 − u` is not in `𝔯`.  It is the least
       positive integer in `J`, because the cofactor group is
       `Z/((1−u)/3) × Z/(1−u)`.  That is `h_eff = 1 − u`.  No 2-D element
       of `J` beats it under the model.
   * **The early-out is not needed.**  Points of order 3 satisfy
     `[u]T = T`, which is the case arkworks' "early-out optimization"
     catches.  The plain test `φ(P) = −u²P` rejects them anyway, because the
     kernel index is exactly `r`.  This is checked in the tests and on 3
     points of order 3 in the run.
   * The modelled 2.07× treats `u²` as a generic 128-bit scalar.  arkworks
     computes `[X]([X]P)` with the sparse `X`, which is cheaper still; that
     is not modelled here.
2. **CP6-782: the prediction holds for membership and is slightly beaten
   for clearing.  The kill criterion is not met.**
   * **Membership.**  `𝔯` is principal, with a generator of norm exactly `r`
     and 187 bits, which is 1.4 bits *under* `log2(r)/2`.  So the gcd
     condition holds trivially, and the test costs a 187-bit 2-D MSM plus one
     chain: 2 982 against 4 301 M_eq, **1.44×** (modelled).
   * **Clearing.**  `J`'s shortest valid elements have 203 bits, 1.5 over
     `log2(h)/2`.  A 204-bit element with cheaper digits costs 3 156 M_eq,
     against 4 806 for `[h]Q` (**1.52×**) and 4 768 for the effective cofactor
     `e = h/6` (403 bits, **1.51×**).
   * The ratios match the curve's GLV-2 ratio (1.494 in the sweep): both
     replace a full-length scalar with two half-length ones at the cost of
     one 65 M_eq chain.
   * The factorisation of `h` was not needed.  Validity is decided by the
     exact index `[O : βO + (π − 1)O]`, which needs only integer gcds.  Trial
     division gives `h = 2²·3²·7·29·77813084807·(357-bit cofactor)` (the
     357-bit factor is not proven prime).  It is used only to build points of
     order 2, 3, 7 and 29.
3. **On Bandersnatch (`h = 4`) a residue test dominates, and clearing has
   nothing to gain.**
   * `E(F_p)[2^∞] = Z/2 × Z/2`: the module predicts
     `#E[2] = #E[4] ∩ E(F_p) = 4`, and the cubic has three rational roots.
     Hence `G = 2E(F_p)`, and `Q ∈ G` if and only if `x − e₁` and `x − e₂`
     are both squares (2-descent; checked on 200 points with no error).
   * By Fermat exponentiation that is 2 Legendre symbols, 770 M_eq (modelled).
     That is 4.2× cheaper than `[r]Q` (3 258) and 2.8× cheaper than the
     `√−2` β-test (2 162).  A faster Legendre-symbol algorithm would widen
     the gap; none is modelled.
   * Clearing is `[2]Q`, the exponent of `Z/2 × Z/2`.
   * Decaf (Hamburg; note KN-LIT-3358, abstract-level only) "effectively
     divides the curve's cofactor by 4 at very little cost to performance"
     for Edwards/Montgomery cofactor-4 curves, removing the membership
     question at the encoding level.  Whether it applies to Bandersnatch's
     parameters was not checked.
   * The arkworks Bandersnatch crate at the pinned commit
     (`ed_on_bls12_381_bandersnatch/src/curves/mod.rs`, SHA-256 `2d4008e6…`)
     does not override the subgroup check.  What the ark-ec default does was
     not checked.
   * All Bandersnatch counts are on its short Weierstrass model.
4. **Among the 17 chain curves, CP6-782 is the only one where this
   matters.**
   * 14 of the 17 have `h = 1`: the GOST pair, mnt2–mnt5 and the three Tom
     curves.  There, membership is the on-curve check, and there is nothing
     to clear.
   * mnt1 has `h = 15337 = 7²·313`, cyclic.  The β-test is 1.54× cheaper
     than `[r]Q` (modelled), but clearing saves only 58 M_eq.
   * Bandersnatch is covered by finding 3.
   * CP6-782 combines a 406-bit cofactor with a 65 M_eq endomorphism.  Both
     operations gain about 1.5×, i.e. 1 300–1 650 M_eq each.
   * Outside the chain list, the `D_K = −3` curves with large cofactors
     (BW6-761/767 `G1`, the BLS `G1` groups) have the free unit `ζ₃`, and
     finding 1 shows the same machinery applies there.  They were not swept
     here.

## Scope

* **Modelled operation counts, not timings.**  Every cost is a formula
  count on the exact digits of a fixed scalar.  The chain counts are the
  optimised evaluator that aburan28/crypto#1423 confirmed on CryptoPro-B,
  and are not measured on CP6-782.  Correctness is measured on points.
* Variable-time, affine-input evaluation, as in the sweep.  The tests assume
  `Q` has already been checked to lie on `E(F_p)`.
* A fixed `β` has short fixed coefficients.  An addition chain or JSF could
  do better than the interleaved wNAF modelled here, but this is not
  explored.
* Lenstra's theorem is used as handed in (*unverified citation*).  Its
  consequences are checked on points and against exact torsion counts, not
  re-proved from the paper.
* No ECDLP or security claim, and no ledger, knowledge or coordination record.
