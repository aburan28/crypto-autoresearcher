# Endomorphism sweep across curves: every standardized and arkworks curve

**Question.**  The chain sweep ([`../endosweep_chainsweep_20261006`](../endosweep_chainsweep_20261006/README.md))
found the cheapest endomorphism of one curve, GOST CryptoPro-B (`D_K = −619`),
proved that no chain outside its catalogue is cheaper under the evaluator's
operation count, built every candidate on the curve, and the crypto
repository measured the result natively: 1.408× fewer counted field
operations per `k·P` than the best width-w NAF, 1.417× in median time
(aburan28/crypto#1423).  This directory asks the same question of every curve
we could collect: which curves have an endomorphism cheap enough to matter,
what is the cheapest one, does it exist on the curve, and what would it buy?

Everything here is generated by two commands (python-flint 0.9.0, four
cores; Tom-521's 29 constructions take about seven minutes, every other curve
under one):

```sh
python -m harness.endosweep.arkworks --commit e2d16a27e2cfa9f972ae9772df827a22730011b4 \
    --out-dir research/endosweep_curves_20261006/arkworks
python -m harness.endosweep.curvesweep --std-curves STD_CURVES_CHECKOUT \
    --extra research/endosweep_curves_20261006 --out-dir research/endosweep_curves_20261006
```

`STD_CURVES_CHECKOUT` is J08nY/std-curves at commit
`77fe6e3585ca2c2225b59d7df24b7c775437276f`; `curves.json` records both
commits.  No timing is produced in this repository.

## What was swept

* **Curves.**  The 175 prime-field curves of the std-curves database and the `G1` of
  the 27 curve crates of arkworks-rs/curves (extracted by `harness/endosweep/arkworks.py`
  into [`arkworks/curves.json`](arkworks/curves.json)): **202 curves, every one
  verified from its constants** (`n` prime, `h·n` in the Hasse interval, a point
  killed by `h·n`; where `n < 4√q`, as for the `G1` of BW6-761, BW6-767 and
  CP6-782, a baby-step giant-step bound on the order of `n·P` that excludes every
  other multiple of `n` in the interval).  47 entries repeat another curve (same
  `p`, `j`-invariant, order and cofactor: the SECG, X9.62, X9.63, Oakley and WTLS
  aliases of the NIST and SECG curves, brainpool's twisted variants, Ed25519 and
  Ed448, arkworks' copies of BLS12-377/381, Pallas, Vesta, secp256k1/r1,
  secp384r1, Curve25519, JubJub and Bandersnatch, …) and are swept once,
  leaving **155 distinct curves**.
* **The scan.**  For each verified curve, the CM discriminant of Frobenius
  is located by one perfect-square test per fundamental discriminant
  (`quadorder.small_discriminant_scan`): either `t² − 4q = f²·D_K` with
  `|D_K| ≤ 2 000 000`, or a certificate that `|D_K| > 2 000 000`, in which case
  every non-scalar endomorphism has degree at least `|D_K|/4 > 500 000`.
* **Each curve with a small discriminant**:
  * `D_K = −3` or `−4`: the unit `ζ₃` or `i`, one multiplication, verified to
    act on a point of order `n` as a cube or fourth root of unity.  No chain
    can beat it, and none is built.
  * otherwise, the chain sweep's pipeline: a catalogue of primitive
    non-scalar elements `a + bω` whose bounds (largest norm, largest prime
    step) grow until a lower bound on every chain outside them exceeds twice
    the cheapest chain inside, so **each curve's catalogue provably holds every
    chain within 2× of its cheapest**; each element costed at its cheapest
    step order under the optimised evaluator that the counting build of
    aburan28/crypto#1423 confirmed exactly (only the first step's position
    matters; the closed form is checked against brute force); 2-isogeny steps
    where 2 splits or ramifies (Vélu in Jacobian coordinates, 7M + 3S, or
    4M + 1S on an affine input); and **every element within 2× built on the
    curve** by `explicit.build_chain_endomorphism` (ω's eigenvalue pinned,
    the element itself rather than its conjugate) and verified on points: a
    closed walk back to `j(E)`, action as the predicted scalar on a point of
    order `n`, and a GLV-2 reconstruction.
  * the whole scalar multiplication `k·P` modelled for random `k` with the
    curve's own arithmetic: its doubling formula (`a = 0` dbl-2009-l 2M + 5S;
    `a = −3`, or a model isomorphic to one, 3M + 5S; small `a` 1M + 8S;
    general `a` 1M + 8S + 1M), add-2007-bl and madd-2007-bl additions, a
    Fermat inversion for its own prime, widths 3–7, affine or Jacobian
    tables, against the best width-w NAF with the same arithmetic.  Edwards
    and Montgomery curves are counted on their short Weierstrass model.

`ω = (1 + √D_K)/2` (`√−2` for Bandersnatch); `M_eq = M + S`.  Each
element is built in its cheapest order only: the chain sweep showed on
CryptoPro-B that every ordering of an element is the same endomorphism (381
of 381 orderings), so the other orderings change the cost, not the map.  The
per-curve list of all 182 elements is in [`curves.md`](curves.md).

**43 distinct curves have `D_K = −3`** — the 22 BN and 7 BLS curves of the
corpus, secp160k1/192k1/224k1/256k1, WTLS8 and WTLS9, Pallas, Vesta,
Tweedledum, Tweedledee, Grumpkin, secq256k1 and the `G1` of BW6-761 and
BW6-767 — and the unit `ζ₃` (one multiplication) acts on a point of order `n`
as a primitive cube root of unity on every one of them.  Their modelled
ratios run from 1.403 (WTLS8, `n` of 113 bits) to 1.479 (the 606- and 638-bit
BN curves), growing with the length of `n`.  No curve in the corpus has
`D_K = −4`.

**95 distinct curves are certified to have no non-scalar endomorphism of
degree below 500 000** (`|D_K| > 2 000 000`): among them P-192 to P-521, the
SECG and X9.62 random curves, brainpool, FRP256v1, SM2, GOST CryptoPro-A and -C,
the GOST 2012 (tc26) sets and `gost512`, the NUMS curves, Curve25519, Curve448,
Ed448-Goldilocks, E-222/382/521, M-221/383/511, Curve1174, Curve41417, JubJub,
the BADA55 curves, MNT4/MNT6-298 and -753, and the Edwards curves on the
BLS12-377, BN254, BW6-761, MNT4-298 and MNT4-753 scalar fields.  On these the
endomorphism ring offers nothing cheaper than a width-w NAF.

## Findings

1. **The 60 curves with a small discriminant are CM constructions.**  A
   random curve over a 112- to 782-bit field has `|D_K|` of the order of `p`;
   a discriminant below two million at these sizes is the signature of the
   CM method.  Besides the 43 `D_K = −3` curves (BN, BLS, the SECG `k1`
   curves, WTLS8/9 and the curves built for proof systems) they are the two
   GOST curves (CryptoPro-B and `gost256`, the RFC 5832 example); the Tom
   curves of ZKAttest (ePrint 2021/1183), whose prime orders equal the
   base-field primes of P-256, P-384 and P-521 (checked); CP6-782;
   Bandersnatch (Masson, Sanso and Zhang, ePrint 2021/1152); and the MNT
   example curves (Miyaji, Nakabayashi and Takano).  The other 95, the NIST,
   SECG random, X9.62, brainpool, ANSSI, SM2, NUMS and Curve25519/448
   families among them, are certified clean.
2. **On all 17 the endomorphism costs little.**  The cheapest chain is 6 to
   228 M_eq, at most 7.7 % of the GLV-2 scalar multiplication it enables
   (Tom-256; 2.2 % on CP6-782), so the chain curves reach the same modelled
   range as the `D_K = −3` curves with their free unit: 1.35–1.53 against
   1.40–1.48.  The spread within each group comes from the curves'
   arithmetic, not from the endomorphism: doubling costs 2M + 5S with
   `a = 0`, 3M + 5S with `a = −3` and 1M + 8S (+ 1M) otherwise, and the GLV
   gain is mostly doublings removed, so the curves with general `a`
   (Tom-384, Bandersnatch's Weierstrass model, mnt1) show the largest ratios.
3. **The cheapest element depends only on `D_K`; the curve decides the
   rest.**  Tom-384 and CryptoPro-B share `D_K = −619` and the same cheapest
   element, `4 + ω` as `7·5·5` at 80 M_eq; the 384-bit curve with general `a`
   models at 1.525, the 256-bit `a = −3` curve at 1.410.
4. **Two positive controls are recovered.**  Bandersnatch's designers
   equipped it with an efficient endomorphism for fast scalar multiplication
   (ePrint 2021/1152); the sweep finds `√−2`, a single 2-isogeny of 6 M_eq,
   as its cheapest endomorphism (`1 + √−2`, one 3-isogeny, ties at 6).  On
   the 43 `D_K = −3` curves it finds `ζ₃`, the classical GLV endomorphism.
   The chains of the other 15 curves were not checked against the
   literature: the sweep does not know whether they have been used.
5. **Only CryptoPro-B has been measured**: aburan28/crypto#1423 counted
   1.408× and timed 1.417× (median) for the configuration modelled here at
   1.410×.  Every other ratio is a model built from the same formulas.  The
   largest modelled gains on a non-unit curve are Tom-384 (1.525),
   Bandersnatch (1.522, on its short Weierstrass model; it is specified in
   twisted Edwards form, whose arithmetic this model does not count) and
   CP6-782 (1.494), the natural next native measurements.
6. **The sweep exposed and fixed four limits of the harness**, each with a
   test: signed constants in the std-curves format (Bandersnatch's
   `a = −0x05`); group orders too small to pin from one point (the BW6/CP6
   `G1`, now pinned by a lower bound on the order of `n·P`); Edwards and
   Montgomery curves (now converted to a verified short Weierstrass model);
   and **steps at a prime dividing the conductor of `Z[π]`**.  On the GOST
   2001 test curve, `43` divides the conductor and Frobenius is the scalar
   `7` on all of `E[43]`: all 44 subgroups are rational, the 43-division
   polynomial splits into 308 cubics, each kernel is a product of 7 of them,
   and the builder's search over products of factors could not finish.
   Grouping the factors by subgroup through `∏ (Y − x([k]P))` finds all 44
   kernels in 10 s and builds `8 + ω` as `43·7` in 12 s.

## Relation to the corpus pass

The corpus pass of #1855 ([`../endosweep_20261005/corpus/corpus.md`](../endosweep_20261005/corpus/corpus.md))
ranked the std-curves part of this corpus with the sweep's first chain model,
which charges CryptoPro-B's `5·5·7` chain 53 M in an affine form.  The
evaluator measured in crypto#1408 spends 128 M_eq on it (a count the counting
build of crypto#1423 reproduced exactly), and that pass's modelled 1.45× for
CryptoPro-B compares with 1.408× counted natively for the best configuration.
The figures here use the confirmed evaluator and supersede that pass's ratios
(1.40–1.52× on these curves).  The cheapest element changes where the two
models price steps differently: on Tom-256 the corpus pass chose `49 + 2ω`
(`5·11³`), which costs 213 M_eq here against 161 for `2 + ω` (`19·5·11`).
That pass also could not parse Bandersnatch, which this one does.

## Scope

* Operation counts (`M_eq = M + S`), expected over random scalars, not
  timings.  The chain counts and the scalar-multiplication formulas are the
  ones a counting build of the crypto repository reproduced exactly on
  CryptoPro-B; on every other curve they are the same formulas, not a
  measurement.  The ratios compare each curve against its own best
  baseline, so a curve with expensive doublings (general `a`) shows a larger
  ratio for the same endomorphism than one with `a = 0`: compare elements
  across curves by `M_eq`, not by ratio.
* Edwards and Montgomery curves are counted on their short Weierstrass
  model, which is not how they are implemented; only Bandersnatch is
  affected (every other Edwards or Montgomery curve in the corpus has
  `|D_K| > 2 000 000`).
* Variable-base scalar multiplication only, variable time, rank-2 GLV.
* In the terms of [`docs/bounds-and-frontiers.md`](../../docs/bounds-and-frontiers.md)
  every figure here is at the primitive-weights level (field operations per
  scalar multiplication, in `M_eq`), modelled rather than measured; no
  bound, frontier or ledger evidence record is produced.
* Nothing here is a security claim: it counts the field operations of `k·P`
  on the same curves.
