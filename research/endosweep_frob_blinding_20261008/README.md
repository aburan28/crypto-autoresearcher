# Frobenius expansions over F_3 and F_4, and lattice-vector blinding for GLV

Two tier-2 follow-ups to the endomorphism sweep:

* **Item 9**: Frobenius-adic scalar expansions on ordinary curves defined
  over F_3 and F_4, with the binary Koblitz curves over F_2 as the
  baseline.
* **Item 10**: lattice-vector blinding ("C5") of GLV decompositions,
  compared with naive blinding.

Both items count operations. Neither is an ECDLP result or a security
claim.

Every output is regenerated by two commands. They take about three minutes
and one minute, single process:

```sh
python -m harness.endosweep.frobenius --out-dir research/endosweep_frob_blinding_20261008 \
    --std-curves STD_CURVES_CHECKOUT
python -m harness.endosweep.blinding --out-dir research/endosweep_frob_blinding_20261008
```

`STD_CURVES_CHECKOUT` is J08nY/std-curves at commit
`77fe6e3585ca2c2225b59d7df24b7c775437276f`. It is used only for the census
of standardized curves. `frobenius` also reads
`research/endosweep_nist_20261006/nist.json` for the measured K-curve
numbers. `blinding` reads
`research/endosweep_20261005/cryptopro_b_chain_5_5_7.constants.json` and
`research/endosweep_curves_20261006/curves.json` for the chain
eigenvalues.

Tests: `python3 -m pytest -q tests/test_endosweep_frob_blinding.py` runs 16
tests in about 5 s.

## Item 9: Frobenius expansions over small characteristic beyond F_2

**Question.** Take an ordinary curve E defined over F_q with q = 3 or 4,
and use it over F_{q^m} with m prime and n = #E(F_{q^m})/#E(F_q) prime.
Here the q-power Frobenius φ satisfies φ² − tφ + q = 0, and it replaces
the doublings. How does the cost per bit of group order compare with
Solinas' τ-adic NAF on binary Koblitz curves, and with prime-field GLV?

### What is checked

1. **Which traces occur.** Every curve over F_3 and over F_4 is
   enumerated by brute force.
   * Over F_3 the ordinary traces are t = ±1 (t² − 4q = −11) and ±2 (−8).
     Ordinary char-3 curves have the form y² = x³ + a₂x² + a₆ with a₂ ≠ 0;
     y² = x³ + a₄x + a₆ is supersingular.
   * Over F_4 they are t = ±1 (−15) and ±3 (−7).
   * Supersingular traces (p | t) are excluded throughout.
2. **Prime-order instances.**
   * For every (q, t) and every prime m in a range giving 130–570-bit
     groups, the order comes from the Lucas sequence:
     #E(F_{q^m}) = q^m + 1 − V_m.
   * n is tested with sympy's `isprime`. Above 2^64 that is a **BPSW
     probable-prime test, not a proof**.
   * N(δ) = n is checked exactly, for δ = (φ^m − 1)/(φ − 1) in Z[φ].
   * Embedding degrees up to 50 are checked. None occur.
3. **Expansions.**
   * The width-w expansion of ρ = k mod δ uses digits that are 0 or the
     minimal-norm representative α_u of a residue class u mod φ^w that φ
     does not divide. Since Z[φ]/(φ^w) ≅ Z/q^w, there are
     q^w − q^{w−1} such classes, stored up to sign.
   * w = 1 is the minimal digit set: q residues mod φ, i.e. {0, ±1} for
     q = 3 and {0, ±1, ±2} for q = 4. For q = 2, w is Solinas' width.
   * **Termination is proven** for every (q, t, w) used, by enumerating
     every lattice point of the invariant disc |ρ| ≤ D/(√q − 1) and running
     each one to 0.
   * Every expansion is checked to evaluate back to ρ.
   * There are 1000 random scalars per instance and width.
4. **Costs.**
   * One scalar-multiplication routine (`frobenius.mul_counted`) runs
     either on real arithmetic or on a counting stub. The routine is: a
     table of α_u P with one batched inversion, then Horner with Frobenius
     maps and mixed additions, then one final inversion.
   * Coordinates and counts:
     * q = 2, 4: López–Dahab, mixed addition 8M + 5S. On the F_4 twist
       class with a = ω it is +1M, charged as a full multiplication by the
       constant.
     * q = 3: projective (X:Y:Z), mixed addition **10M + 2S**. The formula
       was derived for y² = x³ + a₂x² + a₆ and is implemented in
       `Char3Curve.madd`.
   * Frobenius costs: q = 2, 3S; q = 4, 6S; q = 3, 3 cubings.
   * Inversion is Itoh–Tsujii, counted.
   * The 130–570-bit costs are **modelled**: the stub's formula counts
     times the measured digit statistics.
5. **Verification on points (measured).**
   * F_{3^m} is implemented in a trinomial basis with counted M, S, cubings
     and inversions. F_4 curves, with any a, live inside F_{2^{2m}}.
   * One instance per trace is built: q = 3 with t = −2, −1, 1, 2 at
     m = 71, 59, 67, 83; q = 4 with t = −1, 1, 3 at m = 31, 37, 31.
   * On each one these checks hold:
     * φ² − tφ + q = 0 on points;
     * nG = O;
     * φ(G) = [λ]G and δ(λ) ≡ 0 (mod n);
     * kG by the expansion equals double-and-add, at every width;
     * **the stub's counts equal the executed counts exactly, for every
       scalar and width.**
6. **Cross-check with `binary.py`** on K-163 and K-233:
   * δ is the same;
   * the digits equal Solinas' TNAF digit for digit;
   * the stub's counts equal `binary.mul_tnaf_counted`'s executed counts
     exactly.

   The q = 2 model reproduces the measured TNAF numbers of
   `endosweep_nist_20261006`. For example, at K-283 it gives 1.794 M/bit
   with S free, against 1.773 measured, and 7.898 with S = M, against
   7.865. The two runs use 1000 and 32 scalars.

Prime-order instances (n a BPSW probable prime) found in the search
ranges:

| q | t | m (n bits) |
|--:|--:|:--|
| 2 | −1 | 131 (129), 233 (231), 239 (237), 277 (275), 283 (281), 349 (347) |
| 2 | 1 | 163 (162), 283 (282), 311 (310), 331 (330), 347 (346), 359 (358) |
| 3 | −2 | 199 (312.8), 257 (404.8) |
| 3 | −1 | **179 (281.4)** |
| 3 | 1 | none for m in 83–257 |
| 3 | 2 | 83 (130.6), 193 (304.9) |

| 4 | 1 | 67 (132), 79 (156), 163 (324) |
| 4 | ±3 | none for m in 67–201 |

**Cost per bit of n, best width.** All figures are **modelled**: M-equivalents
of the curve's own field for the whole scalar multiplication. "Linear free"
means squarings in characteristic 2 and cubings in characteristic 3 cost
nothing, and a characteristic-3 squaring costs 0.8M (`costmodel.S_PER_M`, an
assumption). "All M" means every squaring and cubing costs 1M.

| family | instance | n bits | linear free (w, table) | all M (w, table) |
|:--|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| q = 2 (τ-adic NAF) | t = −1, m = 283 (K-283) | 281 | **1.79** (w4, 4) | 7.90 (w4, 4) |
| q = 2 | t = 1, m = 331 | 330 | **1.73** (w5, 8) | 7.85 (w4, 4) |
| q = 3 | t = −1, m = 179 | 281 | 2.81 (w3, 9) | **6.20** (w3, 9) |
| q = 3 | t = 2, m = 193 | 305 | 2.94 (w3, 9) | 6.36 (w3, 9) |
| q = 4 | t = 1, m = 163 | 324 | 1.98 (w2, 6) | 9.19 (w2, 6) |
| q = 4 | t = −1, m = 167 (a = ω) | 331 | 2.24 (w2, 6) | 9.49 (w2, 6) |
| binary K-curves, measured (`endosweep_nist_20261006`) | K-163 … K-571 | 162–569 | 1.93 … 1.57 | 8.11 … 7.61 |
| prime field, modelled (`costmodel`) | 2-GLV, 256-bit a = 0 | 256 | 5.72 M_p | |
| prime field, modelled (`costmodel`) | plain wNAF | 256 | 8.60 M_p | |

The M of each row is the multiplication of its own field.

* For q = 2 and q = 4 that is a binary field of about the group's size,
  so the two are directly comparable.
* For q = 3 it is F_{3^m}, and no cost ratio to a binary-field M is
  assumed. The characteristic-3 rows can only be compared with the others
  once such a ratio is measured on a real implementation.

**The law that decides the comparison** (derived, then measured). In a
width-w expansion, a nonzero digit is followed by w − 1 forced zeros, and
the next digit is zero with probability 1/q. So each nonzero digit
consumes log₂q·(w − 1 + q/(q − 1)) bits of n. The measured values agree:

| q | widths | measured bits per nonzero digit | predicted |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 2 (m = 571) | w = 1…6 | 1.99, 3.00, 3.99, 4.98, 5.96, 6.94 | 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 |
| 3 (m = 257) | w = 1…4 | 2.37, 3.95, 5.50, 7.04 | 2.38, 3.96, 5.55, 7.13 |
| 4 (m = 167) | w = 1…3 | 2.66, 4.63, 6.59 | 2.67, 4.67, 6.67 |

The table holds (q^w − q^{w−1})/2 points, so at a fixed table size T a
nonzero digit consumes log₂T + c_q bits, with:

* c_2 = 3;
* c_3 = 1.5·log₂3 ≈ 2.38;
* c_4 = 8/3 − log₂1.5 ≈ 2.08.

### Findings

1. **No standardized curve lives over F_{3^m} or F_{4^m}.**
   * std-curves at commit 77fe6e3 holds 175 prime-field, 70 binary, 2
     extension and 1 tower entries.
   * None is in characteristic 3.
   * No binary curve has coefficients in F_4.
   * The census did turn up something else, which was checked:
     * The five X9.62 curves `c2pnb176w1`, `c2pnb208w1`, `c2pnb272w1`,
       `c2pnb304w1` and `c2pnb368w1` are **subfield curves over
       F_{2^16}**, with m = 11, 13, 17, 19 and 23.
     * Their published cofactor equals #E(F_{2^16}) exactly. This was
       checked with the Lucas sequence of the F_{2^16} trace (t = 147,
       441, 251, 467, 145).
   * The Koblitz entries are the NIST, SECG, X9.63 and WTLS K-curves over
     F_2.
2. **Over F_3, the Frobenius expansion works and is verified on points,
   but under the realistic cost scenario it does not beat binary τ-adic
   NAF per bit.**
   * Each nonzero digit consumes fewer bits at a given table size (the law
     above: c_3 = 2.38 < c_2 = 3).
   * Char-3 squarings are real products.
   * When linear maps are free, the 281-bit q = 3 instance needs 2.81
     M/bit at w = 3, against 1.79 for K-283. These are M of different
     fields.
   * When every squaring and cubing costs 1M, q = 3 needs **6.20** M/bit,
     less than q = 2's 7.90. The Frobenius costs 3 cubings per log₂3 bits
     rather than 3 squarings per bit, and the char-3 mixed addition is 12
     against 13.
   * So q = 3 helps only where cubing is not cheaper than multiplication,
     which is not the polynomial-basis software case.
3. **F_4 is no better than F_2 under either scenario.**
   * Its Frobenius costs the same 3 linear operations per bit (6S per 2
     bits).
   * Its digits consume fewer bits at equal table size: c_4 = 2.08 against
     3.
   * Results: 1.98 against 1.73 M/bit (linear free) and 9.19 against 7.85
     (all M), at about 324–330 bits.
4. **q = 4 with t = −3 can never have prime n.**
   * Those curves are the F_2-Koblitz curves, with φ₄ = τ².
   * So n = [#E(F_{2^m})/#E(F_2)]·[#E'(F_{2^m})/#E'(F_2)], where E' is the
     quadratic twist over F_{2^m}.
   * This identity is checked for all 28 primes m in 67–199.
   * The t = +3 class (a = ω, b = 1) can have prime n (m = 31, 53, 61).
     Its Z[φ] is the maximal order of Q(√−7), so it has a degree-2
     endomorphism β with φ = −β̄². It is a τ-adic curve in disguise.
5. **Prime n is rare at these sizes.**
   * For q = 3 with t = 1 there is none for any prime m in 83–257.
   * For q = 4 there are only two in 160–300 bits (m = 79 at 156 bits, and
     none between 160 and 300; then 163 and 167 at 324 and 331 bits).
   * So instances are scarce rather than tunable.
6. **The model is only as good as its table plan.**
   * Tables are built by expanding each α_u with ±1 digits.
   * At w ≥ 3 for q = 4, and w = 4 for q = 3, this dominates. Smarter
     tables would move the best width up, but not the bits-per-digit law.
   * Avanzi–Heuberger's unit-group digit sets for supersingular char-3
     curves (KN-LIT-3824) report "memory reductions – factors like 3 to 8".
     Nothing equivalent was tried here for ordinary curves.

### Security facts relevant to the study (cited, not tested)

* Supersingular curves are excluded because of the MOV reduction: for
  supersingular curves "the embedding degree satisfies k ≤ 6"
  (KN-LIT-084, Menezes–Okamoto–Vanstone 1993).
* The supersingular char-3 curves are the pairing curves. KN-LIT-6735
  (Hayashi–Shirase–Takagi) reports solving the DLP in GF(3^{6·71}), "of
  676bit size".
* A Frobenius of order m gives Pollard rho the "larger sqrt(2m)-type
  factors for Koblitz/anomalous-binary curves" (KN-LIT-042,
  Wiener–Zuccherato 1998). Subfield curves over F_{3^m} and F_{4^m} carry
  the same kind of speed-up, which is not quantified here.
* Weil descent:
  * Weil descent in characteristic 3 is studied in KN-LIT-7499 (Arita),
    and GHS in odd characteristic in KN-LIT-090.
  * Whether either applies to subfield curves with m prime was **not
    checked here**.
  * KN-LIT-0cb87e relays Koblitz–Koblitz–Menezes' example of Müller's
    curve over F_{2^{3·59}}, defined over F_8, where the risk comes from
    Weil descent to genus 3 over F_{2^59}. Here m is prime, so no
    intermediate field exists.
* φ-expansions for multi-scalar products (φ-JSF) are in KN-LIT-4380
  (Ciet–Lange–Sica).
* Koblitz 1998, Smart 1999 ("Elliptic curve cryptosystems over small
  fields of odd characteristic"), Müller 1998 and Günther–Lange–Stein:
  **no notes in `knowledge/literature/`. Unverified**; nothing here
  depends on them.

## Item 10: lattice-vector blinding for GLV ("C5")

**Question.** Classical scalar blinding k + r·n is erased by GLV
decomposition, because Babai depends only on k mod n. C5 instead blinds
the decomposed vector with a random lattice vector Σ r_i b_i, where b_i is
the reduced basis and r_i has e/d bits. Is its cost e/d extra bits per
coefficient, keeping the doubling ratio d, against 2(b+e)/(b+2e) for naive
blinding after decomposition? And is the distribution of the top bits
sound?

The likely prior art is Ciet, Quisquater and Sica, "Preventing
differential analysis in GLV elliptic curve scalar multiplication", CHES
2002. There is **no note in `knowledge/literature/`, so it is unverified**,
and its contents were not compared.

### What is checked

* **Four lattices:**

  | case | d | eigenvalue source |
  |:--|--:|:--|
  | GOST CryptoPro-B | 2 | the 4 + ω chain of degree 5·5·7, from the committed chain constants |
  | secp256k1 | 2 | ζ₃ |
  | Tom-521 | 2 | the 47 + ω chain of degree 9317, from `curves.json`; n = 2^521 − 1 per std-curves |
  | FourQ | 4 | 1, φ, ψ, φψ, with maps verified on points by `fourq.py` |

  Each eigenvalue is checked against its element's characteristic
  polynomial, up to sign and conjugation.
* **Schemes:**
  * unblinded Babai v(k);
  * classical k + r·n, then decomposition;
  * naive: v + Σ r_i b_i with e-bit r_i;
  * C5: v + Σ r_i b_i with e/d-bit r_i.

  The r_i are uniform and symmetric. The run uses e = 64 and 10 000 random
  k per case.
* **Reconstruction.** Every blinded vector satisfies Σ x_i λ_i ≡ k
  (mod n). Six vectors per case are also checked on points for
  CryptoPro-B (committed test vectors), secp256k1 and FourQ.
* **Min-entropy of the top bits, given k:**
  * exactly, at the window of bits ≥ ⌊log₂ λ₁^∞⌋ (proof in the
    `blinding.py` docstring);
  * as a rigorous lower bound e − log₂ N(B) at the window of bits above
    the unblinded coefficient size;
  * by exhaustive enumeration of all 2^16 blinding vectors at e = 16, for
    8 values of k;
  * per coefficient, as a plug-in estimate from the sample.

**The harness has no leakage model.** It measures cost and the
distribution of what is computed, not side-channel security.

### Results

All results are measured, with e = 64 and 10 000 scalars. D is the
doubling count, the largest coefficient bit length.

| case | d | D unblinded | D C5 | D naive | C5 extra bits (mean / max / provable) | e/d | ratio C5 (pred. d) | ratio naive (pred.) | k + rn decomposes to v(k) |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| CryptoPro-B | 2 | 126.3 | 158.3 | 190.3 | 32.01 / 32 / 31 | 32 | 2.008 (2) | 1.671 (1.667) | 10000/10000 |
| secp256k1 | 2 | 126.8 | 158.8 | 190.8 | 32.01 / 32 / 32 | 32 | 2.009 (2) | 1.672 (1.667) | 10000/10000 |
| Tom-521 | 2 | 259.3 | 291.3 | 323.3 | 31.99 / 32 / 31 | 32 | 2.005 (2) | 1.806 (1.803) | 10000/10000 |
| FourQ | 4 | 60.7 | 76.7 | 124.7 | 16.01 / 16 / 15 | 16 | 4.021 (4) | 2.473 (2.470) | 10000/10000 |

The extra bits stay at e/d over e = 16, 32, 64 and 128 on secp256k1 and
FourQ (2000 scalars each), within 0.04 bits on average.

Min-entropy of the top bits, given k:

| case | λ₁^∞ bits | bits ≥ s0: H∞ | blinding window: N(B), bound e − log₂N(B) | exact at e = 16 (min over 8 k) |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| CryptoPro-B | 128 | 64 (exact) | 1, 64.0 | 16.0 |
| secp256k1 | 128 | 64 (exact) | 3, 62.4 | 15.0 |
| Tom-521 | 261 | 64 (exact) | 1, 64.0 | 16.0 |
| FourQ | 61 | 64 (exact) | 19, 59.75 | 14.0 |

**Per-coefficient MSB statistics.** This bears on the concern of
KN-LIT-1027 (Alpirez Bock–Chmielewski–Miteloudi, 2022/1254), which reports
that "implementations ... easily leak the value of the most significant
bits of the secret scalar". Each blinded coefficient is a sum of d scaled
uniforms, so its top bits are not uniform.

* The probability that a coefficient reaches the provable loop length
  ranges from 0.01 (secp256k1, coefficient 1; FourQ coefficient 0 at
  0.015) to 0.39 (Tom-521).
* The plug-in min-entropy of (sign, top 4 bits) is 3.2–4.6 bits out of 5.
  For classical k + r·n it is 3.3–3.95.
* The MSB of a fixed-length loop is therefore mostly 0. That is a property
  of the distribution, and here it is only measured, not judged.

### Findings

1. **The erasure claim holds.** Decomposing k + r·n gave exactly v(k) in
   40 000 of 40 000 cases. A test checks it too.
2. **The C5 cost prediction holds.**
   * The extra bits are e/d, with mean 32.0 at d = 2 and 16.0 at d = 4,
     well under the kill threshold e/d + 2.
   * The doubling ratio against classical blinding without GLV is d:
     2.005–2.009 and 4.021.
   * Naive blinding after decomposition gives (b+e)/(b/d+e): 1.671 and
     1.672 at 256 bits, 1.806 at 521 bits, 2.473 for FourQ.
   * For d = 4 the gap is larger: 76.7 against 124.7 doublings.
3. **The min-entropy check passes.**
   * The top bits, from the λ₁^∞ position up, carry exactly e bits. That
     is about e/d + 1 bits per coefficient.
   * Restricted to the bits the blinding adds, the exact deficit at e = 16
     is 0–2 bits, against a kill threshold of 4.
   * The rigorous bound at any e loses log₂N(B) bits: 0, 1.6, 0 and
     **4.25 for FourQ**. So for FourQ the bound alone would not clear
     e − 4. The exact enumeration at e = 16 (deficit 2) does.
4. **Status.** C5 is a sound way to keep blinding entropy through GLV at
   e/d bits per coefficient. Whether it is new beyond Ciet–Quisquater–Sica
   2002 is **unverified**: no note exists here, and the comparison has to
   be made against the paper itself.

## Scope

* **Operation counts, not timings.**
  * Item 9's costs at 130–570 bits are modelled from formula counts and
    measured digit statistics. The model equals the executed counts
    exactly on the verification instances (59–131-bit n) and on K-163 and
    K-233.
  * Cross-characteristic comparisons need a measured ratio between an
    F_{3^m} and an F_{2^N} multiplication, which is not available here.
* **Probable primes.** n is a BPSW probable prime for every instance, not
  a proven prime.
* **No security claims.** Item 10 has no leakage model. Item 9's security
  remarks are cited facts. Nothing here creates or edits ledger,
  knowledge or coordination records.
