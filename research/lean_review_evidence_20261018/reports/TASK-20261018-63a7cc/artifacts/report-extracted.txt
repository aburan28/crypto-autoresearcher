Transport and historical transfer
The new source uses AddCommGroup, an actual AddSubgroup, Finite S, additive homomorphisms, and
the explicit hypothesis back (f P) = d • P for every P : S. Applying back to equal images gives equality
after multiplication by d; coprimality makes that multiplication bijective on S. Injectivity is derived, not
stored as a property of a structure merely named “Isogeny.” Neither cyclicity nor a generator for all of S is
needed. The integer-scalar equivalence at line 28 preserves the same scalar and proves both directions.
The historical source has operations without group-law axioms, a Hom that assumes injectivity, a selfreported natural cost, and a deterministic solver with correctness on every solvable instance. Its transfer, transfer_cost, and within_isogeny_cost are a separate abstract solver/cost construction. The
new equivalence supplies a sound algebraic prerequisite under genuine group laws; it does not reproduce
that full construction or justify actual execution cost. The historical comment itself excludes ordinary stepcapped randomized walks unless their fixed tape yields an always-correct solver. Neither source alone
establishes measured conductor-dependent gains.
The curve specialization has genuine group laws from Mathlib. Mathlib’s Affine.Point consists of infinity
and nonsingular affine points (Point.lean, lines 477–480); its AddCommGroup instance is at line 805.
The theorem permits arbitrary Weierstrass models and does not require IsElliptic. It consequently
applies to elliptic curves, and can also describe the nonsingular locus of a singular model. It does not
certify any supplied model as elliptic. The reviewer checked y²=x³+1 over ZMod 7, nonzero discriminant,
a nonzero point (0,1), finiteness, and identity-map transport on its full point subgroup. This is an actualcurve nonvacuity example, not a nontrivial isogeny construction.
Factor-base range
Historical SubfieldCollapse.lean distinguishes the coordinate rescaling, rationality of relation points,
and an operation-generated span. The two new range theorems cover its third step with bona fide
Mathlib subgroup closure. They do not re-prove the coordinate/rationality lemmas, construct Jacobians,
prove the base-change map acts as required on divisor classes, establish image membership of the actual
factor base, find an actual target outside the image, or supply a Weil cardinality bound. The historical
source and new README both identify the missing geometric bridge.
The control embeds ZMod 7 as the first axis of ZMod 7 × ZMod 7. The nonzero base {(1,0)} lies in
the image; (0,1) does not. Both closure containment and target exclusion are obtained by applying the
producer declarations. This avoids relying only on an empty base or a zero map to exhibit satisfiable
assumptions.

Rho formulas and C boundary
At C lines 184–187, sign zero computes c=b1-b2, d=a2-a1, exactly matching R.recover. Lines 188–
192 compute c=b1+b2, d=-(a1+a2), exactly matching R.recover_negated. The two nonzero conditions
are different and were reviewed separately. Both formulas are valid over any field, rather than only a

The group theorem assumes prime p, an additive encoding and its injectivity. Its group collision is written
using encode (b*x). It does not build the encoding k ↦ [k]G from a concrete point of exact order p,
identify the C target as [x]G, or verify that encoded products match the C scalar multiplication and group
representation.
The C implementation skips c=0 at line 194, handles gcd(c,n) and divisibility at lines 195–198, caps
enumeration at 65,536 candidates at line 199, and verifies candidates at line 203. None is modeled by the
new theorems. The caller’s walk/exponent updates and canonicalization also remain separate obligations.

ca_verify_log is another implementation obligation. These observations do not establish a C defect or
