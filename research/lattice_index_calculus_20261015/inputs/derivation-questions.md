# Derivations to challenge in this search

These are working deductions and experiment-design questions, not independently reviewed findings or evidence records.

1. Same-field isogenies preserve the Frobenius trace and point count. For a fixed prime subgroup order r coprime to q, k=ord_r(q) depends only on q and r. An isogeny cannot change that k. This does not assert that all isogenous rational point groups have the identical full abelian-group structure. A map of degree coprime to r is injective on r-torsion.

2. Let a known endomorphism alpha preserve the chosen cyclic subgroup G=<P>, with certified alpha(P)=[lambda]P. For a stable orbit factor base F_(i,j)=alpha^j(F_i), eliminate logs using log(F_(i,j))=lambda^j log(F_i). Applying alpha^t to a relation and then eliminating the orbit columns produces lambda^t times the original row over F_r. It adds no rank in that reduced system. This does not deny solver work-sharing or independent relations from genuinely different decompositions; it also does not apply unchanged when alpha fails to preserve G. The repository already has H-BINSTD-ce4f38 and related designs for this accounting; do not call it novel or assert those designs were scientifically validated.

3. For any fixed factor base F subset G with cardinality M and ordered m-tuples (repetition allowed), sum_R N(R)=M^m. For uniform R in G, E N(R)=M^m/r exactly, so Pr[N(R)>0]<=min(1,M^m/r). No random-distribution heuristic is needed for that upper bound. A constant success probability at M^m comparable to r requires a separate distribution assumption and is not established by the mean. Unordered distinct tuples introduce combinatorial corrections; orbit duplicates and sign choices must not be counted as independent relations. Coordinate window width W is not automatically equal to factor-base size M.

4. Under the explicit model M=r^beta, constant verified rank-increasing yield, fixed m, per-attempt solver work r^sigma, and classical sparse linear algebra cost about M^2, the modeled stages cost r^(beta+sigma) and r^(2beta), plus construction, membership, transfer, individual descent and verification. A strict sub-r^(1/2) outcome in this model would require beta+sigma<1/2 and beta<1/4. Combining beta*m>=1 makes m>=5 necessary in this restricted model. This is bookkeeping, not a universal lower bound: large-prime variants, different linear algebra, sampling, preprocessing or factor-base regimes must be modeled separately. Do not impose these exponents as a discovered speedup claim.

5. For a polynomial f over integers with modular root u mod p, choose shifts x^a f^b p^(t-b), 0<=b<=t. Every lattice combination g vanishes at u modulo p^t. If |u_i|<=W_i and D monomials occur, Cauchy-Schwarz gives |g(u)|<=sqrt(D)*||coeff(g(W_1 X_1,...,W_m X_m))||_2. A strict bound below p^t certifies g(u)=0 over integers. Many such short vectors can still be algebraically dependent or multiples of the original polynomial. Rank of a coefficient matrix is not a zero-dimensional root-extraction certificate. Weight/determinant bounds must match the actual shift basis and root box; mere LLL completion proves none of this.

6. A useful candidate may compare Newton-polytope/sparse weighted shift sets against standard shifts, or use short-norm endomorphism/isogeny chains to simplify an auxiliary map-defined factor base. Both overlap substantial prior work. The next question is the simultaneous density, valid-root recovery, independence and complete-cost frontier, not whether one lattice vector is short.
