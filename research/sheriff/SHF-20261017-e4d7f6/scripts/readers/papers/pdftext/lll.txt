          LLL Failures on the Boneh–Venkatesan Lattice Were
              Floating-Point Artifacts: A Corrected Note
                                                 Anonymous


                                                    Abstract
          An earlier version of this note (PDF of 2026-10-11) reported two LLL failure modes for the
      Boneh–Venkatesan Hidden-Number-Problem lattice on deployed ECDSA curves: a “curve-specific”
      failure for secp256k1 at 256 bits and a uniform failure at ≥ 384 bits attributed to an undersized
      iteration cap. Both were bugs in our floating-point Gram–Schmidt orthogonalisation,
      not properties of the curves or of LLL. For secp256k1, and for every 384-bit curve, the squared
      Gram–Schmidt norms overflow IEEE double precision (n4 > f64::MAX); P-521 additionally suffers
      catastrophic cancellation. Both had been diagnosed and fixed in the companion code before that
      version was built. With the fixes, a re-run on 2026-10-16 recovers the key in every probe at 256 and
      384 bits, secp256k1 included (6/6), and on P-521 with 2048-bit fixed-point Gram–Schmidt (3/3).
      We withdraw the curve-specificity claim and every conclusion drawn from it. What remains is a
      numerical lesson: an f64 Gram–Schmidt step silently fails once the group order passes about 2256
      (basis entries about 2512 ).

This version replaces the note of 2026-10-11. An integrity audit of our results (the sheriff case

as curve properties two failures that our own analysis (research/notes/lattice-hnp/RESEARCH_LLL_
GS_ANALYSIS.md, §10) had traced to numerical bugs and fixed in May 2026. The following claims of the
earlier version are withdrawn:
     that secp256k1’s LLL failure is “a curve-specific arithmetic phenomenon”, “empirically robust”
      and “reproducible”;
     that the ≥ 384-bit failures were caused by the LLL iteration cap;
     that LLL-convergence benchmarks are curve-specific and that reported HNP attack costs do not

     the proposed curve-selection criterion against near-power-of-two group orders, and the open ques-

3    The causes
The basis has rows of size about n2 , so the squared Gram–Schmidt norms the reduction compares are
of size about n4 .

Failure A: overflow. IEEE double precision tops out just below 21024 . Among the three 256-bit curves,
only secp256k1’s order is close enough to 2256 that n4 exceeds f64::MAX; P-256’s and brainpoolP256r1’s
orders are smaller, and their n4 stays below it (an exact rational comparison of the standard orders [3, 4],
recorded in the audit as checks/f64-overflow.txt). The overflowed norms became infinite and then
NaN, and LLL ran to its iteration cap. The “curve specificity” was the position of one group order relative
to a floating-point limit. secp192k1 and secp224k1 escaped because their n4 is far below the limit, which
is also why the earlier near-power-of-two hypothesis seemed refuted. Scaling the basis by 2−(max bits−500)
before orthogonalising removes the overflow (src/cryptanalysis/lattice.rs).

Failure B: overflow, then cancellation. At 384 bits, n4 overflows for every curve, which explains
the “uniform” failure there; the same scaling fixes P-384 and brainpoolP384r1, so the iteration cap was
not the cause. P-521 still fails after scaling: orthogonalising a row against rows of size about n2 subtracts
nearly equal 53-bit quantities, and the residual swamps the true component by many orders of magnitude
(§10.2 of the analysis). A 2048-bit fixed-point Gram–Schmidt (lll reduce hp) removes it.

5    What remains
No curve-specific LLL behaviour remains at these parameters. The lesson is about implementations: an
f64 Gram–Schmidt step fails silently, by overflow, once the squared norms pass 21024 , that is once n
passes about 2256 and the basis entries about 2512 , and loses the small components to cancellation at
larger dynamic ranges. An HNP experiment that reports an LLL failure should state its arithmetic and
rule this out before attributing the failure to the curve.
    This note makes no security claim about any curve. It reports a small number of probes at one
parameter set (m = 8, kbits = 3nbits /4).
