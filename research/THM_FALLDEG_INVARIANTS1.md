## 1. The definitions (Caminata–Gorla §1)

Let `R = k[x_1..x_n]`, `F ⊆ R` a system, `F^top` its top-degree part, `F^h` its homogenization, `B = F_q[x]/(x_i^q − x_i)`.

**Definition 1.3 (first fall degree).** `d_ff(F) = min{ d ∈ N : Syz(F^top)_d / Triv(F^top)_d ≠ 0 }`, where `Triv(f_1..f_r)` is generated by the Koszul syzygies `f_i e_j − f_j e_i` and the field-equation syzygies `f_i^{q−1} e_i`. **Note the ambient ring is `B`, not `R`.**

**Definition 1.4 (degree of regularity).** Assuming `(F^top)_d = R_d` for `d ≫ 0`, `d_reg(F) = min{ d ≥ 0 : (F^top)_d = R_d }`.

**Definition 1.5 (last fall degree).** `V_{F,i}` is the smallest `k`-vector space with `F ∩ R_{≤i} ⊆ V_{F,i}` and closed under `f ∈ V_{F,i}, g ∈ R_{≤ i − deg f} ⟹ fg ∈ V_{F,i}`. Then
`d_F = min{ d ∈ N ∪ {∞} : f ∈ V_{F, max{d, deg f}} for all f ∈ (F) }`.

**The bridge to measurement.** For a **degree-compatible** term order, `V_{F,d} = rowsp(M_d)` — the row space of the degree-`d` Macaulay matrix after reduction (Caminata–Gorla eq. (1), proved in Gorla–Müller–Petit, Thm 1). `V_{F,d}` is therefore *exactly* the object a per-degree rank profile measures, and it is independent of which degree-compatible order is used (Cor. 1.6).

**Definitions 2.5–2.6 (degree fall, made precise).** For `f ∈ (F)`, `d_f = min{d : f ∈ V_{F,d}} ≥ deg(f)`. `f` **has a degree fall in degree `d_f`** iff `d_f > deg(f)`.

## 2. The theorems (verified statements, not paraphrase)

**Theorem 2.8 (CG).** If `F` has a degree fall, `d_F = max{ d_f : f ∈ (F), d_f > deg(f) }`. *The last fall degree is exactly the largest degree at which a degree fall occurs.*

**Theorem 2.9 (CG).** For a degree-compatible order and a Gröbner basis `{g_i}`, `d_F = max{ d_{g_i} : deg(g_i) < d_{g_i} }`.

**Theorem 3.1 (CG) — the main one.** For any degree-compatible term order `σ`,
```
sd_σ(F) = max{ d_F , max.GB.deg_σ(F) }
```
An **equality**. The two — and only two — ingredients of the solving degree are the degrees in the reduced Gröbner basis and the degree falls. `d_F` depends on neither the order nor the coordinates; `sd_σ` and `max.GB.deg_σ` depend on both (Rmk 3.2).

**Theorem 5.2 (Semaev–Tenti, quoted in CG).** For `F = {f_1..f_r, x_1^q − x_1, …, x_n^q − x_n} ⊆ F_q[x]`, **if** `d_reg(F) ≥ max{q, deg f_1, …, deg f_r}`, then `sd(F) ≤ 2·d_reg(F) − 2`.

**Theorem 5.1 (CG).** If `F` contains the field equations, `sd(F) ≤ reg(F^h)`.

**Caminata–Ceria–Gorla, Prop. 4.13 / Cor. 3.4(2).** For a finite Galois extension of degree `n`, and `F` with `(F^top)_d = R_d` for `d ≫ 0`:
```
d_reg(Weil(F)) = n · d_reg(F) − n + 1 = n·(d_reg(F) − 1) + 1
```
An **exact formula**, not a bound.

**CCG Cor. 4.12.** If `F ⊆ F_{q^n}[x_1..x_m]` contains the field equations of `F_{q^n}`, and `t ∤ 0` modulo `F^h`, then
```
sd( Weil(F) ∪ {x_{i,j}^q − x_{i,j}} ) ≤ n · reg(F^h) − n + 1
```

## 3. What this settles for RQ-DREG-bd6c86 — and what it does not

### 3.1 The slide's question has a partial, proven answer, and it is LINEAR

The question was how the degree of regularity grows in the parameters. Along the **descent parameter `n`**, CCG Prop. 4.13 answers it exactly: `d_reg` grows **linearly in `n`**, with slope `d_reg(F) − 1` fixed by the pre-descent system.

For the summation-polynomial system this slope is governed by `S_{m+1}`, whose degree depends on `m` and **not on `n`**. So the growth in `n` is linear with an `n`-independent slope, and by Cor. 4.12 the solving degree of the descended system *with* field equations is bounded by `n·reg(F^h) − n + 1`.

**This is not subexponential, and it does not claim to be.** A solving degree linear in `n` gives Gröbner cost roughly `(mn')^{ω·Θ(n)}` — exponential in `n log n`. The first-fall-degree assumption asserts something far stronger: that the *actual* solving degree is essentially **bounded**, not linear. The open question is therefore exactly the gap between a proven linear upper bound and a conjectured constant.

That is a far sharper statement of the open problem than "it seems to grow slower than random systems", and it is the form in which it can be attacked.

### 3.2 A direction error in this program's framing, now corrected

`RQ-DREG-bd6c86` and `THM_SEMAEV_FALL1` both say the last fall degree "provably bounds the solving degree", meaning it as a safe upper-bound substitute. **That is backwards.** Gorla–Müller–Petit proved `d_F` is a **lower** bound for `sd`; the *upper* bound in Huang–Kosters–Yeo is on the complexity of **their own linear-algebra algorithm**, not on the Gröbner solving degree. Theorem 3.1 gives the exact relation, in which `d_F` is one of two maximands. See CORR-20260916-0c9c0a.

The research consequence is **favourable, not damaging**: a lower bound is precisely what refutes an assumption. `IDEA-20260916-4b7c1e` proposes measuring falls off per-degree Macaulay ranks; by Theorem 2.8 that observable *is* `d_F`, and by `d_F ≤ sd` any fall exhibited at degree `D` certifies `sd ≥ D`. The proposal's logic survives intact and is now underwritten by a theorem instead of an analogy.

### 3.3 The first fall degree is not a safe proxy, and this is proved

CG §4 shows by explicit example that `d_ff` may be **arbitrarily larger or smaller** than the solving degree, the last fall degree and the degree of regularity. Their relation is heuristic only. Two mechanisms are named: `d_ff` is defined in `B` while the others live in `R`, and trivial syzygies with a large degree spread can inflate `d_ff` artificially.

`KN-OPEN-7f0511`'s catalogue of four competing `d_ff` definitions should be read against Definition 1.3 above, which is the one these theorems use.

### 3.4 A rigorous route the program did not have

Semaev–Tenti Thm 5.2 applies to systems **containing the field equations** — which the descended Semaev systems do by construction — and yields `sd ≤ 2 d_reg − 2` under a checkable hypothesis. Combined with Prop. 4.13, `d_reg` of the descended system is computable from `d_reg` of the pre-descent system. This is a proven chain from a small computation to a solving-degree bound, replacing the heuristic the subexponential claims rest on.

## 4. Hypotheses NOT checked here — do not apply these blind

- **`t ∤ 0` modulo `F^h`** (CCG Cor. 4.9, 4.12) and **generic coordinates over `k`** (Cor. 3.4(1)) are real hypotheses. Neither has been checked for any Semaev descent system. Checking them is cheap and is the obvious next task.
- **Prop. 4.13 is stated for `Weil(F)`**, before the `F_q` field equations are adjoined. Adjoining them can only lower `d_reg`, so the formula is an upper estimate for the system actually solved — but the exact statement is about `Weil(F)`, and the distinction must not be dropped.
- `(F^top)_d = R_d` for `d ≫ 0` is assumed throughout; for the summation systems this needs confirming, not assuming.
- Semaev–Tenti's solving degree may differ from Caminata–Gorla's, as CG themselves flag: Semaev and Tenti work in the quotient ring `F_q[x]/(x_i^q − x_i)`. **Do not chain Thm 5.2 with Thm 3.1 without resolving this.**
- Nothing here is a claim about cryptographic-size curves, and no arithmetic in §3.1 has been run against a concrete curve.
