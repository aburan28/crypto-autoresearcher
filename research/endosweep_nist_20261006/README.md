# NIST curves through the endomorphism sweep

**Question.**  Search every NIST curve with the endomorphism sweeper.  The
cross-curve sweep ([`../endosweep_curves_20261006`](../endosweep_curves_20261006/README.md))
already scanned P-192 to P-521 and found no endomorphism below degree
500 000.  It skipped the ten NIST curves over binary fields, because the
pipeline handled prime fields only.  This directory runs all of them:

* FIPS 186-4's fifteen curves, which SP 800-186 also contains: P-192 to
  P-521, the Koblitz curves K-163 to K-571 and the pseudorandom B-163 to
  B-571;
* the two Edwards curves SP 800-186 adds, as std-curves lists them:
  * Edwards25519 is std-curves' Ed25519, the same curve as Curve25519.
  * Edwards448 is RFC 8032's `d = −39081` curve, std-curves'
    Ed448-Goldilocks.  It has the same number of points as Curve448, so it
    is isogenous to it.  std-curves' Ed448 is Curve448 itself in Edwards
    form.

Everything here is generated by one command, which takes under three
minutes.  The factorisations it reads took PARI under five minutes each (see
below):

```sh
python -m harness.endosweep.nist --std-curves STD_CURVES_CHECKOUT \
    --factorizations research/endosweep_nist_20261006/factorizations.jsonl \
    --out-dir research/endosweep_nist_20261006
```

`STD_CURVES_CHECKOUT` is J08nY/std-curves at commit
`77fe6e3585ca2c2225b59d7df24b7c775437276f`, recorded in `nist.json`.

## What is checked on every curve

1. **The group order, from the constants alone.**
   * Prime fields: `targets.verify` (on the short Weierstrass model of an
     Edwards or Montgomery curve).
   * Binary fields: the new `binary.verify` checks that `n` is prime, `h·n`
     lies in the Hasse interval and `n > 4√q`, and that a fresh point is
     killed by `h·n` but not by `h`.  It also checks the published generator.
   * Koblitz curves: the order is also derived from the Lucas sequence of
     `τ`, `#E = 2^m + 1 − V_m`.
2. **The exact CM discriminant.**
   * The scan finds `t² − 4q = D_K·f²` whenever `|D_K| ≤ 2 000 000`.
   * Otherwise `4q − t²` is factored completely into proven primes, which
     gives `D_K`, the conductor `f` and the smallest degree any non-scalar
     endomorphism can have, `(|D_K| + (D_K mod 2))/4`.
   * The factorisations are certificates in
     [`factorizations.jsonl`](factorizations.jsonl).  They were found by PARI
     (`factor`, a 30-minute budget each) and are re-checked whenever they are
     read: the factors are multiplied back and each is tested for primality.
3. **The cheapest endomorphism.**  On a Koblitz curve this is the Frobenius
   `τ(x, y) = (x², y²)`.  It is verified on random points
   (`τ² − μτ + 2 = 0`) and on the generator, where `τ(G) = [λ]G` for a root
   `λ` of `λ² − μλ + 2 (mod n)`.
4. **Counted scalar multiplication on the binary curves.**  Width-w NAF on
   every curve, and Solinas' width-w τ-adic NAF (TNAF) on the Koblitz curves.
   * The TNAF works on the scalar reduced modulo `δ = (τ^m − 1)/(τ − 1)`.
   * Both use López–Dahab coordinates and an affine table converted with
     one batched Itoh–Tsujii inversion.
   * Widths 2–7; 32 random scalars per configuration.
   * The counts are of the field operations actually executed.  Every result
     is compared across all configurations (twelve on a Koblitz curve, six on
     the others), and the first two scalars also against an affine
     double-and-add.

## Findings

1. **The Koblitz curves are the only NIST curves with a usable
   endomorphism, and it is the one they were built for.**
   * On all five the sweep finds `D_K = −7` and `End(E) = Z[τ]`: `Z[τ]` has
     the fundamental discriminant `μ² − 8 = −7`, so it is the maximal order.
     The cheapest non-scalar endomorphism is the Frobenius `τ`, which costs
     three squarings in López–Dahab coordinates.
   * Every check holds on every K-curve: `τ² − μτ + 2 = 0` on random points,
     `#E` from the Lucas sequence equals `h·n`, `h = #E(F_2)`, `N(δ) = n` and
     `τ(G) = [λ]G`.
   * This is a positive control: Koblitz (1991) and Solinas (1997, 2000) put
     the curves forward for exactly this map, and the sweep recovers it with
     nothing given but the curve's constants.
2. **TNAF replaces the `m` doublings with `m` Frobenius maps.**
   * In the counts this is a factor of 2.55 to 2.91 if squarings are free, and
     1.61 to 1.64 if they cost as much as a multiplication.
   * The advantage grows with the field size when squarings are free, and
     stays near 1.62 when they are not.  The remaining cost is about `m/(w+1)`
     additions at 8M + 5S, and with costed squarings the `3m` squarings of
     the Frobenius maps.
3. **Every other NIST curve has no endomorphism to exploit, and this is now
   proven with exact discriminants rather than a bound.**
   * P-192 to P-521, B-163 to B-409, Curve25519 and Curve448 have `|D_K|`
     between 2^162 and 2^523.  Every non-scalar endomorphism therefore has
     degree at least 2^160, and at least 2^220 on every one of these curves
     whose field has 224 bits or more.
   * The conductor is 1 on P-192, P-256, P-384, P-521 and B-163 to B-409.
     There `End(E) = Z[π]`: the curve has no endomorphisms beyond the
     integers and its own Frobenius.
   * P-224 has `f = 3`, and Curve25519 and Curve448 have `f = 2`.  There
     `End(E)` is `Z[π]` or the maximal order; the degree bound holds either
     way.
   * The B-curves have no `τ`: `(x, y) ↦ (x², y²)` maps the curve to the one
     with `b²` in place of `b`, which is a different curve since `b ∉ F_2`.
4. **B-571 is bounded, not settled.**
   * `4q − t² = 137 · 1 502 689 · C`.  `C` is a 165-digit composite that is
     not a perfect square and has no prime factor below 10^7.
   * Its squarefree part is therefore at least 10^7, so
     `|D_K| ≥ 137 · 1 502 689 · 10^7 ≈ 2^50.9`.  That is far beyond any
     useful endomorphism, and about a billion times what the scan alone
     certifies.
   * PARI found no factor of `C` in 30 minutes; a complete factorisation
     needs the number field sieve.
5. **Cross-check.**  std-curves records its own `cm_disc` and `conductor`
   for P-192, P-224 and P-256, and they agree exactly with the factorisations
   here (P-224: `f = 3`).  Its binary-curve entries do not satisfy
   `cm_disc · f² = t² − 4q` with `q = 2^m`, so they were not used.
6. **Two fixes the sweep needed:**
   * binary-field support (`binary.py`);
   * `quadorder.min_nonscalar_degree`, which searched over a range of size
     `|D|` and never returned at cryptographic sizes.  It now uses the closed
     form its own docstring derives, and a test checks that against brute
     force for every discriminant down to −3000.

## Scope

* **Field-operation counts, not timings.**  The counts come from this
  harness's own implementation (López–Dahab formulas, Itoh–Tsujii
  inversion, affine tables).  Other table strategies or a normal basis would
  move the numbers; the squaring weight is bracketed rather than assumed.
* **Koblitz curves and Pollard rho.**  The same `τ` lets Pollard rho work on
  Frobenius orbits of size `2m`, the known speed-up of about `√(2m)` (Wiener
  and Zuccherato; Gallant, Lambert and Vanstone).  Nothing here goes beyond
  that.  Everything else is about computing `k·P`, and nothing here is a
  security claim.
