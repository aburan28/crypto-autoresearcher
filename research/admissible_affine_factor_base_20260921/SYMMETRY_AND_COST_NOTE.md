# Exact symmetry reduction and cost scope

This explanatory note is internal reasoning, not a separate experiment or a status transition. The frozen protocol and its baseline amendment remain normative.

Let O_i be the full signed Frobenius orbit of its supplied point P_i. For any triple of points in O_i,O_j,O_k, choose a common signed Frobenius automorphism sending the point in O_i to P_i. The other two points remain in their original full orbits. Applying the common automorphism sends the target sum into the same signed target orbit. Conversely every anchored sum is an actual sum from the original three orbits. Therefore enumerating P_i+O_j+O_k gives exactly the target-orbit support of O_i+O_j+O_k. Repeated orbit indices do not change this argument: individual points are allowed to repeat, and the common automorphism still acts on each summand. Infinity is excluded only from nonzero target coverage and remains a valid intermediate sum.

A four-orbit base requires the union of the supports of all multisets of three orbit indices, of which there are binomial(6,3)=20. The at-most-three union adds binomial(5,2)=10 pair types and the four singleton types. The full pool therefore has binomial(39,3)=9139 triple support types and binomial(38,2)=703 pair types. These caches are a reusable implementation device within the charged search, not free advice.

For a nonidentity point on the declared prime subgroup, a signed Frobenius orbit has size38 in this experiment. This must be verified, not inferred only by multiplying the number of canonical keys. The implementation enumerates the point orbits and checks distinctness. The 6909 target representatives and their disjoint38-point orbits cover262542 nonidentity points. A minimum x-coordinate over Frobenius identifies a signed orbit because the two rational points sharing a nonzero x are negatives. Field membership, curve membership and group order are independently controlled.

A set of four distinct vectors over F2 is an affine2plane exactly when their XOR is zero. Equal-XOR unordered pairs cover all such four-tuples. The distinct-orbit filter excludes planes whose four seeds would generate fewer than four effective base columns. Simultaneous Frobenius and all pair partitions create duplicates, so the canonical key is the minimum sorted four-vertex tuple across all nineteen common shifts. Retaining all canonical keys makes a complete finite catalogue rather than a sample selected for good coverage.

The plane-compatible winner is selected on exact-three coverage with predetermined tie breaks. The historical global record optimized at-most-three coverage; the additive amendment requires its complete histogram and the chosen base's separate exact/at-most counts to be reproduced. Newly computed global exact-three scores are measurements and carry no inherited expected maximum.

The source-aware reviewer checks full frontier/cache construction and custody. A distinct permanently blind reviewer derives geometry and selected-base coverage from definitions and coordinate inputs. This division is explicit: a selected-base replay alone does not independently recalculate every frontier support cache. No unreviewed global family optimum or exponent claim is attached to this finite computation.

All fresh field preparation, base expansion, geometry, cache construction, scoring, replay, compilation and packaging have their actual cost scopes. The search produces per-curve advice. Even a favorable selected base must pass a separate solver experiment, and a solver gain must survive complete relation-generation and linear-algebra costs before it can support a cold single-target IC comparison.
