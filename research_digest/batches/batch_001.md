# Batch 001 digest

## Overview
- Overwhelmingly a CHES 2004–2006 era batch on side-channel analysis: power/EM attacks (DPA, template, stochastic, higher-order) and hardware countermeasures (masking, dual-rail logic, WDDL, MDPL).
- Recurring institutions: Ruhr University Bochum (HGI), TU Graz (IAIK), UCL Crypto Group Louvain, K.U. Leuven COSIC, Cambridge Computer Lab.
- A hardware-implementation sub-thread covers AES S-boxes, modular arithmetic, ECC/Koblitz scalar multiplication, pairing hardware, and factoring machines (SHARK, COPACOBANA, ECM-on-FPGA).
- Elliptic-curve specific items are relatively few: 001 (SCA on ECC scalar mult), 007 (Koblitz), 008 (HECC), 059 (double-base chains), 030 (Tate pairing).
- Six later papers (079, 099, 10031111–10031132, 2013–2016, likely IACR ePrint) shift to protocols: MPC, broadcast encryption, OT, PAKE, tamper resilience.
- Anomalies: 010.pdf and 029.pdf are corrupted/garbled extractions; 05.pdf is a 1-page presentation abstract, not a full paper.

## Papers
- **001.pdf** — Resistance of Randomized Projective Coordinates Against Power Analysis (Dupuy, Kunz-Jacques). New chosen-ciphertext side-channel attack on ECC scalar multiplication that defeats classical anti-SPA/DPA when optimized NIST-curve parameters are used.
- **002.pdf** — Templates as Master Keys (Agrawal, Rao, Rohatgi, Schramm). Introduces single-bit template attacks and template-enhanced DPA, breaking masked DES/AES smartcards when a test card has a slightly biased RNG.
- **003.pdf** — A Stochastic Model for Differential Side Channel Cryptanalysis (Schindler, Lemke, Paar). Optimizes differential side-channel attacks on block ciphers by approximating the leakage function in a vector subspace with maximum-likelihood key extraction.
- **004.pdf** — A New Baby-Step Giant-Step Algorithm and Some Applications to Cryptanalysis (Coron, Lefranc, Poupard). BSGS variant for structured discrete logs; breaks a GPS identification variant whose key is a product of low-Hamming-weight subkeys.
- **005.pdf** — Further Hidden Markov Model Cryptanalysis (Green, Noad, Smart). Extends Karlof–Wagner IDHMM side-channel modeling to sub-operation errors and incomplete traces, e.g. ECC add/double confusion.
- **006.pdf** — Energy-Efficient Software Implementation of Long Integer Modular Arithmetic (Großschädl, Avanzi, Savaş, Tillich). Instruction-level performance/energy analysis of multiplication and reduction algorithms; Karatsuba-Comba-Montgomery combination wins.
- **007.pdf** — Short Memory Scalar Multiplication on Koblitz Curves (Okeya, Takagi, Vuillaume). Koblitz-curve scalar multiplication matching the fastest known speed while cutting memory use by 70–85%.
- **008.pdf** — Hardware/Software Co-design for Hyperelliptic Curve Cryptography (HECC) on the 8051 µP (Batina, Hwang, Hodjat, Preneel, Verbauwhede). HECC on an 8-bit 8051 plus small field-multiplier coprocessor; 26× speed-up over software-only.
- **009.pdf** — SHARK: A Realizable Special Hardware Sieving Device for Factoring 1024-bit Integers (Franke, Kleinjung, Paar, Pelzl, Priplata, Stahlke). Modular ASIC lattice-sieving machine estimated to sieve a 1024-bit GNFS in one year for under US$200M.
- **01.pdf** — Template Attacks in Principal Subspaces (Archambeau, Peeters, Standaert, Quisquater). Performs template attacks in the principal subspace of traces, automatically resolving point-of-interest selection; validated against RC4 and FPGA AES.
- **010.pdf** — Corrupted extract: title and body are garbled binary/encoding garbage; no content recoverable.
- **011.pdf** — Design of Testable Random Bit Generators (Bucci, Luzzi). Introduces stateless RNGs with entropy verification on post-processed output, adaptive compression, and attack detection.
- **012.pdf** — Successfully Attacking Masked AES Hardware Implementations (Mangard, Pramstaller, Oswald). Custom ASIC experiments show masked AES S-boxes still leak via glitches, enabling DPA despite masking.
- **013.pdf** — Masked Dual-Rail Pre-Charge Logic: DPA-Resistance without Routing Constraints (Popp, Mangard). MDPL logic style combining masking and dual-rail pre-charge, implementable with standard CMOS cells without balanced routing.
- **014.pdf** — Masking at Gate Level in the Presence of Glitches (Fischer, Gammel). Refines CMOS gate power models to include glitches; shows no universal glitch-secure masked gate set exists but identifies a secure family under constraints.
- **015.pdf** — Bipartite Modular Multiplication (Kaihara, Takagi). New residue representation splits the multiplier into two parts processed in parallel (interleaved + Montgomery), potentially doubling modular multiplication speed.
- **017.pdf** — Using an RSA Accelerator for Modular Inversion (Seysen). Modular inversion via plain Euclidean algorithm on double-length integers, over twice as fast as the classical method on RSA smart-card coprocessors.
- **018.pdf** — Comparison of Bit and Word Level Algorithms for Evaluating Unstructured Functions over Finite Rings (Sunar, Cyganski). Modified Horner scheme for multivariate functions; proves optimality and shows bit-level implementations beat word-level.
- **02.pdf** — Templates vs. Stochastic Methods — A Performance Analysis for Side Channel Cryptanalysis (Gierlichs, Lemke-Rust, Paar). Systematic comparison: T-test-based templates win with many profiling traces, stochastic methods with few.
- **020.pdf** — Security Limits for Compromising Emanations (Kuhn). Argues civilian RFI limits are unsuited to emission security and proposes open TEMPEST-style test limits for VHF/UHF eavesdropping.
- **021.pdf** — Security Evaluation Against Electromagnetic Analysis at Design Time (Li, Markettos, Moore). Simulation methodology to assess EM leakage of processors at design time; demodulated-emission DEMA reveals the strongest leakage.
- **022.pdf** — On Second-Order Differential Power Analysis (Joye, Paillier, Schoenmakers). Provides the first exact analysis of Messerges' second-order DPA and generalizations in the Hamming-distance model.
- **023.pdf** — Improved Higher-Order Side-Channel Attacks with FPGA Experiments (Peeters, Standaert, Donckers, Quisquater). Higher-order attacks exploiting measured power distributions defeat masked block-cipher implementations with tractable trace counts on FPGA.
- **024.pdf** — Secure Data Management in Trusted Computing (Kühn, Kursawe, Lucks, Sadeghi, Stüble). Identifies TCG sealed-data availability problems across software updates and hardware migration; proposes software and hardware fixes.
- **025.pdf** — Data Remanence in Flash Memory Devices (Skorobogatov). Shows erased EEPROM/Flash cells retain residual charge, letting attackers recover "erased" data from microcontrollers and smartcards.
- **026.pdf** — Prototype IC with WDDL and Differential Routing — DPA Resistance Assessment (Tiri, Hwang, Hodjat, Lai, Yang, Schaumont, Verbauwhede). Fabricated 0.18 µm WDDL AES chip resists DPA beyond 1.5M traces, rendering the attack practically infeasible.
- **027.pdf** — DPA Leakage Models for CMOS Logic Circuits (Suzuki, Saeki, Ichikawa). Transition-probability-based DPA leakage models for CMOS; shows weaknesses of known hardware countermeasures and suggests secure conditions.
- **028.pdf** — The "Backend Duplication" Method: A Leakage-Proof Place-and-Route Strategy for ASICs (Guilley, Hoogvorst, Mathieu, Pacalet). CAD method achieving 100% placement density and balanced dual-rail routing; demonstrated on a DES ASIC.
- **029.pdf** — Corrupted extract: content is hex-escaped garbage ("/C0/CP/D6…"); no readable title or abstract.
- **03.pdf** — Towards Security Limits in Side-Channel Attacks (Standaert, Peeters, Archambeau, Quisquater). Applies a formal framework combining security and information-theoretic metrics to quantify leakage and countermeasure effectiveness for block ciphers.
- **030.pdf** — Efficient Hardware for the Tate Pairing Calculation in Characteristic Three (Kerins, Marnane, Popovici, Barreto). Parallelizable GF(3^6m) tower-field architecture implementing the Tate pairing via a modified Duursma-Lee algorithm.
- **031.pdf** — AES on FPGA from the Fastest to the Smallest (Good, Benaissa). Two extreme AES FPGA designs: 25 Gbps pipelined throughput and a 124-slice minimal implementation.
- **032.pdf** — A Very Compact S-box for AES (Canright). Optimized subfield/basis choices and isomorphism matrices yield an AES S-box 20% smaller than the best prior compact design.
- **04.pdf** — HIGHT: A New Block Cipher Suitable for Low-Resource Device (Hong et al.). Proposes HIGHT, a 64-bit-block/128-bit-key lightweight cipher needing only 3048 gates for RFID/sensor applications.
- **041.pdf** — Adapting Density Attacks to Low-Weight Knapsacks (Nguyen, Stern). Introduces pseudo-density to show low-weight knapsacks remain vulnerable to lattice reduction; casts doubt on the OTU cryptosystem's parameters.
- **05.pdf** — Integer Factoring Utilizing PC Cluster (Aoki). One-page presentation abstract (not a full paper) arguing PC clusters are the most cost-effective hardware for record integer factorization.
- **059.pdf** — Efficient and Secure Elliptic Curve Point Multiplication using Double-Base Chains (Dimitrov, Imbert, Mishra). Double-base number system chains speed up ECC point multiplication, with side-channel atomicity providing SPA/DPA protection.
- **06.pdf** — Optically Enhanced Position-Locked Power Analysis (Skorobogatov). Laser illumination modulates single-transistor current in power traces, enabling semi-invasive reading of individual SRAM bits on a PIC16F84.
- **07.pdf** — Pinpointing the Side-Channel Leakage of Masked AES Hardware Implementations (Mangard, Schramm). Identifies XOR switching in masked multipliers as the glitch source in masked AES S-boxes; gives timing constraints to eliminate leakage.
- **079.pdf** — Upper Bounds on the Communication Complexity of Optimally Resilient Cryptographic Multiparty Computation (Hirt, Nielsen). Improves MPC communication complexity to O(cn²κ) active and O(cnκ) passive via reduced broadcast usage.
- **08.pdf** — A Generalized Method of Differential Fault Attack Against AES Cryptosystem (Moradi, Manzuri Shalmani, Salmasizadeh). Two general fault models recover the full AES-128 key with as few as 6 faulty ciphertexts.
- **09.pdf** — Breaking Ciphers with COPACOBANA — A Cost-Optimized Parallel Code Breaker (Kumar, Paar, Pelzl, Pfeiffer, Schimmler). 120-FPGA cryptanalysis machine built for under US$10,000, e.g. for exhaustive key search.
- **099.pdf** — Graph-Decomposition-Based Frameworks for Subset-Cover Broadcast Encryption and Efficient Instantiations (Attrapadung, Imai). Generic subset-cover broadcast encryption frameworks via graph decomposition and RSA accumulators, improving ciphertext and key sizes.
- **10.pdf** — Implementing the Elliptic Curve Method of Factoring in Reconfigurable Hardware (Gaj, Kwon, Baier, Kohlbrenner, Le, Khaleeluddin, Bachimanchi). Portable FPGA ECM architecture for NFS relation collection; low-cost FPGAs beat microprocessors by an order of magnitude in price-performance.
- **10031111.pdf** — Size-Hiding Computation for Multiple Parties (Shinagawa, Nuida, Nishide, Hanaoka, Okamoto). Extends size-hiding two-party results to n parties, proving infeasibility for hiding two or more sizes and giving a necessary and sufficient feasibility condition.
- **10031113.pdf** — Trick or Tweak: On the (In)security of OTR's Tweaks (Bost, Sanders). Shows OTR's tweakable-blockcipher instantiation yields colliding input masks, enabling practical one-query attacks on privacy and authenticity.
- **10031120.pdf** — Authenticated Encryption with Variable Stretch (Reyhanitabar, Vaudenay, Vizár). Defines nonce-based variable-stretch AE (nvAE), shows heuristic tag-length encodings fail, and gives secure online constructions.
- **10031130.pdf** — Adaptive Oblivious Transfer and Generalization (Blazy, Chevalier, Germouty). UC-secure adaptive OT with O(log|DB|) cost per subsequent query, plus generalizations of the technique.
- **10031131.pdf** — Structure-Preserving Smooth Projective Hashing (Blazy, Chevalier). Introduces structure-preserving smooth projective hash functions, yielding more efficient one-round PAKE and three-round OT.
- **10031132.pdf** — Public-Key Cryptosystems Resilient to Continuous Tampering and Leakage of Arbitrary Functions (anonymous, ASIACRYPT 2016 submission). First CCA-secure PKE resilient to continuous tampering with arbitrary functions plus memory leakage; impossibility result for signatures.
