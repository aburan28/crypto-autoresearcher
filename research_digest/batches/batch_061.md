# Batch 061 digest

## Overview
- This batch is almost entirely IACR conference papers from ~2003: the 2729xxxxx files match CRYPTO 2003 (LNCS 2729) and the 2887xxxxx files match FSE 2003 (LNCS 2887).
- Dominant themes: symmetric cryptanalysis (stream ciphers, block ciphers, MACs, NESSIE/AES candidates), provable security theory (UC framework, plaintext awareness, RCCA, zero-knowledge), and key-exchange protocols (SIGMA/IKE, group key exchange, braid groups).
- Notable papers: Katz–Yung scalable group key exchange, Krawczyk's SIGMA (basis of IKE), Barkan–Biham–Keller's instant GSM/A5 ciphertext-only break, Oechslin's rainbow tables, Rubin–Silverberg torus-based cryptography (CEILIDH), Cheon–Jun's polynomial-time break of braid Diffie–Hellman.
- Side-channel content is present but minority: Private Circuits (Ishai–Sahai–Wagner), unified ECC formulae attacks (Stebila–Thériault, file "28.pdf"), DES internal-collision power attack, and a hex-encoded Akkar–Goubin high-order DPA paper.
- Elliptic-curve/ECDLP material is thin here: only ECPP-based primality proving, torus-based crypto, and the ECC side-channel paper touch curve mathematics.
- Anomalies: 6 of 50 files are unusable extracts — 5 with garbled/custom-font-encoded text (28870038, 28870078, 28870093, 28870162, 28870263) and 1 with no text at all (28870107); 28870200 is hex-encoded but fully decodable.

## Papers
- **27290110.pdf** — Scalable Protocols for Authenticated Group Key Exchange (Katz, Yung). First constant-round provably secure authenticated group key exchange under DDH, via a passive-to-active compiler applied to a Burmester–Desmedt variant.
- **27290126.pdf** — Practical Verifiable Encryption and Decryption of Discrete Logarithms (Camenisch, Shoup). Practical protocols for proving properties of encrypted data, built on a Cramer–Shoup variant with Paillier.
- **27290145.pdf** — Extending Oblivious Transfers Efficiently (Ishai, Kilian, Nissim, Petrank). Efficient random-oracle-model protocols turning a few oblivious transfers into many, with a new primitive to instantiate the oracle.
- **27290162.pdf** — Algebraic Attacks on Combiners with Memory (Armknecht, Krause). Shows (k,l)-combiners admit low-degree canceling relations enabling algebraic attacks; yields the then-best attack on Bluetooth's E0 keystream generator.
- **27290177.pdf** — Fast Algebraic Attacks on Stream Ciphers with Linear Feedback (Courtois). Exploits recursive structure in algebraic equations to replace Gaussian elimination with near-linear Berlekamp–Massey-type solving, sharply cutting attack complexity.
- **27290195.pdf** — Cryptanalysis of SAFER++ (Biryukov, De Cannière, Dellkrantz). Multiset and boomerang attacks reaching 5.5 of 7 rounds — the best known results on this NESSIE cipher; methods generalize to SPNs with incomplete diffusion.
- **27290212.pdf** — A Polynomial Time Algorithm for the Braid Diffie–Hellman Conjugacy Problem (Cheon, Jun). Breaks braid-group key exchange/encryption by solving DHCP via the Lawrence–Krammer representation in polynomial time.
- **27290225.pdf** — The Impact of Decryption Failures on the Security of NTRU Encryption (Howgrave-Graham, Nguyen, Pointcheval, Proos, Silverman, Singer, Whyte). Shows NTRU decryption failures leak the private key; recommends parameter changes and padding countermeasures.
- **27290247.pdf** — Universally Composable Efficient Multiparty Computation from Threshold Homomorphic Encryption (Damgård, Nielsen). UC-secure MPC against adaptive active minority adversaries from Paillier threshold encryption, no non-committing encryption needed.
- **27290264.pdf** — Universal Composition with Joint State (Canetti, Rabin). Extends UC composition to protocol instances sharing state/randomness (e.g., same CRS or PKI), with sufficient conditions for security preservation.
- **27290281.pdf** — Statistical zero-knowledge proofs with efficient provers: lattice problems and more (Micciancio, Vadhan). Efficient-prover SZK proofs of knowledge for approximate SVP/CVP, giving lattice-based identification schemes.
- **27290298.pdf** — Derandomization in Cryptography (Barak, Ong, Vadhan). Uses Nisan–Wigderson-type generators to derandomize ZAPs and Naor's commitment, yielding one-message WI proofs and noninteractive commitments.
- **27290315.pdf** — On Deniability in the Common Reference String and Random Oracle Model (Pass). Formalizes deniable zero-knowledge; rules it out in natural CRS settings but constructs a 2-round concurrent deniable argument of knowledge in the RO model.
- **27290337.pdf** — Primality Proving via One Round in ECPP and One Iteration in AKS (Cheng). Random primality-proving algorithm in heuristic Õ(log⁴ n) time producing O(log n)-bit certificates verifiable deterministically.
- **27290348.pdf** — Torus-Based Cryptography (Rubin, Silverberg). Introduces torus-based cryptography and the CEILIDH system; gives algebro-geometric interpretation of LUC/XTR and disproves related open conjectures.
- **27290365.pdf** — Efficient Universal Padding Techniques for Multiplicative Trapdoor One-way Permutation (Komano, Ohta). Proposes OAEP-ES, OAEP++-ES, REACT-ES paddings secure under plain one-wayness, tighter than PSS-ES, with one key pair for encryption and signature.
- **27290382.pdf** — Multipurpose Identity-Based Signcryption (Boyen). Defines a stringent IB signcryption security model (CCA, insider unforgeability, anonymity, unlinkability) and gives an efficient pairing-based construction.
- **27290399.pdf** — SIGMA: the 'SIGn-and-MAc' Approach to Authenticated Diffie–Hellman and its Use in the IKE Protocols (Krawczyk). Design rationale for the SIGMA key-exchange family underlying IKEv1/v2 signature modes, with identity protection and forward secrecy.
- **27290425.pdf** — On Memory-Bound Functions for Fighting Spam (Dwork, Goldberg, Naor). Formal model, tight amortized lower bounds, and an RC4-inspired concrete memory-bound proof-of-work for spam deterrence.
- **27290444.pdf** — Lower and Upper Bounds on Obtaining History Independence (Buchbinder, Petrank). Separates weak from strong history independence with strong lower bounds for comparison-based structures (heap, queue) and matching upper bounds.
- **27290462.pdf** — Private Circuits: Securing Hardware against Probing Attacks (Ishai, Sahai, Wagner). Formal threat model and provably secure constructions for circuits whose wires can be probed — theoretical foundations against side-channel attacks.
- **27290480.pdf** — A Tweakable Enciphering Mode (Halevi, Rogaway). CMC mode: two CBC passes with a mask turn a block cipher into a tweakable strong-PRP wide-block enciphering scheme suited to disk-sector encryption.
- **27290497.pdf** — A Message Authentication Code Based on Unimodular Matrix Groups (Cary, Venkatesan). MAC built from products of determinant-±1 matrix embeddings; security analyzed via determinant arithmetic; ~0.5 GB/s preliminary performance.
- **27290510.pdf** — Luby-Rackoff: 7 Rounds are Enough for 2^{n(1-ε)} Security (Patarin). Feistel networks: 4 rounds secure against KPA, 7 against CPA, 10 against CPCA up to ~2ⁿ queries, settling an open problem.
- **27290528.pdf** — Weak Key Authenticity and the Computational Completeness of Formal Encryption (Horvitz, Gligor). Gives a necessary and sufficient "weak key authenticity" condition for computational completeness of formal (Dolev–Yao style) symmetric encryption.
- **27290546.pdf** — Plaintext Awareness via Key Registration (Herzog, Liskov, Micali). Achieves plaintext-aware encryption without random oracles using an untrusted key-registration third party, with application to the Dolev–Yao model.
- **27290563.pdf** — Relaxing Chosen-Ciphertext Security (Canetti, Krawczyk, Nielsen). Introduces Replayable CCA (RCCA) security with three mostly-equivalent formulations, sufficient for most CCA applications while admitting natural non-CCA schemes.
- **27290581.pdf** — Password Interception in a SSL/TLS Channel (Canvel, Hiltgen, Vaudenay, Vuagnoux). Extends padding-oracle attacks to real SSL/TLS CBC implementations, showing IMAP passwords can be intercepted within an hour.
- **27290598.pdf** — Instant Ciphertext-Only Cryptanalysis of GSM Encrypted Communication (Barkan, Biham, Keller). Ciphertext-only break of A5/2 in under a second, extended to A5/1 and protocol-level attacks on GSM/GPRS networks.
- **27290615.pdf** — Making a Faster Cryptanalytic Time-Memory Trade-Off (Oechslin). Introduces the rainbow-table precomputation structure, halving cryptanalysis work; cracks 99.9% of Windows alphanumeric password hashes in seconds.
- **28.pdf** — Unified Point Addition Formulæ and Side-Channel Attacks (Stebila, Thériault). Improves Walter's attack to break Brier–Déchène–Joye unified ECC formulae under standard field arithmetic; suggests more uniform arithmetic countermeasures.
- **288.pdf** — Universally Anonymizable Public-Key Encryption (Hayashi, Tanaka). Defines universal anonymizability (anyone can add key-privacy to ciphertexts without decryption) and realizes it from ElGamal, Cramer–Shoup, and RSA-OAEP.
- **28870010.pdf** — Differential-Linear Cryptanalysis of Serpent (Biham, Dunkelman, Keller). Best known attack on 11-round Serpent (2^125.3 chosen plaintexts) and first attack on 10-round 128-bit-key Serpent.
- **28870023.pdf** — Rectangle Attacks on 49-Round SHACAL-1 (Biham, Dunkelman, Keller). Rectangle attack on 49 of 80 rounds of SHACAL-1, the best known; also fixes flaws in earlier SHACAL-1 attacks.
- **28870038.pdf** — Unusable extract: text is garbled by custom font encoding; title and content unreadable.
- **28870048.pdf** — Analysis of Involutional Ciphers: Khazad and Anubis (Biryukov). Studies SPNs built from involutions; shows 5-round Khazad has 2^64 weak keys under a slide-with-a-twist distinguisher.
- **28870057.pdf** — On plateaued functions and their constructions (Carlet, Prouff). Characterizes bent and plateaued Boolean functions via covering sequences, shows a claimed new class is Maiorana–McFarland, and constructs a new class.
- **28870078.pdf** — Unusable extract: slash-separated glyph codes (custom font encoding); content unreadable.
- **28870093.pdf** — Unusable extract: garbled custom-encoded text; title and content unreadable.
- **28870107.pdf** — No text extracted; content cannot be determined.
- **28870119.pdf** — Cryptanalysis of Sober-t32 (Babbage, De Cannière, Lano, Preneel, Vandewalle). Guess-and-determine attack on Sober-t32 without stuttering, plus two distinguishing attacks on full Sober-t32 better than exhaustive search.
- **28870137.pdf** — OMAC: One-Key CBC MAC (Iwata, Kurosawa). One-key CBC MAC secure for arbitrary message lengths, improving on XCBC's three keys and TMAC's two; later standardized as CMAC.
- **28870162.pdf** — Unusable extract: slash-separated glyph codes (custom font encoding); content unreadable.
- **28870178.pdf** — New attacks against standardized MACs (Joux, Poupard, Stern). Forgery and key-recovery attacks on ISO/IEC 9797-1 MAC algorithms with 64-bit block ciphers; breaks all but one scheme in the norm.
- **28870190.pdf** — Analysis of RMAC (Knudsen, Kohno). Related-key and generic attacks on NIST's RMAC mode beating claimed security bounds; argues against modes using related keys.
- **28870200.pdf** — Generic Protection against High-Order Differential Power Analysis (Akkar, Goubin). Countermeasure securing smart-card implementations against higher-order DPA. (Extract is hex-encoded but fully decodable.)
- **28870216.pdf** — A New Class of Collision Attacks and its Application to DES (Schramm, Wollinger, Paar). Uses power analysis to detect internal collisions in adjacent DES S-boxes, recovering ~10 key bits from as few as 140 encryptions; demonstrated on a smart card.
- **28870234.pdf** — Further Observations on the Structure of the AES Algorithm (Song, Seberry). Analyzes cyclic periods of AES layers; combined nonlinear+linear layers have long periods, giving no algebraic cryptanalytic foothold.
- **28870248.pdf** — Optimal Key Ranking Procedures in a Statistical Cryptanalysis (Junod, Vaudenay). Neyman–Pearson-based framework for optimally combining subkey information; proves weighted Euclidean norm of bias vector is optimal for linear cryptanalysis key ranking.
- **28870263.pdf** — Unusable extract: slash-separated glyph codes (custom font encoding); appears to be a paper front-matter page but is unreadable.
