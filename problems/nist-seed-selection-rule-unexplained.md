## Problem

We cannot explain why eleven otherwise qualifying prime-field curves were passed over in the six timestamp streams reported by Valsorda and Anthropic, because the original candidate-acceptance code, its state and a complete run log are unavailable. The tested set is the eleven unpublished candidates explicitly named in those reports: five over the 239-bit ANSI field, two over P-192's field, one over P-256's field and three over P-224's field. A historical selection conclusion must classify the published and omitted candidates under one independently specified rule, rather than infer motive from the final constants alone.

## Evidence and uncertainty

- **Observed:** [The audit code](../research/nist_seed_skipped_curves_20261016/audit.py) reproduces all ten published 160-bit seeds, reconstructs all eleven omitted seeds and checks their SHA-1-derived curve equations, prime group orders and Hasse uniqueness. The [exact records](../research/nist_seed_skipped_curves_20261016/assessed.json) and [PDF](../research/nist_seed_skipped_curves_20261016/report.pdf) report embedding-degree and bounded CM screens, twist factors, and published-curve controls. All eleven pass the visible order-bit-length rule. The [first failed indexing attempt](../research/nist_seed_skipped_curves_20261016/audit_attempt1.log) is preserved; its corrected run is [here](../research/nist_seed_skipped_curves_20261016/audit_attempt2.log).
- **Derived or proved:** Each tested skipped class has a prime-order group with cofactor one and no same-field group-order match to a published curve. Three omitted 239-bit curves have `n>p`, but so does published prime239v2, so `n<p` cannot be the universal missing rule. These facts are bounded to the named streams and candidates.
- **Bounded selector counterexample:** [The P-224 follow-up](../research/nist_seed_selection_followup_20261016/report.md) verifies that passed-over block 1485 has a 122-bit largest prime factor of its quadratic-twist order, versus 118 bits for published P-224. The published curve was not selected by maximizing this metric among the four known qualifying P-224 blocks. At that stage 19 of the 21 twist orders were fully factored; the 2026-10-23 round below completed the other two. This excludes one proposed preference, not every twist predicate.
- **New derived process constraints:** [The process audit](../research/nist_seed_generation_process_20261016/report.md) checks that the usual exponentiation square-root sign is the prime-order sign for all 17 reported qualifying blocks in fields with `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)`; the four P-224 blocks have isomorphic coefficient signs. It also checks the ordinary X9.62 order-size and MOV screens for all 21 reported blocks. For exact consecutive 20-draw `lrand48` blocks, the full-period recurrence and `gcd(20,2^48)=4` bound the possible candidate-start states to `2^46`, regardless of timestamp retries; extra draws between otherwise consecutive 20-byte windows increase the state ceiling to `2^48`. These ceilings do not cover interleaved byte assembly, are not counts of actual attempts, and do not explain the historical acceptance path.
- **New bounded commonality result (2026-10-23):** [The fixed 21-feature comparison](../research/nist_seed_commonality_20261023/report.md), read with its [additive metadata erratum](../research/nist_seed_commonality_20261023/erratum-20261023.md), found no global threshold or perfect within-field ordering that separates the ten published from eleven passed-over classes. Exact trial division of all 21 Frobenius discriminants by odd primes at most 10,000 found small squared factors in only three rows: omitted 239-bit block 2682 and published prime239v2 (both at 5), plus published P-224 (at 3). Thus a small conductor divisor in this range is not shared by all omissions. The leading coefficient-bit-weight association is 22/25 within-field pairs, but fails in P-224 and has no attack interpretation. The same study completed the two remaining 239-bit quadratic-twist factorizations under a frozen cap, checked by PARI/GP primality and Sage proof-enabled primality plus exact multiplication; the named 21-curve set now has complete recorded twist factorizations. These are arithmetic exclusions and coverage improvements, not a historical decision trace or weak-curve finding.
- **Further bounded screen (2026-10-23):** [An exploratory rational-isogeny screen](../research/nist_seed_commonality_20261023/isogeny_screen/report.md) checked all 1,228 odd prime degrees at most 10,000 on the eleven omitted and ten published exact classes. Sage independently reproduced all 25,788 Frobenius-character entries and the allocation diagnostics. No single degree's rational-isogeny availability or absence perfectly separates the labels. Degrees 2,099 and 9,013 are inert for all eleven omissions, but also for three and six published controls, respectively; 853 of 4,480 within-field label allocations have at least two such all-omission-inert primes when all degrees are searched. A retrospective count comparison puts omissions higher in 20 of 25 same-field pairs (one tie, four reversals), with a fixed-cutoff one-sided allocation tail of 100/4,480; omitted P-256 block 876 reverses that trend against published P-256. The count cutoff was explored after prior smaller-bound work, is not an SEA runtime measurement, and supplies no universal selector, map or advantage.
- **Exact CM-field exclusion (2026-10-23):** [The CM-field screen](../research/nist_seed_commonality_20261023/cm_pair_screen/report.md) computed `Δ=t²−4p` directly from the archived `p,n` for all 21 seed-level classes. An independent Sage check verified all 210 pairwise perfect-square tests, three raw P-224 same-invariant positive controls, and 6,383,328 tests against every negative fundamental discriminant of magnitude at most 1,000,000. No two of the 21 share the same imaginary quadratic CM field, and none has one in that explicit small-discriminant range. This implies every non-scalar endomorphism has degree greater than 250,000, including on an unlisted `F_p`-isogenous endpoint or quadratic twist, which preserves the source CM field. This is a degree bound, not an evaluation-time or ECDLP-cost bound. Among 45 named pairs over the same field prime, none share group order, have opposite trace, or share `j`, so the named curves have no direct rational-isogeny or quadratic-twist pairing. These findings also hold for published controls, so they are not a keep/skip rule. Representative-specific unlisted endpoints and mechanisms spanning different CM fields remain open.
- **Low-class-number list exclusion (2026-10-23):** [The class-number screen](../research/nist_seed_commonality_20261023/class_number_screen/report.md) pinned Kimura's 2026-10-22 released list of 16,453,493 imaginary quadratic fields with class number at most 2,000 by its published SHA-256, then performed 345,523,353 exact discriminant/square tests on the 21 curves. Both synthetic positives and a negative control behaved as expected; Sage separately checked all archived `Δ` values. There are zero CM-field matches among the eleven omissions **and** ten published controls. Conditional on that new paper's completeness theorem and released tables, a low CM-field class-number rule through 2,000 cannot be the missing selector; all 21 would also pass the later ETSI minimum of 200. The paper's broad computational sieve and input tables were not independently reproduced here. ANSI X9.62-1998 did not specify this class-number screen. Neither the exact arithmetic nor the external theorem explains historical evaluation or another weakness mechanism.
- **ANSI base-point workflow exclusion (2026-10-23):** [The source-based check](../research/nist_seed_selection_workflow_20261023/report.md) applies the public 1998 Annex A.3.2 order, MOV, and anomalous gates to all 21 archived prime-field classes. All satisfy the recorded cofactor-one prime-order premise, the standard's floor inequalities, and MOV through `B=100`. For a prime-order full group, any nonidentity point has the required order; therefore a correct execution of Annex A.3.1 cannot reject one of the eleven for a curve-specific base-point weakness. This is conditional on the correct standard routine and the archive's order certificates, and says nothing about unrecovered 1997 code, RNG faults, timeouts, or private criteria.
- **Exact conductor counterexample (2026-10-23):** [The frozen first pair](../research/nist_seed_exact_conductor_20261023/report.md) fully factored `t²−4p` for omitted P-256 block 876 and published P-256 block 1842. Sage proved every factor prime and verified `D_K=Delta_pi`, `f_pi=1` in both, after three synthetic arithmetic controls and two fail-closed launcher controls. Thus a nontrivial Frobenius-order conductor cannot be shared by all eleven omissions; for this omitted class `f_E=1` on every same-field isogenous representative. This does not test another weakness or identify the historical selection rule.
- **Invalid-curve conditional exclusion (2026-10-23):** [The frozen nineteen-curve check](../research/nist_seed_invalid_curve_surface_20261023/report.md) found `a=-3`, nonzero `b`, and a common `E_0:y²=x³−3x`, `Q=(0,0)` order-two off-curve witness in each same-field published and omitted candidate; Sage separately checked all rows. In the specified unvalidated affine short-Weierstrass arithmetic model, the alternate-curve input family depends on `p,a` and is shared regardless of configured `b`. Therefore a unique invalid-curve input surface caused by the omitted coefficient alone cannot distinguish the labels in that model. It does not test a deployed implementation, show protocol leakage, or establish why the original program skipped anything.
- **Complete rational twist-form classification (2026-10-23):** [The exact 21-curve audit](../research/nist_seed_twist_classification_20261023/report.md) checked `p>3`, `a=-3`, `b≠0`, nonsingularity, and `j∉{0,1728}` for all eleven omissions and ten published controls, with UID-bound twist orders matching the prior complete twist archive. Independent Sage verified the five field primes, recomputed all 21 `j` values, and enumerated exactly two rational forms per geometric class: the curve and its quadratic twist. Therefore no quartic or sextic twist form is left uninspected for these exact curves. Wrong-curve inputs outside their geometric isomorphism classes and hidden ordinary-group mechanisms remain outside this conclusion. The original acceptance trace remains missing.
- **Exact embedding-degree exclusion (2026-10-23):** [The frozen nineteen-curve calculation](../research/nist_seed_exact_embedding_degree_20261023/report.md) used complete, previously certified `n−1` factorizations to compute every `k=ord_n(p)` with minimality witnesses; Sage separately agreed for all nineteen. The smallest exact `k` has 189 bits and none is at or below 1,000,000, so a small-MOV-degree threshold through that fixed bound rejects no named omitted candidate. The failed first launcher is preserved separately and did not compute curve results. This removes one specified attack-precondition selector, not every pairing or finite-field algorithm and not the historical unknown.
- **Suspected explanations:** The original program may have consumed RNG output during validation, may have had an implementation error, or may have applied an unpublished test. These are alternatives, not observed causes. [Valsorda](https://words.filippo.io/seeds-solved/) and [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins) report no resolved selection criterion.
- **Unspecified retrospective source:** [Appendix E of the President's Review Group's 2013 report](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/about/civil-liberties/resources/liberty_security_prgfinalreport.pdf) says the appendix was supplied with information from NSA and states that a class of potentially weak elliptic-curve parameters was excluded from a NIST standard. It does not identify the class, candidate curves, period, or program. The statement cannot be mapped to any of the eleven omissions; it is a lead to the underlying briefing or contemporaneous record, not evidence that they were screened for a particular hidden weakness.
- **Retrospective source comparison (2026-10-23):** [The source-bound audit](../research/nist_seed_1997_draft_archive_20261023/report.md) places that 2013 sentence in its ECDH / SP 800-56A context and compares it with a 2001 SEC 1 ECDH evaluation. The latter names MOV/Frey–Rück, anomalous, and binary-field Weil-descent classes; exact prime-field data exclude the first two for all eleven, and binary-field descent does not apply to their fields. There is no documented link between the 2001 list and the 2013 NSA-supplied assertion. This narrows public candidate interpretations but does not resolve the asserted unnamed class.
- **Contemporary document lead (2026-10-23):** [A primary-source archive check](../research/nist_seed_1997_draft_archive_20261023/report.md) found two IETF documents that cite an ANSI X9.62 ECDSA working draft dated 17 November 1997. Neither includes its curve table or acceptance trace. An Internet Archive query for one IEEE file-prefix path found only metadata for the later September 1998 draft with inaccessible archived responses; it does not prove the 1997 draft is absent elsewhere. Recovering the named 1997 draft and adjacent parameter/change sheets is now a specific acquisition target. The draft's actual contents, including whether P-256 or any omitted candidate appears, remain unverified.
- **Later workflow context (2026-10-23):** [Baier and Buchmann's 2002 generation report](../research/nist_seed_1997_draft_archive_20261023/report.md) describes prime-group, anomalous, and MOV gates, plus SEA early abort when a small prime divisor rules out the allowed cofactor. A correct version of those gates accepts the eleven omitted prime-order curves under their archived properties; a small-prime early abort cannot correctly reject one. The 2002 source is not the original 1997 implementation or an acceptance log, so it cannot exclude an implementation error, extra criterion, or unvisited windows.
- **Auxiliary-input arithmetic exclusion (2026-10-23):** [The exact divisor screen](../research/nist_seed_aux_input_divisors_20261023/report.md) used all 38 verified `n±1` factorizations and three frozen divisor-opportunity metrics. The omitted curves are more favorable in only 5/25 same-field pairs for the `n−1` bound, 12/25 for the `n+1` cube-root bound, and 11/25 for the data-limited `n+1` bound. Thus none is a universal omitted-curve property. Cheon's published auxiliary-input attack requires secret-power points absent from ordinary ECDLP, ECDSA and ECDH inputs, and postdates 1997; the arithmetic screen neither identifies a weakness nor attributes a selection rule.
- **Reproduced original correspondence and committee rationale (2026-10-23):** [The source-linked historical report](../research/nist_seed_1997_draft_archive_20261023/report.md) records the 2014–15 Solinas–Bernstein exchange as reproduced by its recipient: Solinas identified Bob Reiter as the programmer, qualified his phrase-hashing recollection, and reported that neither man had found the old archives. Koblitz and Menezes's later account says concern after the 1997 anomalous-curve attack motivated ANSI X9F1 to include randomly generated curves. These sources narrow where to look and explain the public design rationale; neither gives a candidate-level rejection rule. The numerically reconstructed `lrand48` prime seeds prevent using the tentative phrase recollection as a complete explanation of the seed bytes.
- **CM genus-rank exclusion (2026-10-23):** [The nineteen-curve derived check](../research/nist_seed_genus_rank_20261023/report.md) applies Gauss genus theory to the complete, previously certified fundamental CM-field discriminant factorizations. Class-group 2-ranks overlap at 2, 3, and 4 across omitted and published labels; all nineteen class numbers are even. Thus exact rank, parity, and a global rank threshold do not explain the omissions. This is a property of the CM field's maximal order, not an ECDLP shortcut or a historical decision record; three listed representatives may have nonmaximal endomorphism orders.
- **Singular-companion exclusion (2026-10-23):** [The exact nineteen-curve audit](../research/nist_seed_singular_companions_20261023/report.md) derives the only two singular formula-compatible cubics for `a=-3`, with `b'=±2`, and counts each smooth locus as `p−1` or `p+1` using quadratic characters. Every omitted and published curve within a fixed field has the same two choices and orders. A separate Sage check verified all nineteen identities and four small-field enumerations. This excludes an omitted-`b`-specific singular-input surface under unvalidated `b`-independent formulas; it says nothing about actual 1997 code, a deployed protocol, or an ordinary-subgroup attack.
- **Missing capability:** Contemporaneous source code, logs or a retained state trace for the candidate generator and acceptance decision. A present-day reimplementation cannot recover those historical facts by itself.
- **What this evidence does not establish:** It does not establish how many timestamps were tried, how many candidate windows the program evaluated, a deliberate weak-curve choice, the absence of a costly hidden route, or a universal rule from a bounded trait screen. The `2^46` and `2^48` ceilings do not cover earlier choice of field prime, a different seed-generation rule, or a hidden algorithm applying to honestly generated curves. Anthropic explicitly says the surviving numbers do not count generator runs.

## What would unblock it

- [ ] Recover contemporaneous code/logs or independent documentation that specifies the acceptance path, RNG consumption and decision for all named accepted candidates in the six streams, and replay it from the stated timestamps to the published seed ordinals.
- [ ] If no historical artifact is recovered, predeclare a deterministic alternative rule with executable code, parameters and inputs, then show that it classifies all eleven omissions and all ten published seeds and correctly predicts at least one independent held-out historical stream or decision not used to construct it. Record counterexamples as well as matches. The 2026-10-23 fixed 21-feature family did not meet this criterion.

The first condition would establish the operational selection mechanism if the artifact's provenance is sound. The second would establish a predictive reconstruction, but would still require external provenance to claim it was the actual 1997–98 program. Neither condition by itself establishes an ECDLP advantage or rules out a separate hidden mathematical route.
