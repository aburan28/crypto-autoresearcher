## Problem

The requested complete end-to-end comparison for prime, Koblitz, and ordinary binary curves with subgroup order near 2^50–2^63 and decomposition arities 2–8 cannot yet be established: this checkout has several independently reviewed complete Koblitz N53 panels, with the ARM-composition receipt the current reference, but the prime-field complete-solve arity ladder stops at 32 bits and m=7, and an ordinary-binary complete-solve baseline at the requested size is not identified. The original acceptance criterion is a verified measured improvement over each applicable index-calculus baseline with a whole-problem `S` including base construction, relation yield, useful rank, linear algebra, target work and verification; every row also retains matched rho distance.

**Size clarification:** The N53 Koblitz panel is a 53-bit *field* with a 45-bit prime target subgroup. Exact screening of the two standard binary Koblitz curves at field degrees 50–63 finds no prime subgroup of order at least `2^50`; therefore it is not a measured Koblitz baseline in that prime-subgroup-order range. Keep ambient field degree and target order as separate grid coordinates.

## Evidence and uncertainty

- **Observed:** [RUN-KIC-e3dbed](../research/cold_single_ic_20260921/REPORT.md), [RUN-KIC-8b5038](../research/cold_capacity_ic_20260921/REPORT.md), [RUN-KIC-56c897](../research/cold_target_stop_ic_20260921/REPORT.md), and [RUN-KIC-a15078](../research/cold_arm_composition_ic_20260921/REPORT.md) are four distinct reviewed N53 panels. The last composed implementation is the current same-curve/size reference, with its own matched strengthened-rho comparator; cross-panel ratios cannot be chained. The [prime arity ladder](../src/crypto_autoresearcher/index_calculus/README.md) completed m=2..7 through 32 bits and states that its compact numpy path stops below 2^32. [GF(2^51) relation study](../research/conductor_relation_yield_20261014/OBSTRUCTION.md) names an exact arity-3 collector/checker and point-map evaluator gap; it is not a full ECDLP pipeline.
- **Derived:** Corrected 2026-10-16 after [PR #2309](https://github.com/aburan28/crypto-autoresearcher/pull/2309): the tail source's sign-symmetric sorted tuples have upper count `2^(h-1) * binomial(B+h-1,h)`. For a hypothetical wide array with 64-bit x and 64-bit packed code, that tuple upper bound yields about 1.58 GiB at 2^50 and 136.96 GiB at 2^63 for m=8, versus 11.32 GiB and 1,890.96 GiB for m=7. These are *upper estimates for one proposed array payload*, not measured memory or lower bounds. The published report previously used `B^h` and eight-byte entries; that overcounted tails and understated wide-entry size. The existing vectorized path has no >32-bit implementation, and smaller bases or different algorithms change the estimates.
- **Observed preparation, 2026-10-17:** [50/53/63-bit prime-order fixtures](../research/icperf_prime_fixtures_20261017/) now have independent group-order witness checks. Exact [divisor-fit data](../research/icperf_explicit_divisor_20261017/divisor_fit.json) show the fixed 53-bit field lacks a divisor within 15% of `2B` at every `m=2..8`, while the 63-bit field has one at `m=3..8`. The [archived 63-bit m=8 explicit-coset base](../research/icperf_explicit_divisor_20261017/n63_m8_subgroup_seed20261017.json) has 380 realized points and an independently replayed membership/point list. The new constructor avoids trial-factoring all of `p-1` when `d` is supplied. These are preparation and correctness observations, not relations, rank, solver degree or complete-cost measurements.
- **Derived size screen, 2026-10-17:** The [exact Koblitz recurrence and factorization record](../research/icperf_koblitz_size_screen_20261017/orders.json) covers both standard `a∈{0,1}` curves at every field degree 50–63. Its largest prime factor is below `2^50` in all 28 cells. The reviewed N53 `a=0` prime order is 21,044,858,204,113, exactly matching the record; this is a 45-bit subgroup. This corrects the earlier ambiguity between field and subgroup size; it is not a performance observation.
- **Prospective Koblitz subgroup choices, 2026-10-17:** A [bounded search and exact prime-divisor record](../research/icperf_koblitz_subgroup_candidates_20261017/candidates.json) certifies `r=5,513,228,015,079,457` on `a=0,n=71`, `r=86,020,738,150,056,119` on `a=0,n=73`, and `r=5,384,682,420,498,870,703` on `a=1,n=73`. Each lies in `2^50…2^63` and divides the exact Frobenius group order. Empty search rows are bounded unknown, not exclusions. Explicit cofactor-projected generators for those three primes are now in the [fixture record](../research/icperf_koblitz_subgroup_fixtures_20261017/fixtures.json) and [Sage replay](../research/icperf_koblitz_subgroup_fixtures_20261017/sage_replay.json); held-out target schedules and whole-solve IC fixtures remain missing.
- **Ordinary-binary subgroup input, 2026-10-17:** The [exact 30-curve GF(2^61) screen](../research/icperf_ordinary_binary_fixture_20261017/screen.json) selected a non-descended `E_b: y²+xy=x³+b`, `b=31`, with `#E=4r` and prime `r=576,460,752,557,211,763`. The [explicit point fixture](../research/icperf_ordinary_binary_fixture_20261017/fixture.json) and [independent Sage replay](../research/icperf_ordinary_binary_fixture_20261017/sage_replay.json) check the subgroup generator; its full order also follows from the unique multiple of `r` in the Hasse interval. This supplies an input, not a baseline measurement.
- **Ordinary-binary candidate base, 2026-10-17:** A [target-blind four-offset affine F₂-subspace preparation](../research/icperf_binary_subspace_base_20261017/base.json) on that curve selected 249 order-`r` abscissae for m=8, generated an equal-size random-x control, and retained all misses and setup resources. Its affine-x **prefilter** has 13 nonzero field terms, all Frobenius-linear or constant; exact membership additionally needs liftability and the order-`r` subgroup predicate. A [Sage replay](../research/icperf_binary_subspace_base_20261017/sage_replay.json) independently checked every offset, accepted x list and control stream. The missing predicate cost, degree of regularity, relation supply, rank, solver cost and full ECDLP cost are still unknown.
- **Suspected explanation:** Wide-prime table representation and memory may be practical bottlenecks for a direct extension; the upper-bound estimates do not prove they are. Whether a streaming join, alternative base density, or solver-aware structured base yields a lower complete cost is unknown.
- **Missing capability:** A bounded >32-bit prime-field complete-solve runner; a target-blind, charged field/base-selection protocol for the sparse-coset arm, including a different 53-bit field if matched size there is required; a verified ordinary-binary 50–63-bit complete-solve runner; held-out target schedules and complete-solve runners for the certified higher-degree Koblitz prime-subgroup fixtures if the requested size coordinate is subgroup order; and a frozen high-arity direct `S_8` / chained `S_3` comparison that distinguishes seven from eight summands.
- **What this evidence does not establish:** It does not rule out useful factor bases, high-arity decompositions, or end-to-end improvements. A capacity estimate is not a solver runtime or a failed search. Separate families and target panels cannot be pooled into a single speed ratio.

## What would unblock it

- [ ] A committed, runnable frozen protocol identifies at least one exact public curve, prime subgroup and known-answer target schedule per family in the 50–63-bit range, with independently checked scalar recovery and matched rho on the identical unseen points.
- [ ] Its source and receipts support arities 2–8 or preserve a distinct, explicit `unavailable`, `censored`, or `invalid` result for each unexecuted cell; direct `S_8` and eight-summand `S_9` or chained systems have unambiguous labels. Resource caps, seeds, controls and stopping rules are frozen before trials.
- [ ] At least one candidate/base comparison records target-blind base selection, exact realized size and digest, input and observed solving degree, sparsity, every attempt, verified useful rank, complete phase costs, peak memory, and independent replay. A quantitative improvement claim passes `tools.progress_fidelity validate` with a matched baseline and the remaining rho gap.

The first two conditions establish a trustworthy baseline matrix and honest censoring. The third would establish a scoped candidate comparison if it passes review. None alone proves scaling beyond the tested curve, size or target schedule.

## Next discriminating step

- **Action or proof obligation:** Freeze a three-family pilot with the existing Koblitz N53 comparator **as a 53-bit-field/45-bit-subgroup row**, a certified prime fixture and the [explicit ordinary-binary GF(2^61) fixture](../research/icperf_ordinary_binary_fixture_20261017/fixture.json), plus memory preflight for m=2..8. If the target range means prime subgroup order `2^50…2^63`, select one of the [explicitly generated and independently replayed](../research/icperf_koblitz_subgroup_fixtures_20261017/) `a=0,n=71/73` or `a=1,n=73` prime-subgroup fixtures; do not relabel N53. For the prime sparse-coset m=8 arm, use the archived 63-bit 380-point base and equal-realized-size controls, or predeclare and charge selection of a new 50–53-bit field with a suitable divisor. For the ordinary-binary m=8 arm, use the archived 249-point affine-subspace base and equal-size random control. Implement the missing exact runners needed for admission and pair each candidate base with its equation formulation.
- **Inputs and controls:** Public known-answer targets held out from factor-base selection; size-matched random and affine-placement bases; planted correctness and independent point-sum checks; same-point matched rho; rank/duplicate controls. Keep Koblitz, ordinary binary and prime-field representations separate.
- **Execution limits and stop condition:** A Coordinator-approved finite fixture/seed grid and justified per-process memory watchdog; do not infer a campaign budget cap. Stop a cell on its declared resource or time boundary and preserve the partial record as censored.
- **Outcomes and next actions:** A complete candidate improvement advances to independent review and full-cost claim validation. A complete non-improvement redirects base/solver co-design in the exact tested scope. A timeout narrows the implementation/capacity question. An invalid certificate triggers a new source/protocol revision before any repeat.
- **Recheck trigger:** Publication of a runnable full-width runner, a charged matched-size prime-base selection contract, an m=8/direct-`S_8` experiment contract, or a verified 50–63-bit complete-solve receipt.

A planned step is not a completed experiment. Apply existing execution, review, budget and archival rules before acting on it.
