# Binary-majority calibration derivation

Status: unreviewed analytical manuscript. Not a formal proof certificate,
experiment result, accepted finding, or claimed novelty. Associated draft
producer: TASK-20260905-a3f90a. Planned snapshot: TASK-20260905-b77302.

## 1. Definitions and exact candidate identity

Let G be a finite abelian group of order n and f:G->{-1,+1}. X,Y are
independent uniform group elements. Set

    mu = E f(X)
    tau = E f(X) f(Y) f(X+Y)
    q(f) = max_F Pr[f(X+Y) = F(f(X),f(Y))]
    p_max = (1+|mu|)/2.

The maximum is over all functions F:{-1,+1}^2->{-1,+1}; F has no
implementation-cost restriction in this mathematical definition.
The population probability uses all ordered pairs, including the identity.

For a,b,c in {-1,+1}, indicator expansion gives

    Pr[f(X)=a, f(Y)=b, f(X+Y)=c]
    = [1 + mu(a+b+c) + mu^2(ab+ac+bc) + abc tau]/8.

Indeed every pair among X,Y,X+Y is independent and uniform: each relevant
change of two group variables is a bijection. This does not make the triple
independent. Taking the best c separately for each input pair yields

    q(f) = 1/2 + (1/8) sum_{a,b=+-1}
                         |mu + mu^2(a+b) + ab tau|.               (1)

At mu=0, (1) reads q=1/2+|tau|/2. Exact balance is impossible for a binary
sign on an odd-order group, so the unbalanced formula must not be dropped.

At tau=mu^3 the expanded law is the product of three sign laws of mean mu,
and (1) gives p_max. Changing tau changes the four absolute-value arguments
with slope of absolute value one. Therefore

    0 <= q(f)-p_max <= |tau-mu^3|/2.                              (2)

The lower bound follows independently by choosing a constant predictor.
No claim about computational recovery of a group coordinate follows from (1).

## 2. Controls against marginal confusion

For f constant, mu=+-1 and tau=mu^3: q=p_max=1.

For odd n>=5 let b(x)=1 exactly at x=0, and consider its two labels
(equivalently f=2b-1). The optimal successes, separated by input labels, are

    (1,1): 1
    (1,0): n-1
    (0,1): n-1
    (0,0): (n-1)(n-2).

Thus q=(n^2-n+1)/n^2 while p_max=(n-1)/n, giving

    q-1/2 -> 1/2, but q-p_max = 1/n^2.

This is a false-positive control for the assertion that q-1/s measures
nontrivial propagation without balancing or subtracting the largest fiber.
An affine elliptic variant tests x(P)=x(P0), a fiber of size two for a
nonzero point of odd order; constant prediction already succeeds with
probability 1-2/n. No untested degree hypothesis is needed for this issue.

The repository already records marginal artifacts. This manuscript does not
claim their discovery. Its role is an exact calibration against a broad
unbalanced reading of the pending proposal.

## 3. An origin-sensitive pair with equal marginals and power spectrum

Let n=4m+1 be prime, m>=1, and G=Z/nZ. Let f_I be +1 on {0,...,2m}
and -1 elsewhere. Let f_C(k)=f_I(k+m), positive on {-m,...,m}.
Both means equal mu=1/n. Translation preserves every Fourier magnitude.

For f_I, f_I(-x)=-f_I(x) for every nonzero x, and f_I(0)=1.
In the sum defining n^2 tau, terms with x,y,x+y all nonzero cancel
under (x,y)->(-x,-y). On the union x=0, y=0 or x+y=0, inclusion-exclusion
gives total n. Consequently tau_I=1/n, and (1) yields

    q(f_I) = (n+1)/(2n) = p_max.                                 (3)

For S={-m,...,m}, the ordinary integer sum of two elements lies in
[-2m,2m], so no wrap ambiguity occurs modulo 4m+1. The count of pairs
x,y in S with x+y in S is

    M = (2m+1)^2 - 2[1+...+m] = 3m^2+3m+1.

Writing f_C=2*1_S-1, with s=2m+1, gives

    tau_C = 8M/n^2 - 12s^2/n^2 + 6s/n - 1
          = (n^2+1)/(2n^2).

Here tau_C>mu and mu+tau_C-2mu^2>0. The signs in (1) therefore give

    q(f_C) = 1/2 + tau_C/2 = 3/4 + 1/(4n^2).                    (4)

At n=13, (3) gives 7/13 and (4) gives 127/169. These are algebraic
specializations, not numerical run outcomes. The centered-interval count
and simplification were also checked by polynomial simplification in the
root conversation; that checks algebra, not the complete argument.

The two functions have the same size of each fiber, the same complete power
spectrum, and different majority rates. Thus neither marginal matching nor
Fourier magnitudes alone determine q. The earlier f558e4 proposal already
notes a weak binary uncentered-interval control. The exact translated pair
is offered for review, with no external novelty claim.

The planted sequence (-1)^k on any odd cyclic group is also odd away from
zero. It has mu=tau=1/n, so its majority rate equals p_max, despite its
large nonzero Fourier amplitude. The current POS-A tests amplitude only;
passing that gate does not calibrate a positive majority excess.

## 4. Translation-optimized bounds

Use normalized coefficients

    fhat(chi) = E_x f(x) conjugate(chi(x)).

For a nontrivial finite abelian group define A_* as max_{chi != 1}|fhat(chi)|.
Let f_t(x)=f(x+t). Its mean is mu and its Fourier coefficients are
chi(t) fhat(chi). Convolution and Parseval give

    tau_t = sum_chi |fhat(chi)|^2 fhat(chi) chi(t),
    E_t |tau_t|^2 = sum_chi |fhat(chi)|^6.                        (5)

The sum in the first line is real because conjugate characters pair.
Equation (5) implies some t has |tau_t|>=A_*^3.
The product predictor F(a,b)=ab or its negation attains
(1+|tau_t|)/2. Comparing with p_max yields the lower bound below.

For the upper bound, the zero-frequency term is mu^3 and

    |tau_t-mu^3|
       <= A_* sum_{chi != 1}|fhat(chi)|^2
       = A_*(1-mu^2).

Combining with (2),

    (A_*^3-|mu|)_+/2
      <= max_t [q(f_t)-p_max]
      <= A_*(1-mu^2)/2.                                         (6)

Quantifiers: for every finite nontrivial abelian G and every binary f,
there exists a translation with the lower-bound property. This is NOT
a claim that a cheap elliptic statistic with large A_* exists. It is NOT
a claim that a useful translation is supplied at zero cost.

Constant functions yield A_*=0 and both sides zero. The origin-sensitive
pair shows why a bound at one translation cannot substitute for the maximum.

## 5. Scope and charged costs

These formulas concern a population statistic on a finite group. The group
can be an elliptic-curve subgroup, but none of the formulas exploits an
elliptic-curve-specific structural ingredient.

The full DL-labelled sign table has n entries. Producing it by group
enumeration has linear output and storage costs. The usual full Fourier
route is O(n log n) arithmetic on that table, with arithmetic precision and
integer recovery still requiring certification. Formula (5) does not make
that table free and is not a sublinear ECDLP procedure.

A planted DL-interval statistic is available to a toy instrument that knows
its labels. It is NOT an evaluator on an arbitrary point whose scalar is
unknown. Any new low-cost elliptic evaluator is a separate missing object.

No exact floating-point certificate follows from proximity to a rounded
integer. This packet supplies algebraic test values, not a certification of
the existing FFT implementation.

No time exponent, memory exponent, data/query saving or constant-factor
ECDLP improvement is demonstrated. No p-adic degree bound, universal
impossibility, lane closure, hypothesis transition, or breakthrough follows.
The result is at most a candidate calibration derivation pending independent
rederivation, scope review and an explicit Coordinator disposition.
