# Complementary non-index-calculus ECDLP research capsule

## Goal

Investigate seven mechanisms from the non-index ECDLP idea inventory that were not given independent treatment in `NON-INDEX-ECDLP-20260806`.

These lanes must not rely on factor bases, smoothness, relation collection, Semaev decomposition, or scalar-labelled memorization. The objective is to discover either a measurable generic-search improvement, an efficiently computable scalar predicate, a new public representation, or a reusable negative barrier.

## Relationship to the first proposal

The first proposal already covers automatic invariant discovery, spectral/dynamical probes, latent linearization, generic-group walk synthesis, partial-information attacks, lifted representations, circuit/proof complexity, and correspondence discovery.

This proposal isolates ideas that were previously omitted or only implicit:

1. potential-theoretic functional coordinates;
2. categorical and functorial embeddings;
3. orbit and semigroup decomposition;
4. topological data analysis of trajectories;
5. probabilistic constraint propagation;
6. symbolic dynamics and automata;
7. random-matrix and operator-statistics tests.

## Hard exclusions

Reject or reclassify a lane if its effective mechanism requires:

- decomposition over a small factor base;
- collecting enough point relations to solve linear algebra in the scalar;
- hidden scalar labels in training or preprocessing;
- coordinate-specific artifacts that disappear under isomorphism;
- exponential preprocessing omitted from the cost model;
- a transfer map whose construction or inversion already solves ECDLP;
- claims based only on visual clustering, solver behavior, or neural accuracy.

## Research lanes

### L9 — Potential-theoretic functional coordinates

Search for efficiently computable functions on public points or sampled Cayley-graph neighborhoods whose evolution under known additions is predictable.

Candidate objects:

- discrete harmonic functions;
- Green-function approximations;
- heat-kernel signatures;
- martingales for controlled addition walks;
- low-complexity approximate eigenfunctions.

The central question is whether a public observable can estimate displacement, interval membership, or progress toward a collision without encoding the scalar directly.

Promotion gate: a coordinate-invariant function with reproducible predictive advantage over matched random cycles and a concrete reduction to fewer normalized group operations.

### L10 — Categorical and functorial embeddings

Search for public functors or structured embeddings from elliptic-curve point actions into categories where scalar multiplication induces a simpler morphism action.

Candidate settings:

- representation categories;
- sheaf-valued or cohomological summaries;
- groupoids built from public isomorphisms and correspondences;
- derived or filtered invariants with efficient finite truncations.

This lane is theory-first. Every proposed object must be reduced to a finite, executable toy probe.

Promotion gate: an explicit efficiently computable construction that distinguishes scalar action from a matched random cyclic action without assuming the desired logarithm.

### L11 — Orbit and semigroup decomposition

Study actions generated by addition maps, negation, available automorphisms, endomorphisms, and bounded state-dependent operators.

Candidate probes:

- orbit-size and stabilizer distributions;
- semigroup growth and collision profiles;
- quotient-state constructions;
- canonical representatives under public automorphism actions;
- adaptive orbit switching for collision search.

Promotion gate: a decomposition or quotient that reduces effective search cost after canonicalization and orbit-maintenance overhead.

### L12 — Topological data analysis of trajectories

Construct filtrations from Pollard trajectories, addition-chain state graphs, orbit intersections, and sampled neighborhood complexes.

Candidate measurements:

- persistent homology;
- Mapper graphs;
- recurrence-network topology;
- witness complexes from public observables;
- barcode stability across curves and seeds.

Topology is not itself a cryptanalytic result. A positive must identify a computable feature that changes an algorithmic decision.

Promotion gate: a stable topological feature that predicts collisions, useful partitions, or walk pathology and yields an overhead-inclusive improvement.

### L13 — Probabilistic constraint propagation

Represent bounded scalar uncertainty using factor graphs, interval constraints, residue hypotheses, or message-passing states without constructing an index-calculus relation matrix.

Candidate methods:

- belief propagation on addition-chain constraints;
- survey propagation over bounded scalar encodings;
- approximate Bayesian updates from public predicates;
- hybrid SAT/message-passing schedules;
- uncertainty compression under known group operations.

Promotion gate: entropy reduction on uniform hidden scalars that survives random-group controls and composes into an end-to-end algorithm cheaper than generic search.

### L14 — Symbolic dynamics and automata

Encode public addition-walk observations as symbolic sequences and test whether scalar displacement induces non-random language structure.

Candidate objects:

- finite-state predictors;
- subshifts of finite type;
- entropy-rate estimates;
- synchronizing words;
- minimal automata for public observation traces;
- kneading-style invariants for deterministic iteration maps.

Promotion gate: held-out predictive structure that survives coordinate randomization and supports a cheaper partition, predicate, or collision strategy.

### L15 — Random-matrix and operator statistics

Build matrices from public transition operators, sampled addition actions, collision kernels, or observable covariance operators.

Candidate tests:

- eigenvalue-spacing distributions;
- singular-value tails;
- spectral-gap scaling;
- eigenvector localization;
- universality-class deviations;
- low-rank perturbations correlated with scalar displacement.

Promotion gate: a statistically corrected deviation from matched random-cycle ensembles that is stable across held-out curves and has an explicit algorithmic use.

## Initial priority

1. L11 orbit and semigroup decomposition — closest to a concrete generic-search optimization.
2. L14 symbolic dynamics — low-cost falsification and direct integration with rho policies.
3. L9 potential-theoretic coordinates — mathematically grounded extension of spectral probes.
4. L13 probabilistic constraint propagation — clear entropy accounting, but high leakage risk.
5. L15 random-matrix statistics — useful diagnostic lane if paired with an action rule.
6. L12 topological data analysis — exploratory; require strict algorithmic-use gate.
7. L10 categorical embeddings — theory-first, with aggressive finite-executability requirements.

## Shared benchmark contract

- Prime-order short-Weierstrass curves over multiple generic prime fields and toy bit sizes.
- Uniform random scalars and independently sampled generators.
- Matched random cyclic-group and random-cycle controls.
- Coordinate randomization and isomorphic-curve tests.
- Strong negation-aware Pollard rho baseline where applicable.
- Separate online group operations, field operations, memory, wall time, training, and preprocessing.
- Multiple-hypothesis correction for large feature searches.
- No promotion from one curve, one seed, or one bit size.

## Expected harness outputs

Each lane must produce:

- a hypothesis card;
- a minimal finite implementation or a barrier argument;
- preregistered observables and falsification thresholds;
- deterministic seeds and raw artifacts;
- an explicit algorithmic-use analysis;
- a verdict of `SUPPORTED`, `DOES_NOT_SURVIVE`, `INCONCLUSIVE`, or `BLOCKED`;
- an independent red-team note for leakage, disguised index calculus, and omitted cost.
