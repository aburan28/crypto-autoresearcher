## `the_change` — transcribe these four resolving-text blocks VERBATIM

### 1. Element (a) — a third admissible conversion route

CM-1's Route 1 (S-box applications) leaves auxiliary AddRoundKey XORs with no
stated rule, while a separate clause categorically forbids converting non-S-box
work by any factor (RT-20260831-1166f2 `r2_element_dispositions[0]`, HIGH
severity). Resolve by adding a third route built from CM-1's own already-fixed
`add_round_key_byte_xors_per_AEU` figures (176/208/240), rather than an invented
materiality percentage:

> **ADMISSIBLE CONVERSION ROUTE 3 — ADDROUNDKEY-ONLY AUXILIARY XORS.** An
> attack whose cost is dominated by N S-box applications (converted under Route
> 1) accompanied ONLY by AddRoundKey byte-XORs performed to combine partial key
> guesses with data — and by no other bytewise operation (no independent
> ShiftRows/MixColumns computation, no table access outside the S-box route) —
> additionally charges those AddRoundKey XORs at X / X_k AEU-k, where X is the
> count of AddRoundKey byte-XORs the attack performs and X_k is the per-key-size
> `add_round_key_byte_xors_per_AEU` figure already fixed in element (a) (176 for
> AES-128, 208 for AES-192, 240 for AES-256). The total charged cost under
> Routes 1+3 combined is (N/S_k + X/X_k) AEU-k. An attack whose auxiliary work
> includes ANY operation outside S-box applications and AddRoundKey XORs
> (ShiftRows, MixColumns, table accesses not already counted, or any other
> bytewise operation) remains INADMISSIBLE under this route and is recorded as
> UNADJUDICABLE exactly as before; Route 3 may not be combined with any
> materiality-rounding argument — it is an exact additional charge, not a
> threshold below which auxiliary work is ignored.

This closes O-CM1-1 (the materiality threshold) for the AddRoundKey-only case;
state explicitly that O-CM1-1 remains open for any OTHER auxiliary-operation
mix, which Route 3 does not address.

### 2. Elements (c)/(f) jointly — one clarifying sentence

CM-1 leaves it unstated whether a residual encrypt-and-compare search's own
AEU-k units already bundle candidate verification, or whether element (f)'s
per-candidate verification charge applies additionally on top — a spread of
close to a factor of 2 (RT-20260831-1166f2 `r2_element_dispositions[2]` and
`[4]`, HIGH severity, internally consistency-checked against CM-1's own
2^(k-1)-AEU-k reference figure in `required_controls` entry 2):

> A residual search that proceeds by DIRECT ENCRYPT-AND-COMPARE ENUMERATION
> (stopping at the first matching candidate) charges verification as ALREADY
> BUNDLED into each search AEU-k, exactly as the definitional reference itself
> is charged under element (d)'s `relation_to_R1` clause; element (f)'s
> separate N-verification-unit charge ("an attack that reduces the key space to
> a set of size N has paid, at minimum, N verification units on top of whatever
> it paid to produce the set") applies ONLY to a candidate set produced by a
> mechanism OTHER than encrypt-and-compare enumeration — e.g. algebraic
> elimination, sieving, or any method where generating a candidate and
> verifying it against the target ciphertexts are genuinely distinct
> computational acts.

### 3. Element (d) — the data-coordinate domination rule

CM-1 orders data *kind* (known < chosen < adaptive) but never states how kind
combines with *count* into the single order its four-tuple domination rule
needs (RT-20260831-1166f2 `r2_element_dispositions[3]`, MEDIUM severity).
Resolve with a genuine two-axis sub-lattice, which is the only one of the red
team's three offered readings that neither collapses kind to a disclosure label
nor lets a stronger-kind attack win outright regardless of count:

> **THE DATA COORDINATE IS A TWO-AXIS SUB-LATTICE (kind, count)**, not a single
> scalar. Kind is ordered known < chosen < adaptive (weakest to strongest
> requirement). An attack's data requirement A = (kind_A, count_A) is NO WORSE
> than reference data requirement R = (kind_R, count_R) — and therefore does
> not lose the data coordinate of the four-tuple domination comparison — IF AND
> ONLY IF kind_A is no stronger than kind_R AND count_A <= count_R. If kind_A is
> no stronger than kind_R but count_A > count_R, OR if kind_A is strictly
> stronger than kind_R regardless of count, the data coordinate is
> INCOMPARABLE (neither dominates), and the four-tuple comparison as a whole is
> reported as an incomparable point on the frontier — never as beating the
> reference on a scalar time comparison alone — with both (kind, count) pairs
> stated explicitly. A chosen-plaintext requirement, however few queries, is
> therefore a strictly stronger requirement than the reference's known-plaintext
> requirement and never wins the data coordinate outright on count alone.

### 4. Elements (b)/(c) — the distinguisher bridge, lower severity

CM-1's element (b)/(c) is written entirely in "repeated attempts to first
success" vocabulary and states no bridge to a distinguisher's measured
false-positive rate, though RQ-AES-002 R7 keeps distinguisher claims explicitly
in scope (RT-20260831-1166f2 `r2_element_dispositions[1]`, MEDIUM severity).
Resolve by ruling distinguishers compared on a separate, named axis rather than
inventing an unproven cost/advantage analogy:

> **DISTINGUISHER CLAIMS ARE COMPARED ON A SEPARATE, NAMED AXIS AND ARE NOT
> FOLDED INTO THE AEU-k COST-TO-SUCCESS FRAMEWORK OF ELEMENT (b)/(c).** A
> distinguisher's admissibility and strength under this question are reported
> as the tuple (measured false-positive rate against a matched
> random-permutation control at a stated data complexity D, in queries and
> kind per element (d); the round-count decay check result). NO AEU-k figure is
> computed or claimed for a distinguisher under this model, and no comparison
> of a distinguisher against the 2^(k-1)/2^(k-1)/2^(k-1) reference figures is
> made or implied by CM-1. Converting a distinguisher into a key-recovery
> attack, if attempted, is a SEPARATE construction whose own cost is charged
> under elements (a)-(f) as an ordinary key-recovery attack once specified.
