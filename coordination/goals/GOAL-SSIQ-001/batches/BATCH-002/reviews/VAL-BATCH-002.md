## 3. The estimator-bias claim — adjudicated with my own experiment

The producer self-reported that the frozen estimator over the frozen window returns
`alpha = 1.1362` on a surrogate ensemble whose asymptotic `alpha` is `3/2` **by
construction** — a measured bias of `−0.3638`, larger than the decision rule's own
`±0.20`. This is the most consequential claim in the batch. I did not take the
producer's word for it. I built my own object with a known answer.

### 3.1 Method — independent, not a re-run

I wrote my own implementation of the frozen window and the frozen estimator from
the specification text (`ols3`, `window_cells`, `tmax_theorem`), using
`numpy.linalg.lstsq` — a different numerical route from the producer's hand-rolled
3×3 normal-equation inverse. **Cross-check:** my window admits exactly `126822`
cells over `5211` primes with `T ∈ [2,45]`, `p ∈ [1009,265207]` for W-MAIN and
`116075` cells over `4150` primes for W-SENS — identical to the producer's cell
counts and ranges. The window is implemented as frozen.

I then built a null ensemble by a **different construction** from the producer's:
where the producer sampled Goldstein–Mayer *cyclic* lattices
`L_v = {x ∈ Z³ : ⟨x,v⟩ ≡ 0 mod q}`, I sampled **uniform index-q sublattices of
`Z³` via Hermite normal forms** (all HNFs of determinant `q`, weighted `d·f²`),
a strictly larger family with the same Gram determinant `q² ≈ p/4`, at 20000
samples per `q`-class (5× the producer's per-prime cap) under two seeds. For such
an ensemble the Gaussian heuristic gives
`P(λ₁² ≤ T) ~ (2π/3)T^{3/2}/q`, hence with `H(p) = p/12 + O(1)`,
`N(T,p) ~ (π/9)·T^{3/2}·p^{1/2}` — **asymptotic `alpha = 3/2`, `beta = 1/2`, by
construction.** I verified the sampler against the heuristic at `q = 16, 60, 257`
before using it.

### 3.2 Result — the frozen window does NOT recover a known 3/2

| object | true asymptotic α | **frozen window returns** | bias |
|---|---|---|---|
| exact power law `N = c·T^{3/2}·p^{1/2}` (sanity) | 1.5 | **1.500000** | 0.000000 |
| **validator's HNF ternary-lattice null, seed 777** | **3/2** | **1.141553** (se 0.000489) | **−0.358447** |
| **validator's HNF ternary-lattice null, seed 20260805** | **3/2** | **1.141469** | **−0.358531** |
| producer's Goldstein–Mayer null (3 seeds, mean) | 3/2 | 1.136169 | −0.363831 |

Two different lattice families, two independently written code paths, two
different sample regimes — **agreeing to 0.005 on a bias of −0.36**.

**ANSWER TO THE QUESTION AS ASKED: NO.** The frozen `W-MAIN` window does not
recover `alpha = 3/2` from an object that provably has it. It returns `≈1.14`. The
measured bias is **1.8× the decision rule's own ±0.20 tolerance**. The producer's
claim is **CONFIRMED**, independently, and is not an artefact of its surrogate
construction.

The same holds for `beta`: my null returns `0.5945` against a true `1/2`
(bias `+0.0945`); the producer's returns `0.5951` (`+0.0951`). The real data's
`beta = 0.5890` sits inside the same bias band, which is the producer's own `U4`
observation, independently reproduced.

### 3.3 Which is it — a defective contract, or uninformative data?

**It is a DEFECTIVE CONTRACT.** The decision rule could not have been satisfied by
the model it was written to confirm, at any data whatsoever. I established this by
running the frozen window on a second object of the same shape whose true exponent
is the *competing* value.

**The competing-model object.** For a random **rank-4** lattice,
`#{x : |x|² ≤ T} ~ (π²/2)T²/covol`, so `P(λ₁² ≤ T) ~ (π²/2)T²/m` — asymptotic
`alpha = 2` by construction. I pinned `m = (π²/2)(p/2)^{2/3}` by the *same*
`C-ANCHOR-HIGH` condition the real data satisfies (fraction → 1 at
`T = Tmax = (p/2)^{1/3}`), which forces `beta = 1/3` (the pinning relation is
`α/3 + β = 1`, and it returns `β = 1/2` for `α = 3/2` — i.e. it reproduces D1's own
pair). Sampled by the same HNF method, over log-spaced prime subsamples, and
compared against the rank-3 null on the *identical* subsample:

| subsample | true α = 3/2 object → | branch test | true α = 2 object → | branch test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 170 primes, M=1500 | **1.11585** | \|Δ1.5\|=0.384 → **outside ±0.20** | **1.81991** | \|Δ2.0\|=0.180 → *inside ±0.20* |
| 80 primes, M=1200 | **1.12138** | \|Δ1.5\|=0.379 → **outside ±0.20** | **1.76420** | \|Δ2.0\|=0.236 → outside ±0.20 |

**Finding:** the `SUPPORTS-D1` branch was **UNREACHABLE** — an object that *is* D1
lands 0.18–0.19 *beyond* the tolerance, and no sampling choice moves it close. The
`REFUTES-D1-AT-SCALE` branch was **marginally reachable at best**: my two runs
straddle the boundary by ±0.02–0.04, so I record it as *borderline and unresolved*,
noting the rank-4 lattice is a *proxy* for "ingredient (c) contributes `n^{1/2}`",
not that failure mode itself.

So `EXP-SSIQ-4de240` was a **one-sided instrument**: it could conceivably have
fired a refutation and could **never** have fired a confirmation. `INCONCLUSIVE`
was very nearly its only reachable outcome and was certainly its only reachable
non-refuting outcome.

**The data is not the problem.** The dataset is exhaustive below `p = 22000`,
passes every normalisation and endpoint check exactly, and yields a fit with
`R² = 0.9915` and a nominal `se(α) = 0.0005`. It is highly informative *about the
counting function*. What failed is the **map from that measurement to the decision
rule**: a rule stated as absolute `±0.20` bands around 1.5 and 2.0, applied to an
estimator whose bias at this window is `−0.36`, i.e. **mis-centred by more than
1.8× its own width**.

### 3.4 Where the bias comes from — quantified, not asserted

The task asks whether it is finite-`T` truncation (`T ≤ 45`), the nested-cumulative
cell dependence, the excluded top decile, or something else. All four, measured:

1. **Not the OLS.** On an exact power law over the identical cell set the estimator
   returns `1.500000 / 0.500000`. The estimator is unbiased on log-linear data.
2. **Not the `N ≥ 10` cut.** `α` moves by `1×10⁻⁵` between `nmin = 1` and
   `nmin = 10` (1.35273 → 1.35274 on the saturating model).
3. **Not the nested-cumulative dependence — for the point estimate.** That defect is
   real but attacks the *standard errors*, not `α̂`. My cluster bootstrap over
   primes (B = 200, the true unit of independence) gives
   `sd(α) = 0.001044` against the nominal OLS `se = 0.000514` — the SEs are
   **anti-conservative by a factor 2.0** (`beta` 1.4×, `log c` 1.2×). Real, and
   two orders of magnitude too small to explain a 0.36 bias.
4. **Saturation *inside* the retained window — the largest single term, ≈ −0.15.**
   A smooth saturating model with the same asymptotic `3/2` (`N = H(1−e^{−μ})`,
   `μ ∝ T^{3/2}p^{1/2}/H`, pinned at `Tmax`) returns **1.3527** under the frozen
   0.9-decile. The reason: the power law reaches `N = H` at `T ≈ 0.70·Tmax`, so
   roughly the upper *half* of the retained window is saturated. **The excluded top
   decile removes a sliver of a phenomenon that spans the window.** The contract's
   own rationale for the decile ("saturation toward fraction 1 flattens the power
   law by construction") was correct in kind and off by a factor of ~3 in extent.
5. **Finite-`T` lattice discreteness — the remaining ≈ −0.21.** The gap between the
   smooth saturating model (1.353) and the genuine lattice ensemble (1.142) is not
   saturation: at `T ≤ 45` the lattice-point count is far from its volume
   asymptotic (`r₃(n)` fluctuates wildly; many norms — 7, 15, 23, 28, … — are not
   represented at all). This is the sub-toy cost the contract's `scale_relevance`
   clause flagged as "itself testable". It is now tested and it is large.

**No decile choice would have fixed it.** Sweeping the cut on my genuine null
(true `α = 3/2`):

| keep `T ≤ f·Tmax` | 0.90 | 0.70 | 0.50 | 0.30 | 0.20 | 0.10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fitted α | 1.1416 | 1.2138 | 1.2544 | 1.2516 | 1.2027 | 0.8354 |

It never reaches 1.5, peaks near 1.25, and collapses when the window is cut small
enough for discreteness to dominate. The `D1` per-p-vs-pooled decile ambiguity is
therefore immaterial for a deeper reason than the producer gave.

**What would resolve the contract defect:** a successor contract whose decision
boundaries are *calibrated on null objects with known exponents at the achievable
window*, pre-registered before data — i.e. state the rule as "α̂ within ±τ of the
value the frozen window returns on a pre-registered true-3/2 object", with the
calibration runs themselves frozen. Absolute bands on the asymptotic exponent are
not usable at `T ≤ 45`.

### D3 in full — is the sieve selection a selection bias that invalidates W-MAIN?

The measured heterogeneity is real: complete-coverage block (`1000 ≤ p ≤ 22000`,
exhaustive over primes) gives `α = 1.08175`; sieve-selected block (`p > 22000`)
gives `α = 1.18346`; difference **0.10171**, larger than the contract's own
W-MAIN/W-SENS sensitivity shift (0.0066), and `W-SENS` is 96% selected-block cells.
The producer flagged it and asked reviewers to attack it. So I ran the control the
producer did not:

**I split my own null ensemble — which contains no wisde sieve, no selection of any
kind, and is sampled identically at every prime — at the same `p = 22000`.**

| block | data α | **null α (no selection anywhere)** | data − null |
|---|---|---|---|
| `1000 ≤ p ≤ 22000` | 1.08175 | **1.03756** | +0.04419 |
| `p > 22000` | 1.18346 | **1.16299** | +0.02047 |
| **heterogeneity** | **0.10171** | **0.12543** | — |

**The unselected null shows a *larger* block heterogeneity than the data.** The
block-to-block shift in `α` is therefore a **p-range artefact of the frozen window**
(the same finite-`T`/saturation mechanism as §3.4 — small `p` means small `Tmax`
means a shorter, more discreteness-dominated `T` range), **not** a fingerprint of
the sieve. Moreover the data-minus-null gap *shrinks* in the selected block, the
opposite of what a selection inflating the fitted exponent would produce.

**VERDICT: the wisde-sieve selection does NOT invalidate W-MAIN.** Two scope limits
on that clearance, stated rather than glossed: (i) it bounds a bias in `α`, not a
bias in the *level* of `N` or in the upper tail (the sieve selects on the existence
of a curve at the extreme `δ`, and 119 primes attain the bound exactly); and
(ii) the primary metric is void on C-NULL regardless, so W-MAIN's admissibility is
moot for this batch's headline and matters only for a successor.
