6. LOCALISATION -- WHY THE GRID MISSES, AND BY HOW MUCH
======================================================================
Blind section 3 shows that the real-d optimum for m = 2s lies a distance
delta = log2(s/(s-1)) / (2s - 1) below the CALLS = 0 exclusion boundary.
The measured values are 0.117 (s=3), 0.059 (s=4), 0.036 (s=5), 0.024 (s=6)
and 0.017 (s=7), all smaller than one 0.25 grid step. So:
  (a) every grid minimum is the last in-domain grid point. That is why
      the producer's "argmin adjacent to C-2 domain edge" is "yes" at every
      degree (T5), and why 120 of 150 MITM argmin rows have their next grid
      point out of domain (RESULTS, unexpected observations).
  (b) The grid excess is set by where the boundary falls relative to the
      0.25 lattice. Near d* it is about ln2 * s(s-1) * Delta^2 / 2, with
      Delta the distance from the grid point to d*. Measured over
      (n, even m, s = m/2), the excess is 0.0001 to 0.76 bits. It is
      largest at s = 7 and 8 (e.g. n = 233, m = 16: 0.759; n = 571, m = 14:
      0.505).
  (c) At n = 571 the m = 14 grid excess (0.505) is larger than the real gap
      between m = 14 and m = 12 (0.15), so the grid's arg-min moves to
      m = 12. The producer's n = 571 arg-min is a grid artifact.
  (d) n = 239: the m = 10 grid excess is 0.230 bits. n = 163 and 191: the
      m = 8 grid excess is 0.103 bits.
This resolves the item RESULTS left open (headline 5, "Over 0.1 bit from
E-2: n = 239 (+0.229) and n = 571 (+0.152) ... not resolved"; "163 and 191
differ by +0.098"). E-2 is the continuous-d quantity. The blind real-d
values match E-2 to within E-2's two-decimal rounding (|diff| <= 0.006) at
all 10 degrees. Every v2-minus-E-2 difference is the producer's 0.25 grid.

7. WHAT THE BLIND DERIVATION ADDS BEYOND AGREEMENT
======================================================================
A1. Model-family coverage. The producer's primary family covers s = S
    (MITM), s = min(S, floor(log2 B / d)) (MITM_CAPPED) and ENUM. The blind
    scan covers every s in 0..floor(m/2) at every real d and finds the
    global optimum at s = S = m/2 at every degree. The restricted family
    therefore loses nothing at the per-degree minimum.
A2. The "0 sub-rho cells" result (T4, 362,219 in-domain cells) holds for
    all d, not only the grid, and for all N: TOTAL >= sL/(2s-1) > L/2 for
    s >= 2, and TOTAL >= L for s <= 1, so margin > 0.67 bits everywhere.
    T4's zero is a theorem of the cost model, not an empirical tally over
    a grid.
A3. Every per-degree optimum, in both implementations, sits at p > 1. The
    producer's TPR is negative at every per-degree minimum, e.g. -26.71 at
    n = 239 (row d = 28.75); blind log2 p ranges from 17.3 to 46.5. There,
    "trials_per_relation = 1/p" is an expected-decompositions count, not an
    inverse probability. The cost is meaningful only if one oracle call
    returns all ~p decompositions of a target AND those are ~p linearly
    useful relations. Both implementations inherit this from the statement,
    so blind re-derivation cannot surface it as a disagreement. It is
    flagged as an unvalidated modelling assumption. Imposing p <= 1 (blind
    C3, not in the statement) raises every margin by 5.3 to 18.1 bits. That
    is the direction AGAINST index calculus, so the null result is not at
    risk from it, but the margin values are model-dependent at that scale.
A4. Memory at the optimum is within 1.12 to 1.32 bits of TOTAL (store
    |F|^(m/2)). The producer reports it as "log2 table entries (reported,
    not charged)" in T5, and the values agree: 63.75 at the n = 97 grid
    point equals s*d = 3*21.25.
