## Established facts

1. Pollard rho has expected generic cost `N^(1/2+o(1))` group operations for a
   prime-order group of size `N`; Shoup's generic-group result makes the
   square-root exponent a model-specific lower bound, not a lower bound against
   every coordinate-sensitive method. The comparison here is only between
   asymptotic academic cost models.
2. Prime-field point-decomposition index calculus has no established
   asymptotic advantage over that generic reference. The repository's
   `FINDING-PF-IC-001` records toy-scale negatives for model, isogeny, and
   factor-base variations and explicitly does not prove a universal
   impossibility theorem.
3. The repository has already screened source-labelled resultants, common
   norms, quotient algebras, modular composition, power projection, sparse and
   displacement-structured linear algebra, BKK elimination, elliptic nets,
   incidence formulations, generalized-birthday indexing, jets, covers,
   pairings, trace-zero transfer, and global lifts. Their scoped failures do
   not close arbitrary circuits or data structures.
4. P1553 defines an exact target-label object. With two labelled pair decks
   `D_12,D_34`, a squarefree fifth-label polynomial `g_I`, and a fresh target
   `R`, the complete-chart intersection resultant satisfies

   ```text
   r_R(t_a)=0
   iff the labelled fifth occurrence a extends to a pair-pair relation,

   z_R(T)=gcd(g_I(T),r_R(T))
         = product over extendible fifth labels a of (T-t_a).
   ```

   Occurrence labels prevent equal points from merging. Complete signed charts
   are required for identity, infinity, tangent, vertical, repeated, and
   nonreduced strata.
5. If `r_R mod g_I` is already represented, fast univariate gcd and label
   extraction are quasi-linear in `B`. The unresolved cost is construction of
   that residue. Every audited standard represented route exposes
   `Theta(B^3)` target-dependent coordinates when the pair degree is
   `Theta(B^2)` and the fifth-label algebra has width `Theta(B)`.
6. Current 3SUM/kSUM-indexing and preprocessed-universe results do not supply
   this elliptic, signed, dyadically restricted source interface inside the
   repository's cost rectangle. Dinur--Golovnev
   (arXiv:2512.04258v2) improves fixed-list indexing tradeoffs; the natural
   pair-list substitution still requires preprocessing or advice above the
   declared cap. This is a positive-algorithm comparison, not a lower bound.
7. Subproduct trees, truncated resultants, and modular-composition algorithms
   accelerate represented polynomial inputs. Elliptic-net and elliptic
   divisibility-sequence recurrences accelerate supplied term evaluation.
   None of the checked primary sources supplies the compact target-label
   constructor and labelled replay required here.

## Derivations

Put `N=B^5`. The asymptotic generic reference becomes

```text
Pollard-rho reference: N^(1/2+o(1)) = B^(5/2+o(1)).
```

Five decks of size `B` have expected constant five-source mass under the
favourable random-endpoint heuristic. A relation campaign requires `Theta(B)`
known-log targets. To keep total campaign time at most `N^0.45`, one fresh
target operation must fit

```text
setup/advice/state       <= B^(9/4+o(1)),
total online time/state  <= B^(5/4+o(1)),
B relation queries       <= B^(9/4+o(1)).
```

Conditional on an exact subset-stable constructor, the repository's favourable
accounting is

```text
setup                         B^2,
B relation targets            B^(9/4),
sparse factor-log algebra     B^2,
one masked descent            B^(5/4),
conditional time exponent    0.45 in N,
conditional memory exponent  0.40 in N.
```

This is below the square-root exponent only as a conditional formula. It
assumes the missing constructor, constant verified relation and target
success, independent row rank, factor-log completion, and identical
scalar-blind descent.

The exact output has degree at most `B`, but output size does not price input
construction. Componentwise computation performs `B` degree-`B^2` tests.
Representing one degree-`B^2` polynomial over a width-`B` split label algebra
also gives `B^3` base-field coordinates. Consequently, a future construction
must operate on compact generators and cannot merely apply a fast gcd,
remainder tree, resultant, or matrix solver after the expanded input exists.

## Cheapest decisive gate

The first test is theorem-only. A proposed construction must provide a typed
dependency graph for one fresh symbolic target:

1. name every stored representation object and its dimension;
2. name every target-dependent update and prove its construction cost;
3. prove gauge invariance and the exact support biconditional on all admitted
   strata;
4. include every positive and negative dyadic replay call;
5. show that neither a `B^3` represented payload nor a supplied common factor,
   source tuple, scalar residue, or target-fitted selector appears.

Possible outcomes are fixed in advance:

- If an untyped whole-divisor translation, represented `B^3` object, or
  supplied factor is found, the route is a screened backend/renaming and is
  rejected without a toy run.
- If the support biconditional fails, the representation is mathematically
  falsified regardless of cost.
- If the theorem is exact but online work exceeds `B^(5/4+o(1))`, it may be a
  representation identity but does not meet this complexity objective.
- Only if semantics and accounting both pass is a harmless toy check
  warranted. That check may use a fully public synthetic group or
  non-standard educational curve of at most 32 bits, with no hidden scalar,
  and compare support and replay against exhaustive public enumeration.
  Timing is not extrapolated.

The controls are: a supplied-residue fast-gcd positive control, a
componentwise `B^3` negative control, a no-relation unit case, a single-hit
case, collision and exceptional-strata cases, and a fresh-target mutation
after preprocessing.

This theorem audit is the cheapest valid discriminator because it tests the
load-bearing claim—compact input construction—before any solver, curve
fixture, relation campaign, or cryptographic computation.
