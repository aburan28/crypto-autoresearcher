## Classification-gate objection

The proposed `support_action_covariance_classification_gate` is the right
*kind* of pre-compute screening template, but it is not the cheapest decisive
next gate in the present state.

First, there is no typed action to gate. Second, the listed cases do not form a
classification theorem. An automorphism of a selected sparse relation
hypergraph need not extend to an origin-fixing elliptic-curve automorphism or
scalar action. Duplicate occurrences, disconnected isomorphic components, and
accidental finite-instance symmetries give countermodels unless the theorem
fixes relation-richness, extension, asymptotic-uniformity, and source semantics.
The catch-all case “genuinely other” classifies nothing.

The cheapest symbolic mutation is a duplicate-occurrence cyclic blowup.
Replace every factor-base point by \(L\) labelled copies of the same group
element, lift each relation by copy labels, and cyclically permute the copies,
with \(L\) growing with \(N\). This gives a growing public action and exact row
covariance and can display circulant blocks. After quotienting twins, the
orbit disappears, rank does not grow, and the labels are exactly a supplied
source payload. Thus covariance plus orbit size is insufficient to establish
novelty, information, or useful rank.

For any later typed claim, the necessary controls are:

1. freeze the asymptotic factor-base occurrences, signed relation hypergraph,
   action generators, target map, advice, coefficients, and source replay;
2. quotient duplicate/twin columns and disconnected copies before measuring
   orbit growth or rank;
3. remove scalar/source witnesses and compare the complete adaptive transcript
   with a charged generic simulator;
4. prove relation yield and full-rank tails rather than orbiting supplied rows;
5. use the identical action for fresh scalar-blind masked-target descent; and
6. charge indexing, unranking, rejected batches, matrix construction, replay,
   verification, traffic, and peak aggregate memory.

These are controls on a future object, not evidence that such an object exists.
