## Boolean U and T maps

Let n=2s. At degree D=4,...,n+4, list squarefree multiplier masks of weight exactly D-4 in ascending integer order. These form the domain basis E_D. Define U_D as a matrix with one column per multiplier and one row per all n-bit monomial masks in ascending integer order. Its column is the fully reduced Boolean product of that multiplier and f. Define T_D by selecting from U_D the rows whose masks have weight D. Thus T_D=pi_D U_D and both matrices have the same domain columns. For D>n, T_D is the explicit matrix of shape 0 by dim(E_D); its kernel is the whole domain. It is not a missing matrix.

Use exact modular RREF with columns in their prescribed order, leftmost pivots, the first eligible row, unit pivot normalization and elimination from every other row. Record row swaps/scalings/additions and pivot locations. Supply either explicit nonzero pivot minors plus the spanning certificate, or the complete verified elimination certificate giving both rank bounds. Floating ranks and unexplained rank integers are inadmissible.

Produce an explicit basis K of ker(T_D), its image U_D K and a basis of ker(T_D) intersect ker(U_D). Check all kernel equations and basis ranks independently. Report rank(U_D), rank(T_D), dim ker(T_D), dim image(U_D K), and intersection dimension. Define the nonzero fall dimension as dim image(U_D K), independently compare it with rank(U_D)-rank(T_D), and define d_layer as the least scheduled D with positive dimension. Keep every subsequent scheduled D. If none exists report no observed onset over the declared finite range. Distinguish an onset with D>n, where the top layer is exhausted, from a structural early-fall claim.

The load-bearing dimension identity follows without a heuristic. Restrict pi_D to im(U_D). Its image has dimension rank(T_D). Its kernel consists exactly of U_D c with T_D c=0, hence equals U_D(ker T_D). Rank-nullity gives dim U_D(ker T_D)=rank(U_D)-rank(T_D). The implication requires the verified relation T_D=pi_D U_D. A mismatched projection or matrix reconstruction is an artifact, not a counterexample to rank-nullity.

## Ordinary V_F,D closure

For growth use the ordinary ring R=F_p[z_1,...,z_n] with degree-compatible grevlex z_1>...>z_n. The target-derived ordered generator list is exactly [f,z_1^2-z_1,...,z_n^2-z_n]. The native ordinary controls instead use their exact four listed polynomials and w>x>y>z. There is no reduction modulo digit equations during ordinary polynomial arithmetic.

At D=0,...,D_cap, enumerate every ordinary monomial of total degree at most D, including 1. There are binomial(n+D,D) columns. Sort by descending total degree and, within a degree, descending grevlex: at the last differing exponent, the smaller exponent gives the larger monomial. Initialize from all original generators of degree at most D; zero generators contribute no pivot but remain in the source list. Normalize with exact leftmost-pivot RREF. For every current RREF row of degree at most D-1, multiply by each variable in declared order, add the resulting rows, and RREF again. Repeat until rank is stable after a complete such multiplication pass. Rank strictly increases on a productive pass, so the bounded-dimensional iteration terminates if resources permit. D=0 allows constants but no degree-minus-one multiplication.

This computes the least degree-bounded vector space containing admitted generators and closed under permitted products. The descending degree-compatible column order makes low-degree RREF rows a basis for the low-degree intersection of the current space; therefore checking those rows is essential, not an arbitrary restriction to original generators. Variable multiplication and repeated closure generate admissible monomial products. Preserve all row and multiplication derivations as a DAG rooted in the original generators. A rank plateau within a completed fixed-D multiplication pass establishes that closure only. A plateau between D and D+1 never establishes last-fall degree.

Before allocating any next ordinary coefficient, augmented, or certificate matrix, count its logical rows, columns and entries. Preserve the original limits of 20,000 columns and 25,000,000 entries, regardless of sparse storage. Refuse the next matrix if it would exceed a limit; retain its requested shape and the last fully certified state. Do not inspect a partly computed forbidden matrix or lower the declared D_cap silently. The 8-GiB process protection is additional. ## Lower and upper last-fall certificates

At k>=1 use the completed V_F,k and V_F,k-1. A lower witness is a nonzero g with deg(g)<k, a derivation placing g in V_F,k, and exact nonmembership in V_F,k-1. Test the low-degree intersection basis in deterministic RREF order; retain every reported witness and its independently checked membership derivation. Prove nonmembership by an augmented-rank certificate or a separating linear functional that annihilates the earlier basis but evaluates nonzero on g. Such a witness proves d_lf>=k. If the required preceding space was censored or incomplete, do not claim nonmembership. The default lower bound is zero, not an observed degree drop.

For an upper certificate at a completed D, take the nonzero closure RREF rows as candidate G in deterministic row order. Verify: (1) every row is in V_F,D by its derivation; (2) G and the entire original F generate the same ideal, including original generators whose degrees exceed D; (3) every Buchberger S-pair, in lexicographic row-index order, reduces to zero using least-index eligible divisor selection; (4) all arithmetic and the degree-compatible monomial order are correct. Ideal inclusions require explicit generator reduction/combination certificates in both directions. The empty basis is acceptable only when all original generators are zero; a unit ideal still needs its derivation.

Here is the upper-bound lemma and its complete applicability boundary. If G is a degree-compatible Groebner basis of (F) and every g_i lies in V_F,D, division of any f in (F) writes f=sum_i h_i g_i with each nonzero monomial product in the division representation of degree at most deg(f). Set M=max(D,deg(f)). Monotonicity puts every g_i in V_F,M, and degree-bounded product closure puts every term of that representation in V_F,M. Thus f lies in V_F,max(D,deg(f)), establishing d_lf<=D under Definition 1.5. No ideal-equality, Groebner, division-degree, or V-membership prerequisite may be assumed from the output's appearance.

Report the largest certified lower bound and smallest certified upper bound obtained so far. Emit an exact ordinary d_lf only when these coincide. Otherwise emit [L,U], using null for an unavailable U. A conflict L>U invalidates the certificate computation. Output basis degree, solving degree, quotient d_layer, or absence of a new fall is not an upper certificate.

The proof audit must reject the shortcut using only a small Groebner basis degree: F={x^2,x^2-x} generates (x), with G={x}, but V_F,1 contains no nonzero generator and does not contain x. The omitted derivation bound is decisive. Likewise same-ideal generation alone cannot replace the Groebner and degree-compatible division premises. These are conceptual review controls, not additional scientific fixture groups or permission to execute a test now.
