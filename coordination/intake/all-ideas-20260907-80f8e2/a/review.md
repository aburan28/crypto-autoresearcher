RELN uses four laws over signed factor-base pairs: uniform, same-half favored, cross-half favored and nearby-index favored. Every biased arm keeps uniform support. The selector sees only past checked rows and charged costs, with fixed exploration and a frozen score. Exact support-rank checking happens outside the collector after collection; it never provides an oracle about future rows. A common finite full-pair-scan fallback belongs to every policy and is fully charged. If the menu cannot reach U−1, an exact support certificate records that prerequisite failure; the target is not lowered. A paired confidence procedure, fixed bootstrap and tail/fallback reports are specified before measurement. Wilson selection scores are heuristic statistics, and seed-level confidence intervals are explicitly approximate. Neither is presented as a theorem about adaptive drift.

SIG constructs a finite complex from known Boolean principal, Koszul and vanishing-row syzygies, tagged generator multiples and every squarefree ambient column through degree D. It matches only certified unit pairs between syzygies and tagged rows. Original columns are never removed. Acyclicity, all chain-contraction identities, two-way row-image reconstruction and the declared non-model syzygy quotient are checked independently. The baseline streams the same representation and supplies the same outputs; no dense straw baseline or free certificate storage is allowed. Fill, maps, original input, graph search and verifier scratch all enter peak workspace.

The primary Semaev paper was read for the characteristic-two S3 formula and chained-coordinate interface, not for an assumed first-fall/regularity or cryptographic-security conclusion. Skoldberg’s primary algebraic Morse treatment was read for known matching and contraction theory; cancellation itself is prior art. Source-specific finite cost gains remain unverified.
