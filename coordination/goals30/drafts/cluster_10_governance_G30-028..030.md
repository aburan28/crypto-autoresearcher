All three goals are governance/infrastructure goals: they protect the program from

## G30-028: NoveltyGate — mandatory literature screening before any "novel" label
- **Cluster:** GOVERNANCE
- **Type:** governance
- **Long-term chain:** This goal gates whether any idea-generator output may carry a "novel" label; the novelty label gates Coordinator prioritization of new attack directions; credible prioritization is a precondition for spending scarce experiment capacity on candidates that could eventually chain toward a sub-rho result. It protects the break-search from re-investing in published (and refuted) territory.
- **Mechanism:** Build a deterministic screening tool (`tools/novelty_screen.py`) over the 6,345-paper first-page corpus at `research_digest/manifest.jsonl`: (i) exact/normalized keyword and phrase grep (title, abstract, author-cited technique names, mathematical object names); (ii) synonym/paraphrase expansion table seeded from KN-LIT-001..025; (iii) an optional local embedding index (pure-Python or managed-runtime vector store) whose commissioning is conditional on the grep-tier result. The tool emits a machine-readable novelty report (candidate terms, top-k corpus hits with quoted spans, KN-LIT cross-ref checklist) that must be attached to any idea/handoff before the word "novel" appears in a ledger record. KN-LIT cross-references are mandatory schema fields, not prose.
- **Quantified prediction:** On a labeled fixture of 40 claims (20 known-in-literature drawn from KN-LIT-001..025 source papers, 20 synthetic candidate-novel), the grep+synonym tier achieves recall ≥ 0.90 on the known-in-literature half (misses ≤ 2 of 20) and flags ≥ 15/20 of paraphrase-obfuscated variants; if recall < 0.90 the prediction is that the embedding tier recovers ≥ 0.95.
- **Test boundary / scope:** Corpus fixed at the current 6,345-entry manifest (hash-pinned); fixture of 40 labeled claims built once, frozen, with 8 items held out from tool tuning; KN-LIT-001..025 as the cross-ref gold set. No new literature ingestion, no web search inside the gate.
- **Primary metric:** recall on the known-in-literature fixture half / **Secondary metrics:** false-positive rate on the candidate-novel half, paraphrase-robustness rate, screening wall-time per claim (target < 60 s), KN-LIT cross-ref completeness of emitted reports.
- **Controls:** 8 held-out labeled items never used for synonym-table tuning; each flagged hit must quote the matching corpus span (no span = no flag credit); trivial-control: screening a claim constructed from a KN-LIT paper's own abstract must produce recall 1.0 or the run is invalid.
- **Falsification criteria:** The grep+synonym tier misses > 4/20 known-in-literature items AND the embedding tier also fails to reach recall 0.90, or the tool cannot process a claim in < 60 s — then a corpus-local automated screen is not viable at this scale and the protocol is rejected as specified.
- **Negative-outcome value:** If the screen cannot reach the recall bar, the decision changes: no automated novelty gate is adopted, "novel" labels remain human-review-only (slower but honest), and no further capacity is spent on embedding infrastructure. If only grep fails but embedding passes, the decision to commission the embedding index is made with measured justification.
- **Budget:** wall_clock 6h, memory 8 GiB, max_runs 20
- **Stopping rule:** Stop after the frozen fixture is scored for the best configuration and the held-out 8 are evaluated once; no further synonym-table iterations after held-out evaluation (prevents tuning to the fixture).
- **Harness dependency:** To-be-commissioned capability (not in cartography_harness gap list): corpus screening module reading `research_digest/manifest.jsonl`; reuses existing runner.py immutable-run discipline and validate_ledger.py xref checks for the KN-LIT mandatory-field enforcement.
- **Claim tier:** infrastructure
- **Dependencies:** none

## G30-029: ClaimTier Ladder — explicit toy→medium→crypto-relevant escalation gates with alphaXiv-style decomposition
- **Cluster:** GOVERNANCE
- **Type:** governance
- **Long-term chain:** This goal defines the only legal path by which a toy-scale observation can be projected toward medium and crypto-relevant tiers; tier labels gate which claims may motivate scaling-study budget; disciplined projection is what keeps the program's chain toward a cryptographically relevant break from being shortcut by unearned extrapolation (the historical pattern behind AGENTS.md rule 7 violations).
- **Mechanism:** Specify, as ledger-enforceable schema plus checklist, an escalation ladder with explicit criteria per transition. Toy→medium requires: matched-rho-baselined cost accounting on at least two instance sizes with a fitted exponent, censored/invalid runs reported, and independent `review-xhigh` sign-off. Medium→crypto-relevant additionally requires: a validated cost model converting all stage costs into rho-comparable group-op equivalents (harness gap #4), no reliance on MODEL-BOUND toy exponents, and a red-team pass (G30-030). Every claim must first be decomposed alphaXiv-style: an atomic tree of sub-claims, each carrying its own tier, evidence references, and dependency edges; the parent claim's tier is the minimum of required sub-claim tiers (no averaging-up). The toy→medium rung INVOKES G30-003's quantitative extrapolation rule (statistic whitelist, ≥4-size ladder, CI-based tier-rise) rather than defining a competing one. The ladder is validated by blind replay against the existing 34-claim matrix and historical claims with known terminal outcomes.
- **Quantified prediction:** Blind replay of the ladder against ≥ 12 historical claims with known outcomes (e.g., multiplicity-crediting and frozen-selector claims that later failed vs. H-BKKMV-001, H-JETB-001, H-INCB-001, H-SIG-001 that survived) blocks ≥ 75% of the later-failed claims at the correct tier boundary while misclassifying ≤ 1 of the survived claims; two independent raters applying the ladder agree on tier assignment in ≥ 80% of cases.
- **Test boundary / scope:** Claim matrix (34 claims in focus/current_plan.md) plus a frozen set of ≥ 12 historical claims with adjudicated outcomes drawn from the closed-territory ledger; 4 historical claims held out from checklist drafting. No new experiments; this is a replay/specification study.
- **Primary metric:** blocked-rate on later-failed historical claims / **Secondary metrics:** false-block rate on survived claims, inter-rater agreement (Cohen's kappa), decomposition completeness (fraction of replayed claims fully decomposed without schema violations), adjudication wall-time per claim.
- **Controls:** Raters blind to the known terminal outcomes; 4 held-out claims never used to draft checklist criteria; the two campaign-critical reproduced claims (CLM-P1509, CLM-P1510) must pass the ladder as a sanity control or the specification run is invalid.
- **Falsification criteria:** The ladder blocks < 60% of later-failed historical claims, or blocks ≥ 2 of the survived claims, or inter-rater agreement < 0.6 kappa — then the criteria are not operationalizing real signal and the ladder as specified is rejected.
- **Negative-outcome value:** If the ladder does not discriminate better than the current ad-hoc review, the decision changes: no tier gate is added to validate_ledger.py, the program keeps the existing review process, and the cost-model dependency (harness gap #4) is de-prioritized for medium→crypto transitions. A pass makes the gate mandatory and sequences the cost-model module as a prerequisite for any crypto-relevant claim.
- **Budget:** wall_clock 8h, memory 4 GiB, max_runs 10
- **Stopping rule:** One drafting pass on the non-held-out claims, one blind replay pass by each rater, one held-out evaluation; specification frozen after held-out evaluation regardless of score.
- **Harness dependency:** Existing templates/research-records.md claim/evidence schemas (claim_tier toy|medium|crypto) and tools/validate_ledger.py extended with a decomposition-tree validator; medium→crypto gate depends on harness gap #4 (end-to-end rho-equivalent cost model) remaining commissioned.
- **Claim tier:** infrastructure
- **Dependencies:** G30-003 (toy→medium rung invokes its quantitative extrapolation rule), G30-030 (red-team gate is one rung of the ladder); consumes claim-matrix state from other clusters' measurement goals (G30-001..027 range) only as replay material, not as a blocking dependency.
- **Revision note (2026-07-23 review):** Added G30-003 to Dependencies and stated that the toy→medium rung invokes G30-003's quantitative extrapolation rule (statistic whitelist, ≥4-size ladder, CI-based tier-rise) instead of defining a competing one.

## G30-030: PreMortem Gate — standing falsification-route review before any expansion capacity
- **Cluster:** GOVERNANCE
- **Type:** governance
- **Long-term chain:** This goal gates every positive signal before expansion budget (larger instances, more seeds, more runs) is spent; expansion capacity is the program's scarcest resource; preventing expansion on false positives is what keeps the long-term break-search pointed at signals that can actually survive full cost charging — the exact failure mode that killed PO67 (46–246x rho once fully charged) and the frozen-selector family.
- **Mechanism:** Define a mandatory pre-mortem checklist artifact (schema in templates/research-records.md extension, enforced by tools/validate_ledger.py) that must be completed and independently reviewed before the dispatcher admits any capacity-expanding successor task. The checklist operationalizes the eight recurring failure reasons from cartography_closed.md as explicit falsification routes: (1) multiplicity/coset crediting check, (2) rank-deficiency/dependent-relations check, (3) full-cost-charging completeness (preprocessing, relation collection, LA, descent, memory, amortization), (4) selector-regime fragility (fresh-window uniform control required), (5) density/degree barrier consistency, (6) isogeny-class-invariance predicates, (7) adverse toy-exponent MODEL-BOUND labeling, (8) auxiliary-object DLP-gain verification. Each route requires a named test or a stated reason it does not apply; "does not apply" without justification fails the gate.
- **Quantified prediction:** Blind replay of the gate against ≥ 10 historical positive signals with known terminal outcomes (≥ 6 later-collapsed: e.g., PO67 partial win, ECFG graph-feature selectors, multiplicity-fiber results; ≥ 4 survived: the four supported/supported_scoped hypotheses) blocks ≥ 5/6 of the later-collapsed signals before expansion while passing ≥ 3/4 of the survived ones; per-signal review completes in ≤ 45 min of reviewer effort.
- **Test boundary / scope:** Historical positive signals drawn from the closed/active ledgers with adjudicated outcomes; 3 signals held out from checklist drafting; gate applied in replay mode only — it does not block any live dispatch during this goal's evaluation. No new attack experiments.
- **Primary metric:** blocked-rate on later-collapsed positive signals / **Secondary metrics:** false-block rate on survived signals, checklist coverage (fraction of the 8 routes receiving substantive answers vs. boilerplate), reviewer wall-time per signal, schema-validation pass rate of emitted pre-mortem artifacts.
- **Controls:** Reviewer blind to terminal outcomes during replay; 3 held-out signals; trivial control: a signal whose "positive" rests on twist/invalid-curve adjacency or uncharged Jacobian maps must be blocked by routes (3)/(5)/(8) with 100% reliability or the gate run is invalid; survived-signal set drawn only from independently reviewed supported hypotheses.
- **Falsification criteria:** The gate blocks < 4/6 later-collapsed signals, or blocks ≥ 2/4 survived signals, or > 30% of checklist answers are boilerplate that schema validation cannot catch — then a checklist-form gate does not operationalize the failure taxonomy and the protocol is rejected as specified.
- **Negative-outcome value:** If the gate does not discriminate, the decision changes: expansion gating remains a Coordinator judgment call (status quo), no schema enforcement is added, and the failure taxonomy stays documentation rather than a gate. A pass makes a completed pre-mortem artifact a hard dispatcher precondition for every capacity-expanding task, directly preventing a repeat of the PO67-style expansion-on-partial-win pattern.
- **Budget:** wall_clock 8h, memory 4 GiB, max_runs 10
- **Stopping rule:** One checklist drafting pass, one blind replay pass, one held-out evaluation; checklist frozen after held-out evaluation. If drafting exhausts the ≥ 10-signal pool before criteria stabilize, stop and report inconclusive rather than expanding the pool.
- **Harness dependency:** Existing tools/research_dispatch.py (capacity admission point for the gate), templates/research-records.md (artifact schema home), tools/validate_ledger.py (enforcement); no new measurement capability required.
- **Claim tier:** infrastructure
- **Dependencies:** none (G30-029 consumes this goal's output as one escalation rung, but this goal stands alone)
