## A concrete object and two exact shift families

For a certified $E/\mathbb F_p$ and prime cyclic subgroup $G$, define the actual
factor base $F_W=\{T\in G\setminus\{O\}:0\le x(T)<W\}$ using canonical
integer representatives of coordinates. Its size is $M$, not automatically $W$.
Keep both signs as actual points initially; later quotienting by negation needs
explicit coefficient/orbit accounting. Choose $R\in G$ and lift coefficients of

$$f_R(X_1,\ldots,X_m)=S_{m+1}(X_1,\ldots,X_m,x(R))\pmod p$$

to centered integers. The variable box is $0\le X_i<W$, conservatively bounded
by $|X_i|\le W$ for the norm certificate. Every actual m-term decomposition gives
a modular zero, but the converse requires rational lifts, signs and subgroup checks.
At target$O$, use a separately declared homogeneous-relation polynomial $S_m$ or
group oracle; do not substitute an affine x-coordinate for$O$.

This is R1 coordinate/tuple representation, F2 partial-action under translation by
factor-base points. X-coordinates forget signs and can describe extension-field
lifts; that loss is repaired by explicit candidate branching and verification,
not a deterministic lossy full-translation rule. Window membership is an inequality,
an implicit description. It is not a new bounded-degree algebraic locus or a
claimed resolution of KN-OPEN-020. The established-family comparison is explicitly
authorized by the user.

For positive integer$t$ and each$0\le b\le t$, use shifts

$$X^{\boldsymbol a}f_R^b p^{t-b}.$$

If $\boldsymbol u$ is a modular root, every shift, and every integer combination,
vanishes at it modulo$p^t$. The dense baseline uses
$A_b^{\rm dense}=\{\boldsymbol a\ge0:|\boldsymbol a|\le L\}$.

The proposed Newton-target arm is equally explicit. Let
$P=\operatorname{conv}(\{0\}\cup\operatorname{supp}(f_R))$,
$\Delta_m=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_m)$ and
$K=(tP+L\Delta_m)\cap\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m$. Set

$$A_b^{\rm Newt}=\{\boldsymbol a\ge0:
\boldsymbol a+\operatorname{supp}(f_R^b)\subseteq K\}.$$

Actual integer supports, including coefficient cancellations, determine the rows.
Enumerate in descending$b$, then total degree and lexicographic exponent order.
Record all candidate construction costs and the exact selected integer-lattice
basis; HNF/Smith operations must preserve the integer row lattice. Simply dropping
rationally dependent rows can change the lattice index. A monomial count or a
convex-body volume is not its determinant.

Scale the coefficient column for $X^{\boldsymbol e}$ by
$\prod_iW_i^{e_i}$, with declared bounds$W_i$ (here all$W$). Let$D$ be the number
of supported monomials, $n$ the integer row rank, and$B$ a full row basis of this
weighted lattice. They need not be equal. Its Euclidean covolume is
$\sqrt{\det(BB^T)}$; only when$n=D$ and$B$ is square is this$|\det B|$.
The shifted polynomials remain linked to their integer combinations throughout.

For a nonzero achieved vector$v$ and associated polynomial$g$,

$$|g(\boldsymbol u)|\le\sum_{\boldsymbol e}|v_{\boldsymbol e}|
\le\sqrt D\,\|v\|_2.$$

Thus the strict, exactly checkable condition

$$D\|v\|_2^2<p^{2t}$$

together with shift membership implies$g(\boldsymbol u)=0$ over the integers.
This Howgrave-Graham-style soundness certificate is derived here; it is not
attributed to an unread general theorem. It uses achieved norms, not an asserted
LLL determinant guarantee. If a future design invokes an LLL bound, it must specify
rank, basis, metric, coefficient bit lengths and the exact covolume assumptions.

## Sound equations are not a root solver

Many coefficient-independent short vectors can be multiples of the same polynomial.
For example, multiples of$X_1-X_2$ can have large coefficient-matrix rank while
sharing a positive-dimensional zero set. Collecting more such vectors does not
isolate a tuple. Require exact ideal-dimension or an equivalent elimination
certificate, bounded integer-root extraction with all branches accounted for, and
verification in the original modular system. A zero-dimensional integer-equation
ideal is a useful sufficient interface for finite extraction, not a promise that
every modular root system will yield such an ideal under these shifts.

A Semaev zero can be an invalid rational decomposition. For$m=3$,
$S_4(t,t,x_R,x_R)=0$ can be witnessed geometrically by extension-field points
$T,-T,R,-R$ even when there is no rational point above$t$. Include a future control
with$t$ chosen where the curve right-hand side is a nonsquare. All root outputs
must recover each rational y, enumerate/resolve signs, check the actual sum$R$
and verify each leg in$G$. Rational points outside$G$ are also invalid. Repeated
legs or cancelling pairs may produce valid but useless relations; rank is the final
unit. Excluding diagonals/orderings to avoid them changes tuple counts and must
be recorded, including boundary and exceptional branches.

Root support is classical context, not new arithmetic: root's Semaev abstract
receipt establishes the known decomposition framing. Root read only the
[Coppersmith abstract](https://research.ibm.com/publications/small-solutions-to-polynomial-equations-and-low-exponent-rsa-vulnerabilities),
which concerns specific modular univariate and integer bivariate settings.
It does not provide a general modular multivariate solver. The
[Kosters–Yeo abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.08001) warns against treating
first-fall degree as a runtime justification. No first-fall/regularity inference
is used here. All source read scopes remain attributed to root.

## Density before a complexity claim

For any fixed actual$F\subseteq G$, $|F|=M$, count ordered m-tuples with repetition:

$$N(R)=|\{(T_1,\ldots,T_m)\in F^m: \sum T_i=R\}|.$$

Then$\sum_RN(R)=M^m$, so for uniform$R$,

$$\mathbb E N(R)=M^m/r,\qquad
\Pr[N(R)>0]\le\min(1,M^m/r).$$

This exact first moment is a **necessary density test**, not a Poisson model or a
success lower bound. A concentrated sumset can have much smaller support than its
mean suggests. Uniform distribution, near-independence and useful rank yield
each need separate arguments or evidence. Unordered distinct tuples have
combinatorial corrections; sign/orbit duplicates cannot be counted as independent
equations. Actual$M$ may be much less than$W$ because of rational and subgroup
filters. A planted target has a different success law from a uniform one.

In the restricted model$M=r^\beta$, fixed$m$, constant valid success and rank
yield, per-attempt solver cost$r^\sigma$, and classical sparse LA with bounded
row weight and work$M^2$, relation and LA terms are

$$r^{\beta+\sigma}+r^{2\beta}.$$

A strict subroot total in this model requires
$\beta+\sigma<1/2$, $\beta<1/4$ and$\beta m\ge1$, so$m\ge5$ is necessary.
This is not a universal lower bound, nor a sufficient attack condition. Sigma is
unknown, success may decay, LA may differ, setup may dominate, large-prime variants
need another model, and individual descent may cost as much as all relations.
The exact density and HG soundness deductions establish none of those missing costs.

Keeping addition-tree variables can reduce equation degrees, but unconstrained
intermediate sums need not be small as integers. Extra bounds$W'_j<p$ sacrifice
some decompositions; price and count that loss, do not assume a product-uniform
law. The same caution applies to route2's low-degree auxiliary-chain intermediates:
bounds near$p$ can erase norm feasibility. Sparse support and a density-preserving
low-height encoding are missing interfaces, not an automatic composition.

ideal. The label controls deliberately break the coordinate solver's meaning;
they test whether the apparent signal comes only from the exhaustive verifier.

factorization/root finding as the complete baseline, then apply the same box
filter. LLL must not be compared only with brute force when this algebraic baseline
applies. That univariate baseline does not give a general multivariate solver.

certified integer equation and a verified decomposition without hiding root
extraction? If yes, next measure uniform-target coverage and rank/cost. If none,
record the exact norm/independence/dimension bottleneck and change that one part in
a new plan. A cap or unavailable root extractor is an implementation limit, not
proof that small-root methods universally fail.
