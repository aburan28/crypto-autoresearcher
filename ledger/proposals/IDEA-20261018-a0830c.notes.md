## 1. Required ratios

delta*(h) = 1/(2h-1) (IDEA-20261017-bbc53e notes section 2). Over a ladder of 2^k in N the break-even
ratio is R* = 2^(k delta*).

| h | delta* | k = 8 (bits 14..22) | k = 6 (bits 16..22, ca5e01) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.333 | 6.35 | 4.00 |
| 3 | 0.200 | 3.03 | 2.30 |
| 4 | 0.143 | 2.21 | 1.81 |

ca5e01 flags abs(kappa - 1) >= 0.10. A 10% excess at every rung is a ratio of 1.0 across rungs: 40x below
the 4.0 needed at h = 2. A flagged cell that survives replication is therefore a constant-structure finding
unless the N-slope test also passes; the two readings are independent.

## 2. Slope precision from the ca5e01 design

Powered null cells have a bootstrap interval inside [0.90, 1.10], relative sd about 2%. Four rungs
spanning 6 bits of N: the slope sd is about 0.02/(ln 2 * sqrt(sum (x_i - x_bar)^2)) with x in {16,18,20,22}
(spread sqrt(20) = 4.47), so sd(delta) about 0.02/(0.693 * 4.47) = 0.0065, 95% half-width about 0.013. If
kappa is null at every rung the upper bound on delta is about 0.02, against delta* = 0.333: a 15x
sub-break-even margin. Cells sharing curves at the same bits are correlated; the cluster bootstrap over
curves gives the honest interval.

## 3. The Z/N interval positive with known slope

In Z/N an interval base [1, b] has pair sums in [2, 2b] with multiplicities m_s about min(s, 2b - s)/2, so
sum m_s^2 is about (2/3) b^3 / 4 up to the convention, against the random value b^4/(4N) times a constant:
kappa is about N/b times a constant = N^(1 - theta) times a constant. Slope 1 - theta: 0.70 at theta 0.30,
0.65 at 0.35. This is above break-even and exactly computable, so it fixes the estimator's scale. The
exact constant depends on the signed-pair convention; the slope does not.

## 4. The weighted-base plant

F' = {c P : P in F_0, 1 <= abs(c) <= c_0}, c_0 = N^gamma (bbc53e notes section 3). kappa_eff = 2 c_0 =
2 N^gamma for coincidences among distinct-index sums. Slope gamma exactly; gamma = 1/3 at h = 2 is the
break-even point. The plant is realizable in the ca5e01 engine as a base with known multiples of a few
unknown points; it is not a factor base with algebraic membership (IDEA-20261017-078074 tests whether any
label predicate could describe it; expected none).
