## 1. Where the random model is a theorem (Parseval route)

G = E(F_p) cyclic of prime order N, F subset of G, b = |F|, F^(chi) = sum_{P in F} chi(P).
Energy(F) = #{P1+P2=P3+P4} = N^-1 sum_chi |F^(chi)|^4 (Parseval for the 4-fold sum).
The trivial character gives b^4/N. Using sum_chi |F^(chi)|^2 = N b and K = max_{chi!=1}|F^(chi)|:

    Energy(F) - b^4/N = N^-1 sum_{chi!=1} |F^(chi)|^4 <= K^2 * b.

The excess is a small fraction eps of the main term iff K^2 b <= eps b^4/N, i.e. b^3 >= K^2 N / eps.
With K <= C p^(1/2) log p (Kohel-Shparlinski type bound for characters summed over an x-interval;
RECALLED, not read, and the exact form for the prime-order case must be checked) and N ~ p:

    b >= (C^2 p^2 log^2 p / eps)^(1/3) ~ p^(2/3).

So the theorem covers theta >= 2/3 (up to logs). A sharper route (box count on the 3-fold
{P1+P2=P3+P4} by Deligne-type bounds) may give 5/8; not derived here. Any Weil-type K ~ p^(1/2)
gives theta > 1/2, so no such bound reaches the sub-rho regime: |F| >= N^(1/2) already costs rho
to list. This is the closure statement: the proven range is vacuous for beating rho, and the
random model below it is a heuristic (HEUR-2765d2-H2, "recalled") that this idea measures.

## 2. Why rank-d GAPs, and what Choi's theorem does and does not say

Choi (arXiv:2510.03828, abstract only read): for E/Q of rank r>=1, a d-dimensional proper GAP in Q
containing x-coordinates of a positive proportion rho of a set of rational points contains at most
A(E,d,rho)^r of them. In characteristic 0 the group law and the additive structure of x-labels are
rigid. The F_p analogue is open, and false at full size (x(E(F_p)) + x(E(F_p)) = F_p). The toy
question is whether rigidity survives for sets of size <= p^(2/3): a window or GAP base is
label-additively structured by construction, so rigidity predicts group-additive randomness, kappa_E=1.
Choi's companion arXiv:2603.24340 (abstract only) bounds the energy of f(Gamma) for finite-rank Gamma
via uniform Mordell-Lang (Gao-Ge-Kuhne), which is a characteristic-0 tool; the abstract makes no
positive-characteristic statement. Neither paper is an index-calculus result. Both are ingredients to
log in knowledge/literature (no KN-LIT id was minted in this session).

## 3. Why d>=2 is not covered by the existing census

EXP-PFDR-011cd0 and EXP-PFDR-0b3699 census subgroup, small_x and dickson bases (d=1-like). The
rank-d GAPs of IDEA-20260808-486ae2 / e2315e / e0a3e8 and the binary-digit GAP of IDEA-20260830-84cdb7
were studied for small-root reach or Macaulay degree, not for coincidence counts.
A d=2 base {a + c*w : a<B1, c<B2} with generic w is a lattice box; for w=B1 it is the interval.

## 4. Counting conventions and power

Trivial coincidences: (P1,P2) a reordering or negation pair of (P3,P4); P1+P2=O (b of them for F
symmetric). The null symmetrizes a uniform subset of the points with f(x) square.
Ordered 4-tuples number about b^4/N. Distinct unordered coincidences are about one eighth of that
(four orderings, two sides). At p=2^20 and b~120 that is ~25 per curve, so 200 curves give ~1.5%
relative sd and the 0.10 effect is ~6 sd. At p=2^16, theta=0.30 (b~28) it is ~1 per curve, so a cell
needs ~2500 curves for the same power. The Executor sets curves per cell so that expected total
coincidences are >= 2500; a cell that cannot reach that is reported as inconclusive, never null.
