# Notes for idea-2 (last-fall / pinning degree versus chain length)

## 1. Instrument

HKYY (arXiv:1505.02532) Def 2.1: V_{F,i} is the smallest k-space with (i) F intersect R_{<=i} inside it, (ii) g in V, h in R, deg(hg) <= i implies hg in V. It is a MutantXL-style closure, not a single Macaulay matrix: elements of V_{F,i} of degree below i (mutants) are multiplied again by monomials up to total degree i. Compute by fixpoint: S = span of F-rows of degree <= D; repeat { for each basis vector g of S with actual degree deg g and every monomial h with deg h + deg g <= D: add h g reduced; } until stable.
Last fall degree d_F (Def 2.5, Prop 2.6(iii)): largest c with V_c intersect R_{<=c-1} != V_{c-1}. Prop 2.6(ii): a Groebner basis for a degree-refining order inside V_c implies d_F <= c. Prop 2.8: with e solutions and d = max(d_F, e), all solutions are found from V_d at cost polynomial in (m+d)^d.
For a unique-solution instance I = (y_j + z_j), reduced Groebner basis = the N linear forms, so D_pin is the least D with all of them in V_D: an upper bound on d_lf and an instance-level, assumption-free cost certificate. For an unsatisfiable instance the basis is {1}.
Two variants to report separately (EV-SEMBIN-c71cad shows definitions and S_fe membership change values): HKYY-literal in the polynomial ring with S_fe among the generators (degree = polynomial degree), and the Boolean-ring closure (degree after reduction, corrected XOR product).

## 2. Sizes

Boolean-ring multilinear columns sum_{j<=D} C(N,j) with N = km + n(m-2). Examples: (n,k,m) = (5,1,6): N = 26, D = 6: 313,912 columns. (6,1,5): N = 23. (8,2,4): N = 24. Price every cell with the BATCH-9d649f sizing before approval; an unreached cell is a scope limit, not a degree.

## 3. Why a growing D(m) is the expected competitor (heuristic, not a theorem)

First fall: local (min over blocks; idea-1). Last fall: global. Information from R must reach block 1 through m-2 n-bit variables. The partial sums P_i have about 2^{ki} points. If P_i is a generic subset of F_2^n, closure at degree d needs C(n,<=d) > |P_i| (idea-4). At n = 571 and |P| = 2^{285}: sum_{j<=D} C(571,j) >= 2^{285} first at D ~ 64 (entropy estimate: D = 63 gives about 280.6 bits, D = 64 about 284.6, D = 65 about 288). This bounds only a strategy (describe P_i then join), not every Macaulay derivation; hence the experiment.
Semi-regular null (BFSY-type asymptotics, recalled): for a random Boolean system with M = alpha N equations at fixed alpha > 1, the degree of regularity grows linearly in N. KR-IC-fbdb61 records that semi-regular predictions do not describe descended summation systems, so the topology-matched random null (IDEA-20260808-11b8c7) is the control that matters.
Semaev lines 633-637: for satisfiable systems F4 ran at most three more steps with step degrees 5, 6, 7 ("No pairs to reduce"). D_pin > 4 is therefore expected at m <= 6; the slope across m is the datum.

## 4. HKYY Theorem 1.1 applied to the V-restricted chain (not a result; for orientation)

Theorem 1.1 bounds the last fall degree of a Weil descent of a zero-dimensional radical system F over F_{q^n} in m' variables by max(tau(max(d_F, deg F, (m'+1)s, 1), q, m'), m' tau(2s, q, 1), q), tau(r,c,t) = max(floor(2t(c-1)(log_c(r/(2t)) + 1)), 0), s = number of solutions over the closure, independent of n. For the V-restricted chain the restriction equations have degree 2^k, so deg F = 2^k and tau ~ 2 m' k ~ 4n up to constants; the F_{q^n}-level last fall degree d_F, which the bound also needs, is unknown. Sec 6 of HKYY says Theorem 1.1 is not useful for summation-polynomial systems (not zero-dimensional without field equations). Treat this paragraph as orientation only: it suggests the assumption-free ceiling is at least linear in n, so Theorem 1's polynomiality lives entirely in the unproved gap between 4 and that ceiling. The formula was paraphrased by the fetch tool and read from the PDF page image (page 2); the instantiation to the chain is mine and unverified.
