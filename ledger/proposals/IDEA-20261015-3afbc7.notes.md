## Mechanism and its honest boundary

The candidate compares compatible isogeny-adjoint pairing witnesses and explicit
field representations. It retains F4 pairing transfer and F3 isogenies as authorized
known components. Its possible change is in charged arithmetic and reusable setup,
not the field in which the finite-field DLP lives. The representation is R1 point
and field coordinates, with an R2 curve presentation component. The relation stage
is partial-action: finite-field factor-base multiplications update relation labels.
The pairing restricted to the chosen subgroup is **lossless**. This is an authorized
comparison, not a claimed new lossy projection compatible with full translation.

Let $G=\langle P\rangle$ have prime order $r\ne\operatorname{char}(\mathbb F_q)$
and $Q=[a]P$. With a valid Weil-pairing witness $S$, set

$$g=e_r(P,S),\qquad h=e_r(Q,S)=g^a.$$

Verify $g\ne1$ and $g^r=1$. Since $r$ is prime, $g$ then has order $r$ and
the map on $G$ is injective. Taking both arguments on the same cyclic line gives
the degenerate alternating pairing; being able to write a pairing formula does not
supply $S$. Field of definition, point construction, Miller exceptional divisors
and final verification all belong to the cost. The reduced Tate alternative needs
its own domain, quotient and nondegeneracy checks; do not mix its slots with Weil's.

The embedding degree is $k=\operatorname{ord}_r(q)$, while target field cardinality
is $q^k$. A same-$\mathbb F_q$ isogeny does not change $q$. For degree $d$ coprime
to $r$, it is injective on $G$, so the same $r$ is retained. Therefore it cannot
change $k$. This deduction does not assert that entire rational point groups of
isogenous curves have identical abelian-group structure. Nor does a small $k$
alone price a finite-field solver: characteristic, polynomial/tower choice,
precomputation, subgroup size and individual descent matter.

For a compatible pair of witnesses under $\phi$, the Weil-pairing adjoint identity
gives

$$e_r(\phi P,\phi S)=e_r(P,S)^d,\qquad
e_r(\phi Q,\phi S)=e_r(Q,S)^d.$$

Thus $(g,h)$ becomes $(g^d,h^d)$ and the logarithm remains $a$. No inverse-degree
factor is needed when the destination base is exactly $\phi(P)$. A different
destination generator or use of the dual requires its explicitly derived
normalization. The adjacent indexed H-ECDLP-ed5162 contains a different transfer
formula and safety argument; neither is adopted here or edited. Any official
correction would need its normal review route.

For uniform $a\in\mathbb F_r$, $h$ is uniform in $\mu_r$. Because $d$ is invertible
modulo $r$, $h\mapsto h^d$ is a permutation. For a fixed deterministic field descent
cost function $C$, the average of $C(h^d)$ over the entire subgroup equals the
average of $C(h)$. This is a limited sampling deduction: it does not rule out a
cheaper pairing presentation, better polynomial selection, different solver, or
target-specific variation. It rules out inferring an average field-descent gain
merely by selecting favorable images of that power permutation.

## The real public lattice in special-$\ell$ sieving

In a number field $K=\mathbb Q(\theta)$, with defining polynomial $f$, let $s$ be
a simple root of $f$ modulo a good prime $\ell$. The degree-one ideal
$\mathfrak l=(\ell,\theta-s)$ divides $(a-b\theta)$ precisely when

$$a-bs\equiv0\pmod\ell.$$

This is the publicly specified rank-two lattice

$$L_{\ell,s}=\mathbb Z(\ell,0)+\mathbb Z(s,1),\qquad \det L_{\ell,s}=\ell.$$

All entries are available before any logarithms are computed. Short lattice
coordinates organize a sieve over algebraic norms; smooth principal ideals on
both number-field sides, unit/virtual-log corrections, filtering and independent
relations are still required. Divisibility by one ideal and a small norm do not
themselves constitute a usable discrete-log relation.

Contrast the ECDLP relation lattice for target factor-base points $F_1,\ldots,F_M$:

$$L_E=\{c\in\mathbb Z^M:\textstyle\sum_i c_iF_i=O\}.$$

If those points generate $G$, it has index $r$ and covolume $r$ in the usual
coordinate lattice. Writing it as $\sum c_i\log_P(F_i)=0\pmod r$ reveals the
missing data: the logarithms are unknown. Group operations test candidate
membership, but the determinant does not provide a cheap basis or a short-vector
oracle. Applying LLL to an unspecified basis of this lattice would assume the
relation construction being sought. This is the concrete distinction between
NFS lattice sieving and an imagined direct copy of its congruence into ECDLP.

The public-ideal derivation here is elementary. No full NFS complexity proof was
read in the supplied source scopes, and no precise NFS constant is imported from
memory. A future implementation should add the exact primary algorithm and its
regime before making an NFS performance claim.
