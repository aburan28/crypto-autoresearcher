## Parametrisation
aP+bQ=O, gcd(a,b)=1, choose (u,v) with bv+au=1, H=vP-uQ. Then bH=bvP-buQ=bvP+auP=P (using bQ=-aP) and -aH=-avP+auQ=bvQ+auQ=Q.
H is independent of the Bezout choice (shifts add multiples of aP+bQ=O). Reduction: H^=v*P^-u*Q^, unique.
Integrality of H (torsion-free case): a prime q with v_q(x_H)<0 puts H in E_1(Q_q), a subgroup for any integral Weierstrass model,
so bH in E_1(Q_q): P non-integral, contradiction.
Lift coordinates: x_H=x^+p*i, y_H=y^+p*j, A=-1+p*k, B=y_H^2/x_H-x_H^2-A*x_H; integer iff x_H | y_H^2; B^ = 16 follows from H^ on E^.
Pair family: (s,t,w,z) four integers with A=(w^2+/-z^2)/(s^2 +/- t^2)-(...) integral (one divisibility, S0 notes in IDEA-20261016-bf8f49);
frozen box = 4 t-reps x 4 t-reps x 17 x 17 = 4624 cells per eligible arm (matches targets.json "proposal_cells": 4624).

## Heights (why the H-box is a cap, not a bound)
h(bH)=b^2 h^(H)+O(1) and the canonical/naive difference grows like log(|A|+|B|); here A,B reach ~1e7 so the difference is comparable to the
box height. A rigorous H-box would need an explicit Silverman-type difference bound per curve; out of scope.

## Integral multiples
Dependent integer pairs need H and two multiples integral. Nagell-Lutz: an integral point whose double is non-integral is of infinite order;
conversely integral doubles require 4*y_H^2... divisibility x(2H)=(x^2-B)^2/(4y^2) in Z. Ingram (arXiv 0802.2651, abstract read only):
for minimal models at most one integral [n]H with n>C(Tamagawa number). The program already holds the toy analogue in function fields (KN-FIND-003/010).
