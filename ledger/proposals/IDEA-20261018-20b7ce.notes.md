## 1. Forced sharing probability

Certificate of a refuted r: (mu, lambda) with mu S + lambda T(r) = e_1; the solution set Lambda(r) is an
affine subspace of F_2^c (c = number of row coefficients) of dimension s(r) = left-kernel dimension of
[S; T(r)]. Sharing (IDEA-20261017-845d35 (C)): Lambda(r) and Lambda(r') meet. For two affine subspaces
with linear parts L, L' of dimensions s, s' and offsets differing by v: they meet iff v is in L + L'. If L
and L' are in generic position, dim(L + L') = min(c, s + s') minus a corank k with the limiting law
P(k) = alpha 2^(-k^2)/prod_{i<=k}(1 - 2^(-i))^2, alpha about 0.2888 (Kolchin; Brent-McKay, recalled). For
s + s' > c the sum is all of F_2^c except on the corank event, so P_forced = 1 - O(2^(-(s + s' - c))); for
s + s' < c a uniform v lies in L + L' with probability 2^(-(c - s - s')). Probe cell: s about 9000, c =
17619: s + s' = 18000 > c, P_forced near 1, matching 8/8. The 845d35 null (random pencil matched on the
support histogram) does not fix s and so does not reproduce this.

The real systems share trivial syzygies (Koszul relations among the S_3 blocks, field equations x^2 = x)
that inflate s identically for every target; matching the (rank, s) profile absorbs them. Measuring
"sharing modulo trivial syzygies" (the probe's first redesign item) is the same thing as reading pi_share
against P_forced.

## 3. The two caps

Let p be the SAT fraction of targets, about 1/t! at the balanced base size (yield mean about 1/t!).
Classify-all: each coset certificate is one negative group test; identifying the p 2^n SAT targets among
2^n needs at least 2^n H_2(p) tests (IDEA-20261017-bd337d (C)), so a certificate library saves at most
1/H_2(p) in test count: 1.54, 4.0, 14.4, 65.9 at t = 3..6. Skip-only: index calculus does not classify,
it harvests; a free oracle that says UNSAT lets the solver run only on SAT targets, saving at most 1/p =
t!. Both are constants in N. On the t-axis 1/p = t! is the per-slot m! that IDEA-20261017-3db2a8 and
IDEA-20260913-8138a0 identify as exponent-carrying, and IDEA-20261018-f2cd4d shows symmetry breaking
already recovers one such m! on the solver side (a different quotient: orderings of a SAT solution, not
SAT/UNSAT selection). Whether a refutation-side t! composes with the solver-side m! is a question for the
RQ, not a claim here; the honest statement is that every SBRG reuse lead lives between 1 and t!.
