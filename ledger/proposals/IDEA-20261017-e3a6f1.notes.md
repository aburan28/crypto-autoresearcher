## 1. K1: the cocycle and its primitive

E/F_p, P of prime order N, gcd(N, p-1) = 1 (so no pairing into F_p^*, and F_p^* = (F_p^*)^N). Let f = f_{N,P}
with div f = N(P) - N(O), normalised monic at O (leading coefficient 1 in the uniformizer x/y). For R not in {O,P}
put T(R) = f(R) in F_p^* (Miller's algorithm computes it from P and R alone).

For R, S, R+S outside {O,P}: g_{R,S} = line(R,S)/vertical(R+S), div g = (R)+(S)-(R+S)-(O), monic at O. Weil
reciprocity, f(div g) = g(div f), gives T(R)T(S)/T(R+S) = +-g_{R,S}(P)^N (sign from the tame symbols; N odd, so a
fixed sign passes through N-th roots). Define U(R) = T(R)^(N') with N' = N^(-1) mod (p-1), the unique N-th root. Then

    U(R) U(S) / U(R+S) = +- g_{R,S}(P) = +- (y_P - y_R - lambda_{R,S}(x_P - x_R)) / (x_P - x_{R+S}).

The right side is an explicit function of the coordinates of P, R, S. Uniqueness: two primitives of one cocycle with
U(O) = 1 differ by a homomorphism Z/N -> F_p^*, and Hom(Z/N, F_p^*) = 1 because gcd(N, p-1) = 1. When N divides
p-1 the N-th root is not unique and the cocycle is a coboundary only modulo N-th powers: the Tate pairing lives
there, and the primitive does not exist. (Edge cases R, S in {O, P}: define by the derivative-normalised value or
by a once-per-curve constant U(P); a record-level confounder.)

## 2. K2: the EDS form

Over C, f(z) = c0 [sigma(z - z_P)/sigma(z)]^N e^{kappa z}, N z_P in the period lattice. With
sigma(j z_P) = psi_j(P) sigma(z_P)^(j^2):
f(k z_P) = c0 sigma^N (psi_{k-1}/psi_k)^N (e^{kappa z_P} sigma^(-2N))^k. So T(kP) = c' r_k^N A^k with
r_k = psi_{k-1}(P)/psi_k(P). N-periodicity in k plus psi_{k+N} = +- D C^k psi_k (quasi-periodicity of a divisibility
sequence with psi_N = 0) forces A = C, giving U(kP) = c'' r_k C^(k/N). The identity is polynomial in the data, so
it reduces mod p where no pole occurs; the toy test fits c'', C from k = 2, 3 and checks the rest.

Telescoping consequence: x(kP) = x(P) - psi_{k-1} psi_{k+1}/psi_k^2 gives r_k/r_{k+1} = x_P - x_{kP}. So
ell(k+1) - ell(k) = -log_g(x_P - x_{kP}) + (log_g C)/N: ell is a prefix sum of F_p^*-logs of x-differences along the
multiples of P. Locating k from a prefix-sum value is inversion of a pseudo-random walk, which is the content of H1.

## 3. Why the special form of p is not the lever here

A discrete logarithm in F_p^* for a 256-bit p is feasible by general NFS (published records are above 700 bits,
recalled), and the special form only improves constants. So U sends E(F_p)[N] into a group where discrete logs are
cheap for every ECC-size p. Whether anything is gained is therefore entirely the question whether ell leaks k; the
special and matched-random arms in the record test p-form invariance of that question, not a sieve advantage. This
differs from the H3 picture (the sieve sees p only through F_{p^k}^*, k huge): here the transfer target is F_p^*
itself, at the price of a non-homomorphic map with a computable defect.
