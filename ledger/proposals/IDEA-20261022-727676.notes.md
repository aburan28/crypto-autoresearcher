## 1. Two points where the brief and the repo disagree (read before ranking)

1. The brief says the repo's audit (docs/ic/GROEBNER_F4F5F6_AUDIT_20261010.md, around line 265) "suggests symbolic
   preprocessing is a small share of a step at these sizes". The text at lines 264-268 says the opposite, for `f4_fp`
   (the generic F_p engine, quad_n8/n9 at p = 65521, not `f4_fp_tower`): "The dense residue elimination is a small share
   of a step at these sizes; the symbolic preprocessing and the sparse pair-row reduction ... are the larger part".
   Callgrind (section 4.6): 58 percent of instructions in the pair-row reduction and 12 percent in dense residue elimination
   before the Faugere-Lachartre change; after it, "allocation, copying and hashing had been about 30 percent of instructions"
   in symbolic preprocessing, then reduced. Section 4.5 (Boolean/F_2 inherited F4, a different engine) says the root build is
   2-3 percent of a target. So the share of symbolic work in `f4_fp_tower` is unmeasured and could be large. Consequently this
   draft makes no point prediction for the time share U_time; only the owner's prediction on multiply-adds (Z) is stated.
2. The brief lists "an identical pivot structure across targets" as a falsifier of the <5 percent prediction. Committed
   evidence already makes identical structure close to certain: RESEARCH_PKM_TOWER_ORACLE.md section 11 (K1 table) records
   "At each N the two targets give the same D, width and step count", nonzero and multiply-add counts identical across
   primes at the two large primes, and "a few parts per million" different at the 20-bit prime from cancellations;
   f4_generic_field_20261015 records 481,961,879,776 multiply-adds for Kummer m=2 N=20 at 45, 52, 61 and 224 bits.
   Identity is the precondition for replay; it says nothing about the payoff. So the draft uses identity as an expected
   outcome and a validity precondition (Jaccard >= 0.999), and takes the falsifier of "the lever is negligible" to be
   Z >= 0.25 or U_time >= 0.25.
   The unit also matters: the engine's unit is F_p multiply-adds in the eliminations, and symbolic preprocessing has no
   multiply-adds in that unit, so "share of multiply-adds" can only measure Z (work on rows that turn out zero).

## 2. What replay can and cannot remove [derived]

Per step the engine does: pair selection and symbolic preprocessing (`ms_rows`, boundary read in f4_fp_tower.rs: from the
pair partition at line 1915-1916 to the S-row ordering at line 2076), the reduced reducer block B' (`ms_reduce`), the S-rows and
their echelon (`ms_echelon`), and the basis update (`ms_update`). Everything in `ms_rows` is structure: which pairs, which
monomials, which rows lead where. B', the S-row reduction and the echelon are arithmetic whose values depend on x_R: a
replay still performs them, only without searching and without the rows the trace flags as zero. Joux-Vitse record "which
reductions are useful" and replay [KN-LIT-c137bd abstract only]. So the removable amount is
(ms_rows + ms_update + the elimination time spent on zero-outcome rows). Amdahl: speedup <= 1/(1 - U_time). Even
at U_time = 0.5 that is 2x; D (6-7) and the width W(m,N) are unchanged, so no exponent moves and the N=21 memory bound
(B' dense block, 130-150 GB projected for m=3 N=21, README encyclopedia) is untouched.
Z is read from a new split of muladds by row outcome. With `full_rank_exit` on (default), rows after the echelon is
full are skipped and never reduced; the round-6 note records -39.8 percent muladds at m=4 N=12 from that alone, so much
zero-reducing work is already harvested and Z under default options is expected small [derived expectation]; Z with the
exit off shows the size of what the exit took.

## 3. Instrument (low cost) [proposed]

- StepTrace already carries degree, critical_pairs, tower_pairs, s_rows, reducer_rows, promoted_rows, cols, nnz,
  residual_rows/cols, full_rank_after, skipped_rows, fresh, muladds and the four ms fields. Add: (i) a 128-bit digest of the
  sorted column monomial list, of the set of fresh pivot leads, of the (generator, multiplier) descriptors of the S-rows and
  of the promoted rows; (ii) the list of fresh pivot leads itself (for Jaccard) behind a flag, because a step can have 10^5 leads;
  (iii) muladds attributed to S-rows whose residue is zero vs rows that yield a pivot (counter in `eliminate`/`echelon`).
- The instrument must not change `muladds`, the basis fingerprint, `solving_degree_max` or `ms` by more than noise: that is
  the regression control, and the A/A control (same target twice) must give identical digests and muladds exactly.
- Cross-target comparison: Jaccard of fresh pivot leads per step, equality of cols and nnz, equality of s_rows/residual_rows.
- Committed traces do not hold the ms splits (round-2 and round-7 directories do not contain `ms_rows`), so the cells are
  re-run. `--trace`, `--targets 1` and the committed row parameters are in examples/pkm_tower_pilot.rs.

## 5. Controls and what they would mean

- A/A identical: instrument sanity; any difference means nondeterminism (thread order) leaks into the digest.
- Shape null (S_3 support with random coefficients, `--controls null`, `--null-chain` at m >= 3): the encyclopedia and
  round notes say the null has the same D, width and step count as the tower system, so structure identity should hold there
  too; this separates "identity from shape" from "identity from the tower arithmetic".
- Instrument power: compare Kummer to the isogeny tower at one (m,N). The two families differ in width (K0 33,446 vs I0 35,158
  columns at m=2 N=20), so the digest must see a difference; if it does not, the digest is too coarse.
- Planted vs random target at a small cell (t <= 7 has both): a decomposable target ends differently (the staircase stop fires),
  so structure should coincide until the last steps and diverge there.
- H1 tail check: 16 targets at m=2 N=14, at p0 (20-bit) and a 31-bit prime; the number of steps with any digest difference
  should scale like (number of cancellation events)/p; the committed 20-bit ppm differences are the calibration.

## 6. Optional arm B (value reuse), proposed by the Idea Generator, droppable [derived/proposed]

At m >= 3 the pilot's summation part is the chain S_3(x_1,x_2,u_1), S_3(u_k,x_{k+2},u_{k+1}), S_3(u_{m-2},x_m,x_R):
examples/pkm_tower_pilot.rs `summation()`. Only the last link contains x_R, so m-2 of the m-1 links are literally
target-independent (m=3: 1 of 2 links, m=4: 2 of 3). Arm B: compute the Groebner basis of the target-independent part
(chain links without x_R, with the tower relations built into the ring) to degree D_cap = the full run's solving degree, once; then
run F4 on {that basis, the last link} per target, and compare muladds and the widest matrix with the full run, checking the
same inconsistent/D outcome. Report C_seed(target)/C_full and the slope of log2 C_seed vs N over N = 9, 12, 15 against the
full-run slope (the encyclopedia quotes 2.1 bits per tower variable at m=3). By analogy with IDEA-20260829-fa8235, which
derives a reuse ceiling of 1/(target-dependent row fraction) in the F_2 Boolean setting (t-1 for a chain of t-1
links), a first sanity bound here is a ceiling of about m-1 (2 at m=3, 3 at m=4) from generator count alone; that
analogy is NOT checked for F4 over R. A measured ratio beyond it would mean reuse of more than bookkeeping.
This is an addition beyond the brief; arm A stands alone.

## 7. Decision rule and what each outcome means

- LIVE (Z >= 0.25 or U_time >= 0.25, with Jaccard >= 0.999): a replay prototype (a separate record, with a new contract)
  can give at most 1/(1-U) at the stage; moves no exponent; not an improvement of the end-to-end baseline by itself.
- DEAD (Z < 0.05 and U_time < 0.05): close the lever at these cells with the numbers; nothing else follows.
- Between: report the Amdahl bound and stop.
- Jaccard < 0.99 on a random-target step: structure depends on x_R; replay is invalid; this would itself be a finding
  about the generic-specialisation assumption (H1).
