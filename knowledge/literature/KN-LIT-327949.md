## Contribution

Analyzes the point decomposition problem (PDP) for binary elliptic curves:
given `R` and a factor base `B` of points with x-coordinates in an
`n0`-dimensional `F2`-subspace `V` of `F_{2^n}`, find `P_1..P_m in B` with
`R = P_1 + ... + P_m`. Semaev (`KN-LIT-001`) reduced PDP to a root of the
`(m+1)`-st summation polynomial `S_{m+1}` in `V^m`; the standard approach
(FPPR `KN-LIT-023`, Petit-Quisquater `KN-LIT-005`) Weil-descends that single
equation directly. This paper's contribution is a different system of
equations for the same problem: introduce **intermediate points as auxiliary
variables** so that only low-order Semaev polynomials (`S_3`, sometimes `S_4`)
appear, trading more variables for lower degree, then Weil-descend the
resulting *multi-equation* system instead.

**Two distinct constructions, not one** (a distinction the paper itself does
not flag as sharply as this note does):

- **Sections 3.1-3.3 (`m = 3, 4, 5`, one intermediate point).** Split
  `R = P_1+...+P_m` into two summands via a single new point (e.g. for `m=5`:
  `P_1+P_2+P_3-P_123=0`, `P_4+P_5+P_123-R=0`), giving a 2-equation system
  using `S_3` and `S_{m-1}` (so `S_4` at `m=5`). Proved first-fall bounds:
  `D_FirstFall <= 4` (`m=3`), `<= 7` (`m=4`), `<= 9` (`m=5`). Table 1
  reports Magma Groebner runs (grevlex, `n0 = floor(n/m)`, 5 random instances
  each, Intel Xeon E31240 3.30GHz) up to `n=26` (`m=3`), `n=19` (`m=4,5`),
  observing `D_reg = 4` (`m=3`), `7` (`m=4`), `<= 8` (`m=5`) -- consistent
  with the proved ceiling but not tight to it at `m=5`.
- **Section 5 (`m = 5` only, three intermediate points, all-`S_3`).** Pairs
  points two at a time (`P_12`, `P_34`, `P_50`) and closes with a fourth `S_3`
  equation on the three intermediates: 4 equations, all `S_{3}`, 8 variables
  total. Proved `D_FirstFall <= 4` -- markedly better than the direct
  route's `<= 9`. Table 2 (same machine, `n = 11..19`) measures `D_reg = 4`
  at every tested `n`, and reports that setting Magma's `ReductionHeuristic`
  to 4 and Groebner-reducing the two "wing" sub-blocks before merging cuts
  cost sharply -- at `n=15`: `(393.170 s, 6421.9 MB) -> (7.130 s, 256.3 MB)`.
  This is the construction the paper's own introduction previews ("our
  polynomial system consists of third Semaev polynomials `S_{3,i}`
  (`i=1,2,3,4`)") and the one `KN-LIT-e77232`'s summary of Karabina's `m=5`
  proposal actually refers to.

## Cost estimates -- re-checked here

Both estimates use `Cn,m,n0 ~ 2^{n0} \cdot 2^n m! / 2^{mn0} \cdot \binom{N+D_reg-1}{D_reg}^w + 2^{w'n0}` (eq. 3, `w=3`, `w'=2` sparse linear algebra), *assuming* `D_reg` stays at its observed small value as `n -> infinity` -- an extrapolation the paper itself calls only "tempting" and does not defend beyond the tested range.

| Construction | `N` (variables) | Assumed `D_reg` | Formula (paper's own) | Worked example | Crossover vs. Pollard rho |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sec. 3.3, direct `S_4` (`m=5`) | `5n0+n ~ 2n` | `<= 9` | `~ 2^34 * 2^{n/5} * n^27 + 2^{2n/5}` | `n~1200`: `2^550` vs rho `2^600` | not stated exactly; re-derived here at `n ~ 1020-1050` |
| Sec. 5, all-`S_3` (`m=5`) | `5n0+3n ~ 4n` | `<= 4` | `~ 2^31 * 2^{n/5} * n^12 + 2^{2n/5}` | `n~550`: `2^250` vs rho `2^275` | **stated exactly: `n > 457`** |

Both worked examples and the stated `n > 457` threshold were recomputed
independently from the paper's own formulas and matched (within rounding).
The direct-`S_4` route states no analogous exact crossover; `n ~ 1020-1050`
above is this entry's own solution of the same formula, not a value the
paper gives. **The two constructions are not the same claim at two scales**:
Section 5's all-`S_3` route needs roughly half the extension degree of the
direct route to reach the same qualitative claim, entirely because of the
smaller assumed `D_reg` (4 vs. 9), holding the same heuristic-extrapolation
risk in both cases.

## Version note

Confirms the open question in `KN-LIT-e77232`: ePrint 2015/319 and
arXiv:1504.02347 are the **same paper** (identical title, near-identical
abstract). But this entry reads the ePrint archive's **final** revision
(2015-10-27, third of three), not the version current when Galbraith wrote
the ellipticnews post on 2015-04-13 -- that post is contemporaneous with
arXiv v1 (2015-04-09) / v2 (2015-04-18). The blog's crossover figure
("might start to beat rho once `q = 2^n > 2^700`") matches neither of this
revision's own stated examples (`n~1200` / `2^550`, or `n>457` / `2^250`
exactly); it may be Galbraith's own rounding, or drawn from a construction or
constant present in an earlier revision and refined by the time of the final
text. Left unreconciled rather than guessed at.

## The paper's own acknowledgment -- corroborates the blog's dispute from inside the source

Karabina thanks Semaev and Kosters by name for comments on an earlier draft,
and states: Semaev's ePrint 2015/310 (`KN-LIT-009`) claims complexity
`2^{c*sqrt(n ln n)}` assuming `D_reg <= 4`, or `2^{o(c*sqrt(n ln n))}` under
the weaker `D_reg = o(sqrt(n / ln n))` -- "the techniques used in [Semaev
2015/310] and in this paper are similar." He then records, without
disputing it: "Kosters and Yeo [`KN-LIT-7604`] provide experimental evidence
that the degree of regularity of the underlying polynomial systems is likely
to increase as a function of `n`, whence the conjecture `D_reg ~ D_FirstFall`
may be false." The final ePrint revision therefore already carries, in the
author's own words, the objection `KN-LIT-e77232` records from the blog
comment thread -- this is the same three-way exchange from the other side.

## Relevance to this program

1. **Direct prior art for the chained-`S_3` object.** Section 5's
   construction is Karabina's version of the same "unrolled resultant" idea
   `EXP-DREG-001` / `KN-FIND-006` / `KN-OPEN-d6ad3f` measure, independently
   of Semaev's. Any proposal on that object now has two first-hand-read
   sources (`KN-LIT-009`, this entry) plus the secondary account
   (`KN-LIT-e77232`).
2. **A second, harder variant already in the literature.** Sections 3.1-3.3's
   direct-`S_4` route (`D_FirstFall <= 7` at `m=4`, `<= 9` at `m=5`) is a
   *different* auxiliary-variable construction from the all-`S_3` one this
   program has been citing, with its own proved first-fall bound and its own
   (weaker) asymptotic claim. It is a legitimate comparison object for any
   experiment on chained-Semaev degree: same problem, deliberately not fully
   degree-reduced.
3. **A concrete, reproducible engineering lever.** The `ReductionHeuristic`
   parameter and partial-system-then-merge trick (Section 5) are stated as
   the source of a ~50x time and ~25x memory reduction at `n=15`, `m=5` on a
   real solver (Magma) -- a specific, checkable instrument-tuning claim,
   distinct from any mathematical claim about the systems themselves.
4. **Scale.** Every measured figure here is `n <= 26` (`m=3`) or `n <= 19`
   (`m=4,5`); every asymptotic claim is conditional on `D_reg` staying at its
   observed small value indefinitely, a conjecture the paper's own
   acknowledgment reports contrary evidence for.
