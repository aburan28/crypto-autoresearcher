## Contribution
We prove complexity lower bounds for the tasks of hardness amplification of one-way functions and construction of pseudo-random generators from one-way functions, which are realized non-adaptively in black-box ways. First, we consider the task of converting a one-way function f : {0, 1}n → {0, 1}m into a harder one-way function f ̄ : {0, 1}n̄ → {0, 1}m̄ , with n̄, m̄ ≤ poly(n), in a black-box way.

## Key claims (as reported)
- The hardness is measured as the fraction of inputs any polynomial-size circuit must fail to invert.
- We show that to use a constant-depth circuit to amplify hardness beyond a polynomial factor, its size must exceed 2poly(n) , and to amplify hardo(n) ness beyond a 2o(n) factor, its size must exceed 22 .
- Moreover, for a constant-depth circuit to amplify hardness beyond an n1+o(1) factor in o(1) a security preserving way (with n̄ = O(n)), it size must exceed 2n .
- Next, we show that if a constant-depth polynomial-size circuit can amplify hardness beyond a polynomial factor in a weakly black-box way, then it must basically embed a hard function in itself.
