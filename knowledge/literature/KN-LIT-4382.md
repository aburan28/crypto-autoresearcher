The security of lattice-based cryptosystems such as NTRU, GGH and Ajtai-Dwork essentially relies upon the intractability of computing a shortest non-zero lattice vector and a closest lattice vector to a given target vector in high dimensions.

## Key claims (as reported)
- The best algorithms for these tasks are due to Kannan, and, though remarkably simple, their complexity estimates have not been improved since over twenty years.
- Kannan’s algorithm for solving the shortest vector problem (SVP) is in particular crucial in Schnorr’s celebrated block reduction algorithm, on which rely the best known generic attacks against the lattice-based encryption schemes mentioned above.
- In this paper we improve the complexity upper-bounds of Kannan’s algorithms.
- The analysis provides new insight on the practical cost of solving SVP, and helps progressing towards providing meaningful key-sizes.
