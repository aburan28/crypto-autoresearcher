## Contribution

Abstract (verbatim from the publisher page): "Using a number field sieve,
discrete logarithms modulo primes of special forms can be found faster than
standard primes. This has raised concerns about trapdoors in discrete log
cryptosystems, such as the Digital Signature Standard. This paper discusses
the practical impact of these trapdoors, and how to avoid them."

This is the origin of the **hidden special-form prime** trapdoor: a prime
`p = f(m)` for a low-degree polynomial `f` with small coefficients admits the
special number field sieve, and the paper is the first to ask (a) how to
construct such a `p` so that it looks random and (b) whether the special
form can be detected from `p` alone. The DSA concern it names was realized
practically twenty-four years later at 1024 bits by KN-LIT-7635.

## Key claims (as reported; only the abstract and bibliographic page were read)

- Primes of special form give the SNFS a strictly smaller `L(1/3)` constant
  than the general sieve, so a trapdoored `p` is weaker than a random one of
  the same size.
- The practical impact at the time was limited by the then-current reach of
  the sieve; the recommended mitigation is a verifiably random generation
  of `p` (the approach later adopted in FIPS 186 for DSA parameters).

Not verified here: the exact detection results and the concrete polynomial
degrees the paper discusses. Read the full text before citing those.
