## Contribution
We initiate a study of the security of cryptographic primitives in the presence of efficient tampering attacks to the randomness of honest parties. More precisely, we consider p-tampering attackers that may efficiently tamper with each bit of the honest parties’ random tape with probability p, but have to do so in an “online” fashion.

## Key claims (as reported)
- Our main result is a strong negative result: We show that any secure encryption scheme, bit commitment scheme, or zero-knowledge protocol can be “broken” with probability p by a p-tampering attacker.
- The core of this result is a new Fourier analytic technique for biasing the output of boundedvalue functions, which may be of independent interest.
- We also show that this result cannot be extended to primitives such as signature schemes and identification protocols: assuming the existence of one-way functions, such primitives can be made resilient to (1/poly(n))tampering attacks where n is the security parameter.5
