## Contribution
We consider weakly-verifiable puzzles which are challengeresponse puzzles such that the responder may not be able to verify for itself whether it answered the challenge correctly. We consider k-wise direct product of such puzzles, where now the responder has to solve k puzzles chosen independently in parallel.

## Key claims (as reported)
- Canetti et al have earlier shown that such direct product puzzles have a hardness which rises exponentially with k.
- In the threshold case addressed in Impagliazzo et al, the responder is required to answer correctly a fraction of challenges above a threshold.
- The bound on hardness of this threshold parallel version was shown to be similar to Chernoff bound, but the constants in the exponent are rather weak.
- Namely, Impagliazzo et al show that for a puzzle for which probability of failure is δ, the probability of failing on less than 2 (1 − γ)δk out of k puzzles, for any parallel strategy, is at most e−γ δk/40 .
