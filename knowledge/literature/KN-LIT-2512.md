## Contribution
Random delays are often inserted in embedded software to protect against side-channel and fault attacks. At CHES 2009 a new method for generation of random delays was described that increases the attacker’s uncertainty about the position of sensitive operations.

## Key claims (as reported)
- In this paper we show that the CHES 2009 method is less secure than claimed.
- We describe an improved method for random delay generation which does not suffer from the same security weakness.
- We also show that the paper’s criterion to measure the security of random delays can be misleading, so we introduce a new criterion for random delays which is directly connected to the number of acquisitions required to break an implementation.
- We mount a power analysis attack against an 8-bit implementation of the improved method verifying its higher security in practice.
