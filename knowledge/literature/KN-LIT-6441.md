## Contribution
Physically unclonable functions (PUFs) exploit the unavoidable manufacturing variations of an integrated circuit (IC). Their input-output behavior serves as a unique IC ‘fingerprint’.

## Key claims (as reported)
- Therefore, they have been envisioned as an IC authentication mechanism, in particular for the subclass of so-called strong PUFs.
- The protocol proposals are typically accompanied with two PUF promises: lightweight and an increased resistance against physical attacks.
- In this work, we review eight prominent proposals in chronological order: from the original strong PUF proposal to the more complicated converse and slender PUF proposals.
