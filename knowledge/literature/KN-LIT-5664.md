## Contribution
Structure-preserving signatures are signatures defined over bilinear groups that rely on generic group operations. In particular, the messages and signatures consist of group elements and the verification of signatures consists of evaluating pairing product equations.

## Key claims (as reported)
- Due to their purist nature structure-preserving signatures blend well with other pairing-based protocols.
- We show that structure-preserving signatures must consist of at least 3 group elements when the signer uses generic group operations.
- Usually, the generic group model is used to rule out classes of attacks by an adversary trying to break a cryptographic assumption.
- In contrast, here we use the generic group model to prove a lower bound on the complexity of digital signature schemes.
