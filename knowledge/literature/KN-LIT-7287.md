## Contribution
Polynomial hashing as an instantiation of universal hashing is a widely employed method for the construction of MACs and authenticated encryption (AE) schemes, the ubiquitous GCM being a prominent example. It is also used in recent AE proposals within the CAESAR competition which aim at providing nonce misuse resistance, such as POET.

## Key claims (as reported)
- The algebraic structure of polynomial hashing has given rise to security concerns: At CRYPTO 2008, Handschuh and Preneel describe key recovery attacks, and at FSE 2013, Procter and Cid provide a comprehensive framework for forgery attacks.
- Both approaches rely heavily on the ability to construct forgery polynomials having disjoint sets of roots, with many roots (“weak keys”) each.
- Constructing such polynomials beyond naı̈ve approaches is crucial for these attacks, but still an open problem.
- In this paper, we comprehensively address this issue.
