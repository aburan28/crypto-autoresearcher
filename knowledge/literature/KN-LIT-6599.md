## Contribution
We introduce the short-lived proof, a non-interactive proof of knowledge with a novel feature: after a specified period of time, the proof is no longer convincing. This time-delayed loss of soundness happens “naturally” without further involvement from the prover or any third party.

## Key claims (as reported)
- We propose definitions for short-lived proofs as well as the special case of short-lived signatures.
- We show several practical constructions built using verifiable delay functions (VDFs).
- The key idea in our approach is to allow any party to forge any proof by executing a large sequential computation.
- Some constructions achieve a stronger property called reusable forgeability in which one sequential computation allows forging an arbitrary number of proofs of di↵erent statements.
