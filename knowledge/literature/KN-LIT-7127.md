## Contribution
We revisit the question of minimizing the randomness complexity of protocols for secure multiparty computation (MPC) in the setting of perfect information-theoretic security. Kushilevitz and Mansour (SIAM J.

## Key claims (as reported)
- Math., 1997) studied the case of n-party semi-honest MPC for the XOR function with security threshold t < n, showing that O(t2 log(n/t)) random bits are sufficient and Ω(t) random bits are necessary.
- Their positive result was obtained via a non-explicit protocol, whose existence was proved using the probabilistic method.
- We essentially close the question by proving an Ω(t2 ) lower bound on the randomness complexity of XOR, matching the previous upper bound up to a logarithmic factor (or constant factor when t = Ω(n)).
- We also obtain an explicit protocol that uses O(t2 · log2 n) random bits, matching our lower bound up to a polylogarithmic factor.
