## Contribution
In this paper we identify the (P, Q)-DDH assumption, as an extreme, powerful generalization of the Decisional Diffie-Hellman (DDH) assumption: virtually all previously proposed generalizations of DDH are instances of the (P, Q)-DDH problem. We prove that our generalization is no harder than DDH through a concrete reduction that we show to be rather tight in most practical cases.

## Key claims (as reported)
- One important consequence of our result is that it yields significantly simpler security proofs for protocols that use extensions of DDH.
- We exemplify in the case of several group-key exchange protocols (among others we give an elementary, direct proof for the Burmester-Desmedt protocol).
- Finally, we use our generalization of DDH to extend the celebrated computational soundness result of Abadi and Rogaway [1] so that it can also handle exponentiation and DiffieHellman-like keys.
- The extension that we propose crucially relies on our generalization and seems hard to achieve through other means.
