## Contribution
One of the fundamental hardness assumptions underlying isogenybased cryptography is the problem of finding a non-trivial endomorphism of a given supersingular elliptic curve. We show that this problem is related to the problem of finding a good splitting of a principally polarized superspecial abelian surface.

## Key claims (as reported)
- We provide formal security reductions, as well as a proof-ofconcept implementation of an algorithm to compute endomorphisms of elliptic curves by solving the splitting problem.

## Relevance to this program
Relevant to the isogeny cluster: bears on supersingular/isogeny-based constructions and the isogeny-path problems that compete with and inform ECDLP work. Relevant to pairing-based reductions and endomorphism speedups (MOV/Frey-Rück special cases, GLV/GLS) that bound which curve classes are safe baselines.
