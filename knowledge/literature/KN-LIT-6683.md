## Contribution
Waters’ variant of the Boneh-Boyen IBE scheme is attractive because of its efficency, applications, and security attributes, but suffers from a relatively complex proof with poor concrete security. This is due in part to the proof’s “artificial abort” step, which has then been inherited by numerous derivative works.

## Key claims (as reported)
- It has often been asked whether this step is necessary.
- We show that it is not, providing a new proof that eliminates this step.
- The new proof is not only simpler than the original one but offers better concrete security for important ranges of the parameters.
- As a result, one can securely use smaller groups, resulting in significant efficiency improvements.

## Relevance to this program
Relevant to pairing-based reductions and endomorphism speedups (MOV/Frey-Rück special cases, GLV/GLS) that bound which curve classes are safe baselines.
