## Contribution
KeeLoq is a block cipher used in wireless devices that unlock the doors and alarms in cars manufactured by Chrysler, Daewoo, Fiat, GM, Honda, Jaguar, Toyota, Volvo, Volkswagen, etc [8, 9, 33, 34]. KeeLoq is inexpensive to implement and economical in gate count, yet according to Microchip [33] it should have “a level of security comparable to DES”.

## Key claims (as reported)
- In this paper we present several distinct attacks on KeeLoq, each of them is interesting for different reasons.
- First we show that when about 232 known plaintexts are available, KeeLoq is very weak and for example for 30 % of all keys the full key can be recovered with complexity of 228 KeeLoq encryptions.
- Then we turn our attention to algebraic attacks with the major challenge of breaking KeeLoq given potentially a very small number of known plaintexts.
- Our best “direct” algebraic attack can break up to 160 rounds of KeeLoq.
