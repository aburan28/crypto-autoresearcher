## Contribution
We generalize and extend results obtained by Boneh and Venkatesan in 1996 and by González Vasco and Shparlinski in 2000 on the hardness of computing bits of the Diffie-Hellman key, given the public values. Specifically, while these results could only exclude (essentially) error-free predictions, we here exclude any non-negligible advantage, though for larger fractions of the bits.

## Key claims (as reported)
- We can also demonstrate a trade-off between the tolerated error rate and the number of unpredictable bits.
- Moreover, by changing computational model, we show that even a very small proportion of the most significant bits of the Diffie–Hellman secret key cannot be retrieved from the public information by means of a Las Vegas type algorithm, unless the corresponding scheme is weak itself.
