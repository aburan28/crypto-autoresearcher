## Contribution
We address the question of whether or not semantically secure public-key encryption primitives imply the existence of chosen ciphertext attack (CCA) secure primitives. We show a black-box separation, following the methodology introduced by Impagliazzo and Rudich [23], for a large non-trivial class of constructions.

## Key claims (as reported)
- In particular, we show that if the proposed CCA construction’s decryption algorithm does not query the semantically secure primitive’s encryption algorithm, then the proposed construction cannot be CCA secure.
