## Contribution
Content moderation is crucial for stopping abusive and harassing messages in online platforms. Existing moderation mechanisms, such as message franking, require platform providers to be able to associate user identifiers to encrypted messages.

## Key claims (as reported)
- These mechanisms fail in metadata-private messaging systems, such as Signal, where users can hide their identities from platform providers.
- The key technical challenge preventing moderation is achieving cryptographic accountability while preserving deniability.
- In this work, we resolve this tension with a new cryptographic primitive: asymmetric message franking (AMF) schemes.
- We define strong security notions for AMF schemes, including the first formal treatment of deniability in moderation settings.
