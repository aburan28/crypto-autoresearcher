## Contribution
Iterated attacks are comprised of iterating adversaries who can make d plaintext queries, in each iteration to compute a bit, and are trying to distinguish between a random cipher C and the ideal random cipher C ∗ based on all bits. In EUROCRYPT ’99, Vaudenay showed that a 2d-decorrelated cipher resists to iterated attacks of order d when iterations make almost no common queries.

## Key claims (as reported)
- Then, he first asked what the necessary conditions are for a cipher to resist a non-adaptive iterated attack of order d.
- Secondly, he speculated that repeating a plaintext query in different iterations does not provide any advantage to a non-adaptive distinguisher.
- We close here these two long-standing open problems.
- We show that, in order to resist non-adaptive iterated attacks of order d, decorrelation of order 2d − 1 is not sufficient.
