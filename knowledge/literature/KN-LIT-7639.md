## Contribution (as reported)

On **2026-08-01** OpenAI published ten results in mathematics and theoretical
computer science stated to have been produced by an **internal version of
"Astra"**, described as the company's next major model family. The claim is that
the core mathematical arguments were generated by the model; humans wrote the
arguments up into manuscripts, and the model then **formalized each proof in
Lean 4** against `mathlib`, so that each result ships with a machine-checkable
certificate rather than only a human-readable proof.

The framing in the coverage is that each problem had seen no progress on its
core question for **at least a decade**, and considerably longer in most cases.

## The ten results (as reported)

Relayed from search summaries; **no theorem statement below has been read in its
original form, and none has been checked by this program.** The grouping into
exactly ten is the coverage's, and is not fully determinate — items 1 and 2 may
be one paper, and items 9–10 together account for the "three Erdős problems"
the coverage cites.

| # | Area | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | High-dimensional geometry | New **upper bounds on sphere-packing density**, approaching the Cohn–Elkies threshold |
| 2 | Coding theory | **Exponentially improved** bounds on binary and spherical codes |
| 3 | Group theory | Construction establishing the existence of a **non-sofic group** — reported as the first explicit such construction |
| 4 | Operator algebras | **Disproof of Connes's rigidity conjecture** |
| 5 | Circuit complexity | New **lower bounds on arithmetic circuit complexity** for computing the permanent |
| 6 | Quantum complexity | **Parallel repetition** extended to general two-player entangled/quantum games |
| 7 | Lattices / crypto | **Polynomial-factor hardness of approximation for the Closest Vector Problem (CVP)** |
| 8 | Discrete geometry | Resolution of **Ehrhart's volume conjecture** in every dimension |
| 9 | Ramsey theory | **Superexponential lower bound** on multicolor triangle Ramsey numbers — closes **Erdős problem 183** |
| 10 | Extremal graph theory | **Compactness and degeneracy conjectures** — resolves **Erdős problems 146 and 180** |

Third-party coverage additionally described item 1 as the first improvement to
the *general* sphere-packing exponent **since 1978**. That framing comes from a
social-media summary, not from the announcement text as relayed. *(The 1978
reference is presumably the Kabatiansky–Levenshtein bound — that identification
is this program's own inference and appears in no source consulted.)*

## Method, cost, and credit (as reported)

- **Verification.** Every result is formalized in **Lean 4** and published in a
  repository reported as `openai/ten-proofs`. The stated property is that anyone
  can recompile against `mathlib` and confirm validity **without trusting the
  model or its operators**. Chain-of-thought walkthroughs are reported to
  accompany the proofs.
- **Cost.** The tokens spent discovering all ten results are stated to be worth
  **≈ $2,000** at GPT-5.6 "Sol" API rates. Note this is a *discovery-token*
  figure, not a program cost.
- **Credit.** OpenAI is reported to state that claiming human authorship for an
  AI-generated proof would misrepresent both the system's contribution and the
  nature of human intellectual work; it frames the human role as manuscript
  preparation and Lean formalization, takes responsibility for correctness, and
  credits the mathematical arguments to Astra. Coverage reports the announcement
  nods to the **Leiden declaration on AI and Mathematics** and asks the
  mathematical community to *engage with* rather than simply accept the results.
- **Architecture (second-hand, weaker).** A tweet citing a report in *The
  Information* describes Astra as a **multi-agent system** trained for
  **long-horizon tasks**. This is two sources removed from anything primary.
- **Naming (speculation).** Some coverage glosses Astra as "GPT-6". Nothing in
  the relayed announcement text supports that; treat it as third-party guesswork.

## Stated limitations (as reported)

The follow-up post from the same account

coverage as saying, in substance: *they did try other major problems without
success; no Millennium Prize problems (yet); they did not spend much per problem,
and test-time compute could be pushed much further.*

Coverage also records the standard caveat directly: **a Lean-checked argument is
not a peer-reviewed one.** The human community still has to agree that the
formalized statement is the statement it cares about. Formalization closes the
"can we check it" gap, not the "is it the right theorem" gap.

## Relevance to this program

**Nothing here bears on the ECDLP.** No result touches elliptic curves, discrete
logarithms, isogenies, or index calculus. The entry is recorded as
**methodological literature plus one adjacent hardness datum**, not as a result
this program can build on.

1. **The CVP result is a hardness result, not an attack.** Polynomial-factor
   hardness of approximation for CVP *strengthens* confidence in a lattice
   assumption; it does not weaken any primitive and gives this program no attack
   surface. Its only contact with the corpus is the leakage-model lattice/HNP
   lane ([[KN-OPEN-011]], [[KN-OPEN-018]]), and even there it is background, not
   input. Recorded so a future ideation pass does not mistake "lattice
   cryptography result" in a headline for an opening.

2. **This is the certificate-first design of `docs/claims-and-verification.md`,
   executed by someone else.** [[KN-LIT-7594]] recorded the sharpest
   counter-datum available to this program: one week to *discover* the AES
   attack, nearly a month for two researchers to *believe* it — verification, not
   discovery, is the scarce resource, and the results that were cheap to trust
   were the ones that **run end-to-end**. This announcement's answer is to attach
   a machine-checkable certificate to every claim, which is this program's
   solution-certificate rule generalized from *solve/relation* certificates to
   *theorem* certificates. It is external support for the program's existing
   preference for cheap-to-falsify, certificate-carrying claims.

   The caveat travels with it and matters more here than in the coverage: **a
   certificate binds a claim to a formal statement, not to the informal claim a
   reader will infer.** That gap is exactly what this program's claim-tier and
   scope-of-claim discipline exists to police (`AGENTS.md` rule 4), and it would
   reappear immediately in any attempt to formalize a cryptanalytic claim here.

3. **The negative base rate was volunteered, and that is the honest form.**
   "We tried other major problems without success" and "no Millennium Prize
   problems (yet)" state a denominator that ten headline successes otherwise
   hide. That is the reporting posture `docs/inventor-protocol.md` and
   [[KN-TECH-056]] demand (`dominated_by` / `sota_delta` honesty), arriving
   unprompted from the claiming party. Symmetrically, the announcement is a
   further data point against **premature closure** — the failure mode
   [[KN-LIT-7594]] and [[KN-OPEN-019]] flag as this program's real exposure:
   ten targets that had been static for a decade or more were not, in fact,
   saturated.

4. **The cost figure is not comparable to [[KN-LIT-7594]]'s and must not be
   quoted against it.** ≈$2,000 total across ten results here versus ≈$100k per
   result there is a two-order-of-magnitude gap that is almost certainly an
   accounting artifact — different domain, different model and price sheet, and
   a *discovery-token* figure here against a *program-cost* figure there. Useful
   only as a reminder that per-result cost priors in this corpus are soft.

5. **Shape matches `docs/target-result-profile.md`; provenance does not.** A
   sphere-packing exponent improvement and a polynomial-factor CVP hardness gap
   are exponent-moving results on central problems — the shape the profile asks
   for. But unlike the profile's canonical Wesolowski exemplar
   (`inputs/P13-WESOLOWSKI-2026/`), **none of these results is refereed as of
   this entry**, and this program has read none of them. Use as shape exemplars
   only; do not cite as established mathematics.

**A precisely statable question this raises** (not promoted to a `KN-OPEN` entry
without a Coordinator decision): the results certified here are *theorem-shaped*,
which is what Lean formalizes well. This program's claims are largely
*algorithmic and empirical* — solver runs, relation counts, scoped cost
measurements — for which the certificate analogue is a re-executed run receipt,
not a proof term. Whether the certificate-first posture demonstrated here
transfers to cryptanalytic claims at all, or stops at the theorem/experiment
boundary, is open and would sharpen `docs/claims-and-verification.md` if
answered.
