## The technique, and what it does not give

Face **F2** of the lifting taxonomy (`KN-TECH-06bb4e`) — the canonical lift of
`S, T ∈ E(F_p)` to points of the same prime-to-`p` order `n` in `E(Q_p)` — is
closed for **every group-theoretic invariant**, and the closure is an
isomorphism statement, not a "no method is known" statement.

`KN-LIT-6935a1` states the obstruction as: the relation `T̂ = mŜ` survives the
unique torsion lift, but the only known route to a computable logarithm
multiplies by `n` and degenerates to `Ô = mÔ`, and "no one knows an efficient way
to solve the ECDLP in `E(Q_p)` without moving into the formal group". The
sharpening below says there is nothing to know at that level.

## Derivation

Let `E/Q_p` have good reduction, `p >= 3`, and let `n` be the order of
`S ∈ E(F_p)` with `p ∤ n`. The reduction map

```
red : E(Q_p) --> E(F_p)
```

is surjective with kernel the formal group `Ê(pZ_p)`, which is a pro-`p` group
and hence torsion-free away from `p`. Therefore `red` restricted to the
prime-to-`p` torsion is injective, and combined with surjectivity it gives a
group isomorphism onto the prime-to-`p` torsion of `E(F_p)`:

```
E(Q_p)[n]  ≅  E(F_p)[n].
```

This is exactly the uniqueness statement that makes the canonical torsion lift
well defined. Restricting to the cyclic subgroup generated by `S`,

```
red : <Ŝ>  -->  <S>
```

is an isomorphism of cyclic groups of order `n` carrying `T̂` to `T`. Hence the
lifted problem "find `m` with `T̂ = mŜ` in `<Ŝ>`" is the *same* ECDLP instance
under an isomorphism, not a related one. Consequences:

- Any invariant that is a function of the abstract group structure alone takes
  identical values on both sides, so no such invariant can distinguish them.
- The lift is information-theoretically empty: `Ŝ` is a deterministic function of
  `S`, computable to any precision from `E(F_p)` data alone by Hensel lifting plus
  the order condition. Nothing is learned that was not already available.
- Therefore F2 cannot be escaped by a better logarithm, a better precision
  schedule, or a different `p`-adic group-theoretic construction. It can only be
  escaped by an invariant that is **not** group-theoretic — one reading the
  `p`-adic coordinates or valuations of `[k]Ŝ` — and such an invariant would offer
  at most a *computational* handle, never an information gain.

## What remains open

Exactly the coordinate/valuation question, filed as `KN-OPEN-3417fc` and made
falsifiable as `ECDLP-IDEA-436`.

## Falsification

Exhibit a group-theoretic invariant on `<Ŝ> ⊂ E(Q_p)` that is not constant on the
isomorphism class of `(Z/nZ, S ↦ 1)`. Under the derivation above no such
invariant exists, so a counterexample would indicate an error in the good-reduction
or torsion-freeness step rather than a new algorithm.
