## Mechanism

Suppose the standardizer knows a class `W` of weak curves that the public
does not, of density `2^-k` among curves over the target field. A
"verifiably random" procedure derives curve coefficients from `Hash(seed)`.
The standardizer tries seeds until `Hash(seed)` yields a curve in `W`
(expected `2^k` trials, each a hash plus point counting to check the order),
then publishes that seed as the nothing-up-my-sleeve proof. Bernstein, Chou,
Chuengsatiansup, Huelsing, Lambooij, Lange, Niederhagen and van Vredendaal
(KN-LIT-4290) demonstrate this end to end for several standard-style
procedures and show that "rigid" procedures with unexplained constants leave
the same freedom by other routes.

## What it is and is not

It is a **meta-trapdoor**: it cannot create a weakness, it can only plant a
curve from a class that is *already* weak to the standardizer. The entries it
would deliver are the hidden-weakness constructions (KN-TECH-8ef4c3 over
extension fields, a prime-field analogue if KN-OPEN-cc1988 is ever answered
positively) or the unchecked weaknesses a standardizer expects implementations
to miss (KN-TECH-90e273).

## Detectability

The published seed is consistent with the published curve by construction, so
the seed itself proves nothing either way. The only public defence is to
remove the freedom: fully specified generation with no free seed, a minimal
`b` search order, and the smallest acceptable parameters, which is the
"rigidity" criterion of SafeCurves. Koblitz and Menezes (eprint 2015/1018,
"A riddle wrapped in an enigma"; not yet a KN-LIT entry) analyse the specific
case of the NIST P-curves whose seeds are unexplained, and conclude that a
secret weak class of usable density would have to exist for the concern to
be real; none is publicly known.

## Applicability limits

- Density: a class of density `2^-k` needs `2^k` point-counting runs; the
  paper treats `k` up to roughly 25 as plainly affordable and larger `k` as
  a matter of budget, not principle.
- The weak class must be *secret*; a public class is excluded by the
  standard's own checks.

## Relevance

This entry is the reason KN-OPEN-cc1988 matters beyond theory: a positive
answer there (a rare, non-invariant prime-field weakness of density
`2^-k` with `k` in the affordable range) would immediately compose with this
mechanism into a deployable trapdoor on "verifiably random" prime-field
standards.
