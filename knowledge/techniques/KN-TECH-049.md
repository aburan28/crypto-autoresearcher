## Method
Position every lattice feasibility claim against the public challenge records,
the way KN-TECH-036 positions ECDLP claims against published record
computations. Three distinct challenge families exist and must not be
conflated:

- **The Lattice Challenge** (KN-LIT-120): Ajtai-style bases with a
  worst-case-hardness pedigree, dimension `m >= 500`, norm-bound reduction
  format.
- **The SVP Challenge**: random lattices in the sense of Goldstein-Mayer. This
  is the family whose dimensions the sieving literature quotes; KN-LIT-106
  reports solving previously unsolved dimensions 151, 153 and 155, with SVP-151
  found 400 times faster than the prior SVP-150 record.
- **The LWE Challenge** (KN-LIT-121): LWE instances generated by multi-party
  computation so that no party knows the secret, indexed by dimension `n` and
  relative error `alpha = sigma/q`; KN-LIT-106 reports solving instances
  including `(75, 0.005)`.

A "dimension `d` record" is meaningless without saying which family it belongs
to.

## Why records beat exponents for sanity-checking
An exponent says what happens asymptotically; a record says what has actually
been computed by people trying hard, on real hardware, with a publicly
checkable answer. The gap between the two is where over-claiming lives. Two
properties make these challenges especially good instruments:

- **The organisers need not know the answers.** The LWE Challenge's MPC
  construction guarantees this by design, and its `check_challenge` protocol
  lets anyone audit generation. Nothing rests on the organisers' word -- the same
  property the program requires of its own certificates.
- **They are adversarially attacked continuously.** A dimension that remains
  unsolved has been attacked by the people who wrote the best implementations.

Practical rule: an internal claim implying feasibility far above the current
records needs an explicit argument for the gap, and a claim that merely restates
a record is not a program result.

## Applicability limits
Records are hardware-, effort- and calendar-dependent, so they bound
*demonstrated* capability, not capability. They are also not directly
convertible into core-SVP bit-security numbers (KN-TECH-040) -- the challenge
lattices are not the lattices arising from deployed schemes, and the LWE
Challenge's parameters, while chosen to resemble real instances, are not them.
Use records to bound plausibility claims, not to derive security levels.

## Verified vs reported
The challenge constructions and protocols are read from KN-LIT-120 and
KN-LIT-121; the solved instances and speedup are KN-LIT-106's reported results,
not independently verified against the halls of fame. The SVP Challenge hall of
fame showed a dimension-210 entry (norm 3808, seed 0, Ding and Zhao) when
latticechallenge.org was retrieved on 2026-07-24; that is a website reading
subject to caching, is not frozen here, and should be re-checked before use.
