## What this record is

Two visibly attractive routes from the post-SIDH higher-dimensional isogeny
literature into prime-field ECDLP both collapse for **structural reasons that
can be checked by counting**, before any experiment. Recording them so the
program does not spend cycles on either.

Scope: this is a boundary statement about two specific mechanisms. It is not a
claim that isogeny methods are useless for ECDLP, and not a claim about any
other member of that literature.

## Route 1 — Smith's tractable subgroups do not specialise to genus 1

`KN-LIT-39d9ed` moves a genus-3 DLP from a hyperelliptic Jacobian `J_H`
(index calculus `Õ(q^{4/3})`) to a non-hyperelliptic one `J_C` (`Õ(q)`) by an
explicit `(2,2,2)`-isogeny whose kernel is the **tractable subgroup**

```
S = <[W_1 - W_2], [W_3 - W_4], [W_5 - W_6], [W_7 - W_8]>  ≅  (Z/2Z)^3
```

generated by differences of the eight Weierstrass points of `H`. The appeal of
the mechanism is that the kernel is read off the *branch locus* and the isogeny
is then computed by a construction on `P^1` (Recillas' trigonal construction,
Donagi–Livné), rather than by Vélu/Kohel on the curve.

At genus 1 the construction degenerates. An elliptic curve `E : y^2 = f(x)` with
`deg f = 3` has exactly **three** finite Weierstrass points plus the point at
infinity, and those Weierstrass points *are* the nontrivial elements of `E[2]`.
The subgroup generated by their differences is therefore `E[2]` itself, not a
proper subgroup of it, and every subgroup available to the mechanism is a
subgroup of `E[2]`. Quotienting by any of them is an ordinary 2-isogeny or the
multiplication-by-2 map. Consequently:

- the quotient is again an elliptic curve, so there is no change of moduli
  stratum and no change of plane-model degree;
- by `KN-LIT-a45b7b` the index-calculus exponent is driven by plane-model
  degree, which is pinned at 3 on both sides;
- the exponent-moving step that makes the genus-3 attack work has no genus-1
  counterpart.

The genus-3 gain requires *two distinct geometries at the same genus*
(hyperelliptic and non-hyperelliptic), and genus 1 has only one.

## Route 2 — the Kani/SIDH torsion budget is unaffordable for `[m]`

The SIDH break recovers an unknown isogeny `φ` of **known degree** `d` by
embedding it in a higher-dimensional isogeny (Kani), which requires the action
of `φ` on a full basis of `E[N]` with `N > d`.

Casting ECDLP in that shape fails on two independent counts:

1. **Wrong object.** The ECDLP datum is a single point image `Q = [m]P`, i.e. the
   image of *one* point, not the image of a basis of `E[N]`. Kani's embedding
   needs the full torsion action.
2. **Budget.** The map whose recovery would yield the scalar is the endomorphism
   `[m]`, of degree `m^2`. With `m` of size `≈ p`, the requirement `N > deg` reads
   `N > m^2 ≈ p^2`. Torsion of that modulus is not available in `E(F_p)` (whose
   order is `p + 1 - t`), and constructing or evaluating the action on `E[N]` for
   `N > p^2` is not a step this program can charge as cheap. Circularly, the
   advice that would be needed already encodes `m`.

The SIDH break is a statement about *published auxiliary torsion for a
low-degree secret isogeny*. ECDLP publishes no torsion advice, and its implicit
map has degree quadratic in the secret. Both preconditions fail.

## Falsification

Either route is reopened by exhibiting, respectively: (a) a kernel available on a
genus-1 curve that is not contained in `E[2]` and whose quotient has plane-model
degree below 3; or (b) an ECDLP-legal source of `E[N]` action with `N > m^2` whose
construction cost is charged and sub-`rho`. Neither is claimed impossible.

## Scope labels

`DERIVATION`, prime-field, no experiment run, no toy data. Scoped negative for the
two named mechanisms only. Not a no-go for isogeny methods in general, and
explicitly not a lower bound.
