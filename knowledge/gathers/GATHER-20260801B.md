## The audit was wrong twice, in opposite directions

Worth recording ahead of the findings, because it changed what got written.

**False gap.** The keyword audit reported `lattice cost model` at **0** entries,
`lattice estimator` at 1, `LWE estimator` at 2, `Kannan embedding` at 0 — suggesting the
corpus had lattice *problems* but nothing on how their cost is estimated. That was an
artifact of vocabulary. The corpus in fact holds **`KN-TECH-038`** (primal attack),
**`KN-TECH-039`** (dual attack and the dual-sieve dispute), **`KN-TECH-040`** (core-SVP
costing and the cost-model zoo), **`KN-TECH-041`** (basis profiles, GSA, BKZ
simulation), **`KN-TECH-042`–**`044`** (enumeration, sieving, charging memory), and
**`KN-TECH-049`** (calibration against public challenge records) — thorough coverage
under different words.

A draft `KN-TECH-082` on "concrete lattice attack cost models" was **written and then
discarded** as a duplicate of `KN-TECH-039`/`040`. Four literature entries that had been
drafted claiming to "fill the corpus's largest measured lattice gap" were corrected
before commit to cite the existing technique entries instead. **A keyword miss is not a
coverage gap**; the check that matters is reading the technique-entry titles, which is
cheap and was done second rather than first.

**Real gap.** The same read surfaced a genuine one: **zero occurrences of "hybrid"
across all six lattice technique entries** (`KN-TECH-021`, `022`, `023`, `038`, `039`,
`040`), even though the hybrid family is where three of 2026's more consequential
concrete results landed. That is what `KN-TECH-082` became.

Elliptic-curve gaps that were real and are partly patched: `trace zero variety` 0,
`biased nonce` 1, `NIST P-256` 1, `twist security` 1, `smart attack` 1, `invalid curve`
2, `MOV attack` 3, `anomalous curve` 4, `CM method` 6, `SEA algorithm` 8.

## A third dedup failure mode, and a census

`CORR-20260801-003` records that identifier dedup fails in both directions. This sweep
found a **third** mode and then measured the underlying cause.

`iacr:2026/1465` (*On Reduction Probability Models in Lattice Sieving*) came back as
NEW from a `grep "iacr:2026/1465"` — but is held as **KN-LIT-081** with
`eprint: 2026/1465`, **without the `iacr:` prefix**. A prefix-anchored grep cannot see
it. Caught by title grep, and excluded from ingestion.

The census that prompted, over all 7649 literature entries at the time:

| `eprint:` field | Count |
|---|---|
| `null` | **7007** |
| `iacr:NNNN/NNN` | 635 |
| bare `NNNN/NNN` | 7 |

**92% of the literature corpus carries no ePrint identifier at all.** Identifier dedup
is therefore not merely imperfect for this corpus — it is structurally unable to work on
the overwhelming majority of it, and **title-based checking is the primary mechanism,
not the backstop**. Every candidate in this sweep was checked both ways: a
format-agnostic identifier regex `eprint: ["']?(iacr:)?2026/NNN["']?`, and a grep for a
distinctive title phrase.

This is a sharper statement of `CORR-20260801-003`'s systemic finding and is **flagged
for the Coordinator** as a corpus-schema issue, not repaired here.

## What the two sweeps together changed

The first sweep opened [[KN-OPEN-024]] on the gap between [[KN-LIT-7641]]
(PIP in `M_g(O)`, `g ≥ 2`, heuristic polynomial time) and [[KN-LIT-7647]] (SoliloQuat
assuming SG-PIP hard at `g = 1`), and named **verifying the Eurocrypt 2025 module-LIP →
nrd-PIP reduction** as the cheapest first move because it would decide whether
quaternion-order PIP sits upstream of HAWK.

This sweep found that reduction **already being used as an attack** ([[KN-LIT-7670]]),
by an overlapping author set — Nelson and Mendelsohn wrote SoliloQuat; Cong Ling wrote
the DEFI LIP break ([[KN-LIT-7648]]). The same small group is proposing schemes on this
assumption and attacking it. Together with [[KN-LIT-7671]]'s AM ∩ coAM ceiling, **LIP
now has two concrete attack routes and a complexity upper bound**, which is a materially
different risk profile from "believed hard."

That convergence was not visible from either sweep alone and is the main reason the
second one was worth running.

## Provenance

All 18 entries are `confidence: reported`, `citation_verified: web`. **No full texts were
read.** Every claim is relayed from an ePrint abstract page retrieved 2026-08-01, and
every entry's "Not verified here" section says so and enumerates what was not checked.
Citations were confirmed against the ePrint `citation_title`/`citation_author` metadata
for all 15 literature entries.

**Six of the retrieved abstracts are truncated** in the ePrint records
(`2026/607`, `2026/279`, `2026/1048`, `2026/1060`, `2026/096`, `2026/1318`). In each case
the affected entry states what is missing rather than completing it by inference — most
consequentially [[KN-LIT-7662]], which records **no direction of effect on Dilithium**,
and [[KN-LIT-7670]], which leaves the Heuristic-4 concession incomplete.

Every literature entry states explicitly whether it bears on the ECDLP. **None does.**
Three are elliptic-curve structural or model results; twelve are lattice cryptanalysis
and concrete-security entries held because the corpus tracks lattices as an adjacent
field.

**No standardised parameter set is claimed compromised by any entry in this sweep** —
not ML-KEM, Kyber, Dilithium, Falcon, or HAWK. The largest figures recorded (13–15 bits)
apply to FHE sparse-secret parameter sets, are model-relative, and are parameter-selection
inputs rather than breaks.

No experiment was run. No evidence or decision record is created or modified. No
hypothesis status changes. `KN-OPEN-026` and `KN-OPEN-027` are questions, not claims.
