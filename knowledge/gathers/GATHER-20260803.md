# GATHER-20260803 — Classic McEliece bibliography, complete ingest

Full ingest of the Classic McEliece project's own "Papers" bibliography on user
request: *"Please aggregate all these papers into my knowledge base in this
repo"*, supplied as a markdown table.

**162 entries screened, 25 already held, 137 written.** All 137 are `KN-LIT`;
no `KN-TECH`, `KN-FIND` or `KN-OPEN` records were created — this sweep imports
prior art, and nothing in it was read closely enough to justify a technique
entry.

The user supplied the bibliography as a table rather than a URL. The page was
retrieved anyway and the table checked against it, because a transcribed
bibliography is an assertion until the bytes are seen — the same distinction
`knowledge/README.md` draws between a hash recomputed and a hash relayed.

**That check found a discrepancy, and it went the safe direction.** The supplied
table's summary line reads *"ISD 52 · Other attacks 33 · Chosen-ciphertext 8 ·
Side-channel/fault 18 · Further 40 · Total 151"*. The page holds **60 / 33 / 8 /
18 / 43 = 162**. The supplied *table* is in fact complete at 162 rows; only its
`Counts:` header was wrong, for two sections. No paper was missing from the
user's list. Had the sweep trusted the header and stopped at 151, eleven papers
would have been silently dropped.

The source page also carries metadata the supplied table had dropped: publisher
DOIs for four papers, a second published version for two, the TCHES link for
*Leaky McEliece*, and "original title" annotations for five. The page was used
as ground truth for all frontmatter.

  were compared against the bibliography for each.
- **Crossref: 99 of 137 matched at ≥0.90 title similarity.** Matches below that
  threshold were read by hand and **rejected**, not accepted at a lower bar —
  38 rejections, several of which were plausible-looking near misses (Crossref

McEliece. Matching ran in two passes and **the second pass was necessary**:

## Identifiers: random, because sequential collides

`KN-LIT` IDs in this corpus are mostly legacy 4-digit sequentials (max 7675
after merging `origin/main`). This sweep minted **137 random 6-hex suffixes**,
per CLAUDE.md.

The rule was tested rather than assumed. `KN-LIT-7670` — the next sequential ID
— **is already taken**, on branch
`f38924e3:coordination/goals/GOAL-HAWK-001/batches/BATCH-001/BATCH-001-OPENING.md`.
A max+1 allocation would have collided on its first identifier and on 137
consecutive ones after it, against records that are immutable by the time the
collision surfaces at merge.

All 6-hex `KN-LIT` identifiers across every remote branch were enumerated first
(5 in use: `1c9474`, `4c1133`, `7c2620`, `95256d`, `b9e1a8`) and excluded.
Allocation was seeded at 20260803 for reproducibility.

## What this sweep deliberately did not do

- **Did not read a single paper.** Every entry is `confidence: reported` and
  every one carries a "Not verified here" section stating that the full text was
  not read and that no complexity figure, benchmark or security estimate in it
  has been reproduced. Where an entry's description of an algorithm comes from
  general knowledge rather than the cited source — Prange, Stern, Leon,
  Lee–Brickell, Niederreiter, McEliece 1978 — it says **"recalled, not read from
  this source"** in those words.
- **Did not characterise [[KN-LIT-7c4620]]'s impact.** Briaud–Lemoine–
  Randriambololona–Tillich, *A heuristic subexponential attack on the McEliece
  cryptosystem* (2026), is the most consequential item in the bibliography and
  the closest published analogue to this program's own target profile. The entry
  relays its title-level claim and explicitly records that **nothing about its
  validity, scope, exponent, heuristic assumptions, or applicability to Classic
  McEliece parameters is verified here.** It is flagged as a high-priority read,
  not as a fact.
- **Did not supersede the 25 held entries**, several of which are thin
  bulk-seeded stubs with null identifiers and mis-assigned relevance text
  (e.g. [[KN-LIT-4875]] carries `tags: [cryptanalysis, pairing, pqc, quantum]`
  and a relevance paragraph about pairing-based reductions, for a paper about
  ISD). This sweep verified ePrint identifiers for several of them. Correcting
  them means writing superseding entries, which is a separate, larger job —
  recorded here as follow-up, not done silently.
- **Did not promote anything to `KN-TECH`.** Several entries are strong
  candidates on their methodological content — [[KN-LIT-7965a1]] (LLL for binary
  codes), [[KN-LIT-71d1a0]] (syzygy distinguisher), [[KN-LIT-fab214]] (punctured
  syndrome decoding as a named problem) — but a technique entry states
  applicability conditions and known limits, and this sweep read none of them.

## What the corpus gained, honestly stated

Almost nothing here bears on the ECDLP, and the entries say so individually —
the phrase *"Does not bear on the ECDLP"* appears in most of them. What the
sweep adds is **calibration and method**, in four specific forms:

1. **A sixty-four-year exponent record.** Prange 1962 ([[KN-LIT-6a786b]]) to the
   2026 sieving-style algorithms, with every intermediate step held. The ISD
   exponent moved modestly across the whole of it — and Canto Torres–Sendrier
   ([[KN-LIT-fa9bc8]]) reports that in the sub-linear-weight regime McEliece
   actually uses, the advanced variants asymptotically collapse back toward
   Prange. This is the reference class `docs/target-result-profile.md` needs when
   judging whether a proposal here is exponent-moving or constant-factor.
2. **Worked cross-domain transfers.** [[KN-LIT-7965a1]] builds LLL for binary
   codes; [[KN-LIT-71d1a0]] computes syzygies against codes; [[KN-LIT-182bfb]]
   carries lattice sieving to codes. All three are exactly the object-first move
   `docs/inventor-protocol.md` prescribes — **and none of them broke anything.**
   That is the realistic prior for a technique-transfer proposal, and it is
   recorded as such rather than as encouragement.
3. **A repeated, decisive lesson about structure.** Every McEliece variant that
   added algebraic structure for efficiency was broken — GRS, quasi-cyclic,
   quasi-dyadic, BCH, wild-over-quadratic-extensions — while the conservative
   1978 original stands. This is the null-object control argument with
   forty-eight years of evidence behind it.
4. **The premature-closure counterexample.** Statistical decoding was judged
   uncompetitive for two decades ([[KN-LIT-fb3102]] → [[KN-LIT-70266b]] →
   [[KN-LIT-288b99]]) and then, reformulated as a dual attack on LPN, became one
   of two serious paradigms ([[KN-LIT-6796]], [[KN-LIT-38b647]]). A well-founded
   "not competitive" verdict about a *formulation* was routinely read as a
   verdict about the *idea*. `docs/inventor-protocol.md` treats premature
   closure as symmetric with overclaiming; this is the citable instance.

## Method

```text
retrieve papers.html            → 162 entries parsed from 5 <h3> sections
build corpus index              → 7,814 files, 719 eprint tokens, 7,802 titles
dedup pass 1 (id + title ≥0.80) → 24 held, 5 false positives
dedup pass 2 (title fragments)  → 1 more held (OCR-mangled), 4 false positives
verify: IACR ePrint × 75        → 75/75 resolved, 3 title drifts found
verify: Crossref × 137          → 99 accepted ≥0.90, 38 rejected by hand
verify: arXiv × 4               → 4/4 resolved
verify: DBLP                    → UNREACHABLE, no entry claims it
enumerate hex IDs across refs   → 5 in use, excluded
mint 137 random 6-hex ids       → seed 20260803
emit 137 KN-LIT entries         → frontmatter machine-built, prose hand-written
```

Frontmatter was generated from verified metadata so that no identifier was typed
by hand; every "Contribution", "Key claims", "Relevance" and "Not verified here"
section was written per paper.
