# Extension-field summation-polynomial techniques transferable to F_p, and the 2019–2026 literature

Scope note. Sources were read in full text wherever a copy could be obtained (Vitse's article directory, arXiv, Birmingham/FLVC/ICCS/Zenodo/Kyushu repositories, Petit's and Galbraith's pages). IACR ePrint (Cloudflare), HAL (Anubis), Springer, De Gruyter, ScienceDirect and the Semantic Scholar API (HTTP 429 on every retry) were unreachable from this session; OpenAlex, Crossref and the arXiv API were used for the bibliographic sweep instead. Claims are tagged PROVEN / HEURISTIC / EXPERIMENTAL as in the source. "Over F_p" below always means a prime field with no Weil descent available.

Standing facts used throughout (all PROVEN in the sources):
- Semaev's m-th summation polynomial S_m is symmetric for m ≥ 3 and has degree 2^{m−2} in each variable — [Joux–Vitse, Prop. 2](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/DLFq5.pdf); [GGMP 2020, Thm 2.1](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/files/104974339/On_Index_Calculus_Algorithms_for_Subfield_Curves.pdf).
- Gaudry–Diem system over F_{q^n}: S_{n+1}(x_1..x_n, x_R)=0 with x_i ∈ F_q becomes, by Weil restriction, n equations in n variables of total degree 2^{n−1}; written in e_1..e_n the ideal has generic degree 2^{n(n−1)} (Bézout) — [Joux–Vitse §2.1](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/DLFq5.pdf).

## KQ1. Faugère–Gaudry–Huot–Renault, "Using symmetries in the index calculus for elliptic curves discrete logarithm" (J. Cryptology 27(4):595–635, 2014; ePrint 2012/199)

### Takeaway
FGHR exploit the rational 2-torsion point of twisted Edwards / twisted Jacobi-intersection curves: translation by T is an affine sign change on the chosen coordinate, so the PDP system is invariant under the dihedral Coxeter group D_n = (Z/2Z)^{n−1} ⋊ S_n, the ideal degree drops by |D_n| = 2^{n−1} n!, and together with a new change-of-ordering algorithm this yields a claimed factor 2^{ω(n−1)} on the PDP. Everything except the Weil-restricted system itself is field-independent, but over F_p there is no system to which the symmetry can be applied, and the curve must have even order (and a 4-torsion point with rational x for the affine normal form).

### Cited Findings
- Abstract (journal metadata): the paper applies Semaev/Gaudry's decomposition to Edwards curves and "exploit[s] symmetries of twisted Edwards and twisted Jacobi intersections curves to gain an exponential factor 2^{ω(n−1)}" on the PDP, where ω is the matrix-multiplication exponent; for q ≈ 2^64 the authors estimate 2^160 operations for generic algorithms versus 2^130 32-bit word multiplications for their method, and say the original (Gaudry–Diem) algorithm cannot solve the PDP for those parameters; "the main tool ... relies on the use of the symmetries and the quasi-homogeneous structure induced by these symmetries during the polynomial system solving step", plus "a new algorithm for the change of ordering of a Gröbner basis" (HEURISTIC complexity) — [IP-Paris research portal record](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/using-symmetries-in-the-index-calculus-for-elliptic-curves-discre/); also listed as [ePrint 2012/199](https://eprint.iacr.org/2012/199) and HAL hal-00700555.
- Full text could not be retrieved (ePrint behind Cloudflare, HAL behind Anubis, Springer behind a client challenge); the following structural facts are taken from papers that restate FGHR:
  - FHJRV 2014 §1: "Faugère et al. [4] have proposed to speed up the relation search using a 2-torsion point T naturally present on elliptic curves in the Edwards or Jacobi models ... in these models, the translation by T corresponds to a simple symmetry of the curve. This implies that the corresponding multivariate polynomial systems also admit an additional symmetry" — [FHJRV](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
  - FHJRV §4.1: the group G_2 = (Z/2Z)^{n−1} ⋊ S_n is "(called dihedral Coxeter group in [4])"; Prop. 7 (invariance of P_{φ,n} under G_2 when f_T is affine): "A special case of this proposition has already been proved in [4]"; Prop. 8: "The first part of Prop. 8 generalizes the results given in [4], where the morphism φ is obtained as a projection onto a coordinate for curves in twisted Edwards form" (PROVEN) — [FHJRV](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
  - Gebregiyorgis thesis (Auckland 2016) §2: "the dihedral coxeter group D_n having order 2^{n−1} n! given by the semi-direct product ... invariant ring of D_n (as shown in [FGHR13]) is given by (p_2, ..., p_{2(n−1)}, e_n) or (s_1, s_2, ..., s_{n−1}, e_n)" (power sums / elementary symmetric functions of the squares, plus the product e_n); "D_n is a pseudo-reflection group" so the invariant ring is a polynomial ring — [Gebregiyorgis thesis](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/Shishay.pdf).
  - Same thesis §3.2.3: rewriting in D_m-invariants "reduces the number of solutions by the order of the group D_m ... reduces the degree of the ideal ... by the same amount. As a result, the complexity of the FGLM algorithm ... is reduced by a factor of (m! 2^{m−1})^3" — [Gebregiyorgis thesis](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/Shishay.pdf).
  - FHJRV §5.2 (timings that quote FGHR): on a random Legendre-form curve over F_{(2^31+413)^5}, "in [4] only one 2-torsion was accounted for (in a twisted Edwards model) and the authors reported a timing of 2 732 sec for one decomposition test" (EXPERIMENTAL) — [FHJRV](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
  - Dzierzkowski–Wroński 2024 §5.1 restate the mechanism for twisted Edwards: adding the 2-torsion point T_2 to P=(x,y) "gives P + T_2 = (−x, −y)"; a known decomposition P_1⊕…⊕P_n = R yields "2^{n−1} decompositions of R obtained by adding an even number of times a 2-torsion point T_2" — [Dzierzkowski–Wroński 2024](https://sciendo.com/pdf/10.2478/tmmp-2024-0021).
- Exact degree/variable bookkeeping after symmetrization (from FHJRV, which subsumes FGHR's 2-torsion case): with φ = h∘x of degree 2, the summation polynomial P_{φ,n} has degree 2^{n−2} in each variable (Prop. 5 ff.); after rewriting in the generators of K[X]^{G_2} = K[s_1,…,s_{n−1},e_n] (odd characteristic, s_i = i-th elementary symmetric polynomial in X_1^2..X_n^2, e_n = X_1⋯X_n), the number of monomials drops from 5/36/940/– (symmetrized Semaev, n=3,4,5,6) to 5/13/182/4125 (Table 2) — [FHJRV Prop. 13, Table 2](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
- Field condition for the affine normal form (odd characteristic): there exist T ∈ E(K)[2] and a degree-2 φ with φ(P+T) = −φ(P), φ(−P) = φ(P) "if and only if there exist T' ∈ E[4] such that x(T') ∈ K. In this case T = [2]T' and the curve E has an equation of the form y^2 = x^3 + ax^2 + bx where T = (0,0) and b is a square in K; moreover φ is of the form λ (x(P)+√b)/(x(P)−√b)"; "in odd characteristic, about half of the curves with a 2-torsion point have a coefficient b that is a square ... Overall, this proposition applies in odd characteristic to about 3/4 of curves with a 2-torsion point" (PROVEN; stated for an arbitrary field K, so it holds verbatim over F_p) — [FHJRV Prop. 8(i), Remark 9](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
- Where the extension field enters: the factor base is F = {P ∈ E(F_{q^n}) : φ(P) ∈ P^1(F_q)} and "To solve the equation (4), we take the scalar restriction with respect to a linear basis of the extension F_{q^n}/F_q, leading to a multivariate polynomial system defined over F_q"; factor-base invariance under translation by T requires "the associated homography f_T is in PGL_2(F_q)" — [FHJRV §2, §3.2](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).

### Inferences
- The two ingredients of FGHR separate cleanly: (a) the algebra (D_n-invariant presentation; ideal degree / |D_n|; quasi-homogeneity) is proved over an arbitrary field and so is literally available for a curve over F_p of even order with a rational-x 4-torsion point (e.g. Montgomery/Edwards curves of order 8ℓ); (b) the payoff is realised only inside a Weil-restricted zero-dimensional system whose FGLM step costs O(nD^3) (D = ideal degree). Over F_p there is no such system — the constraint "x_i small" (Semaev 2004) is not algebraic — so the 2^{ω(n−1)} saving has no carrier. The only F_p use found is Dzierzkowski–Wroński's QUBO trick (KQ7 item 14): a symmetric interval V = [−p^{1/n}/2, p^{1/n}/2] is stable under x ↦ −x, so each PDP instance has 2 rather than 1 solution.
- Curve applicability over F_p: secp256k1 (prime order, j=0) and NIST P-256 (prime order) have no rational 2-torsion, hence none of FGHR/FHJRV applies; Curve25519/Ed25519-type curves (order 8ℓ, full 2-torsion) satisfy Prop. 8(i)/Prop. 10 algebraically.
- The factor 2^{ω(n−1)} rather than (2^{n−1})^3 is explained by FGHR replacing cubic FGLM by their change-of-ordering algorithm with exponent ω; the (m!2^{m−1})^3 figure in the thesis is the plain-FGLM accounting.

### Gaps
- FGHR's full text (exact invariant variables for the Jacobi-intersection model, the quasi-homogeneous weights, Section/Table numbers, the exact F_{p^n} experiment list) could not be retrieved; all FGHR facts above are second-hand from FHJRV, the Gebregiyorgis thesis, and the publisher abstract.
- No statement by FGHR about prime fields F_p was found in any secondary source.

## KQ2. Faugère–Huot–Joux–Renault–Vitse, "Symmetrized summation polynomials: using small order torsion points to speed up elliptic curve index calculus" (EUROCRYPT 2014, LNCS 8441, 40–57)

### Takeaway
FHJRV generalise summation polynomials to any morphism φ: E → P^1 and show that an m-torsion point T gives an equivariance φ∘τ_T = f_T∘φ with f_T ∈ PGL_2 of order m, forcing m | deg φ; the induced action of (Z/mZ)^{n−1} ⋊ S_n on the zero set lets one write the polynomial in invariant generators, which for m = 2 cuts monomial counts by one to two orders of magnitude (8th polynomial computed for the first time), divides the factor base by m (linear algebra by m^2) and multiplies the decomposition probability by m^{−(n−1)}. The symmetrization is proved over an arbitrary field; the index-calculus use needs F_q ⊂ F_{q^n}.

### Cited Findings
- Setting and claim: "Decomposition-based index calculus methods are currently efficient only for elliptic curves E defined over non-prime finite fields of very small extension degree n ... the largest Semaev polynomial ever computed is the 6-th"; the paper computes "up to the 8-th symmetrized summation polynomial and give[s] new timings for the computation of relations with degree 5 extension fields" — [FHJRV abstract](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
- Definition 1 / Prop. 2 (PROVEN): for any non-constant φ: E → P^1 there is an irreducible P_{φ,n}, unique up to a constant, symmetric for n ≥ 3, with S(a_1..a_n)=0 ⇔ ∃P_i, φ(P_i)=a_i, ΣP_i = O. Heuristic degree in each variable ≤ (deg φ)^{n−1}; halved to (deg φ)^{n−1}/2 when φ(−P)=φ(P); if φ = φ'∘ψ for an isogeny ψ then P_{φ,n} = P_{φ',n} — [FHJRV §2](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf). "Note that this definition, and in fact a large part of what follows, is actually independent of the index calculus context" (ibid.).
- Prop. 3 (PROVEN): if T ∈ E[m] and φ∘τ_T = f_T∘φ with f_T of order m, then m divides deg φ. Prop. 4 (PROVEN): the only subgroups G ⊂ E(K) admitting an injective χ: G → PGL_2(K) are E[2], cyclic ⟨T⟩ with m prime to char K, or E[char K]; "in the finite field case, PGL_2(F_q) has a subgroup isomorphic to D_m if and only if m | (q−1) or m | (q+1) or m = char(F_q) when m > 2" — [FHJRV §3.1](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
- Automorphisms: "Besides small torsion points, we would also like to take into account the automorphisms of the curve E; for most curves this only means the involution [−1]"; the group generated by τ_T and [−1] is the dihedral group D_m = Z/mZ ⋊ Z/2Z — [FHJRV §3.1](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf). (No treatment of j = 0 or j = 1728 automorphisms.)
- §3.2 factor-base reduction (HEURISTIC accounting): with f_T ∈ PGL_2(F_q), F is τ_T-invariant, so one keeps one representative per class, stores [m]R = [m]Q_1 + … + [m]Q_n, needs ≈ #F/m relations, and the relation matrix dimension is divided by m "providing a speed-up by a factor m^2. On the other hand, this decreases the probability that a random point R decomposes by a factor m^{n−1}"; with full 2-torsion and [−1] "a division by 8 of the size of the factor base" — [FHJRV §3.2](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
- Prop. 5 (PROVEN): every degree-2 φ is h∘x∘τ_{−Q}; the associated P_{φ,n} has degree 2^{n−1} in each variable if Q ∉ E[n] and 2^{n−2} otherwise; Lemma 6: every degree-2 φ is equivariant for every 2-torsion point — [FHJRV §4](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
- Prop. 7 (PROVEN): if f_T is affine then for n ≥ 3, P_{φ,n} is invariant under G_2 = (Z/2Z)^{n−1} ⋊ S_n (|G_2| = 2^{n−1} n!). Prop. 13 (PROVEN, any characteristic): K[X_1..X_n]^{G_2} = K[e_1, s_2, …, s_n] in characteristic 2 (s_i elementary symmetric in X_j^2 + X_j) and K[s_1, …, s_{n−1}, e_n] otherwise (s_i elementary symmetric in X_j^2) — [FHJRV §4.1, §4.3](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
- Full 2-torsion (odd characteristic), Prop. 10/12 (PROVEN): for the twisted Legendre form y^2 = cx(x−1)(x−λ) one gets involutions {z↦−z, z↦1/z, z↦−1/z} iff at least two of Δ_0=λ, Δ_1=1−λ, Δ_2=−λ(1−λ) are squares (equivalently 4-torsion points with rational x; "about half of the curves in twisted Legendre form"), and then Q_{φ,n} = P_{φ,n}/(X_1⋯X_n)^{2^{n−3}} is invariant under G_4 = (Z/2Z×Z/2Z)^{n−1} ⋊ S_n of order 4^{n−1} n!; Prop. 14: K(X)^{G_4} = K(σ_1,…,σ_n, w_0) with Z_i = X_i^2 + X_i^{−2}, σ_i elementary symmetric in the Z_i, w_0, w_1 explicit degree-4 elements — [FHJRV §4.2–4.3](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
- Sizes (EXPERIMENTAL, Tables 1–2): characteristic 2, λ=0: Semaev symmetrized monomials 3/6/39/638/–/– vs P_{φ,n} 2/3/9/50/2 247/470 369 for n = 3..8, timings 0 s/0 s/0 s/1 s/383 s/40.5 CPU h (the 8th by a dedicated sparse interpolation; direct attempts "stopped after at least one month"); odd characteristic: Semaev 5/36/940/–, P_{φ,n}(s_1..s_{n−1},e_n) 5/13/182/4125, Q_{φ,n}(σ,w_0,w_1) 3/6/32/396 for n = 3..6 — [FHJRV §5.1](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
- Explicit small cases (odd characteristic, Legendre form with λ = (1−t^2)/(1+t^2)): Sem_3 = e_2^2 − 4e_1e_3 + 2e_2λ − 4e_3(λ+1) + λ^2; P_{φ,3} = t^3 e_3^2 + 2(1−t^4)e_3 + t^3 s_1 − t s_2 − t; Q_{φ,3} = t^3 w_1 − t w_0 − 2t^4 + 2 — [FHJRV §5.1](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
- Index-calculus timings (EXPERIMENTAL): IPSEC Oakley 'Well Known Group' 3 curve over F_{2^155} = F_{(2^31)^5} (order 12·prime; "we can only consider the action of the 2-torsion or the 3-torsion points"): reduced factor base 536 864 344 ≈ 2^31/4; one decomposition test 10.28 s with FGb (3.44 s Gröbner + 6.84 s FGLM) vs 995 s + ~6 h with Magma; expected time per relation 2^4·5!·10.28 s ≈ 5.5 h versus "about 37 years on a single core" estimated for the n−1 approach of Joux–Vitse; random Legendre curve over F_{(2^31+413)^5} with full 2-torsion: 6.66 s per test (2.82 s GB + 3.84 s FGLM) vs 2 732 s in FGHR (one 2-torsion point, twisted Edwards) — [FHJRV §5.2](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).
- Conclusion: "The framework we have developed also applies to higher order torsion points, which will be more detailed in an extended version of this article" — [FHJRV §6](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/Semaev2torsion.pdf).

### Inferences
- What transfers to F_p: Definition 1–Prop. 14 are stated over an arbitrary field, so for an F_p curve with a rational 4-torsion point (Prop. 8(i)) or full rational 2-torsion with two square Δ_i (Prop. 10) the sparse invariant presentations P_{φ,n}(s_1..s_{n−1}, e_n) / Q_{φ,n}(σ, w) exist verbatim. A j = 0 curve y^2 = x^3 + b over F_p has a rational 2-torsion point iff −b is a cube in F_p (always when p ≡ 2 mod 3), but the curves of cryptographic interest (secp256k1: prime order) have none.
- What does not transfer: the factor base F = φ^{−1}(P^1(F_q)) and the Weil restriction. Over F_p the analogue of "φ(P) ∈ P^1(F_q)" would be "φ(P) ∈ V" for a non-algebraic set V; f_T-stability of V is then a combinatorial condition (V = −V for f_T = −z; V = V^{−1} for z ↦ 1/z). Prop. 4 shows that beyond E[2], only cyclic torsion of order prime to p (requiring m | p±1) or E[p] (empty for ordinary curves over F_p) could act, and the m-torsion case needs deg φ ≥ m, which raises the polynomial degree to ≈ m^{n−1} (Prop. 2) — a trade-off never evaluated in print.
- The promised extended version on higher-order torsion was not found in any index (OpenAlex/Crossref/arXiv); treat higher-order torsion symmetrization as unpublished.

### Gaps
- No published follow-up on 3-torsion or m-torsion (m ≥ 3) symmetrized summation polynomials was found.
- Constant-factor savings for the Gröbner step (as opposed to monomial counts and FGLM) are only given as end-to-end timings.

## KQ3. Joux–Vitse, "Elliptic curve discrete logarithm problem over small degree extension fields — application to the static Diffie–Hellman problem on E(F_{q^5})" (J. Cryptology 26(1):119–143, 2013; ePrint 2010/157)

### Takeaway
The n−1 trick replaces the square Gaudry–Diem system (n equations, n unknowns, degree 2^{n−1}, ideal degree 2^{n(n−1)}, FGLM needed) by an overdetermined one (n equations, n−1 unknowns, degree 2^{n−2}, generically empty or a single point, grevlex basis suffices) at the price of a 1/((n−1)! q) decomposition probability; this plus direct computation of symmetrized polynomials and the F4Remake variant made n = 5 practical. The symmetrization recipes, F4Remake and the oracle-assisted SDHP framework are field-independent; the factor base, Weil restriction and the complexity theorem are not. The paper contains no sieving — Joux–Vitse sieving lives in their Cover-and-Decomposition paper.

### Cited Findings
- Gaudry–Diem baseline: probability of decomposition ≈ (q^n/n!)/q^n = 1/n!; c(n,q) ≤ Poly(n! 2^{n(n−1)} log_2 q) by resultants; with double large primes Õ(q^{2−2/n}) for fixed n with hidden constant 2^{O(n^2)}; full estimate n! Poly(n! 2^{n(n−1)} log_2 q) q^{2−2/n} — [Joux–Vitse §2.1](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/DLFq5.pdf).
- The variant (§2.2): decompose R = ±P_1 ± … ± P_{n−1} using the n-th (not (n+1)-th) summation polynomial; "the resulting system of n polynomials is in (n−1) variables and of total degree only 2^{n−2}"; Assumption 1 (HEURISTIC): the number of decomposable points is Ω(q^{n−1}/(n−1)!), so expected trials O((n−1)! q^2); "generically, i.e. when R ∉ ς(C^{(n−1)}), this ideal is the whole polynomial ring ... Otherwise ... the ideal is maximal and its grevlex Gröbner basis contains only linear polynomials" so no FGLM is needed; Assumption 2 (HEURISTIC, "verified in all our experiments"): the maximal degree in the grevlex computation is below the Macaulay bound d = n2^{n−2} − n + 1 — [Joux–Vitse §2.2](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/DLFq5.pdf).
- Theorem 1 (under Assumptions 1–2): DLP cost Õ(((n−1)! 2^{(n−1)(n−2)} e^n n^{−1/2})^ω q^2); comparison table: Gaudry–Diem n! systems of n eq./n var./degree 2^{n−1}; hybrid (Bettale et al.) n! q systems of n eq./(n−1) var./degree 2^{n−1}; this work (n−1)! q systems of n eq./(n−1) var./degree 2^{n−2}; "Pollard rho or other generic methods remain faster than our variant for n ≤ 4, thus our approach is actually relevant only for n ≥ 5"; for n = 5 the Macaulay estimate predicts ~10^14 F_q multiplications while F4 needs ~10^10 — [Joux–Vitse §2.2–2.3](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/DLFq5.pdf).
- Practical numbers (EXPERIMENTAL): a decomposition test over F_{p^5}, p of 32 bits, takes 8.5 s (2.6 GHz Core 2 Duo) versus 30 ms over F_{2^160} = F_{(2^32)^5} ("Semaev's summation polynomials are sparser [in characteristic 2] ... Note however that the bounds given for the degree of regularity and the degree of the ideals remain the same"); ≈3·10^9 F_p multiplications per test for odd p and 2·10^7 in F_{2^d}; the method beats Pollard rho "as soon as the cardinality of the base field is greater than 2^60 in the odd characteristic case, or 2^45 in characteristic 2" — [Joux–Vitse §2.4](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/DLFq5.pdf).
- Symmetrized polynomials (PROVEN, stated for any field K of characteristic ≠ 2,3): Prop. 5 — f̃_3(e_{1,2}, e_{2,2}, X_3) = (e_1^2 − 4e_2)X_3^2 − 2(e_1(e_2 + a) + 2b)X_3 + (e_2 − a)^2 − 4b e_1 and f̃_{m+1} = Sym_m Res_Y(f̃_m(e_{1,m−1},…,Y), f_3(e_{1,1}, X_{m+1}, Y)) (partial symmetrization after every resultant step); Prop. 6 — the roots of F_m(X) = g_{m,1}(X)^2 − (X^3 + aX + b) g_{m,2}(X)^2 (deg g_{m,1} ≤ ⌊m/2⌋, deg g_{m,2} ≤ ⌊(m−2)/2⌋) are exactly the x-coordinates of m points summing to O, so the m-th symmetrized polynomial is the generator of the elimination ideal J ∩ K[e_1..e_m]; timings for the 5th symmetrized polynomial over F_{p^5}: resultant+symmetrization 11.99/12.21/33.39 s for 8/16/32-bit p vs 1.04/1.04/3.57 s (method 1) and 1.75/1.77/11.12 s (method 2); memory 510 MB vs 66 MB vs 22 MB; the 6th could only be obtained with X_6 instantiated (≈10 min, 60 MB); the 5th partially symmetrized polynomial has 3 972 terms in odd characteristic vs 100 in characteristic 2 — [Joux–Vitse §3](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/DLFq5.pdf).
- F4Remake (§4, HEURISTIC/EXPERIMENTAL): precompute the list of useful critical pairs on one instance, replay it on all subsequent instances; success probability ≥ c(p)^{n_step} with c(p) = 1 − 1/p + O(1/p^2); on 5 equations of degree 8 in 4 variables over F_p: 8-bit p: F4Remake 2.844 s vs F4 5.903 s vs Magma 9.660 s (failure prob 0.11); 32-bit p: 8.444 s vs 24.56 s vs 1046 s (failure prob 5.8·10^{−9}); F5 produced 17 249 labelled polynomials vs ≤ 2 789 for F4 and was "unsuitable for these specific systems" — [Joux–Vitse §4, Fig. 2](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/DLFq5.pdf).

- Sieving: the word "sieving" does not occur in this paper (full-text search); Joux–Vitse's sieving is §3.2 "Sieving for quadratic extensions" of Cover and Decomposition (EUROCRYPT 2012), used for genus-3 hyperelliptic Jacobians over F_{p^2} obtained as covers of E(F_{p^6}) ("Sieving on Jac_H(F_{p^2}), g = 3 ... Õ(p^{12/7})"), and was later reformulated by Vitse–Wallet ("Sarkar and Singh have proposed a sieving technique, inspired by an earlier work of Joux and Vitse ... speed-up by a factor approximately 3 for the relation search in Diem and Kochinke's methods") — [Cover and Decomposition](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/CoverDecomp.pdf); [Improved sieving on algebraic curves](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/SievingB.pdf).
- Conclusion on larger n: for a factor base of x-coordinates in a d-dimensional F_q-subspace the probability becomes ≈ q^{md−n}/m! and linear algebra Õ(mq^{2d}); "the equations given by the Weil restriction of Semaev's polynomials are no longer invariant under the full symmetric group S_{md} ... but only under the smaller group S_m"; SAGBI-Gröbner bases suggested — [Joux–Vitse §6](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/DLFq5.pdf).

### Inferences
- Field-structure-independent: (i) the symmetrization recipes of Props. 5–6 (any K, char ≠ 2,3), (ii) F4Remake (its success bound improves with p, so large-characteristic F_p systems are the favourable case), (iii) the SDHP oracle framework (needs only a factor base plus a decomposition routine), (iv) the principle of trading decomposition probability for an overdetermined, lower-degree system. Over F_p with Semaev's base |V| ≈ p^{1/m}, decomposing into m−1 instead of m points lowers the decomposition probability from ≈ 1/m! to ≈ (p^{1/m})^{m−1}/((m−1)!·p) = 1/((m−1)!·p^{1/m}) while lowering the summation polynomial degree from 2^{m−1} to 2^{m−2} per variable (same bookkeeping as Joux–Vitse's 1/n! → 1/((n−1)!·q)); no paper evaluates this trade-off over F_p.
- Field-dependent: the factor base x ∈ F_q, the Weil restriction to n equations, Condition 3 (Frobenius σ), Assumption 1's geometric justification (symmetric power of C ⊂ Res_{F_{q^n}/F_q}E), the Õ(q^2) accounting.

## KQ4. Galbraith–Gebregiyorgis, "Summation polynomial algorithms for elliptic curves in characteristic two" (INDOCRYPT 2014, LNCS 8885, 409–427; ePrint 2014/806) — invariant rings, SAT

### Takeaway
On binary Edwards curves the coordinate t = x + y is invariant under negation and under translation by T_2 = (1,1), and when d_1 = d_2 the 4-torsion point T_4 = (1,0) acts by t ↦ t + 1, so the PDP polynomial lies in F_{2^n}[t]^{D_m} with explicit generators e_1, s_2, …, s_m (s_k elementary symmetric in t_i^2 + t_i), cutting ideal degree by m!2^{m−1} and the factor base by 8; experiments with Minisat vs F4 and with symmetry-breaking factor bases were inconclusive, and Pollard rho stays far ahead. The invariant-ring method and factor-base reduction are characteristic-free; the t = x+y trick, the Weil descent and SAT are F_2-specific.

### Cited Findings
- Abstract (ePrint listing): new variable choices for binary Edwards curves "lower the degree of the summation polynomials"; "a symmetry-breaking factor base"; "an experimental comparison of SAT solvers against Gröbner basis methods"; "the symmetry-breaking factor base and SAT solver results were not conclusive"; "Pollard rho remains much faster than index calculus for the ECDLP over prime-degree binary fields of practical size" — [ePrint 2014/806 listing](https://eprint.iacr.org/2014/806) (as summarised in search results; full text behind Cloudflare).
- Thesis §3.2.2 (PROVEN): P + T_2 = (x+1, y+1) so t(P + T_2) = t(P); a decomposition yields 2^{m−1} decompositions (3.5); the factor base keeps one of {P, −P, P + T_2, −P + T_2}, so #F ≈ ¼#V and linear algebra cost drops by (1/4)^2 — [Gebregiyorgis thesis](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/Shishay.pdf).
- Thesis §3.2.3 (PROVEN): for d_1 = d_2, T_4 = (1,0), P + T_4 = (y, x+1), t(P + T_4) = t(P) + 1; D_m = (Z/2Z)^{m−1} ⋊ S_m acts, invariants under t_i ↦ t_i + 1 are t_i^2 + t_i "(this is mentioned in Section 4.3 of [FHJ+14])"; generators e_1, s_2 = Σ(t_i^2+t_i)(t_j^2+t_j), …, s_m = Π(t_i^2 + t_i) (e_1 itself stays invariant because the T_4 translations cancel); FGLM complexity reduced by (m!2^{m−1})^3; factor base reduced by 8, linear algebra by 1/64 — [Gebregiyorgis thesis](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/Shishay.pdf).
- Thesis §3.5 (EXPERIMENTAL): Experiment 1 uses e_1..e_m (T_2 action), Experiment 2 uses e_1, s_2..s_m (T_4 action), Experiment 3 adds symmetry breaking in the factor base; Minisat vs F4 for m = 4: "SAT solvers can be faster and, more importantly, handle larger range of values for ℓ", but "running time of SAT solvers varies a lot" and they are "slightly slow when no solution exists", motivating an early-abort strategy; "Exploiting greater symmetry (in this case Experiment 2) is" better for m = 3 and m = 4; for symmetry breaking (Experiment 3, m = 3) the thesis contrasts its degree of regularity with the value that "is 4 for most values in Experiment 2" (exact Experiment-3 values not transcribed) — [Gebregiyorgis thesis](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/Shishay.pdf).
- Degree-splitting alternative (thesis §3.6, after Karabina): f_5(x_1..x_4, x(R)) "is of degree 8 in each of the 4 variables", whereas the chained system f_3(x_1,x_2,x(R_1)) = 0, f_4(x_3,x_4,x(R_1),x(R)) = 0 etc. uses "derived 3rd summation polynomials ... of degree 2 in each of the 3 variables" at the cost of auxiliary variables x(R_1), x(R_2) ∈ F_{2^n} (+2n binary variables) — [Gebregiyorgis thesis](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/Shishay.pdf); the same trade-off is Karabina's: "We modify the underlying system of equations by introducing some auxiliary variables. We argue that the trade-off between lowering the degree of Semaev polynomials and increasing the number of variables is worth" — [Karabina 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.02347).
- Order-3 automorphism experiment (thesis §4.2–4.3, characteristic 3): ψ = φ + 1: (x,y) ↦ (x+1, y) is an order-3 endomorphism (char. poly x^2 − 5x + 7); G = Z_3 × S_m; factor base reduced by 3 (by 6 with negation), linear algebra by 36; primary invariants for m = 2 computed with Singular/Kemper: s_1 = x_1 + x_2 + x_3, s_2 = x_1^2 + x_1x_2 + x_2^2, s_3 = x_1 − x_1^3 + x_2 − x_2^3; but "the invariant variable S_1 no more lies in our chosen subspace for the factor base but instead lies in F_{3^n} ... the number of variables in the resulting system is increased and the resulting system is relatively harder to solve which cannot be compensated due to the group action. This is also verified in our experiment which is not reported here"; conclusion: "an automorphism of an elliptic curve can speed up the point decomposition problem by reducing the factor base ... But an automorphism of an elliptic curve is worse in the actual polynomial system solving" (EXPERIMENTAL, negative) — [Gebregiyorgis thesis](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/Shishay.pdf).

### Inferences
- Carries to odd characteristic / F_p: the invariant-ring methodology (Chevalley–Shephard–Todd; FHJRV Prop. 13 gives the odd-characteristic generators s_1..s_{n−1}, e_n), the factor-base orbit reduction, and the Karabina-style degree/variable trade-off (chained S_3's) — the latter is purely algebraic and could be used over F_p (each S_3 of degree 2 per variable) if a solving method for the "x_i ∈ V" constraint existed.
- Does not carry: t = x + y (binary Edwards only; the odd-characteristic analogue is FHJRV's φ = λ(x+√b)/(x−√b)), Weil descent, field equations, SAT over F_2 (the F_p analogue in the literature is Wroński's QUBO bit-blasting, KQ7).

## KQ5. Huang–Petit–Shinohara–Takagi (IWSEC 2013; Pacific J. Math. Industry 2015) and Semaev 2015 — what is F_{2^n}-specific

### Takeaway
HPST's gain comes from a Weil-descent device: with the factor base V = ⟨1, ω, …, ω^{n'−1}⟩ the elementary symmetric functions σ_k of the x_i have controlled degree in ω, so the system can be written in both symmetric variables d (mn Boolean) and non-symmetric variables c (mn' Boolean), lowering the observed d_reg from 6–7 to 3–4 and giving ≈85× speed-up at n = 53, m = 3. Semaev 2015 replaces S_{m+1} by a chain of cubic S_3-equations and derives 2^{c√(n ln n)} under a first-fall-degree-4 assumption; he states explicitly that it is unclear how to solve the chained system when q is a large prime. Everything that makes these work (descent to F_2, field equations, subspace factor base, sparsity of S_3 in characteristic 2) is absent over F_p; the symmetric-function presentation itself is not.

### Cited Findings
- HPST abstract: symmetrization "already provided practical improvements in several previous works for composite-degree extension fields, but its application to prime-degree extension fields has been more challenging. To exploit symmetries ... we specialize the definition of factor basis ... to replace the original polynomial system by a new and simpler one" — [HPST 2013 paper](https://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/IWSEC2013.pdf).
- Mechanism: "Our re-writing of the system involves both symmetric and non-symmetric variables. The total number of variables is increased ... symmetric variables in our system allows reducing the degrees of the equations significantly" (§1); the symmetrized Semaev equation is Weil-descended "in the mn binary variables d_{j,ℓ}", linked to the "mn' binary variables c_{i,ℓ}" by relation polynomials, giving a system "in the mn + mn' binary variables c_{j,ℓ} and d_{j,ℓ}" (§3.1) — [HPST 2013 paper](https://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/IWSEC2013.pdf).
- Slide table "Comparison for different multivariate polynomial systems": d_reg "7 or 6" for FPPR's s_{m+1} vs "4 or 3" for the symmetric-function system; "time and memory costs are respectively roughly #var^{2·d_reg} and #var^{3·d_reg}" (HEURISTIC) — [HPST 2013 slides, Kyushu repository](https://catalog.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/opac_download_md/1434304/p145.pdf).
- Experiments (EXPERIMENTAL; Magma V2.18-9, 16-core Opteron, 512 GB), m = 3, columns n, n', D_reg, #var, #poly, #mono, t_trans, t_groe (s), mem (MB): ImpFPPR n=23,n'=3: 6, 9, 23, 2792.97, 5.47, 1.06, 29.10 vs ImpOurs: 3, 24, 38, 1079.60, 0.91, 1.04, 15.59; n=53,n'=6: ImpFPPR 7, 18, 53, 245891.33, 366.92, 2967.03 s, 7311.44 MB vs ImpOurs 3, 51, 86, 11748.00, 10.48, 34.82 s, 151.04 MB (ratio 2967/34.8 ≈ 85×); n=23, n'=6: 3888.70 s vs 5150.12 s (ImpOurs slower, D_reg 4) — [HPST 2013 slides](https://catalog.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/opac_download_md/1434304/p145.pdf).
- Context on the degree in the composite case: Weil restriction of S_{n+1}(x_1..x_n,x_R) over F_{q^n} yields "a system of n symmetric polynomials, which ... can be written as a system of polynomials of total degree 2^{n−1} in terms of the elementary symmetric functions e_1..e_n" — [Joux–Vitse §2.1](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/DLFq5.pdf).
- Semaev 2015 (arXiv 1504.01175): S_3 in characteristic 2 is (x_1x_2 + x_1x_3 + x_2x_3)^2 + x_1x_2x_3 + B; the chained system S_3(u_1,x_1,x_2)=0, S_3(u_i,u_{i+1},x_{i+2})=0 (1 ≤ i ≤ t−3), S_3(u_{t−2},x_t,R_X)=0 has degree "only 3" vs 2^{t−1}; after descent "n(t−1) coordinate equations in n(t−2)+kt variables" with k = ⌈n/m⌉; Assumption 1: F4 regularity degree ≤ 4 (HEURISTIC; "the first fall degree assumption is 'not generally correct' but 'appears correct' for these systems"); resulting complexity 2^{c√(n ln n)}, c ≈ 1.69, m ≈ √((2 ln 2)n/ln n); Table 2 sample: n=21, m=3, k=7: 133.54 s, 2437.8 MB vs 6910 s, 27 235 MB for the earlier method — [Semaev 2015 via ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1504.01175).
- Semaev on prime fields (verbatim): "First, q is a prime number, then V is a set of residues modulo q bounded by q^{1/n+δ} for a small δ ... However no algorithm to find the zeros in V of summation polynomials was suggested in [24]" (§2); "It is not quite clear how the system (4) may be resolved in case q is a large prime number. However, for q = p^n, where n is large, a Gröbner basis algorithm is applicable" (§4.2); "A similar assumption looks correct for odd p too but the computations are tedious already for p = 5" (§2) — [Semaev 2015 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.01175).
- Critiques of the first-fall heuristic: Kosters–Yeo show "the Weil descent to F_2 of S_3 for ordinary curves in general has first fall degree 2, which is much lower than expected. The reason is the existence of a group morphism to F_2 which gives a linear polynomial after Weil descent" and "raise doubt on certain Gröbner basis heuristics which claim that the first fall degree is close to the degree of regularity" (PROVEN for the first-fall statement) — [Kosters–Yeo 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.08001); PKM cite Huang–Kosters–Yeo (CRYPTO 2015) "for a criticism of the analysis of [Petit–Quisquater]" — [PKM 2016](https://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/16PKC_primeECDLP.pdf).
- Granger's sparsity remark (restated): "in characteristic 2 ... Semaev's summation polynomials are sparser than in the odd characteristic case, so that the corresponding systems are much easier to solve" — [Joux–Vitse §2.4](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/articles/DLFq5.pdf).

### Inferences
- F_{2^n}-specific: Weil descent to Boolean equations; field equations x^2 = x; the subspace factor base with a monomial basis {1, ω, …} (which is what bounds deg_ω σ_k); Frobenius-friendly linearised polynomials; the 100-vs-3972-term sparsity of S_5; Semaev's u_i ∈ F_q auxiliary variables (over F_p they are p-valued unknowns with no descent). The only field-free element is "write S_{m+1} in e_1..e_m"; over F_p the "x_i ∈ V" constraint has no polynomial encoding of controlled degree (PKM's L(x) = Π_{α<B}(x−α) has degree |V|), which is exactly the obstruction PKM and Semaev name.

## KQ6. GLV / automorphism exploitation (j = 0: x ↦ βx, β^3 = 1; j = 1728: x ↦ −x; Frobenius)

### Takeaway
Three sources use curve endomorphisms in summation-polynomial index calculus, all to shrink the factor base / relation count rather than to lower the degree of the summation polynomial: Tsakou–Ionica 2021 (GLV j=1728 and j=0 curves and the Longa–Sica GLV-GLS curve over F_{q^n}, n > 1; classes of size up to 6, speed-up ≈ class size), Galbraith–Granger–Merz–Petit 2020 (Frobenius-invariant factor bases for Koblitz curves over F_{q^n}; 1/n systems, n^2 linear algebra) and Gebregiyorgis 2016 (order-3 endomorphism in characteristic 3; rewriting in Z_3 × S_m invariants made the system harder). Every one of them needs x(P) ∈ F_q (a subfield) for the orbit to stay in the factor base; no paper does this over F_p, and no paper symmetrizes S_m by the j = 0 or j = 1728 automorphism.

### Cited Findings
- Tsakou–Ionica, Math. Cryptology 1(2):102–114 (2021; ePrint 2021/721): "We generalize the reduction of the factor base based on the use of the automorphism [−]: P ↦ −P and the Frobenius endomorphism and show that any efficient computable endomorphism φ of the Jacobian may be used to speed up index calculus ... we redefine the factor base so that it would be invariant under the action of the endomorphism"; relations get coefficients "powers of the eigenvalue of the endomorphism"; "for elliptic curves with rational 2-torsion, we show that our definition of equivalence classes on the factor base is compatible to the one in [FHJRV]"; Magma implementation "obtained a speed up factor close to the size of our equivalence classes" (EXPERIMENTAL) — [Tsakou–Ionica](https://journals.flvc.org/mathcryptology/article/download/130882/133614/231845).
  - Example 2 (j = 1728): q ≡ 1 mod 4 prime, E_1: y^2 = x^3 + ax over F_{q^n}, φ(x,y) = (−x, αy), α of order 4, X^2 + 1 = 0; "x(φ(Q)) ∈ F_q whenever x(Q) ∈ F_q", class {Q, φ(Q)} halves the factor base "as compared to the classical algorithm considering the equivalence class {Q, −Q}".
  - Example 3 (j = 0): q ≡ 1 mod 3 prime, E_2: y^2 = x^3 + b over F_{q^n}, "β ∈ F_{q^n} ... the cubic root of 1 in F_q", φ(x,y) = (βx, y), eigenvalue λ with X^2 + X ≡ −1 mod r, factor base F_{E_2,x}.
  - Example 4 (GLV-GLS, Longa–Sica curve y^2 = x^3 + 9u over F_{q^2}, q = 2^127 − 58309, prime order r of 254 bits): with μ = Tr_ψ(x) = x(Q) + u^{(1−q)/3} x(Q)^q and φ(x,y) = (βx,y), φ^2 + φ + 1 = 0, ψ^2 + 1 = 0, the class {Q, φQ, φ^2Q, ψQ, ψφQ, ψφ^2Q} "allows us to gain a factor 6 speed up in the relation search step".

- GGMP, SAC 2020 (LNCS 12804:115–138; ePrint 2020/1315): "we define factor bases that are invariant under the q-power Frobenius automorphism of the field F_{q^n}, reducing the number of polynomial systems that need to be solved. ### Inferences
- Structural property check for F_p curves. j = 0 (secp256k1): Aut(E) has order 6, generated by (x,y) ↦ (βx, −y) with β ∈ F_p iff p ≡ 1 mod 3 (true for secp256k1). Because this is a group automorphism, (P_i) ↦ (ρP_i) preserves W = {ΣP_i = O}, so S_m(βX_1, …, βX_m) = c·S_m(X): from the explicit S_3 for A = 0 (S_3 = (x_1−x_2)^2x_3^2 − 2((x_1+x_2)x_1x_2 + 2b)x_3 + (x_1x_2)^2 − 4b(x_1+x_2), read off from [Tsakou–Ionica Example 1](https://journals.flvc.org/mathcryptology/article/download/130882/133614/231845) with A = 0) every monomial has total degree ≡ 1 mod 3 (degrees 4 and 1), i.e. S_3 is quasi-homogeneous for the Z/3-grading; for j = 1728 (B = 0) all monomials have even degree, so S_3(−X) = S_3(X). This is the "quasi-homogeneous structure" FGHR exploit — but a diagonal scalar action by μ_3 or μ_2 is not generated by pseudo-reflections, so its invariant ring is not polynomial and no FGHR-type variable change lowering the degree exists; the usable consequence is only the factor-base orbit reduction (Tsakou–Ionica), which needs a set V ⊂ F_p stable under x ↦ βx (Example 3 relies on β ∈ F_q and x ∈ F_q). Semaev's V = small residues is not β-stable (β is a generic element of F_p), and a β-stable V has no small-height description, so over F_p the j = 0 automorphism gives at most the generic ×3 (×6 with negation) on the number of relations if one accepts an unstructured factor base — exactly the situation Gebregiyorgis found unfavourable.
- Frobenius-based ideas (GGMP, GLS part of Tsakou–Ionica) are void over F_p (π = id on E(F_p)).

### Gaps
- No paper symmetrizes the summation polynomial itself by the j = 0 or j = 1728 automorphism, over any field; the quasi-homogeneity statement above is an inference from the explicit S_3, not a citation.
- Tsakou–Ionica's Tables 2–3 (actual timings for the GLV-GLS curve) were not transcribed.

## KQ7. 2019–2026 literature on summation-polynomial / point-decomposition index calculus

### Takeaway
The 2019–2026 record is thin and mostly extension-field: two theoretical degree studies (Kousidis–Wiemers 2019; the AI-assisted Zenodo preprint 2026), one lower-bound paper on Semaev's naive prime-field method (Yokoyama et al. 2020), two endomorphism/Frobenius factor-base papers (GGMP 2020, Tsakou–Ionica 2021), one SAT line for F_{2^n} (Trimoska–Ionica–Dequen 2020 and thesis 2021), one curve-form comparison (Cheng–Kodera–Miyaji 2019), and a quantum-annealing (QUBO) line for toy prime fields (Wroński 2021; Dzierzkowski–Wroński 2024). ## KQ8. Papers treating the point decomposition problem (PDP) over F_p as a problem in its own right

### Takeaway
Only four sources isolate the prime-field PDP: Semaev 2004/2015 (defines it, offers no algorithm, calls the large-prime case unclear), Petit–Kosters–Messeng 2016 (recasts it as a "generalized root-finding problem" f(X) = 0, L(X_i) = 0 and measures Gröbner degrees), Kosters–Yeo 2015 (NP-completeness of deciding whether a summation polynomial vanishes, for general F_q under an assumption and unconditionally for fixed p ≥ 3 on the singular curve y^2 = x^3), and Yokoyama et al. 2020 (lower bound: the naive PDP ideal's Gröbner computation cannot beat brute force). Everything else over F_p (Amadori–Pintore–Sala, Kudo et al., McGuire–Müller, Wroński) treats the PDP as a step of index calculus.

### Cited Findings
- PKM Problem 1 (generalized root-finding problem): "Given a finite field K, given f ∈ K[X_1,…,X_m], and given L ∈ K(X), find X_i ∈ K such that f(X_1,…,X_m) = 0 and L(X_i) = 0 for all i"; "An even more important problem is to design a dedicated algorithm for the generalized root-finding problem, which would not rely on Gröbner basis algorithms at all"; Semaev's original base "implicitly uses L(x) = Π_{α<B}(x − α)"; "When K = F_p, Semaev originally proposed to use V = {x ∈ Z_{≥0} | x < p^{1/m}} ... However, Semaev did not suggest any algorithm to compute relations with this factor basis" — [PKM 2016 §2.1, §5](https://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/16PKC_primeECDLP.pdf).
- Kosters–Yeo Theorem 3.5 (PROVEN): (i) under Assumption 3.3 (efficient construction of curves with a point of prescribed large order), deciding whether S_{A,r}(x_0..x_{r−1}) = 0 over F_q is NP-complete (reduction from subset sum); (ii) for fixed p ≥ 3, deciding S_{(0,0,0,0,0),r}(a_0..a_{r−1}) = 0 over F_{p^n} is NP-complete unconditionally (singular curve); Remark 3.7: "it does not suggest that ECDLP itself is a hard problem"; abstract: "This suggests limitations in the usage of summation polynomials" — [Kosters–Yeo 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.08001).
- Yokoyama et al. 2020: PDP ideal has "a special structure", Gröbner computation = "extension of the extended Euclidean algorithm" with signatures, "precise analysis of a lower bound on the cost" (statistical assumptions on summation polynomials); naive index calculus "cannot be more efficient than even the brute force method" — [Indico abstract](https://indico.math.cnrs.fr/event/5017/); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/bb64b7a98fd2462c84a20ffe0d32dd6b).
- Semaev 2015 §4.2: "It is not quite clear how the system (4) may be resolved in case q is a large prime number" — [Semaev 2015 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.01175).
- Karabina 2015 defines PDP abstractly ("Given a point R ∈ G, find P_i ∈ B such that R = Σ P_i ... or conclude that R cannot be decomposed") but works only over F_{2^n} — [Karabina 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.02347). Wallet's 2016 thesis "Le problème de décompositions de points dans les variétés Jacobiennes" (HAL tel-01407675) treats PDP on Jacobians over extension fields (title only) — [HAL record](https://theses.hal.science/tel-01407675v2).
- Galbraith–Gaudry survey (DCC 78:51–72, 2016; ePrint 2015/1022) has a dedicated Section 9 on point decomposition and states the authors "believe elliptic curves over characteristic two fields of prime degree are not threatened by such methods" (from the ePrint abstract/search summary; full text blocked) — [ePrint 2015/1022](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/1022).

### Inferences
- The prime-field PDP literature has converged on one obstruction, stated three ways: the constraint x_i ∈ V is non-algebraic (Semaev), its algebraic encoding has degree |V| ≈ p^{1/m} (PKM/Amadori), and the resulting ideal's Gröbner cost is bounded below by brute force (Yokoyama). The extension-field tricks catalogued in KQ1–KQ6 all act after an algebraic encoding of V exists; none of them lowers deg L.

### Gaps
- Yokoyama et al.'s exact bound (dependence on m, |V|, p) and whether it covers the symmetrized/Amadori variant could not be verified without the full text.
- No 2019–2026 paper studies PDP over F_p with lattice/Coppersmith methods; searches combining "point decomposition", "Coppersmith", "lattice" and "summation polynomial" returned only unrelated Coppersmith applications.
