# Prior art for three candidate PDP strategies over F_p (V = multiplicative subgroup / V = interval / combinatorial–generic)

Setting used throughout: E/F_p, Semaev summation polynomial S_{m+1}(x_1,…,x_m,x_R) of degree 2^{m-1} in each variable, factor base F = {(x,y) ∈ E(F_p): x ∈ V}, |V| ≈ p^{1/m}; the point decomposition problem (PDP) is to find (x_1,…,x_m) ∈ V^m with S_{m+1} = 0. Labels: PROVEN = theorem in a refereed/arXiv source; HEURISTIC = stated by source under heuristic assumption; FOLKLORE = standard community statement or my own derivation from cited formulas (marked "inference").

Local copies of the primary texts used (pdftotext output) are in `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Volumes/d720c7ea-8652-42d3-b2d2-90031fc91317/scratchpad/pdfs/` (files `wb_jm2006.txt`, `wb_gg2015.txt`, `wb_maylll.txt`, `wb_semaev04.txt`, `wb_aps2017.txt`, `pkm2016.txt`, `bl_cuberoot.txt`, `gal_ch13.txt`, `gal_ch15.txt`, `gal_ch19.txt`, `maurer05.txt`, `wagner2.ps`).

## Idea (A): V = multiplicative subgroup H = μ_B (or coset cH) — norm/resultant trick, shifted-subgroup intersections, hidden shifted power problem, cyclotomic numbers

### Takeaway
The subgroup/coset factor base has already been proposed and implemented for prime-field ECDLP by Petit–Kosters–Messeng (PKC 2016), who reduce the PDP to a Gröbner-basis system S_{m+1}=0, x_i^{p_j}-chains, give the exact cost formula m!·p/(deg L)^{m-1}·T + (deg L)^ω, and state that beating Pollard rho requires solving the restricted-root problem in time below p^{1/2-1/m}; they report no asymptotic complexity and no dedicated (non-Gröbner) algorithm. The analytic literature (Garcia–Voloch, Heath-Brown–Konyagin, Shkredov–Vyugin, Corvaja–Zannier, Mérai–Shparlinski, Bourgain–Garaev–Konyagin–Shparlinski) gives PROVEN upper bounds on how many points a low-degree curve can have in H×H (≪ d^{4/3}|H|^{2/3} for the general curve; ≤ 4|H|^{2/3} for a line), plus sublinear-in-|H| algorithms only for the very special "hidden shifted power" structure (x+s)^e; I found no algorithm sublinear in |H| for deciding whether a general low-degree curve has a point in H×H, and no hardness result for it.

### Cited Findings

**A.1 The subgroup factor base has already been proposed for prime-field ECDLP (PKC 2016) — HEURISTIC/EXPERIMENTAL**
- Petit, Kosters, Messeng (PKC 2016, "Algebraic approaches for the ECDLP over prime fields") define the factor base as F = {(x,y) ∈ E(K) | L(x) = 0} where L is a composition of low-degree maps L = L_{n'} ∘ … ∘ L_1 with |{x: L(x)=0}| ≈ p^{1/m}; "When K = F_p, Semaev originally proposed to use V = {x ∈ Z≥0 | x < p^{1/m}} … However, Semaev did not suggest any algorithm to compute relations with this factor basis." — [Petit–Kosters–Messeng PKC 2016, §2.1, §2.3](https://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/16PKC_primeECDLP.pdf)
- Section 3.3 ("Attack when p−1 has a large smooth factor"): "assume that p − 1 = r ∏_{i=1}^{n'} p_i where the p_i are … all smaller than B, and ∏ p_i ≈ p^{1/m}. … We define V as the subgroup G of order ∏ p_i in F_p^*. We then set L_j(x) = x^{p_j} for j = 1,…,n'−1, and L_{n'}(x) = 1 − x^{p_{n'}}. … Alternatively, we could also choose V as a coset aG of G." The PDP is then the system S_{m+1}(x_{11},…,x_{m1},X)=0, x_{i,j+1} = L_j(x_{i,j}), 0 = L_{n'}(x_{i,n'}) (their system (4)), solved by Gröbner bases. — [PKM 2016, §3.1, §3.3](https://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/16PKC_primeECDLP.pdf)
- Exact cost model (§3.2): with T(E,m,L) the time to solve system (4) and P(p,m) precomputation, expected number of solutions per random point ≈ (deg L)^m/(m!·p), and total cost "P(p,m) + (m!·p/(deg L)^{m−1})·T(E,m,L) + (deg L)^ω. Our algorithm will outperform generic discrete logarithm algorithms when this complexity is smaller than p^{1/2}. When (deg L)^m ≈ m!·p, this will happen when one can solve T(E,m,L) more efficiently than p^{1/2−1/m}." — [PKM 2016, §3.2](https://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/16PKC_primeECDLP.pdf)
- They note NIST P-224 has p−1 = 2^96·3·5·17·257·641·65537·274177·6700417·67280421310721, "hence it satisfies the prerequisites of our attack already for m ≥ 3 and B = 2"; §3.4 generalises to arbitrary p via an auxiliary curve E'/F_p of smooth order and V = x-projection of a coset of a subgroup of E'. — [PKM 2016, §3.3–3.4](https://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/16PKC_primeECDLP.pdf)
- Experiments (§4): V = subgroup of order 2^{n_1} in F_p^*, m = 2 (P = P_1 + P_2) with Magma Gröbner bases; conclusion (§5): "The attacks are not practical at the moment and we do not know their asymptotic complexity." They formalise "Problem 1 (Generalized root-finding problem): Given a finite field K, f ∈ K[X_1,…,X_m], and L ∈ K(X), find X_i ∈ K such that f(X_1,…,X_m)=0 and L(X_i)=0 for all i" and call designing a dedicated non-Gröbner algorithm for it "an even more important problem". — [PKM 2016, §4–5](https://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/16PKC_primeECDLP.pdf)
- Follow-up: Amadori–Pintore–Sala (FFA 2018, eprint 2017/609) use m disjoint factor bases and a single relation; total cost p·m^m/s^m · T(E,m,V); "In order to reach an improvement with respect to algorithms for generic cyclic groups, this complexity should be smaller than p^{1/2}. We do not claim this result. Indeed our complexity analysis is partial, since we are unable to estimate the complexity of solving our polynomial equation system." — [Amadori–Pintore–Sala, eprint 2017/609 §3.2 (Wayback copy)](https://web.archive.org/web/2020id_/https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/609.pdf)
- Galbraith–Gaudry survey (DCC 2016): "In the case of elliptic curves defined over prime fields, Semaev [107] proposed to use for F the set of points whose abscissa is small when viewed as an integer. However, the elliptic group law is deeply incompatible with the multiplication of the integers representing the abscissae of the points. As a consequence, right now, there is no known efficient decomposition algorithm for that choice of F, and elliptic curves over prime fields remain unaffected by index calculus algorithms." — [Galbraith–Gaudry, eprint 2015/1022 §8 (Wayback copy)](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/1022.pdf)

**A.2 Norm trick / resultant with X^B − 1 — PROVEN (textbook)**
- Res(g, X^B − 1) = ± ∏_{ζ^B=1} g(ζ) and Res(f,g) = lc(f)^{deg g} ∏_{f(α)=0} g(α) = Res(f, g mod f)·(…), so ∏_{x∈μ_B} g(x) is obtained from h = (X^B − 1) mod g (O(log B) multiplications modulo g, i.e. O(M(deg g) log B)) followed by one resultant of two polynomials of degree ≤ deg g, which costs O(M(deg g) log deg g) (von zur Gathen–Gerhard, Modern Computer Algebra, Ch. 6 and 11; fast resultant/gcd Cor. 11.21). Standard textbook facts; not re-verified from the text in this session. — [von zur Gathen & Gerhard, Modern Computer Algebra, 3rd ed.](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139856065)
- Related structural statement (PKM): the constraint x ∈ V is equivalent to L(x) = 0 with L = ∏_{v∈V}(z − v); for V = μ_B this is L = z^B − 1, and PKM decompose it as a chain x ↦ x^{p_j}. — [Amadori–Pintore–Sala §2.2 restating PKM](https://web.archive.org/web/2020id_/https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/609.pdf)

**A.3 Counting points of curves with both coordinates in a subgroup — PROVEN**
- Line / single shift: Garcia–Voloch: for a subgroup R ⊂ F_p^* with |R| < (p−1)/((p−1)^{1/4}+1) and any μ ≠ 0, |R ∩ (R+μ)| ≤ 4|R|^{2/3}; Heath-Brown–Konyagin reproved and generalised this by Stepanov's method and proved |R ± R| ≫ |R|^{3/2} for |R| ≪ p^{2/3}. — [Shkredov–Vyugin, arXiv:1102.1172, Intro eq. (1)–(3)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1102.1172)
- Multiple shifts (Shkredov–Vyugin, Sbornik Math. 2012): Corollary 1.2: for distinct nonzero μ_1,…,μ_k, if 32k·2^{20k log(k+1)} ≤ |R| and p ≥ 4k|R|(|R|^{1/(2k+1)}+1), then |R ∩ (R+μ_1) ∩ … ∩ (R+μ_k)| ≤ 4(k+1)(|R|^{1/(2k+1)}+1)^{k+1}, i.e. ≪_k |R|^{(k+1)/(2k+1)} → |R|^{1/2+o(1)}. Theorem 5.5: if |R| ≪ p^{1/2} then |R ± R| ≫ |R|^{5/3}/log^{1/2}|R|. No improvement of the exponent 2/3 for a single shift is given. — [Shkredov–Vyugin, arXiv:1102.1172, Thm 1.1, Cor 1.2, Thm 5.5](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1102.1172)
- Same bound inside BGKS: Lemma 3: if p ≥ (2m⌊e^{1/(2m+1)}⌋+2m+2)e then #(𝒢_e ∩ (λ_1𝒢_e+μ_1) ∩ … ∩ (λ_m𝒢_e+μ_m)) ≪ e^{(m+1)/(2m+1)} (𝒢_e = subgroup of order e, i.e. μ_e). — [Bourgain–Garaev–Konyagin–Shparlinski, arXiv:1110.0812, Lemma 3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1110.0812)
- General curve, both coordinates in subgroups (Corvaja–Zannier, JEMS 2013, in the form of Karpinski–Mérai–Shparlinski): for F ∈ F̄_p[X,Y] of degree d not of the form αX^mY^n + β or αX^m + βY^n, and multiplicative subgroups G, H with W = #G·#H: N_F(G,H) ≪ max{d²W/p, d^{4/3}W^{1/3}}. — [Mérai–Shparlinski, arXiv:1803.02165, Lemma 3.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1803.02165); original: [Corvaja–Zannier, JEMS 15 (2013) 1927–1942](https://ems.press/journals/jems/articles/10688)
- Interval × subgroup (Mérai–Shparlinski Thm 1.3): for F with total degree d, local degrees d_X, d_Y ≥ 1, F(X,Y^n) irreducible for all n, I = [1,H], G a subgroup of order e: N_F(I,G) ≪ d_X^{1/2} H^{1/2} max{d e p^{−1/2}, d^{2/3} e^{1/3}, d_X^{1/2} d_Y^2}. — [Mérai–Shparlinski, arXiv:1803.02165, Thm 1.3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1803.02165)
- Intervals inside cosets of small subgroups (BGKS Lemmas 36–37): if 𝒜 = {x+s : 1 ≤ x ≤ h} ⊆ r𝒢_e with e < p^δ then h = O(e^{c_0 δ}); if 𝒜 ⊆ 𝒢_e then h = O(e^{c_0 δ^{1/3}}). — [BGKS, arXiv:1110.0812, Lemmas 36–37](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1110.0812)
- None of these papers gives an algorithm for finding the points; all are counting bounds. — [Mérai–Shparlinski abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02165)

**A.4 Hidden shifted power problem (BGKS, SIAM J. Comput. 41(6) 2012) — PROVEN (deterministic/probabilistic algorithms, with complexities)**
- Problem: p prime, e | p−1, oracle 𝒪_{e,s}(x) = (x+s)^e; recover s. Baseline: e+1 queries + interpolation, time e(log p)^{O(1)}. — [BGKS §1.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1110.0812)
- Theorem 38: e ≤ p^{1−δ}, known candidate set S_0 ∋ s with #S_0 ≤ e: deterministic, O(1) queries, time e^{1+ε}(log p)^{O(1)}. Corollary 39: given ℓ-th power nonresidues for all primes ℓ | e, same bounds without S_0 (one query at x = 0 gives s^e; Lemma 8 finds all e roots of x^e = A in e(log p)^{O(1)}). Corollary 40: e ≤ p^{1−δ}, no assumptions: deterministic, O(1) queries, time O(e·p^ε). Corollary 41 (ERH): e^{1+ε}(log p)^{O(1)}. Theorem 42 (probabilistic): e ≤ p^{1−δ}, O(1) expected queries, expected time e(log p)^{O(1)}, failure ≤ 1/p. Theorem 43 (any e | p−1): O(log p / log(p/e)) queries, time p(log p)^{O(1)}. Corollary 44: with nonresidues, O(log p/log(p/e)) queries and e^{1+ε}(log p)^{O(1)} time. — [BGKS §3.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1110.0812)
- Identity testing s = t with t known: Theorem 45: e ≤ p^{1−δ}: deterministic, time e^{1/4+o(1)}(log p)^{O(1)}; Theorem 46: e ≤ (p−1)/2: time p^{1/4+o(1)}; Theorem 47: e ≤ p^δ: time e^{c_0 δ}(log p)^{O(1)}. — [BGKS §3.2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1110.0812)
- Identity testing with t unknown (two oracles): Theorem 48: time p^{1/2+o(1)}; Theorem 49: time max{e^{1/2}p^{o(1)}, e^2 p^{−1+o(1)}}; combined T_p(e) = p^{o(1)}·{e^{1/2} (e < p^{2/3}); e^2/p (p^{2/3} ≤ e < p^{3/4}); p^{1/2} (e ≥ p^{3/4})} ≤ e^{2/3}p^{o(1)}; Theorem 50: e ≤ p^δ: e^{c_0 δ^{1/3}}(log p)^{O(1)}. — [BGKS §3.3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1110.0812)
- Tools: Lemma 1 (Cilleruelo–Garaev): #{(x,y): (x+u)(y+u) ≡ v mod p, 1 ≤ x,y ≤ H} ≤ H^{3/2}p^{−1/2} + H^{o(1)}; Lemma 32: for h < p^{1/max(ν²−1,1)}, the number of solutions of (x_1+s)…(x_ν+s) ≡ λ with 1 ≤ x_i ≤ h is < exp(c(ν) log h/log log h); Lemmas 22–24 use a quantitative Nullstellensatz (Krick–Pardo–Sombra) and Chang's lemma. Open Question 51 asks for p^{o(1)}-query deterministic recovery for general dense 𝒜. — [BGKS §2, §4](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1110.0812)
- Follow-ups: Ivanyos–Karpinski–Santha–Saxena–Shparlinski extended [BGKS] "to arbitrary (non linear) polynomials" over prime fields; for fixed degree d and e = p^{o(1)}, "one can test whether f = g in time e^{o(1)}(log p)^{O(1)} in e^{o(1)} oracle calls"; Shparlinski separately improved the naive algorithm; the naive method uses e·d+1 oracle calls. — [arXiv:1907.02302 (intro)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.02302)

**A.5 Cyclotomic numbers of order e and their computation — PROVEN (identities) / GAP (fast algorithms)**
- Cyclotomic numbers (a,b)_e and Jacobi sums J_e(i,j) determine each other by the finite Fourier inversion e²(a,b)_e = Σ_{i,j} ζ^{−(ai+bj)} J_e(i,j) (eq. (1.2) there); Dickson settled e ≤ 6, 8, 10, 12, with later corrections (Baumert–Fredrickson for 9, 18; Muskat for 12, 14); "cyclotomic numbers of order e over F_q … in general, cannot be determined only in terms of p and e" — a quadratic partition of q is also needed. — [arXiv:1908.04263 (Jacobi sums of order l², 2l²)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.04263); [arXiv:1807.07708](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.07708)
- Gross–Koblitz expresses Gauss sums as products of Morita p-adic Gamma values; used e.g. by Wu–Wang for determinants of Jacobi-sum "cyclotomic matrices" (p − 1 = kn). — [arXiv:2409.13307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13307v3); [arXiv:2502.01109](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01109v2)
- Costa–Kedlaya–Roe compute hypergeometric traces mod p^e for all p ≤ X in time quasilinear in X by incorporating "the p-adic Gamma function" into Harvey's average-polynomial-time framework (so per-prime cost is polylog on average, but the method is amortised over many primes, not sublinear for a single p). — [Costa–Kedlaya–Roe II, arXiv:2310.06971](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.06971); [ANTS XIV paper](https://msp.org/obs/2020/4-1/p10.xhtml)
- Factorials/Gauss-sum products for a single prime: Costa–Gerbicz–Harvey state that for a single (p−1)! mod p² the best known bound is Strassen-type p^{1/2+ε} ("essentially due to Strassen", improvable by a log factor via Bostan–Gaudry–Schost), while their accumulating remainder tree gives all Wilson quotients for p ≤ N in N log^{3+ε} N (Theorem 1), and Theorem 2 gives the window version in time M log^{2+ε}M + (N−M+√N) log^{3+ε}N, space O(N−M); in the proof of their Theorem 10 they compute γ = ∏_{c coprime to e} g(ω^{c^{−1}})^c (a product of Gauss-sum-type quantities) mod p² with O(e²) arithmetic operations. — [Costa–Gerbicz–Harvey, arXiv:1209.3436](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1209.3436)
- Bostan–Gaudry–Schost: the N-th term of a linear recurrence with polynomial coefficients (hence N! mod p) in time Õ(√N) by baby-step/giant-step on polynomial matrices (Chudnovsky–Chudnovsky improved). — [Bostan–Gaudry–Schost, "Linear recurrences with polynomial coefficients…" (SIAM J. Comput. 2007)](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~eschost/publications/cartier.pdf)

**A.6 Hardness-type statements relevant to restricted-root PDP**
- Kosters–Yeo, Theorem 3.5(i): under Assumption 3.3 (one can construct F_q and E/F_q with a point of order ≥ n in time poly(log n)), it is NP-complete, given F_q, E in Weierstrass form, r ≥ 3 and x_0,…,x_{r−1} ∈ F_q, to decide whether S_{A,r}(x_0,…,x_{r−1}) = 0; Theorem 3.5(ii) is unconditional for the cuspidal curve y² = x³ over F_{p^n}, p ≥ 3; Remark 3.7: this "does not imply that ECDLP is NP-complete". Inputs are individual field elements, not sets V_i. — [Kosters–Yeo, arXiv:1503.08001](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1503.08001)
- No hardness result or reduction for "does f(x_1,x_2)=0 have a point in H×H" was found (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- The subgroup factor base (Idea A) is not new: PKM 2016 §3.3 is exactly V = subgroup of order ≈ p^{1/m} (or a coset), with the p_j-th-power chain as the "decomposition map"; what is new in Idea A is only the proposed solver (norm/resultant trick instead of Gröbner bases). Their cost formula shows the target: the restricted-root problem must be solved in time T < p^{1/2−1/m} per instance (with m!·p^{1/m} instances) to beat rho. [inference from PKM §3.2]
- The norm trick alone does not obviously give sublinear time in |H| = B for the curve problem: for fixed x_1 ∈ H, gcd(S_{m+1}(x_1,x_2,·), x_2^B−1) costs Õ(2^{m−1} log B), so scanning x_1 ∈ H is Õ(B·2^{m−1} log B), linear in |H|; eliminating x_2 first via Res_{x_2}(S, x_2^B − 1) gives a univariate polynomial in x_1 of degree ≈ 2^{m−1}B, again linear in B. [inference; the structural identity Res(X^B−1,(X+a)^B−1) = a^B∏_{u∈μ_e}(a^B−u)^{r_u} in the brief was not found in any source — treat as unverified]
- The only sublinear-in-|H| algorithms found are BGKS's identity tests (e^{1/4+o(1)}, e^{2/3+o(1)}) for the very rigid "shifted e-th power" structure; they rely on additive-combinatorial product-set estimates for intervals (Lemmas 32–34) and the coset-intersection bound Lemma 3, not on polynomial arithmetic. They are evidence that structure in H can be exploited sublinearly, but do not transfer directly to S_{m+1} = 0.
- The counting bounds (A.3) say a degree-d curve has at most ≈ d^{4/3}|H|^{2/3} points in H×H (Corvaja–Zannier) when |H|² ≪ p; for the PDP one needs |V|^m ≈ p, i.e. |H| = p^{1/m} with m ≥ 2, so for m = 2 the regime |H|² ≈ p is exactly at the edge where the bound degenerates to d²W/p ≈ d² (consistent with the expected O(1) decompositions per point). For m ≥ 3 the relevant object is a hypersurface in H^m, for which no analogous sparsity bound was found.
- Cyclotomic numbers: computing all e² values exactly from Gauss sums via Gross–Koblitz needs p-adic Gamma values to precision beyond mod p (Gauss sums vanish mod the prime above p), which in the single-prime setting costs factorial-type products of length ≈ p (Õ(√p) each via Bostan–Gaudry–Schost); I found no source claiming all cyclotomic numbers of order e in time sublinear in p for a single prime. [inference; see Gaps]

### Gaps
- No source found that states or refutes an algorithm sublinear in |H| for "does f(x,y)=0 have a point in H×H" for general low-degree f; no reduction from ECDLP/DLP to this problem, and no NP-hardness statement for the subgroup-restricted version (Kosters–Yeo's input is a single tuple, not a set).
- The identity Res(X^B − 1, (X+a)^B − 1) = a^B∏_{u∈μ_e}(a^B − u)^{r_u} (with e = gcd-structure exponents r_u) was not located in the literature; the search on cyclotomic numbers returned only the Fourier-inversion relation and order-by-order evaluations (e ≤ 24 and prime-power orders).
- No paper applying BGKS-style additive combinatorics to Semaev polynomials was found.
- Shparlinski's and Cilleruelo–Garaev's specific papers on #{x ∈ H : x+a ∈ H} beyond the Garcia–Voloch/Heath-Brown–Konyagin/Shkredov–Vyugin chain (e.g. Shkredov's later single-shift improvements) could not be retrieved; the ar5iv text of arXiv:1102.1172 explicitly gives no improvement over the exponent 2/3 for one shift.
- The "Wu–Wang" and Costa–Kedlaya–Roe papers establish only that Gross–Koblitz / p-adic Gamma are computationally usable; no explicit complexity for computing a single Jacobi sum of large order e over F_p in sublinear time was found.

## Idea (B): V = interval [0, p^{1/m}) — multivariate Coppersmith / Jochemsz–May bounds; points of E with x in an interval / small boxes

### Takeaway
Semaev himself (2004) framed prime-field index calculus as "finding bounded solutions to some explicit modular multivariate polynomial equations" with x_i ≤ p^{1/n+δ}, and left the solver open; Galbraith's textbook states flatly that "Coppersmith's method … seems not to be useful for this task". The best general (heuristic) Coppersmith bound for a modular polynomial of degree δ in each of n variables (box/rectangle Newton polytope) is X_1⋯X_n < N^{2/((n+1)δ)} (Jochemsz–May basic strategy), and for total degree δ it is X_1⋯X_n < N^{1/δ}; applied to S_{m+1}(·, x_R) with δ = 2^{m−1} this allows only ∏X_i < p^{2/((m+1)2^{m−1})}, versus the ∏X_i ≈ p needed for the PDP, a gap of many orders in the exponent. Counting results: points of a cubic y² = f(x) in an M×M box number M^{1/3+o(1)} for M < p^{1/8} and the range p^{1/3} < M < p^{1/2} is open; equidistribution results for points with x in an interval (Kohel–Shparlinski, Kim–Tibouchi) exist but give no algorithmic leverage.

### Cited Findings

**B.1 Semaev's original proposal and the "bounded solutions" formulation — HEURISTIC (Semaev), FOLKLORE (dismissal)**
- Semaev (eprint 2004/031) abstract: "The construction presented here is based on the problem of finding bounded solutions to some explicit modular multivariate polynomial equations … given the algorithm for solving such equations, which works in polynomial or low exponential time in the size of the input, one finds discrete logarithms faster than by means of Pollard's methods." Section 3: solve f_{n+1}(x_1,…,x_n,x) ≡ 0 (mod p) with "x_i^0 … integer numbers bounded by p^{1/n+δ} for some small δ > 0 or x_i^0 … rational numbers the numerator and the denominator of which are bounded by p^{1/(2n)+δ}"; "One needs about p^{1/n+δ} nontrivial solutions"; if the bounded-solution finder costs t_{p,n}, total "t_{p,n} p^{1/n+δ} + p^{2/n+2δ} operations. When n ≥ 5, even for some exponential t_{p,n}, this amount may be less than O(p^{1/2})"; "The exciting question arising here is whether or not it is true for (5) and (7)?" — [Semaev, eprint 2004/031 (Wayback copy), Abstract and §3](https://web.archive.org/web/2020id_/https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/031.pdf)
- Galbraith, *Mathematics of Public Key Cryptography*, §15.8.1: factor base B = {(x,y) ∈ E(F_p): 0 ≤ x ≤ p^{1/n}}, decomposition = solving Summ_{n+1}(x_1,…,x_n,x_R) ≡ 0 (mod p) with 0 ≤ x_i ≤ p^{1/n} (eq. 15.12); "the expected number of points R that have to be selected before a relation is obtained is about n!"; "Unfortunately, no efficient algorithm is known for solving the polynomial equation (15.12) even for n = 5 (in which case the equation has degree 16 in each of its 5 variables). Coppersmith's method (see Section 19.2) seems not to be useful for this task." Theorem 15.8.2: Summ_n is symmetric and has degree 2^{n−2} in each x_i. — [Galbraith, Ch. 15 (author's PDF)](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/crypto-book/ch15.pdf)

**B.2 Coppersmith-type bounds — exact statements**
- Univariate modular (PROVEN): May's survey Theorem 1: N with divisor b ≥ N^β, f monic of degree δ, c ≥ 1: all x_0 with f(x_0) ≡ 0 mod b, |x_0| ≤ c N^{β²/δ} in time O(cδ^5 log^9 N); Theorem 4 (Coppersmith, b = N): |x_0| ≤ N^{1/δ} in O(δ^5 log^9 N); Theorem 3 gives the ε-version with lattice dimension O(ε^{−1}δ). — [May, "Using LLL-reduction for solving RSA and factorization problems" (Wayback copy), Thms 1, 3, 4](https://web.archive.org/web/2019id_/https://www.cits.ruhr-uni-bochum.de/imperia/md/content/may/paper/lll.pdf)
- Multivariate modular is heuristic: "Coppersmith's method generalizes in a natural way to modular multivariate polynomials … For ℓ ≥ 2, this is a heuristic method because although the LLL-algorithm guarantees linear independence of the coefficient vectors, it does not guarantee algebraic independence of the corresponding polynomials"; bivariate integer is rigorous, ≥ 3 variables integer is heuristic; Coron reduced integer to modular. — [May survey §2](https://web.archive.org/web/2019id_/https://www.cits.ruhr-uni-bochum.de/imperia/md/content/may/paper/lll.pdf)
- Bivariate modular, total degree d (HEURISTIC): Galbraith Theorem 19.2.1: if XY < M^{1/d−ε} one computes in time poly(log M, 1/ε) polynomials F_1, F_2 with F_1(x_0,y_0) = F_2(x_0,y_0) = 0 over Z for every root with |x_0| < X, |y_0| < Y (independence of F_1, F_2 not guaranteed — "heuristic method in general"). — [Galbraith, Ch. 19 §19.2](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/crypto-book/ch19.pdf)
- Bivariate integer, degree d in each variable separately (PROVEN, Coppersmith/Coron): Galbraith Theorem 19.3.1: W = max|F_{ij}|X^iY^j; if XY < W^{2/(3d)} all roots with |x_0| ≤ X, |y_0| ≤ Y are found in time poly(log W, 2^d); Coron's version works for XY < W^{2/(3d)−1/k}2^{−9d}. — [Galbraith, Ch. 19 §19.3](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/crypto-book/ch19.pdf)
- Jochemsz–May (ASIACRYPT 2006) general strategy (HEURISTIC under their Assumption 1): Lemma 1 (Howgrave-Graham, n variables): if h has ≤ ω monomials, h(x^{(0)}) ≡ 0 mod N with |x_i^{(0)}| < X_i and ||h(x_1X_1,…,x_nX_n)|| < N/√ω, then h(x^{(0)}) = 0 over Z. Basic strategy (modular): monomial sets M_k := {monomials of f_N^m whose quotient by l^k is a monomial of f_N^{m−k}} (l = leading monomial), shifts g = (x^i/l^k)·(f'_N)^k N^{m−k}; the determinant condition det(L) < N^{m(ω+1−n)} reduces to "∏_{j=1}^n X_j^{s_j} < N^{s_N}, for s_j = Σ_{x^i∈M_0} i_j and s_N = Σ_{k=1}^m |M_k|" (their (1)); integer version (their (2)): ∏ X_j^{s_j} < W^{s_W}, s_j = Σ_{x^i ∈ M∖S} i_j, s_W = |S| with S, M the monomials of f^{m−1}, f^m. "Assumption 1: The resultant computations for the polynomials h_i yield non-zero polynomials." Extended strategy adds extra shifts x_1^j, 0 ≤ j ≤ t = τm. — [Jochemsz–May 2006 (Wayback copy), §2.1–2.2](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://www.iacr.org/archive/asiacrypt2006/42840270/42840270.pdf)
- Jochemsz–May Appendix A (modular, known shapes reproduced by the basic strategy): "Generalized Rectangle (generalization of a bound of Coppersmith): f_N(x_1,…,x_n) is a polynomial such that the degree of x_i is λ_iD. The bound X_1^{λ_1}⋯X_n^{λ_n} < N^{2/((n+1)D)} can be obtained with the basic strategy using x^i ∈ M_k ⇔ i_j = λ_jDk,…,λ_jDm"; "Generalized Lower Triangle: … The bound X_1^{λ_1}⋯X_n^{λ_n} < N^{1/D}"; Boneh–Durfee: X_1^{2+3τ}X_2^{1+3τ+3τ²} < N^{1+3τ}; Blömer–May (3 vars): X_1^{1+4τ}X_2^{2+4τ}X_3^{1+4τ+6τ²} < N^{1+4τ}. Appendix B gives the integer analogues with W in place of N (e.g. rectangle: ∏X_j^{λ_j} < W^{2/((n+1)D)}). — [Jochemsz–May 2006, Appendices A–B](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://www.iacr.org/archive/asiacrypt2006/42840270/42840270.pdf)
- Newton-polytope formulation: Feng–Nitaj–Pan (eprint 2024/1330) show the Jochemsz–May bound "can be obtained by calculating the leading coefficient of some Hilbert function, which is exactly the volume of the corresponding Newton polytope", extending Meers–Nowakowski's "Automated Coppersmith" (ASIACRYPT 2023) to systems. — [eprint 2024/1330](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1330)
- Blömer–May, "A Tool Kit for Finding Small Roots of Bivariate Polynomials over the Integers", EUROCRYPT 2005, LNCS 3494, pp. 251–267 (shape-dependent bounds: Upper Triangle X_1^{(λ+τ)²}X_2^{2(λ+τ)} < W^{(λ+2τ)/D}; Extended Rectangle, as restated in Jochemsz–May App. B). — [Blömer–May record](https://ris.uni-paderborn.de/record/3006); [JM App. B](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://www.iacr.org/archive/asiacrypt2006/42840270/42840270.pdf)

### Inferences
- Applying the Jochemsz–May "generalized rectangle" bound to the PDP: S_{m+1}(x_1,…,x_m,x_R) with x_R fixed is a modular polynomial in n = m variables of degree δ = 2^{m−1} in each (box-shaped monomial support up to sparsity), so the basic strategy requires ∏_{i=1}^m X_i < p^{2/((m+1)2^{m−1})}; the PDP needs X_i = p^{1/m}, i.e. ∏X_i = p. For m = 2: p^{1/3} vs p; m = 3: p^{1/8} vs p; m = 5: p^{1/48} vs p. Even the (unreachable) total-degree-type bound ∏X_i < p^{1/δ_tot} is far below p. Hence Coppersmith-style lattice methods cannot reach the PDP regime by orders of magnitude in the exponent; this matches Galbraith's statement. [inference from JM App. A and Galbraith §15.8.1 — HEURISTIC bounds, FOLKLORE conclusion]
- The information-theoretic reason: a single equation mod p over a box of volume ∏X_i has ≈ ∏X_i/p expected solutions; Coppersmith only finds roots when they are "unique and forced" (∏X_i ≪ p^{c}, c ≤ 1/δ), whereas the PDP sits at ∏X_i ≈ p where solutions exist only with probability ≈ 1/m!. [inference]
- The box-counting theorems (B.4) are about M×M boxes with M ≤ p^{1/3}; the PDP's relevant range for m = 2 is M = p^{1/2}, exactly inside the open range p^{1/3} < M < p^{1/2} where no nontrivial bound is known; for m ≥ 3 the relevant sets are products of m intervals of length p^{1/m} and no counting results beyond Weil-type main terms were found.
- The Cilleruelo–Garaev bound H^{3/2}p^{−1/2} for the hyperbola (an m = 2, degree-(1,1) analogue) shows that at H = p^{1/2} the number of box points is ≈ H^{3/2}p^{−1/2} = p^{1/4}, consistent with "few decompositions" rather than many. [inference]

## Idea (C): Combinatorial / generic-group approaches — Wagner k-list, MITM decomposition and the p^{1/2+1/m} folklore bound, Shoup/Nechaev/Maurer–Wolf generic lower bounds

### Takeaway
Wagner's own long version states precisely when the k-tree algorithm applies: whenever the group has a chain of lg k normal subgroups {e} = H_0 ⊂ … ⊂ H_{1+lg k} = G with |H_j| ≈ |G|^{j/(1+lg k)} and efficiently computable quotient maps G → G/H_j (for (Z/mZ,+) one uses interval joins instead); and it proves (Theorem 2, due to Wei Dai, and Corollary 1) that a k-sum solver in a cyclic group gives a DL solver in the same time, so every generic k-sum algorithm needs Ω(√p) — the k-list approach provably fails in a generic prime-order group such as E(F_p). The folklore cost of prime-field index calculus with MITM decomposition, ≈ m!·p^{1/2+1/m}, follows directly from Semaev's own formula t_{p,n}p^{1/n+δ} + p^{2/n+2δ} with t = p^{1/2}, and from PKM's formula; PKM state the break-even threshold T < p^{1/2−1/m}. Shoup's bound (success probability O(m²/ℓ) after m group operations) and Maurer's remark that index calculus "exploit[s] that the elements are integers that can be factored into primes" (hence is non-generic) are the standard generic-model statements.

### Cited Findings

**C.1 Wagner's k-tree algorithm and its group requirements — PROVEN (complexities), explicit statements**
- Complexity: the k-tree algorithm (k a power of 2) "requires O(k·2^{n/(1+lg k)}) time and space and uses lists of size O(2^{n/(1+lg k)})"; k = 4 gives O(2^{n/3}); for k not a power of two one uses k' = 2^{⌊lg k⌋} ("runs essentially no faster for k = 2^i + j than for k = 2^i"); unrestricted k gives O(2^{2√n}). — [Wagner, "A Generalized Birthday Problem", long version §2](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~daw/papers/genbday-long.ps)
- "Operations other than xor. The k-sum problem has so far been described over the group (GF(2)^n, ⊕), but it is natural to wonder whether these techniques will apply over other groups as well. We note first that the tree algorithm above transfers immediately to the additive group (Z/2^nZ, +) … The reason this works is that a ≡ b (mod 2^ℓ) implies (a + c mod 2^n) ≡ (b + c mod 2^n) (mod 2^ℓ): the carry bit propagates in only one direction. We can also apply the tree algorithm to the group (Z/mZ, +) where m is arbitrary" using interval joins L_1 ⋈_{[a,b]} L_2 with [a,b] = [−m/2^{ℓ+1}, m/2^{ℓ+1} − 1], ℓ = (1/3) lg m. "More generally, the 4-list tree algorithm will apply whenever we have a group (G, ×) with an efficiently computable homomorphism ρ: G → G/H whose kernel ker ρ = H forms a normal subgroup of G of size |H| ≈ |G|^{1/3} … Similarly, the k-list tree algorithm applies to group G whenever we have a chain of lg k normal subgroups {e} = H_0 ⊆ H_1 ⊆ ⋯ ⊆ H_{1+lg k} = G such that |H_j| ≈ |G|^{j/(1+lg k)} along with efficiently computable homomorphisms ρ_j: G → G/H_j with kernel ker ρ_j = H_j for each j." — [Wagner long version, §2 "Operations other than xor"](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~daw/papers/genbday-long.ps)
- Lower bounds (§3): Theorem 1: "The computational complexity of the k-sum problem is Ω(2^{n/k})" (information-theoretic, all groups). "Theorem 2 (W. Dai). If the k-sum problem over a cyclic group G = ⟨g⟩ can be solved in time t, then the discrete logarithm with respect to g can be found in O(t) time as well." "Corollary 1. Every generic algorithm for the k-sum problem in a group G has running time Ω(√p), where p denotes the largest prime factor of the order of G." (citing Shoup and Nechaev). "Moreover, Theorem 2 shows that we cannot hope to find a polynomial-time algorithm for the k-sum problem over any group where the discrete log problem is hard. For example, finding a solution to x_1 × ⋯ × x_k ≡ 1 (mod p) is as hard as taking discrete logarithms in (Z/pZ)^*". Theorem 3 (converse): if DL in G = ⟨g⟩ of order m takes time t and k-sum over (Z/mZ,+) with lists of size ℓ takes t', then k-sum over G takes t' + kℓt. — [Wagner long version, §3](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~daw/papers/genbday-long.ps)
- Open problems (§5): "is there any group operation + where we can find solutions to x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 0 more efficiently than a naive square-root birthday search?" — [Wagner long version, §5](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~daw/papers/genbday-long.ps)
- Minder–Sinclair, "The extended k-tree algorithm", J. Cryptology 25(2) 2012 (also SODA 2009): interpolates between the classical time/space tradeoff and Wagner's k-tree for intermediate list sizes (XOR setting); no statement about generic groups was retrieved. — [SIAM/SODA record](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/1.9781611973068.65)
- Joux–Kippen–Loss (eprint 2024/282) run a "degraded" k-list algorithm in a 256-bit prime field Z_q with k = 8 (ROS-type problems), i.e. in (Z/qZ,+) where Wagner's interval joins apply. — [eprint 2024/282](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/282)
- Dinur's framework: k-tree time/memory ≈ N^{1/(⌊log K⌋+1)}; Nikolić–Sasaki tradeoff T²·M^{lg k − 1} = k·N. — [Dinur, "An algorithmic framework for the generalized birthday problem" (DCC 2019)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/312)

### Inferences
- Why Wagner fails for PDP in E(F_p): E(F_p) of prime order has no nontrivial subgroup chain, and even in the abstract cyclic group Z/ℓ no efficiently computable "interval" or quotient map on points exists without discrete logs; Wagner's Theorem 2/Corollary 1 make this rigorous: any generic k-list solver in E(F_p)[ℓ] would solve DL in O(t), so t = Ω(√ℓ). The ROS-type uses (Joux–Kippen–Loss) work only because the lists live in (Z/qZ,+) with its integer ordering, i.e. after the DLs are known (Wagner's Theorem 3). [inference from cited Wagner statements]
- The p^{1/2+1/m} folklore: a MITM decomposition of R over F with |F| = p^{1/m} stores ∑_{i≤m/2}P_i (p^{1/2} elements, once) and matches R − ∑_{i>m/2}P_i (p^{1/2} per R), so T = p^{1/2} per trial; with m! trials per relation and p^{1/m} relations the index-calculus cost is ≈ m!·p^{1/2+1/m} (plus p^{ω/m} linear algebra). This is exactly Semaev's "t_{p,n}p^{1/n+δ}" with t = p^{1/2} and PKM's formula with T = p^{1/2}; it is always worse than rho, which is why PKM phrase the goal as T < p^{1/2−1/m}. No source states "p^{1/2+1/m}" verbatim; Semaev 2004, PKM 2016 §3.2 and Galbraith §15.8.1 together imply it. [FOLKLORE/inference]
- Bernstein–Lange-style precomputation (ℓ^{1/3} per DL after ℓ^{2/3} precomputation) does not change the picture for PDP, because each decomposition is a fresh k-sum instance, not a DL in a fixed group with reusable tables; and precomputation-based methods are explicitly non-generic only in the sense of exploiting a shared table, not group structure. [inference]
- Any PDP algorithm that beats p^{1/2−1/m} must be non-generic in Shoup's sense (it must read the x-coordinates as field elements), consistent with Galbraith–Gaudry's remark that decomposition "is the place where it is always necessary to use the structure of F".

### Gaps
- Shoup's 1997 PDF text could not be extracted (font encoding); the exact theorem statement (Theorem 1: success probability O(m²/p)) is quoted only via Bernstein–Lange and Wagner.
- Maurer–Wolf's papers were not fetched; only bibliographic data and secondary descriptions are available. No source was found that states, in a generic model, a formal "index calculus is impossible for generic groups" theorem beyond Maurer's informal remark and the structured-GGM results.
- Minder–Sinclair and Bernstein–Lange do not contain (in retrieved text) an explicit statement that Wagner "fails in a generic group"; the explicit statement is Wagner's own Theorem 2/Corollary 1.
- Galbraith–Ruprai (DLP in an interval using equivalence classes) and lecture notes stating p^{1/2+1/m} verbatim were not found.

## Cross-cutting: has anyone already proposed or refuted ideas (A), (B), (C) for ECDLP?

### Takeaway
(A) proposed and implemented (Gröbner-based) by Petit–Kosters–Messeng 2016, with no asymptotic analysis and an explicit call for a dedicated algorithm; not refuted. (B) proposed by Semaev 2004; dismissed as a Coppersmith target by Galbraith's textbook; no positive result exists. (C) refuted at the level of generic algorithms by Wagner's Theorem 2/Corollary 1 and Shoup's bound; the MITM/BSGS version is implicitly the baseline that all prime-field index-calculus proposals must beat (PKM's p^{1/2−1/m} threshold).

### Cited Findings
- (A) proposed: PKM 2016 §3.3 (subgroup of smooth order ≈ p^{1/m}, or a coset). — [PKM 2016](https://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/16PKC_primeECDLP.pdf)
- (A) status: "not practical at the moment and we do not know their asymptotic complexity" (PKM §5); APS 2018 "We do not claim this result" (beating p^{1/2}). — [PKM 2016](https://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/16PKC_primeECDLP.pdf); [APS eprint 2017/609](https://web.archive.org/web/2020id_/https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/609.pdf)
- (B) proposed: Semaev 2004 (x_i ≤ p^{1/n+δ}); dismissed: Galbraith §15.8.1 ("Coppersmith's method … seems not to be useful"); GG survey: "no known efficient decomposition algorithm for that choice of F". — [Semaev 2004](https://web.archive.org/web/2020id_/https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/031.pdf); [Galbraith Ch. 15](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/crypto-book/ch15.pdf); [GG survey](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/1022.pdf)
- (C) refuted generically: Wagner long version Theorem 2 and Corollary 1 (Ω(√p) for any generic k-sum algorithm). — [Wagner long version](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~daw/papers/genbday-long.ps)
- Hardness context for all three: Kosters–Yeo NP-completeness of the vanishing problem for S_r on arbitrary inputs (Theorem 3.5), with the caveat that this says nothing about ECDLP itself (Remark 3.7). — [arXiv:1503.08001](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1503.08001)

### Inferences
- The unexplored piece in (A) is precisely PKM's "Problem 1" with L = z^B − 1 (or its coset version): any algorithm for the generalized root-finding problem with cost T < p^{1/2−1/m} would, by PKM's formula, beat rho. Nothing in the retrieved literature rules this out, and nothing achieves it.

### Gaps
- No source evaluates the "norm trick"/resultant elimination as a PDP solver for subgroup factor bases; no source gives lower bounds for the restricted-root problem in the algebraic (non-generic) model.
