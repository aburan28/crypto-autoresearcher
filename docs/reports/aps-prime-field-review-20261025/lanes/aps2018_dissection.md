## Key Question 1: Exact algorithm — factor base, polynomial system, solver, and how one relation yields the log

### Takeaway
The factor base is NOT fixed in advance: it is the set of s random points R_j = u_j P + v_j Q themselves (so every factor-base point has a known "log coordinate" (u_j, v_j)); the only algebraic step is to find ONE solution (x_1,…,x_m) ∈ V_1×…×V_m of S_m(X_1,…,X_m)=0, where V_i are the x-coordinates of a disjoint m-part partition of the factor base, by computing a grevlex Gröbner basis in Magma of {S_m, f_1(X_1),…,f_m(X_m)} with f_i(X_i)=∏_{v∈V_i}(X_i−v); the resulting relation ±R_{j_1} ± … ± R_{j_m} = ∞ gives λP+µQ=∞ and w = −λ/µ mod r.

### Cited Findings
- Setting (Sec. 3, AM p.6): "Let us consider an elliptic curve E defined over a finite field F_q (where q is a prime or a prime-power) and having a prime number of rational points, i.e. |E(F_q)| = r and r prime integer. With P and Q we denote two points of E(F_q) such that Q = wP." — [AM p.6](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/e3835194-60e8-72ef-e053-3705fe0ad821/Amadori%2c%20Pintore%2c%20Sala%20-%20On%20DLP%20for%20prime-field%20EC%20%28rev%29.pdf)
- Relation collection step (Sec. 3, AM p.6), verbatim: "1. The factor base F is not fixed, rather it is initialized as the empty set. The final size s of F is fixed together with an integer m, named decomposition constant. As usual (see for example [17, pag.7]) we take s^m ≈ q. 2. For random integers u, v, the point R = uP + vQ is computed and added to the factor base F. 3. R does not need to be written as a sum of multiples of points in F, since R itself is a point of F. 4. The procedure in item 2 is repeated until at least s different points are added to F." — [AM p.6](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/e3835194-60e8-72ef-e053-3705fe0ad821/Amadori%2c%20Pintore%2c%20Sala%20-%20On%20DLP%20for%20prime-field%20EC%20%28rev%29.pdf)
- Relation solving step (Sec. 3, AM p.6), verbatim: "1. A linear dependency among points in F is determined using summation polynomials instead of linear algebra. (7) If the equation has not suitable solutions, then we come back to the relation collection step, in order to create a new factor base F. Otherwise, if an appropriate solution has been found, it is trivial to determine m points P_i such that −P_i ∈ F or P_i ∈ F, and P_1 + · · · + P_m = ∞. (8) Hence, the linear congruence λP + µQ = ∞, with λ, µ ∈ Z, is deduced. 2. The discrete logarithm w of Q with respect to P is recovered solving the modular equation λ + µw = 0 (mod r)." — [AM p.6](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/e3835194-60e8-72ef-e053-3705fe0ad821/Amadori%2c%20Pintore%2c%20Sala%20-%20On%20DLP%20for%20prime-field%20EC%20%28rev%29.pdf)

### Inferences
- Reconstruction of system (11) with degrees (inference from Theorem 2 and (10)): m unknowns X_1..X_m; S_m has degree 2^{m−2} in each X_i (S_3: degree 2 in each variable, total degree 4; S_4: degree 4 in each variable); each f_i(X_i) is a dense univariate polynomial of degree |V_i| ≈ s/m ≈ q^{1/m}/m, which is also the Bézout-type bound on the number of candidate x_i per variable. For the m=4 experimental variant there are 5 unknowns (X_1..X_4, Y) with two bi-degree-(2,2,2) S_3's; for m=5, 6 unknowns with one S_3 and one S_4 (degree 4 in each of X_3,X_4,X_5,Y).
- Note the index shift versus the standard method: APS's m counts ALL points in the relation (P_1+…+P_m = ∞ uses S_m), whereas the standard/PKM method with m factor-base points plus R uses S_{m+1}. Thus APS "m=3" (S_3 in three unknowns, with three univariate constraints) is the degree-analogue of a standard "m=2" decomposition R = P_1+P_2 (S_3(X_1,X_2,x(R)) with two unknowns).
- How the log is read off (inference, the paper only says "trivial"): each factor-base point is R_j = u_j P + v_j Q with known (u_j,v_j); a solution (x_1,…,x_m) identifies R_{j_i} ∈ F_i with x(R_{j_i}) = x_i; choosing signs ε_i ∈ {±1} with Σ ε_i R_{j_i} = ∞ gives λ = Σ ε_i u_{j_i}, µ = Σ ε_i v_{j_i}. Because the V_i are disjoint, x_i ≠ x_j for i≠j, so no cancelling pair P_i = −P_j can occur.
- Since the factor base has no algebraic structure, the univariate constraints f_i are the whole "membership test"; nothing like PKM's low-degree decomposition of L is available, so the system is small in number of variables (m) but has one very high-degree generator per variable (degree ≈ q^{1/m}/m).

### Gaps
- The paper does not describe how solutions are extracted from the Gröbner basis (e.g. whether the ideal is zero-dimensional and solutions are read from a lex/FGLM basis, or whether Magma's Variety is used), nor the variable order, nor the Gröbner algorithm.
- The paper does not state whether points R with the same x-coordinate (R and −R both drawn) are deduplicated beyond "s different points".

## Key Question 2: The "only one relation needed" mechanism, signs, and failure probability

### Takeaway
One relation suffices because every factor-base element already has a known expression u_jP + v_jQ (it was generated that way), so any single vanishing signed sum of m factor-base points is immediately a relation λP+µQ=∞; signs are determined by trial since ±R share an x-coordinate; the paper does not quantify the probability that µ ≡ 0 (mod r), only calling it "extremely unlikely".

### Cited Findings
- Core mechanism (Sec. 1, AM p.3): "At the beginning of our algorithm (item 1 of the relation collection step), we define the size of F but not F itself, which instead will be defined on the run. Indeed, every point R produced in item 2 of the relation collection step is appended to F, giving a trivial decomposition of R in item 3. We proceed in this fashion until F contains the predetermined size. At this point a linear dependency among points in F is obtained by solving a point decomposition problem with summation polynomials. Consequently, our proposal reduces the index calculus algorithm for elliptic curves to the computation of a single linear relation among points of the factor base." — [AM p.3](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/e3835194-60e8-72ef-e053-3705fe0ad821/Amadori%2c%20Pintore%2c%20Sala%20-%20On%20DLP%20for%20prime-field%20EC%20%28rev%29.pdf)
- Three stated differences from the standard algorithm (Sec. 3, AM p.6): "First, F is not fixed deterministically at the beginning of the algorithm. Instead, it is constructed step by step adding the random points R progressively. This prevents the computation of many relations (i.e. resolutions of the PDP), which traditionally requires expensive Grobner basis computations. Second, every element in V corresponds to 2 points on the curve, whereas in previous methods it is heuristically expected that half of the element in V correspond to 2 rational points. Third, in our second step (which is traditionally the linear algebra step) we actually do not execute linear algebra. ... If the considered equation has not a suitable solution, then we change the factor base F and we solve a new equation. Usually more than one attempt should be made to find the single relation among points of the factor base F, but the number of computed Groebner bases is decreased dramatically." — [AM p.6](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/e3835194-60e8-72ef-e053-3705fe0ad821/Amadori%2c%20Pintore%2c%20Sala%20-%20On%20DLP%20for%20prime-field%20EC%20%28rev%29.pdf)
- Signs: solutions give "m points P_i such that −P_i ∈ F or P_i ∈ F, and P_1 + ··· + P_m = ∞ (8)" (AM p.6); the paper offers no further detail on sign resolution. — [AM p.6](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/e3835194-60e8-72ef-e053-3705fe0ad821/Amadori%2c%20Pintore%2c%20Sala%20-%20On%20DLP%20for%20prime-field%20EC%20%28rev%29.pdf)
- Useless-relation case: the only statement is in the generic description (Sec. 1, AM p.2): "w is recovered from the linear congruence λ+µw = 0 (mod r), which is solvable except in the extremely unlikely case when µ = 0", and in Sec. 2.1/3: "if it is solvable" (UNSUPPORTED/unquantified in the paper). — [AM pp.2, 5, 6](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/e3835194-60e8-72ef-e053-3705fe0ad821/Amadori%2c%20Pintore%2c%20Sala%20-%20On%20DLP%20for%20prime-field%20EC%20%28rev%29.pdf)
- Coefficient set: coefficients of the relation are in {−1, 0, +1} by construction (each P_i or −P_i in F_i, one point per part); the paper's generic index-calculus description allows "integers ℓ_F's ranging in a small coefficient set" (eq. (1), AM p.2), but the APS relation (8) uses exactly m points with unit coefficients. — [AM pp.2, 6](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/e3835194-60e8-72ef-e053-3705fe0ad821/Amadori%2c%20Pintore%2c%20Sala%20-%20On%20DLP%20for%20prime-field%20EC%20%28rev%29.pdf)
- A secondary description (Dzierzkowski–Wroński 2024, §3.2.3 "Variant of Amadori et al. [1], [16]") restates the construction as: "The decomposition base is given by B = {±R ∈ E(F_p) : R = [α]P + [β]Q}, for randomly chosen 0 ≤ α, β ≤ n − 1, where n = Ord(P). It is worth noting that both points R and −R are included in the decomposition base. The size of the decomposition base is then #B ≈ p^{1/m}. ... If any solution is found, we can obtain a relation P_1 + ··· + P_m + R = O, where P_1,…,P_m ∈ B. From the construction of the decomposition base, each point P_i is equal to [α_i]P + [β_i]Q for some known α_i, β_i. Therefore, it is possible to find linear congruence [γ]P = [δ]Q and solve ECDLP. In an improved variant, the decomposition base B is partitioned into m partitions with almost the same sizes ... In this case, the size s of the decomposition base should fulfil s ≥ m p^{1/m}." — [Dzierzkowski & Wroński, Tatra Mt. Math. Publ. 87 (2024), pp.74–75](https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.2478/tmmp-2024-0021.pdf)

### Inferences
- Sign resolution costs at most 2^{m−1} point additions per candidate solution (fix the sign of P_1 and try the rest) — trivial relative to the Gröbner basis; this is what "trivial to determine" must mean.
- Probability the single relation is useless: since the v_j are uniform random mod r and r is prime, µ = Σ ε_i v_{j_i} is uniform mod r, so Pr[µ ≡ 0] = 1/r; likewise the relation is non-trivial (λ,µ not both 0) with probability 1−1/r^2 effectively 1. This is my computation, not the paper's.
- A subtler failure mode the paper does not discuss: if the Gröbner computation finds a solution whose sign pattern requires P_i = −P_j for the same underlying point (impossible with disjoint V_i) or a solution in F̄_q \ F_q — the latter cannot occur because f_i forces x_i ∈ V_i ⊂ F_q and every x ∈ V_i is the abscissa of two rational points (the "Second" difference above). Hence every solution of (11) is a genuine relation.
- Dzierzkowski–Wroński's restatement (relation "P_1+…+P_m = R" and a system "f_m(x_P,…,x_m)") does not match APS's actual system (11), where there is no distinguished R and S_m is in m factor-base unknowns only; treat their §3.2.3 as a loose paraphrase.

### Gaps
- No statement in the paper on the expected number of solutions of (11) per success, on multiplicities, or on how many factor-base rebuilds were needed in practice (only total average time per DLP instance is reported).

## Key Question 3: Complexity analysis — formulas, assumptions, comparison with Semaev 2004 and PKM 2016, Pollard rho

### Takeaway
The entire analysis is the counting heuristic: expected number of m-sums ≈ (s/m)^m, success probability per system ≈ s^m/(q·m^m) (eq. 12), total cost (q·m^m/s^m)·T(E,m,V), versus PKM's (p·m!/s^{m−1})·T'(E,m,V)+s^ω; under the explicit unproven assumption T ≈ T' (eq. 13) APS beats PKM once s·m! > m^m, i.e. p^{1/m} > m^m/m! (Table 1: 7/14/24/37/52/70 bits for m = 3..8); no degree-of-regularity, Macaulay-bound or asymptotic exponent is given, T is explicitly unestimated, and the authors explicitly do NOT claim to beat Pollard rho.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Table 1 reproduces exactly from p > (m^m/m!)^m: m=3: (4.5)^3 = 91 → 7 bits; m=4: (10.67)^4 ≈ 1.29·10^4 → 14 bits; m=5: (26.04)^5 ≈ 1.20·10^7 → 24 bits; m=6: (64.8)^6 ≈ 7.4·10^10 → 37 bits; m=7: (163.4)^7 ≈ 3.1·10^15 → 52 bits; m=8: (416.1)^8 ≈ 9.0·10^20 → 70 bits (bit size = ceil(log2)).
- With the design choice s^m ≈ q, the expected number of Gröbner computations in APS is ≈ m^m (27 for m=3, 256 for m=4, 3125 for m=5) independent of q, versus ≈ m!·s ≈ m!·q^{1/m} systems for PKM/Semaev (one PDP per needed relation, s relations needed); the "one relation" saving is thus a factor ≈ m!·q^{1/m}/m^m, which is polynomial-in-q^{1/m} and exponential in m, and does not change the exponent of the dominant cost T unless T is itself much smaller than in PKM. APS drops the APS-side analogue of PKM's precomputation P(p,m) (APS needs none) and the linear algebra s^ω (APS needs none).
- Assumption (13) equates the cost of solving S_m in m unknowns with univariate constraints of degree ≈ q^{1/m}/m (APS) with the cost of PKM's S_{m+1} in m unknowns plus t·m low-degree map equations with deg L ≈ p^{1/m}; these are structurally very different systems (APS: few variables, one huge-degree generator per variable; PKM: many variables, all low degree). The paper itself concedes (13) is unproven and only "heuristically corroborated".
- No claim of any asymptotic exponent is made anywhere in the paper; the only "exponent-like" statement is the target "smaller than r^{1/2}", explicitly not claimed. The p^{2/3} figure is from Dzierzkowski–Wroński (2024), attributed there to the Kudo et al. (2018) analysis, and is not in APS.

### Gaps
- No degree-of-regularity, Hilbert-series, Macaulay-bound, first-fall-degree or memory analysis appears in APS; nothing is said about the growth of T with the degree of the f_i.
- The precise text of Kudo et al. (CANS 2018) and Yokoyama et al. (JMC 2020), which are the papers that actually bound the cost of this type of system, could not be retrieved (see Key Question 7).

- The x-coordinate-only factor base makes the "half of V has no rational point" loss of the standard method disappear (the "Second" difference, AM p.6), which is a factor-2^m gain in the count (s/m)^m relative to a structured V of the same size.

### Gaps
- None of the listed refinements (symmetrization of S_m by elementary symmetric functions, 2-torsion/automorphism quotients, n−1 decompositions, elimination orders) is evaluated in APS; whether they combine with the random-factor-base idea is addressed only by later authors (Kudo et al. 2018), whose text could not be retrieved.
