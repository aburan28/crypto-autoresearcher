# N83 K899: the next factor-base comparison

**Preparatory search record, 26 October 2026.** Question: does a larger, target-blind Frobenius orbit base reduce the *fully charged* cold cost of the current N83 index-calculus producer? This record reviews published mechanisms and existing internal receipts. It authorizes no experiment, changes no research status, and makes no new quantitative speedup claim.

## Literature and prior-work check

The relevant primary sources are [Semaev's summation-polynomial construction](https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/031), [Faugère–Perret–Petit–Renault's binary-field decomposition systems](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/petit/files/Eurocrypt2012.pdf), and [Huang–Petit–Shinohara–Takagi's FPPR improvement](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40736-015-0012-6). The first makes summation-polynomial decomposition a known mechanism. The second explicitly couples a factor base with its polynomial-system solver and makes its complexity conclusion conditional on a rank heuristic. The third motivates choosing a vector space by the structure of its product spaces; a sparse input or low product-space dimension alone is therefore neither novel nor evidence of a cheaper full solve. These are retrieved primary papers; the FPPR article was checked at its publisher page, while the full paper was not re-derived here. The local [factor-base choice](../../../knowledge/leads/ecdlp/ic-binary/LEAD-ICB-factor-base-subspace-choice.yaml), [Frobenius orbit](../../../knowledge/leads/ecdlp/ic-binary/LEAD-ICB-frobenius-orbit-factor-base.yaml), and [Koblitz setup](../../../knowledge/leads/ecdlp/ic-binary/LEAD-ICB-koblitz-setup-phase-cost.yaml) leads delimit existing tests. The repository's `search_knowledge`/`get_context` retrieval tools were unavailable in this session, so the checked local records and primary sources are listed explicitly; this is not a claim of exhaustive literature coverage.

## Discriminating design for Coordinator review

The next comparison should freeze the current N83 binary, subgroup, one-arity PDP4 recipe, independent held-out public targets, and rho reference. Construct K600 and K899 without using targets; time their generation, point/subgroup certification, file load, and index build separately on the same host. Run alternating K600/K899 order on matched target and rank seeds with A/A repeats and a planted relation. Retain every failed/censored attempt and the actual useful-rank increments, sparse matrix dimensions and density, full-point relation certificates, target scalar replay, CPU/wall/RSS, and cache hashes. Report cold `T=1` and explicitly amortized `T>1` totals separately. Predeclare a wall/RSS guard based on K899's larger index and memory. This is a proposed protocol boundary; it needs a complete `experiment.protocol/v1`, controls, sample/power plan, archive coverage, read-only preapproval result, and Coordinator decision before execution.

The *falsifier* for the narrow K899 hypothesis is a paired, fully charged cold total that fails to improve over K600 within the frozen effect threshold, or a construction/index cost that cancels the rank saving. An online-only improvement would be recorded as such. A positive narrow result would justify an arity-specific extension, not a three-family ECDLP conclusion. Prime and ordinary-binary 50–63-bit rows and arities 2–8 remain separate obligations in the [full-width problem record](../../../problems/full-width-ic-arity-grid-unmeasured.md). Direct `S_8` represents seven summands plus the target; eight summands need direct `S_9` or a correct chain.
