# Gamma-bound binary m8 successor: sorted summands as a solver diagnostic

**Design record, 2026-10-18.** [EXP-ICPERF-554db4](../../experiments/EXP-ICPERF-554db4/specification.yaml) is `review_required`, with no scientific call authorized. It covers one certified ordinary-binary curve over `GF(2^61)`, prime subgroup order `576460752557211763`, eight summands represented by seven chained `S3` equations, and two target-blind factor bases of 316 projected columns. The design asks whether an exact coordinate-order symmetry constraint helps a bounded solver certify the two already constructed positive controls. It is an adaptation of a standard symmetry idea to this specific representation, with no novelty or performance conclusion.

## Why this gate is first

In the predecessor [EXP-ICPERF-018b0c](../../experiments/EXP-ICPERF-018b0c/specification.yaml), both independently satisfiable planted XCNFs reached a 300-second solver wall cap. Their [run record](../../experiments/EXP-ICPERF-018b0c/runs/RUN-ICPERF-712e85/raw-result.json) reports one weighted call and one local-random call, each `wall_censored`; sampled solver RSS was about 0.2 GiB per call. All 64 planned natural calls remained gated. The independent [review decision](../../ledger/decisions/DEC-20261017-3b87bf.yaml) classified the instrument result as inconclusive. These are `internal` observations about two bounded calls, not zero natural yield, an unsatisfiable system, or a measured degree of regularity.

The same review found an input-identifiability gap: the frozen statement rejected the pole `x=gamma` without defining `gamma` in the documents available to its blind reviewer. The [problem record](../../problems/icperf-k11-gamma-admission-unbound.md) gives two admissible interpretations that change the target schedule. The successor prospectively binds the field representation and `gamma=b^(1/4)=x([r]P_source)=70364449406991`. It states `T=[r]P_source=(70364449406991,2147483661)` and the exact four-channel pole predicate. An independent reviewer must derive the first eight targets and 32 channels from the new statement and fixture **before** opening predecessor manifests. A changed domain, the pole point, and the alternative literal gamma are required controls. Agreement would clear only this successor's schedule-identifiability gap; it would not establish solver feasibility.

## Sorted-summand mechanism and obligations

The current XCNF has eight 11-bit base-coordinate blocks, each with the same membership predicate, and seven chained `phi3` equations. The proposed encoder adds seven adjacent unsigned comparisons, `t0 <= t1 <= ... <= t7`. For a **certified full-point relation**, move each whole signed point and its torsion lift with its coordinate, sort stably, and recompute the six intermediate point sums. Associativity and commutativity preserve the raw sum and its cofactor-four projected row. This is a point-level argument; the sorted constraint may discard Boolean roots that fail point certification. The new comparator clauses and all preparation/checking costs must be charged.

The [draft transfer assessment](../../experiments/EXP-ICPERF-554db4/transfer-assessment.json) marks finite encoding fidelity and measured advantage open. Each original positive formula and complete known model is byte-bound. The executor must generate two sorted formulas and complete sorted witnesses, then independently check every CNF clause, native XOR row, curve lift, sign, raw sum, and projected row. Reversing each planted point tuple, re-chaining it, and sorting it again tests that the order constraint removes a presentation without losing the point relation. Repeated-coordinate, opposite-sign, and one-bit-flip controls probe the exceptional cases. No sorted formula or such control receipt exists yet.

The top-level operator independently rehashed all 11 currently bound predecessor inputs, including the installed CryptoMiniSat binary, and reported exact digest agreement on 2026-10-18. This is an input-integrity check for the prospective design; it is not a sorted-formula, solver, or ECDLP observation.

## Frozen comparison planned after implementation

For each base, the prospective order is original unsorted, sorted, then an identical unsorted repeat, with one seed, one thread, the same frozen binary, and a 1200-second/8-GiB sampled-RSS guard per call. The two identical unsorted calls are an A/A timing spread; a byte-for-byte independent re-emission is a separate input-integrity check. Six calls are the maximum scientific samples under this prospective protocol, with 7200 seconds of cumulative solver-call admission, plus separately measured preparation, checking, packaging, and failed-call work. The two bases' planted targets differ, so only within-base comparisons are interpretable. All censoring is retained. A solver timing difference smaller than the A/A spread decides no representation advantage.

The sorted-positive prediction is [partially in sample](../../experiments/EXP-ICPERF-554db4/specification.yaml): the predecessor's original formulas, witnesses, and censored unsorted calls were known at design time; the sorted calls have not been run. If both sorted positives certify, the next scientific protocol can freeze a **separate** common-target natural panel under the new gamma binding. Natural relation yield, useful rank, linear algebra, target solving, complete cold-start index-calculus cost, and matched rho are outside this design. The reviewed complete Koblitz `GF(2^53)` reference uses a 45-bit subgroup, outside the requested `2^50`–`2^63` range; this binary instrument therefore does not establish an in-range end-to-end baseline or improvement.
