## 2. CryptoPro-B: structural audit and public CM maps

We investigated Russian GOST curve construction and focused on CryptoPro-B’s small CM field and same-crater structure. The current audit completes four previously outstanding tasks:

- **Certified CM identification:** Arb ball arithmetic at 2,048 bits certified the degree-five Hilbert class polynomial for discriminant **−619**. Its reduction has five distinct roots over the prime field, including the standardized curve’s j-invariant. The audit identifies the actual endomorphism ring as the maximal order.
- **Exact embedding degree:** factorization and primality certificates establish **ordₙ(p) = (n−1)/2**. The earlier bounded order checks are superseded by this exact result.
- **All degree-103 maps:** constructed and verified all **104 rational cyclic 103-isogenies**, including kernels, target models and rational point maps. They descend to conductor 103; construction took approximately **239.3 seconds**.
- **Degree-155 CM endomorphism:** constructed the degree-5 → degree-31 → isomorphism chain, checked its exact polynomial identities, and verified its scalar action on the prime-order subgroup.

The distinction between the Frobenius-order conductor and the actual endomorphism-ring conductor was central. The Frobenius conductor has five distinct prime factors, while the curve’s endomorphism conductor is **1**. This gives **32 possible conductor values**, with local depth one in each prime direction. The five maximal-order curves have degree-5 and degree-7 horizontal connections. There are no rational degree-2 or degree-3 isogenies from that surface.

The first two conductor levels have class numbers **520** and **31,090**. Their existence does not supply a cheaper relation algorithm. Descending also raises the minimum noninteger endomorphism degree: from **155** on the surface to **1,641,743** at conductor 103.

**Evidence:** current **cryptopro_b_audit.txt**, revision 3, and **cryptopro_b_evidence.zip**, revision 2. The current audit supplies the later certification and explicit maps; earlier structural reports retain the qualifications of their earlier stages.

## 3. CryptoPro-B: arithmetic optimization and curve sweep

The original Python GLV experiment measured **1.147744 ms → 0.904905 ms**, a **1.268×** variable-base scalar-multiplication gain. We then added a compiled backend and optimized map circuits, coordinate handling, field arithmetic and joint multiplication.

The later code evaluates kernel polynomials without expanding squared denominators, carries map evaluation through Jacobian coordinates, uses native Montgomery arithmetic, normalizes the two precomputation tables together, and selects recipes and window widths using training inputs.

The bounded five-curve sweep completed in **55.035 seconds**. It produced **34 verified evaluation recipes per curve**, **170 total**, across degrees 155, 175, 245, 875, 1715 and 3125. It evaluated **680 recipe/window configurations**, then measured training-selected profiles on held-out inputs.

## 4. Jacobian correspondence and actual CM map checks

We reviewed four mechanisms: exceptional CM point decomposition, exceptional Jacobian relation geometry, a favorable descendant representation, and cheap endomorphism circuits. The strongest unresolved question is whether the CM correspondence supplies unusually cheap **independent verified relations**.

The structural report derives the principal polarization

$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}12&\omega\\\bar\omega&13\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \omega^2-\omega+155=0.
$$

It has **determinant 1** and **Hermitian minimum 12**. Under the certified maximal-order framework, the report’s indecomposability argument yields a genus-two Jacobian over the base field whose underlying abelian variety is E × E. The coordinate inclusions have restricted polarization degrees 12 and 13.

A separate torsion-gluing review supports **40 labeled gluing data** across the five surface models. That number is not a count of distinct genus-two curves. Neither investigation constructed an explicit genus-two equation and transfer formulas.

The later actual-curve correctness package tested the supplied degree-155 elliptic CM map on **39 known-input cases** and **16 additivity pairs**, with **zero failed checks**. It checked component identities, chain compatibility, subgroup scalar action, the CM polynomial, negation, and the proposed polarization determinant on the subgroup.

The package explicitly records that the genus-two transfer was not tested and relation solving was not benchmarked. Jacobian existence and forward-map correctness leave the computational relation advantage unresolved.

## 5. Relation-solving and synthetic sweeper results

### CM two-term relation pilot

The saved pilot compares compiled direct search, ordinary F5B equations and symmetric F5B equations on synthetic CM-discriminant −619 curves over **p = 227, 911 and 3461**. It uses five CM curves and ordinary controls, public target labels, paired inputs, signed point checks and incremental rank.

**All 144 clean arms reached rank eight.** An independent audit replayed **9,207 target attempts**, including **7,722 misses**; the clean arms had no timeouts. Contended measurements were retained separately and rerun sequentially.

| Field p | Base points | Direct control, ms/independent row | Ordinary F5B, ms/row | Symmetric F5B, ms/row | Paired symmetric gain |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 227 | 8 | 0.48 | 80.78 | 76.50 | 1.058× |
| 911 | 8 | 0.71 | 167.25 | 153.79 | 1.047× |
| 911 | 12 | 0.75 | 147.71 | 166.63 | 0.876× |
| 3461 | 12 | 1.29 | 449.57 | 546.24 | 0.849× |

The gain column is the median paired ratio, rather than the ratio of the displayed cost medians. The direct column uses an already-built binary; charging the full **0.774-second build** to each rank-eight direct arm raises its cost to approximately **97–98 ms per row**. The report retains both conventions.

The symmetric equations become smaller, but their advantage reverses when the nested base grows from eight to twelve points over the same field. **No repeatable 2× improvement over the best tested control was demonstrated.**

These are tiny two-term relation screens. They do not benchmark actual CryptoPro-B, genus-two divisor decomposition, isogeny transfer, a complete relation matrix or final DLP linear algebra. The recorded S-polynomial degrees are diagnostics, not certified solving-degree measurements.

**Evidence:** **cm_relation_benchmark.pdf** and **cm_relation_benchmark.zip**.

### Generic polynomial parameter sweeper

We built and ran a separate bounded polynomial-system sweeper for redundant-equation augmentation, varying solver and instance settings. Its completed paired run has:

- **192 completed runs**, **96 equivalent pairs**, eight seeds and three paired repetitions.
- Both Buchberger and F5B, three and four variables, prime 101.

The tested augmentation generally added cost. These systems contain no elliptic-curve or CM correspondence, so the result cannot settle the CryptoPro-B hypothesis. Intermediate solving degree and matrix dimensions were unavailable and were left unknown.
