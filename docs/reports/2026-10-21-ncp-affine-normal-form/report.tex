\section*{Statement and proof}
Words apply letters from left to right. The shadow sign is $(-1)^{\#\nu}$;
each translation's signed count uses the number of negations to its right.
For scalars $s_i$ in $\mathbb Z/n$, the checked theorem states, for every
word $w$ and starting scalar $x$,
\[
  \operatorname{action}_w(x)
  = \varepsilon(w)x + \sum_i a_i(w)s_i.
\]
The specialization at $x=1$ is the orbit-value law. A second theorem proves
the stronger group form for translations $s_iP$ in any additive commutative
group: $\operatorname{action}_w(X)=\varepsilon(w)X+(\sum_i a_i(w)s_i)P$.
Equal shadows induce identical actions. Induction on the word proves these
identities: a translation adds a signed term and a negation flips the sign of
the earlier translations.
