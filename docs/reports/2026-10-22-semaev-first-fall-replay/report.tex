\section*{A direct multiplication witness}
For $n=4$, $n'=4$, seed index $s=1$, multiply the first component equation by
$u_0$ (mask $1$). The archived set product retains masks $1$, $17$, and $129$;
all three cancel in the $\mathbb F_2$ product. On the complete 256-assignment
truth table, the archived product disagrees with evaluation of $f\,u_0$ in 64
assignments, and the parity-corrected product agrees in all 256. The replay
checker asserts this witness, compares 21,760 finite-field products and 12,800
direct $S_3$ assignments, and independently computes both rank profiles on
all 80 Semaev and 200 null systems. All archived distributions match the
frozen table.

\section*{Finite first-fall counterexample}
The same $n=4,n'=4,s=1$ system has $x_R=1$, basis $[9,3,2,12]$, and field
modulus $X^4+X+1$. Its trace residual $u_0+u_3+w_0+w_3$ is already the fourth
input equation. Under proper Boolean parity, $\dim V_1=1$ and
$\dim(V_2\cap R_{\le1})=1$, hence $\mathrm{new\_falls}(2)=0$. Also
$\dim V_2=11$, $\dim V_3=53$ and $\dim(V_3\cap R_{\le2})=12$, giving
$\mathrm{new\_falls}(3)=1$. Thus first fall is three even with $x_R\ne0$.
The archived set rule instead reports $\dim V_3=59$ and seven new falls at
three; its grade happens to agree for this draw.

\section*{Proof boundary and exact exceptions}
The existing \texttt{DegreeTwoFall.lean} proves quadratic trace cancellation
under $x_R\ne0$, not a general nonzero quotient class. The corrected finite example
contradicts a uniform degree-two conclusion for this toy Boolean model; it
does not refute the narrower Lean identity or imply a solving-degree or ECDLP
cost result. A new finite Lean certificate
now checks the transcribed four-equation system: its kernel-reduced ranks give
no new fall at degrees one or two and one new fall at degree three. The audited
theorems use only \texttt{propext}. A second Lean theorem evaluates direct
$S_3=e_2^2+e_3+1$ in $\mathbb F_2[X]/(X^4+X+1)$ and the four literal component
polynomials on all 256 Boolean assignments, proving equality for this exact
sample. Its 256 field products and 256 direct $S_3$ values also match an
independent Python reference. A third Lean certificate checks explicit
invertible row transforms and pivots for the degree-one and degree-two
matrices. Mathlib proves their $\mathbb F_2$ row-space dimensions are
$1$, $11$, and $10$ for degree one, degree two, and the degree-two projection;
the rank difference is $11-10-1=0$. A fourth certificate checks the full and
projected degree-three matrices: an XOR-to-$\mathbb F_2$ lemma, explicit row
transforms, inverse matrices, and echelon pivots yield actual row-space ranks
$53$ and $41$. A fifth Lean certificate proves the projection rank-nullity
identity, checks $V_3$ has no terms above degree three, and identifies the
projection kernel with $V_3\cap R_{\le2}$ of dimension $12$. It proves
$V_2\subseteq V_3\cap R_{\le2}$ with dimension $11$, hence a new low-degree
class exists. The executable ranks agree for this exact sample. Generic
\texttt{rankOn} correctness and symbolic $S_3$ descent remain open. The frozen target
and historical measurement were not edited.
