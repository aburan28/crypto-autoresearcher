## Frozen comparison and exact predicates

The certified prime curve has `p=9223372021248595841`, prime group order `r=9223372018953067627`, and a target-blind [475-point d=896 coset base](2026-10-17-prime-coset-896-search.md). The [control constructor](../../tools/icperf_prime_475_controls.py) used the existing `FactorBase.random` with fixed seed `20261017` and `FactorBase.small_x` with the same **realized size 475**. Neither construction saw a target or relation outcome. The complete random draw stream and sequential small-x stream are in the record; all misses and repeat decisions are retained. No x-coordinate overlaps the selected coset base in either control, as expected for these particular deterministic draws but not required for correctness.

For the coset, `x^896−c=0` has **896 field roots**; the curve equation filters 421 of them. The random and small-x polynomials are the exact products `∏(x−x_i)` over their 475 accepted x-coordinates, so their field roots are exactly the selected x set. Their dense coefficient support is an observed algebraic input fact, not evidence that their full point predicates or solver costs are worse. The small-x base includes `x=0`; its product therefore has zero constant coefficient and 475 rather than 476 nonzero terms. Exact polynomial coefficients and point-list digests are in the record.

The control builder's recorded preparation took **0.616345 s wall and 0.560137 s process CPU** on this host. It includes base construction, draw-stream reconstruction and membership-polynomial calculation, but excludes interpreter startup, subsequent git provenance subprocesses, JSON writing, the independent Sage replay, earlier field/base search and future use. Constructor-call wall timings were 0.037526 s (random) and 0.027454 s (small-x), with process-level sampled peak RSS of 36,585,472 and 37,011,456 bytes respectively. These are descriptive setup observations under current host load; the d=896 scan used a different preparation path and host interval, so these timings are **not a controlled speed comparison**. A full pipeline must charge actual cold selection and demonstrate any reused setup over declared targets.

The independently implemented [Sage checker](../../tools/icperf_prime_475_controls_sage_verify.py) read the frozen draw streams, regenerated the random sequence and small-x range, checked liftability and each stored curve point, recomputed point digests and all coefficients of both degree-475 product polynomials, and checked the coset reference binding. Its [write-once replay](../../research/icperf_prime_475_controls_20261017/sage_replay.json) reports the same 941/923 draws, 466/448 misses, 476/475 terms, and zero cross-base x overlap. The first control-builder attempt stopped on an incorrect assumption that both polynomials had 476 nonzero terms. The first Sage replay passed its in-memory checks but failed JSON serialization of a Sage integer; its exact 108-byte [partial output](../../research/icperf_prime_475_controls_20261017/sage_replay_failed_partial.txt.gz) was preserved in lossless gzip. Both implementation failures and their scopes are documented in the [failure record](../../research/icperf_prime_475_controls_20261017/preparation_failures.md); neither was interpreted as mathematical evidence.
