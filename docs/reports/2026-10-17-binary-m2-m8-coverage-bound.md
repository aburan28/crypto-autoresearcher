# Signed-multiset coverage ceilings for binary m=2–8 — 2026-10-17

## Question and scope

How many prime-subgroup targets could an **ideal** m-term decomposition oracle represent using the three prepared ordinary-binary factor bases, before any solver cost or duplicate relation accounting? This is an exact combinatorial ceiling for the certified `y²+xy=x³+31` curve over `GF(2^61)`, subgroup order `r=576460752557211763`, cofactor 4. The prepared bases contain 249 direct subgroup points, 265 raw-x projected columns, and 267 torsion-coordinate projected columns. A hypothetical 287-column size is shown only as a reference.

## Bound

Let the factor base supply `B` distinct sign-folded subgroup columns `Q_i`; an m-term decomposition chooses from the `2B` signed values `±Q_i`, with repetition and without order. There are exactly `C(2B+m−1,m)` such signed multisets. The number of **distinct subgroup sums** is at most this count, because different multisets can collide or cancel. Thus for a target drawn uniformly from this subgroup, its representable fraction is at most `min(1, C(2B+m−1,m)/r)`. This is an upper bound on mathematical coverage, not a relation-yield prediction: the exact base geometry, pole and sign constraints, solver failures, duplicate rows, and target scheduling can reduce useful yield.

The four raw target channels in the [m8 chain](../../research/icperf_binary_torsion_m8_chain_20261017/chain.json) are preimages of the **same** subgroup target under `[4]`. They are needed to cover raw lifts but cannot multiply projected subgroup coverage by four. The bound applies to their union at the projected level.

For the prepared 267-column torsion base, at most **29.9713%** of uniformly drawn subgroup targets can be eight-sums under this count; at m=7 the ceiling is **0.4432%**. Those are derived upper bounds, not observed success rates. They make m=8 or a much larger base the relevant representation-capacity regime for this prepared base. They do not show that any m8 system is efficiently solvable or that its relation rows have useful rank.
