6 Con lusions
We have argued that the elds F2N , where N 2 [185; 600℄ is divisible by 5, are
weak for ECC. The fundamental open problem is to determine whether there
are any elds that are bad for ECC. We have provided some eviden e that the
  eld F2210 is a prime andidate for being bad.
    Another andidate for a bad eld is F2161 . For 294 of the 2162 isomorphism
 lasses of ellipti urves E over F2161 , the GHS redu tion yields a hyperellipti
 urve C of genus (7 or) 8 over F223 , where the HCDLP is feasible. In our notation,
we have R = E 280, R1 = ( J + S )237, and R2 = r 247, where E denotes the
time to perform an ellipti urve addition in E (F2161 ), J is the time to perform
an addition in JC (F223 ), S is the time to test whether a moni polynomial
a 2 F223 [u℄ of degree 8 is 1-smooth, and r is the time to perform a multipli ation
modulo a 160-bit prime. If an arbitrary ECDLP instan e over F2161 an be
eÆ iently mapped to an ECDLP instan e for an isogenous ellipti urve that
belongs to the aforementioned lass of 294 urves, then one would on lude that
F2161 is bad for ECC (see also [15℄ and [27, Remark 20℄). No su h mapping is
known so far (see also [42℄).
    An important open question in hyperellipti urve ryptography is whether
there are algorithms for solving the HCDLP urve that are faster than the Enge-
Gaudry algorithm. Be ause of the relevan e to solving the ECDLP, improve-
ments by a onstant fa tor would be of interest. For example, the possibility of
using sieving (see [9℄) to generate relations needs to be further explored.
    Galbraith [14℄ has shown that Weil des ent an be used to atta k the HCDLP
over some low genus hyperellipti urves de ned over hara teristi two nite
  elds of omposite extension degrees. An open problem is to determine whether
there are any weak elds for genus two hyperellipti urve ryptography.
