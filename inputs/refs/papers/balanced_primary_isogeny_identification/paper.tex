\begin{abstract}
Self-pairings on oriented elliptic curves can reveal the image of a torsion
generator under an unknown isogeny only up to sign.  Passing to the Kummer
line removes these signs, but unrestricted rational interpolation still
expects $2d$ samples for a degree-$d$ isogeny.  We prove that the
homomorphism structure gives a strictly stronger identification criterion.

For pairwise coprime odd generator orders $n_1,\ldots,n_r$, define
\[
 \betaP(n_1,\ldots,n_r)=
 \min_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}}
 \left(\prod_{i\in S}n_i+\prod_{i\notin S}n_i\right).
\]
If two degree-$d$ homomorphisms have equal target $x$-coordinates on these
generators and $\betaP>4d$, then the maps differ by at most a global sign.
The proof partitions the primary subgroups between the kernels of
$\phi-\psi$ and $\phi+\psi$ and applies the degree parallelogram identity.
An exact version replaces the products by generated-subgroup orders and
allows unequal candidate degrees; a minimum-distance corollary treats erased
and corrupted primary rows.

We validate the full information path on an ordinary ascending degree-$5$
isogeny over $\F_{34651}$.  Genuine Frobenius-oriented self-pairings at orders
$3,7,11$ supply three sign-free generator rows with $\betaP=32>20$, while
their additive Kummer-orbit capacity is $9<10$.  A public two-parameter
Schoof--V\'elu decoder recovers the unique kernel in three independent-seed
runs, and a separate post-freeze verifier reconstructs the pairings and the
withheld edge.  A stronger baseline is also decisive: all nine orbit rows
leave a rational-interpolation pencil, and the public codomain curve identity
resolves that pencil faster on this toy.  More decisively, the source has one
rational degree-$5$ edge, so a zero-row source-only enumeration recovers it
faster still.  Thus the new result is a compressed-generator identification
criterion and interface validation, not evidence that self-pairing rows
improve recovery on this fixture.  It removes local sign ambiguity as an
information-theoretic defense, but does not reduce torsion acquisition or
explicit-output costs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Recovering an isogeny between two fixed elliptic curves is a central problem
in algorithmic number theory and isogeny-based cryptography.  Recent
self-pairing techniques extract partial information about an unknown oriented
isogeny by constraining its action on torsion
\cite{castryck-selfpairings,macula-stange,ggr-volcanoes}.  In the ramified
cyclic setting, the image of one exact-order point can be determined only up
to a scalar square root.  For an odd primary modulus, the two roots differ by
sign.

The sign disappears under the Kummer quotient $P\mapsto x(P)$.  This gives
two conceptually different ways to use the leaked action.  One can generate
all local Kummer orbits and apply unrestricted rational interpolation when
their additive size reaches $2d$.  Alternatively, one can retain only one
generator from each primary component and use the fact that a competing map
must realize every local sign through either $\phi-\psi$ or $\phi+\psi$.
The second viewpoint yields the product-partition criterion of this paper.  We
use ``balanced-primary'' specifically for the minimum complementary-product
sum defined below; it is not the balanced Kani regime used elsewhere in the
isogeny-interpolation literature.

A classical agreement bound states that sufficiently many exact point images
determine a bounded-degree isogeny \cite{urbanik-jao}.  Our observation differs
in that every Kummer observation may carry an independent sign.  It partitions
the observed primary subgroups between two kernels and controls their combined
degree with the parallelogram identity.

The distinction is substantial.  For four balanced primary orders of size
$\Theta(\sqrt d)$, the new criterion holds while their total Kummer-orbit
capacity is only $O(\sqrt d)$.  Thus a constant number of generator images
can identify the map in a regime where generic rational interpolation is
short by a linear factor.  This is an information improvement, not a
small-torsion improvement: the product of those orders is $\Theta(d^2)$.

\subsection{Contributions}

This paper makes five scoped contributions.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(\roman*)]
\item We prove a balanced-primary uniqueness theorem.  If
      $\betaP(n_1,\ldots,n_r)>4d$, one sign-free image per pairwise-coprime
      primary order determines a degree-$d$ isogeny up to global sign.
\item We prove the generated-subgroup and unequal-degree form of the theorem,
      and derive erasure and adversarial-row unique-decoding conditions.
\item We show a strict separation from raw additive primary-orbit
      interpolation, both in an asymptotic four-primary family and in a
      deterministic sweep for degrees $5$ through $1021$.
\item We give a full-path ordinary ascending toy validation.  Actual
      self-pairings in separate torsion fields feed a three-row target-scaling
      decoder; three acquisition-seed runs and three executions of a separate
      verifier recover the same degree-$5$ map with no map access observed in
      the audited producer source.
\item We delimit the algorithmic and cryptographic implication with a strong
      additive-plus-curve-identity baseline and a zero-row source-only
      ablation.  The theorem eliminates local sign ambiguity, but the toy does
      not show that the rows improve recovery and does not reduce
      torsion-field, pairing-DLP, or explicit-output costs.
\end{enumerate}

The degree parallelogram law and self-pairing ingredients are known.  The
claimed contribution is their use as a complementary-product uniqueness test
for independently signed primary images.  A focused literature search found
no explicit statement of this criterion, but this is not an absolute priority
certificate.

\section{Preliminaries}

\subsection{Sign-free primary data}

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic different from $2$, let
$E_0,E_1/K$ be elliptic curves, and let
\[
  \phi:E_0\longrightarrow E_1
\]
be a separable isogeny of known degree $d$.  The ordinary case may use the
natural Frobenius orientation; oriented supersingular curves give the second
main setting \cite{ggr-volcanoes}.

We abstract the output of the ramified self-pairing construction as follows.

\begin{definition}[Primary sign datum]
Let $n=\ell^e$ be an odd prime power coprime to $d\operatorname{char}K$.
A primary sign datum consists of exact-order points
$P\in E_0[n]$, $Q\in E_1[n]$, and a unit square
$v\in(\Z/n\Z)^\times$ such that
\[
  \phi(P)=[\alpha]Q,\qquad \alpha^2=v\pmod n
\]
for an unknown square root $\alpha$.
\end{definition}

For an odd prime power, a unit square has exactly two roots, $\alpha$ and
$-\alpha$.  Consequently the datum determines
\[
  x(\phi(P))=x([\alpha]Q)
\]
without selecting a root.  The same holds for all multiples of $P$.
Producing this datum is not free: local primitivity, torsion construction,
orientation evaluation, pairing evaluation, and the pairing-target discrete
logarithm are separate charged operations.

\subsection{Degree as a quadratic form}

The group $\operatorname{Hom}(E_0,E_1)$ carries the positive-definite
quadratic form $q(f)=\deg f$, with $q(0)=0$.  Its polarization gives the
parallelogram identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parallelogram}
 \deg(f+g)+\deg(f-g)=2\deg f+2\deg g.
\end{equation}
This identity holds independently of the separability of $f+g$ and $f-g$.
If a nonzero homomorphism kills a reduced subgroup of order $N$ prime to the
characteristic, then $N$ is at most its degree.

\section{Balanced-primary uniqueness}

Let $P_i\in E_0(\Kbar)$ have pairwise-coprime odd exact orders $n_i$, all
coprime to $d\operatorname{char}K$.  The points may be defined over unrelated
extension fields.

\begin{definition}[Balanced-primary parameter]
For $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}$, put
\[
 N_S=\prod_{i\in S}n_i,
\]
with an empty product equal to one, and define
\[
 \betaP(n_1,\ldots,n_r)
 =\min_S\bigl(N_S+N_{S^c}\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Balanced-primary uniqueness]\label{thm:balanced}
Let $\phi,\psi:E_0\to E_1$ have degree $d$.  Suppose
\[
 x(\phi(P_i))=x(\psi(P_i))
 \quad\text{for all }i.
\]
If $\betaP(n_1,\ldots,n_r)>4d$, then $\psi=\phi$ or $\psi=-\phi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The target points are nonzero because $\gcd(n_i,d)=1$.  Equality of their
$x$-coordinates therefore gives a sign $\epsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}$ with
\[
 \phi(P_i)=\epsilon_i\psi(P_i).
\]
Let $S$ contain the indices for which $\epsilon_i=1$.  For $i\in S$, the
point $P_i$ lies in $\ker(\phi-\psi)$; for $i\notin S$, it lies in
$\ker(\phi+\psi)$.  Pairwise coprimality implies that these kernels contain
reduced subgroups of orders $N_S$ and $N_{S^c}$, respectively.

If neither $\phi-\psi$ nor $\phi+\psi$ is zero, then
\[
 N_S+N_{S^c}
 \le \deg(\phi-\psi)+\deg(\phi+\psi)
 =4d
\]
by \cref{eq:parallelogram}.  This contradicts the definition of $\betaP$.
Hence one of the two homomorphisms is zero, giving $\psi=\pm\phi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The theorem is an identification statement among degree-$d$ homomorphisms.
It does not assert that arbitrary bivariate equations derived from the samples
are zero-dimensional, nor does it make torsion acquisition inexpensive.
\end{remark}

The product formula is convenient, but the proof yields a sharper statement
that also records the exact degree dependence.

\begin{theorem}[Generated-subgroup form]\label{thm:generated}
Let $\phi,\psi:E_0\to E_1$ be homomorphisms, and let $P_1,\ldots,P_r$
have orders prime to the characteristic and to both map degrees.  For
$S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}$ define
\[
 H_S=\langle P_i:i\in S\rangle,
 \qquad
 \beta_H=\min_S\bigl(\#H_S+\#H_{S^c}\bigr).
\]
If $x(\phi(P_i))=x(\psi(P_i))$ for every $i$ and
\[
 \beta_H>2(\deg\phi+\deg\psi),
\]
then $\phi=\psi$ or $\phi=-\psi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the observed signs to define $S$ as in
\cref{thm:balanced}.  Then $H_S\subseteq\ker(\phi-\psi)$ and
$H_{S^c}\subseteq\ker(\phi+\psi)$.  If neither difference is zero,
\[
 \#H_S+\#H_{S^c}
 \le \deg(\phi-\psi)+\deg(\phi+\psi)
 =2(\deg\phi+\deg\psi),
\]
a contradiction.
\end{proof}

For candidates of degree at most $D$, the sufficient threshold is
$\beta_H>4D$.  Without pairwise coprimality, one may lower-bound
$\#H_S$ by the least common multiple of the corresponding exact orders.
Repeated or nested points on one primary line must not be multiplied: their
actual generated-subgroup order is the quantity in the theorem.

The same statement gives a simple coding consequence.  Suppose at most $t$
of $r$ primary rows are corrupted.  Two candidate maps agreeing with at least
$r-t$ received rows agree with each other on at least $r-2t$ rows.  Hence
unique decoding up to sign follows if every subset $I$ of $r-2t$ rows
satisfies $\beta_H(I)>4D$.  Erasures are handled by recomputing the criterion
on the surviving rows.  This is an information theorem; it does not provide
an efficient noisy-row decoder.

\subsection{Relation to additive Kummer interpolation}

Define the additive orbit capacity
\[
 \Cr=\sum_{i=1}^r\frac{n_i-1}{2}.
\]
The earlier sign-oblivious interpolation condition is $\Cr\ge2d$.  The new
condition subsumes it.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:additive}
For pairwise-coprime positive integers $n_i$,
\[
 \betaP(n_1,\ldots,n_r)\ge 2+2\Cr.
\]
Consequently $\Cr\ge2d$ implies $\betaP>4d$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every subset $S$,
\[
 N_S=\prod_{i\in S}\bigl(1+(n_i-1)\bigr)
 \ge 1+\sum_{i\in S}(n_i-1).
\]
Apply the same inequality to $S^c$ and add.
\end{proof}

The converse fails.  For $d=13$ and
$(n_i)=(3,5,7,11)$, one has $\Cr=11<26$, while the minimizing
partition is $(3\cdot11,5\cdot7)$ and
\[
 \betaP=33+35=68>52=4d.
\]

Let $m=\prod_i n_i$.  Since $N_SN_{S^c}=m$, AM--GM gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-bound}
 \betaP\ge2\sqrt m.
\end{equation}
Thus $m>4d^2$ is a simple sufficient condition.  Conversely, the empty
partition gives $\betaP\le1+m$, so the theorem implies $m>4d-1$; no new
small-product region is created.  Exact $\betaP$ can be computed by a
meet-in-the-middle closest-subset-product search.

\subsection{Asymptotic separation}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:asymptotic}
Fix $\varepsilon>0$.  Suppose four pairwise-coprime odd orders satisfy
\[
 (\sqrt2+\varepsilon)\sqrt d<n_i<C\sqrt d
\]
for a fixed constant $C$.  For sufficiently large $d$,
$\betaP>4d$ while $\Cr=O(\sqrt d)=o(d)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every two-versus-two partition has both products greater than
$(\sqrt2+\varepsilon)^2d>2d$, so its sum exceeds $4d$.  A one-versus-three
sum is $\Omega(d^{3/2})$, and the empty-versus-four sum is $\Omega(d^2)$.
The additive bound follows directly from the upper bound on the four orders.
\end{proof}

Hence four generator images can identify the map in a family where the total
number of sign-free Kummer-orbit samples is asymptotically insufficient for
unrestricted rational interpolation.  The associated torsion product is
$\Theta(d^2)$.

\section{A toy target-scaling decoder}

Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced} proves uniqueness but does not itself construct the
map.  We describe the toy backend used in our experiments.  Its ingredients
are Schoof's one-parameter kernel expansion and modified V\'elu formulas
\cite{schoof-counting,bmss,takahashi-kernel,velu}.

Assume short-Weierstrass models in characteristic sufficiently large for the
formal recurrence:
\[
 E_j:y^2=x^3+A_jx+B_j.
\]
Write
\[
 \phi(x,y)=\bigl(R(x),c\,yR'(x)\bigr),\qquad \lambda=c^2.
\]
Since $R(x)\sim\lambda x$ at infinity, put $R=\lambda U$.  Then $U$ is a
normalized isogeny to
\[
 \overline E_1:y^2=x^3+\frac{A_1}{\lambda^2}x
                         +\frac{B_1}{\lambda^3}.
\]

For odd $d$, let $s=(d-1)/2$ and write the monic kernel polynomial as
\[
 F(X)=X^s+T X^{s-1}+\cdots.
\]
Schoof's formal expansion expresses every remaining coefficient of $F$ in
$K(T,\lambda)$.  Modified V\'elu formulas give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:velu-x}
 U(X)=\frac{N_{T,\lambda}(X)}{D_{T,\lambda}(X)},
 \qquad D_{T,\lambda}=F^2.
\end{equation}
Each sign-free row $(u_i,z_i)=(x(P_i),x(\phi(P_i)))$ gives the residual
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sample-residual}
 \lambda N_{T,\lambda}(u_i)-z_iD_{T,\lambda}(u_i)=0.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Elimination-degree bound]\label{prop:elimination}
After clearing powers of $\lambda$, every sample residual has bidegree at most
$(d,d)$ in $(T,\lambda)$.  If two residuals have no common component, their
resultant in $T$ has $\lambda$-degree at most $2d^2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Give $X$ weight one in the expansion at infinity.  If $f_j$ is the coefficient
of $X^{s-j}$ in $F$, the formal recurrence has the shape
$f_j=C_j(T,\lambda)/\lambda^j$, with
$\deg_T C_j,\deg_\lambda C_j\le j$.  Multiplying $F$ by $\lambda^s$ and
substituting in the modified V\'elu numerator and denominator therefore gives
the stated residual bidegree.  The standard bidegree bound for a resultant of
two polynomials of bidegrees $(a,b)$ and $(c,e)$ is $ae+bc$; here this is at
most $2d^2$.
\end{proof}

The prototype factors this resultant, intersects the specialized
$T$-roots from all rows, and verifies each candidate by requiring:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(\arabic*)]
\item the specialized polynomial is the kernel polynomial of a finite cyclic
      subgroup of order $d$;
\item scaling the normalized V\'elu codomain by $\lambda$ gives the marked
      target model;
\item $\lambda$ is a square over the base field when a base-field map is
      required; and
\item every supplied sample is matched.
\end{enumerate}

These are public acceptance conditions.  The withheld map is used only for a
post-run whole-group audit.

\begin{remark}[Completeness boundary]
A selected pair of residuals can have zero resultant or exceptional
specializations.  A complete implementation must try other pairs or add the
exact differential/codomain coefficient equations.  Dense bivariate
elimination is polynomial in $d$, but the current implementation is a toy
verified-list decoder, not a claimed replacement for compact Kani or
higher-dimensional representations.  Writing the explicit map already costs
$\Omega(d\log q)$ bits.
\end{remark}

\section{Experimental evidence}

The artifact set separates fixture search, public recovery, and post-freeze
verification.  The fixture search uses trace, endomorphism-ring, and modular
polynomial data but no degree-$5$ map.  The recovery producer contains no
degree-$5$ enumeration, hidden-kernel, or rational-map read.  The verifier is
a separate program that enumerates the withheld edge only after the complete
producer payload has been hashed.  These are source and hash audits, not a
filesystem or syscall sandbox; they justify ``no forbidden access observed,''
not that such access was technically impossible.

\subsection{A genuine ordinary ascending fixture}

Let $p=34651$ and consider
\[
\begin{split}
 E_0 &: y^2=x^3+15912x+20007,\\
 E_1 &: y^2=x^3+16688x+3859.
\end{split}
\]
Both curves have trace $2$ and $34650$ rational points.  The Frobenius
discriminant factors as
\[
 t^2-4p=-138600=(-616)\,15^2.
\]
The source endomorphism ring has conductor $15$, while the target has
conductor $3$ and discriminant $-5544=(-616)3^2$.  The source has one rational
degree-$5$ edge, and its codomain is $E_1$ up to the frozen nontrivial model
scaling.  Thus the edge is the unique ascent that removes the factor $5$ from
the conductor.  The target's six rational degree-$5$ neighbors have conductor
multiset $\{3,3,15,15,15,15\}$, as predicted by the split-crater, depth-one
volcano.

The word ``ramified'' in the self-pairing literature refers here to
degeneracy modulo the discriminant of the orientation order.  The primes $7$ and
$11$ divide the fundamental discriminant $-616$.  The prime $3$ does not; it
enters through the conductor factor of $\Z[\pi]$, with valuation two in the
Frobenius discriminant.  Its measured nonscalar nilpotent Frobenius action and
primitive pairing certify the required local interface.

For primary orders $3,7,11$, the exact Frobenius orders on full torsion are
$3,7,11$, respectively.  The producer constructs these three fields
separately.  On each endpoint it samples a cyclic $\Z[\pi]$-generator $P$,
forms $Q=(\pi-1)P$, and verifies that $e(P,Q)$ has exact primary order.  The
pairing equations leave the root pairs
\[
 \{1,2\},\qquad\{2,5\},\qquad\{2,9\}.
\]
Multiplying the target $Q$ by either root gives the same $x$-coordinate.
Consequently one base-field Kummer generator row is obtained from each local
field without evaluating the secret isogeny.

Here
\[
 \betaP(3,7,11)=32>20=4d,
 \qquad
 \Cr=9<10=2d.
\]
The three rows enter the target-scaling decoder.  Its residuals have bidegree
$(5,5)$; all three pairwise resultants are nonzero, and their common
specialization gives one verified pair.  The recovered kernel polynomial is
\[
 F(X)=X^2+9768X+21203,
\]
with target $x$-scale $\lambda=16205$.  Post-freeze enumeration finds exactly
one rational degree-$5$ source edge and the same kernel and explicit $x$-map.

Three acquisition seeds produced different torsion generators but the same
normalized candidate hash.  Diagnostic total producer times were
$0.320$--$0.362$ seconds; the decoder subroutine itself took
$0.043$--$0.059$ seconds.
Across the runs, torsion acquisition used $78$--$136$ sampled points,
$176$--$276$ scalar multiplications, and six Weil pairings, with no pairing
failure.  Three executions of the same separate verifier passed all
registered checks, including wrong-image, wrong-root, wrong-order,
wrong-degree, wrong-target, and map-coefficient mutations.  All executions
share the same Sage trust boundary and are not independent implementations.

\subsection{A stronger additive baseline}

The inequality $\Cr<2d$ rules out a one-dimensional nullspace certificate
from unrestricted rational interpolation, but it does not exhaust the public
algebraic structure.  We therefore give the additive baseline all nine local
half-orbit rows.  The resulting $9\times11$ matrix has rank $9$ and a
two-dimensional nullspace.  Writing its rational-map pencil as
$R_u=N_u/D_u$, a homomorphism to the public target must satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-identity}
 s(x^3+A_0x+B_0)(N'_uD_u-N_uD'_u)^2
 =D_u(N_u^3+A_1N_uD_u^2+B_1D_u^3)
\end{equation}
for some differential square $s$.  The gcd of the coefficient minors in the
pencil parameter has degree one and recovers the same map.

This baseline subroutine took $0.006$--$0.011$ seconds, faster than the
three-row Schoof--V\'elu decoder on this toy.  The experiment therefore establishes a
strict separation from raw linear interpolation and a three-versus-nine row
reduction, but not an advantage over interpolation augmented by the public
curve identity.  Scalar-multiple orbit rows are cheap once a generator has
been acquired, so the row reduction is not by itself a cryptographic speedup.

\subsection{Zero-row ablation}

The fixture has a still stronger negative control: because $E_0$ lies on the
degree-$5$ floor, it has exactly one rational degree-$5$ edge.  A source-only
enumerator therefore recovers the same kernel without the target or any
self-pairing row.  In 30 fresh-curve repetitions, every run returned the
kernel $X^2+9768X+21203$; the median enumeration time was $0.00190$ seconds
(minimum $0.00157$, maximum $0.0605$ including the cold run).

This does not invalidate the self-pairing producer, decoder, or theorem.  It
does mean that this fixture validates compatibility of the complete
information path but cannot demonstrate that balanced-primary data improve
recovery.  The ``three versus nine'' observation is a compressed-generator
separation from a structure-blind linear-rank certificate, not a three-versus-
nine acquisition saving.

\subsection{Below-threshold non-uniqueness}

The theorem is not asserted without its quantitative hypothesis.  Over
$\F_{157}$, let
\[
 E:y^2=x^3+x
\]
and let $\iota$ be the order-four CM automorphism.  The two endomorphisms
\[
 \phi=[3]+[5]\iota,\qquad \psi=[3]-[5]\iota
\]
have degree $34$ and satisfy $\phi\ne\pm\psi$.  Nevertheless, their target
$x$-coordinates agree on one exact-order $3$ point and one exact-order $5$
point.  The local signs split between $\phi+\psi$ and $\phi-\psi$, and
\[
 \betaP(3,5)=8\le136=4d,
 \quad \deg(\phi+\psi)=36,
 \quad \deg(\phi-\psi)=100.
\]
This is a concrete negative control below the theorem threshold.

\subsection{Evidence boundary}

The ordinary degree-$5$ experiment implements the claimed self-pairing
information path end to end, but only at toy scale and in one isogeny degree;
the zero-row ablation shows that its rows are unnecessary for recovering this
particular edge.
Sage's finite-field, endomorphism-ring, pairing, modular-polynomial, and
post-freeze isogeny backends are trusted.  At the primary $3$ the Frobenius
discriminant has valuation two; effectiveness is certified by the observed
nonscalar nilpotent action and primitive pairing, not by a valuation-one
shortcut.  The experiment does not implement Kani's construction, benchmark
a protocol action, or establish a scaling law for the decoder.  The
degree-$13$ case remains a supplied-row regression and is not used to support
the self-pairing acquisition claim.

\section{Cryptographic implications}

In the ramified self-pairing setting, each primary component may expose
\[
 \phi(P_i)=\pm[\alpha_i]Q_i.
\]
When $\betaP>4d$, these nominally independent signs cannot encode two
different degree-$d$ isogenies.  Thus the local $2^r$ ambiguity is not
information-theoretic protection in this regime.

This suggests an additional parameter audit.  For every accessible odd
ramified primary order, record:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(\arabic*)]
\item the exact order and local-primitivity hypotheses;
\item its torsion-field degree and pairing/DLP cost;
\item the exact or lower-bounded value of $\betaP$; and
\item the reconstruction backend and required output representation.
\end{enumerate}

If $\betaP>4d$, local sign tuples cannot represent distinct degree-$d$ Kummer
maps.  Conditional on a decoder that consumes these Kummer samples directly,
one may therefore avoid explicit local-root enumeration.  This is not an
unconditional $2^r$ runtime saving: existing higher-dimensional algorithms
reuse work across root choices, and the replacement decoder has different
arithmetic and output costs.  In the four-primary asymptotic family the
nominal $2^r=16$ factor is constant.  The theorem's unconditional content is
that the local roots do not carry $r$ independent bits of map identity once
the complementary-product threshold is crossed.

These gains come with strong qualifications.  The product order is at least
on the scale already required by subgroup interpolation and is
$\Theta(d^2)$ in the balanced asymptotic example.  Torsion-field arithmetic
and pairing DLPs may dominate.  The explicit output is linear in $d$, whereas
Kani and higher-dimensional methods can retain compact evaluators.  The
published SCALLOP examples analyzed by Galbraith, Gilchrist, and Robert are
not shown vulnerable by this criterion \cite{ggr-volcanoes}.  The defensive
consequence is a parameter audit: a design should not count local square-root
multiplicity as security without first checking $\betaP$ and the cost of a
direct Kummer decoder.  We make no claim about deployed SCALLOP or
PEARL-SCALLOP parameters, SQIsign, ordinary ECDLP, or generic class-group
vectorization.

\section{Related work and novelty boundary}

Urbanik and Jao record a classical bounded-degree agreement lemma for exact
point images \cite{urbanik-jao}.  Petit exploits exact torsion images in
isogeny recovery \cite{petit-torsion}.  Neither setting includes independent
Kummer signs on separate primary components.

The abstract proof architecture has a close antecedent in real phase
retrieval.  Balan, Casazza, and Edidin partition measurements according to
whether two phaseless observations place the difference or the sum in the
corresponding kernel, and use the complement property to force a global sign
\cite{balan-phase}.  Our arithmetic specialization replaces linear spanning
by generated torsion-subgroup sizes and combines the two branches through the
degree norm.  We do not claim the sign-partition idea itself as new.

Castryck et al. introduced self-pairing attacks on weak class-group-action
instances and explicitly account for the $2^{r-1}$ square-root classes left by
several primary factors \cite{castryck-selfpairings}.  Macula and Stange
developed the sesquilinear framework and analyzed the remaining local
candidates \cite{macula-stange}.  Galbraith, Gilchrist, and Robert give the
ramified cyclic relation used here and recover through an auxiliary quotient
and higher-dimensional isogenies \cite{ggr-volcanoes}.

General interpolation from sufficiently large torsion action is developed in
the literature on isogeny interpolation; a public account gives the familiar
$4d+1$ point threshold \cite{castryck-ants,robert-survey}.  The earlier
additive Kummer-orbit observation processes each primary sign before Chinese
remaindering.  Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced} goes further by using the degree
quadratic form to couple all local signs without enumerating their tuple.

Bostan, Morain, Salvy, and Schost compute normalized isogenies quasi-linearly
in the degree using formal expansions \cite{bmss}.  Takahashi et al. make
kernel and intermediate-codomain coefficients symbolic and solve the
resulting equations algebraically \cite{takahashi-kernel}.  We therefore treat
the target-scaling decoder as a sample-aided implementation variant and
experimental witness, not as a standalone novelty claim.

The balanced complementary-product criterion was not found in the inspected
public primary sources as of July 20, 2026.  The closest defensible novelty
claim is an arithmetic complement-property criterion for Kummer evaluations
of bounded-degree elliptic-curve homomorphisms.  It is a short synthesis of
standard identities, and the in-preparation isogeny-interpolation manuscript
cited through its public ANTS slides remains a priority gap.  Novelty should
therefore remain qualified until broader expert review.

\section{Limitations and open problems}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The theorem is sufficient, not necessary; no sharp converse is claimed.
\item Pairwise coprimality and exact point orders are essential to the product
      lower bound.
\item The constructor is toy-scale and assumes short-Weierstrass models in
      sufficiently large characteristic.
\item The end-to-end experiment uses one degree-$5$ ordinary ascending
      fixture.  Its local field construction, sampling, pairings, and
      pairing-root search are charged, but it does not establish scaling.
\item At the order-$3$ primary, local effectiveness is measured rather than
      inferred from a valuation-one discriminant condition.
\item A zero or exceptional sample resultant requires additional rows or full
      differential equations; a complete specialization-safe implementation
      is open.
\item The dense explicit-map decoder is not directly comparable with compact
      higher-dimensional representations without conversion and output costs.
\item The additive nullspace-pencil baseline recovers the same toy map faster.
      No runtime advantage over a structure-aware interpolation baseline is
      claimed.
\item The source-only zero-row baseline also recovers the unique toy edge, so
      this fixture cannot show that the pairing rows improve recovery.
\item Producer custody is established by source/API audit and hashes, not by a
      read-isolated execution environment.
\item No same-instance Kani/theta implementation or deployed parameter set is
      benchmarked.
\item A focused literature review cannot establish absolute priority.
\end{itemize}

The decisive next experiment is a multi-degree family of genuine
ramified-orientation fixtures with a fully charged same-instance comparison
against the root-enumerating Kani/theta route, the additive curve-identity
solver, and normalize-then-BMSS recovery.

\section{Conclusion}

Independent local signs are coupled by the global degree form.  Partitioning
pairwise-coprime primary generators between $\ker(\phi-\psi)$ and
$\ker(\phi+\psi)$ yields the criterion $\betaP>4d$, which determines a
degree-$d$ isogeny up to global sign.  The generated-subgroup formulation
also handles bounded unequal degrees and gives an error/erasure uniqueness
condition.  The criterion strictly improves the sample threshold of raw
additive Kummer interpolation and gives an asymptotic constant-generator
identification regime.

The ordinary $\F_{34651}$ experiment realizes the full self-pairing path on a
genuine conductor-$15$ to conductor-$3$ ascent: three rows at orders
$3,7,11$ recover the unique degree-$5$ kernel in three acquisition-seed runs.
The baselines then supply two essential negative results.  Nine orbit rows
plus the curve identity recover the same map faster, and the source's unique
rational edge permits zero-row recovery faster still.  What survives is exact
and useful but narrower than a break: local square-root multiplicity is not
independent map entropy beyond the complementary-product threshold.  Turning
that fact into a cryptographic speed gain requires nontrivial instances and a
decoder and representation that beat the best structure-aware alternatives
with every acquisition and output cost charged.
