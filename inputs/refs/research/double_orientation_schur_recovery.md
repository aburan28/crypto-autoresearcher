## One-sentence result

Two coherently transported, noncommuting effective orientations whose torsion
actions generate the full `2 x 2` matrix algebra determine the action of a
known-degree isogeny on the full reconstruction torsion up to local signs;
this replaces the conic-sized branch set from one quadratic orientation by at
most `2^omega(n)` candidates.

## Schur-selector theorem

Let `n` be odd and prime to the characteristic.  Put `R=Z/nZ`, and choose
symplectically normalized bases of `E0[n]` and `E1[n]`, so the Weil pairings of
the two ordered bases agree.  Let

```text
phi : E0 -> E1
```

be a separable isogeny of known degree `d`, with `gcd(d,n)=1`, and let
`X in GL_2(R)` be its unknown torsion matrix.  Suppose public effective
endomorphisms `alpha_i,beta_i` have matrices `A_i,B_i` on `E_i[n]` and are
coherent with `phi`:

```text
A_1 X = X A_0,       B_1 X = X B_0.
```

Assume the `R`-algebra generated by `A_0,B_0` is all of `M_2(R)`.  Then the
simultaneous intertwiner module

```text
I = {Y in M_2(R) : A_1Y=YA_0 and B_1Y=YB_0}
```

is exactly the scalar line `R X`.

If `X_0` is any generator of that line, the determinant-compatible candidate
torsion actions are

```text
lambda X_0,       lambda^2 det(X_0) = d mod n.
```

Since `X_0` and the true invertible action `X` both generate `R X`, there is a
unit `u` with `X_0=uX`; the equation is equivalent to `(lambda*u)^2=1`.
There are exactly two solutions at each odd prime power dividing `n`, hence
exactly

```text
2^omega(n)
```

candidate matrices modulo `n`.  For prime `n`, they are the realized sign pair
`+/-X`; they give the same x-coordinate isogeny map.  For composite `n`, mixed
CRT signs need not lift to isogenies and must be tested by reconstruction.

### Proof

The true `X` is invertible.  For any simultaneous intertwiner `Y`, the matrix
`C=X^(-1)Y` commutes with both `A_0` and `B_0`, hence with the algebra they
generate.  The centralizer of `M_2(R)` inside itself is its center `R I_2`, so
`C=lambda I_2` and `Y=lambda X`.  Conversely every scalar multiple of `X` is
an intertwiner.

Compatibility of the Weil pairing with `phi`, together with the common
pairing generator used by the normalized bases, gives `det(X)=d mod n`.
Since `det(lambda X_0)=lambda^2 det(X_0)`, determinant normalization is the
displayed square-root equation.  The generator condition makes it an equation
of the form `z^2=1`.  Over every odd prime power this has exactly two roots.
The Chinese remainder theorem gives the global count.

The generation hypothesis can be checked locally.  Over a prime field it is
enough to check that `I,A_0,B_0,A_0B_0` span a four-dimensional space.  Over a
prime power, an invertible determinant for these four flattened matrices is a
sufficient certificate.  More general generating sets use the same argument.

## Recovery algorithm

```text
DOUBLE-ORIENTATION-RECOVER(E0,E1,d,n,alpha0,beta0,alpha1,beta1):
    sample symplectic bases U0,U1 of E0[n],E1[n]
    normalize U1 so e_n(U0[0],U0[1]) = e_n(U1[0],U1[1])
    evaluate alpha_i,beta_i and form A_i,B_i
    reject any local factor where <A_0,B_0> is not M_2(Z/ell^e Z)
    solve A_1 X = X A_0 and B_1 X = X B_0
    for each CRT square root satisfying det(X)=d mod n:
        reconstruct phi from the candidate action using interpolation or Kani
        retain candidates whose endpoint/map checks pass
    return the surviving isogeny orbit
```

When `n^2>4d`, standard isogeny interpolation/Kani machinery can recover the
isogeny from its full action on `E0[n]`.  This work changes the information
recovery step, not that reconstruction theorem.

## Charged complexity

Let `C_tor(n)` include construction of both full torsion bases and all field
extensions, and let `C_eval(n)` include four effective orientation evaluations
on those bases.  Solving the constant-size linear system costs only a
polylogarithmic number of operations in `n`.  If `C_rec(d,n)` is the charged
cost of one interpolation or Kani reconstruction, the total is

```text
C_tor(n) + C_eval(n) + O~(log n) + 2^omega(n) C_rec(d,n).
```

For `omega(n)=O(log log q)`, the candidate multiplier is polylogarithmic.  By
contrast, a single quadratic orientation leaves a rank-two intertwiner module;
determinant normalization is a norm conic with approximately `n` candidates
at a prime modulus.  The improvement is therefore in branch entropy, not in
the torsion-field or higher-dimensional backend.

## Cryptographic implication

The result is a conditional public-interface criterion.  If both endpoint
pairs are coherent with the same secret isogeny and generate the full torsion
matrix algebra, the interface projectively determines the secret torsion
action and leaves only determinant-compatible CRT signs.  No deployed or named
protocol is shown to expose this data.

The direct defensive rule is:

```text
Do not expose two coherently transported, efficiently evaluable noncommuting
orientations at both endpoints unless the resulting simultaneous centralizer
has been included in the security analysis.
```

The test is concrete and parameter-local: compute the generated algebra (or
equivalently its centralizer) modulo every reconstruction prime.  Independent
blinding of the second target orientation breaks the intertwining equations;
merely changing bases does not.

This does not by itself change the security estimate of a scheme that exposes
only one orientation.  It is most relevant to future double-oriented group
actions, auxiliary endomorphism certificates, and side channels or transcripts
that reveal a second transported endomorphism action.

## Literature boundary

- Macula--Stange Theorem 12 already recovers a known-degree isogeny's torsion
  action from two known orientations generating a rank-four suborder.  It uses
  sesquilinear pairings, module generators, and discrete logarithms to produce
  a polynomial-size list.  That work has priority for the broad
  two-orientation recovery claim.
- Cha and Chen define double-oriented supersingular isogeny graphs and analyze
  their graph structure.  Their framework supplies the natural setting for
  the theorem and includes a rational scalar-centralizer mechanism, but does
  not state the torsion intertwiner line, determinant normalization, or the
  exact `2^omega(n)` candidate count.
- Eisentraeger and Scullard compute a supersingular endomorphism ring from two
  noncommuting endomorphisms.  Their target is local maximal-order recovery via
  Bruhat-Tits trees and endomorphism division, not direct recovery of a known-
  degree isogeny's torsion matrix between two doubly oriented endpoints.
- Galbraith--Gilchrist--Robert and Castryck et al. supply the one-orientation
  pairing precursor.  De Feo--Fouotsa--Panny classify residual level-structure
  matrix groups and provide the right framework for the conditional protocol
  statement.
- General reductions between EndRing and Isogeny establish broad equivalence,
  but do not give this direct degree-sensitive candidate bound.

The potentially priority-distinct residue is the exact simultaneous-
intertwiner formulation, full-algebra certificate, pairing-light selection
step, and `2^omega(n)` determinant-compatible list-size theorem.  It remains
`NOVELTY-UNVERIFIED` as a sharpening, not as first two-orientation recovery.

## Failure modes and next proof obligations

- The pair may fail to generate the full matrix algebra at primes dividing the
  discriminant of the generated quaternion order.
- A rational transported orientation may have a denominator sharing a factor
  with `n`, preventing effective torsion evaluation.
- Torsion-field construction or Kani reconstruction may dominate even after
  branch collapse.
- Current exact geometry covers degree `2` and prime torsion `3`; no geometric
  prime-power, CRT, or cryptographic-size fixture is claimed.
- The three processes share Sage arithmetic, although the selector is
  secret-isolated and the verifier independently reconstructs geometry.
